### Title & Source Summary

**Episode:** BEMA 502: The Second Pillar, Community  
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon and Brent Billings  
**Focus:** Philippians 2:1–11; Galatians 1:15–2:10; Genesis 1:26–27; Genesis 2:18–25; 1 Corinthians 12:12–27; John 9:35–41; Numbers 11:24–29

This episode covers the second of BEMA's four pillars: **text, community, discipleship and wrestling**. Marty starts by explaining why he puts the pillars in this order for himself. He then defines biblical community as *radical fellowship with people you don't agree with*, which is different from friendship or affinity. He gives three parts of that community: **accountability** (respecting the voices of others), **being only a piece** (no single person carries the whole image of God), and **learning** (other people see facets of truth that I can't). He draws on Impact Campus Ministries' definition of success, Paul's humility hymn in Philippians, Paul's trip to Jerusalem in Galatians, the creation stories, the body of Christ, the man born blind, and the story of Eldad and Medad. He closes with Dietrich Bonhoeffer's warning about loving our *dream* of community more than the real community in front of us.

### Key Takeaways

- **The order of the pillars is personal, not fixed.** Marty goes text → community → discipleship → wrestling. That order fits his own tendencies, but listeners can rearrange the pillars to suit their own needs.
- **Biblical community is not mere friendship or affinity.** It means choosing to stay in relationship with people who differ from us in age, ethnicity, economics, ideology and politics.
- **Spiritual formation is "[never]" a solitary endeavor.** In principle, closeness with God grows inside community. Marty brackets "never" to allow for extreme cases such as solitary confinement.
- **Community can be practiced anywhere.** It can happen at work, at school, in a "third space" like a coffee shop, or in a neighborhood, not only at church.
- **Accountability means respecting other voices because I might be wrong.** Even Paul went back to Jerusalem after 14 years to make sure he "was not running in vain."
- **The gospel calls us to give up selfish ambition.** Philippians 2 says Jesus is exalted *because* he chose to lose for others, not because he defeated his enemies.
- **No single person holds the whole image of God.** Humanity together, "male and female," and the many-membered body of Christ make up the whole.
- **Claiming to see clearly can be its own blindness.** Seeing truly includes knowing there are things you don't see (John 9).
- **Community has fuzzy edges.** The "70 or 72" tradition (Eldad and Medad) reminds us that God's Spirit often works in people outside our boundaries.
- **Idealized community destroys real community.** Bonhoeffer: whoever loves their dream of community more than the community itself becomes its destroyer.
- **Community is a gift you have to go and unwrap, and it is mutual.** We give as well as receive.

### Main Concepts & Theories

#### 1. The Four Pillars and Their Order

Marty explains his personal order:

1. **Text**: This is his foundation. Even in a graduate program where few share his view of Scripture, he holds that the text is "the sword of the Spirit," where God's power moves.
2. **Community**: He puts it second because it is the pillar he most needs to be challenged on. Left to himself, he would put it last.
3. **Discipleship**: He places it after community so that he doesn't turn his own method of discipleship into a rigid, fanatical one.
4. **Wrestling**: It comes last, "like the putt." After building on the first three, he must go back and re-examine everything. He doesn't start with wrestling because that can slide into the "wishy-washy" side of deconstruction.

Brent offers a **stool metaphor**. Three legs (text, community, discipleship) will stand, but the fourth leg (wrestling) makes the stool stable. Marty points out that "three, then four" is a very Jewish pattern.

#### 2. Impact's Definition of Success

Marty reads the definition of success from Impact Campus Ministries, which he leads:

> "Success is developing intimacy with God and community with each other through a living relationship with Jesus."

Two themes run through it:

- **Intimacy with God**: In BEMA language, "create a space; God fills it."
- **Community with one another**: Every mention of "making an impact" is tied to relationships.

We can't control impact or fruitful ministry. We *can* control whether we develop intimacy with God and stay devoted to one another. For Marty, the text is where he finds intimacy with God, the way prayer works for others, but that formation always happens with other people.

#### 3. Community vs. Affinity

- Marty takes loneliness seriously. A recent U.S. Surgeon General called it a public health epidemic, and Marty made a YouTube video titled "Loneliness Sucks." He affirms that friendship and affinity matter deeply.
- The **pillar** of community is something else: **intentionally choosing ongoing relationships with people unlike us**.
- **Jesus' disciples are the model.** His *chavurah* (close group of disciples) included Pharisees, a tax collector, zealots, probably Herodians, and someone with ties to the priests. These people would never have chosen to spend time together, which is why they kept arguing about who was greatest.
- This is not a one-time ministry moment or a retreat. It is how the church is supposed to form itself.

#### 4. Element One: Accountability

Accountability means respecting and valuing other people's voices, because "I think I'm right, but I don't know."

- **Intentional, not blind.** These are relationships built on trust. Some relationships are destructive, and community doesn't mean trusting everyone.
- **Leadership example.** Marty is president and CEO, but he rarely moves forward without his team's buy-in. If he can't get consensus, either his leadership isn't what it should be or the idea isn't good or ready yet.
- **Not manipulation.** Using community as a tool to get your way distorts it.
- **Right-handed vs. left-handed power** (from Reed's chapter in their recent book):
  - *Right-handed power* coerces: "I'm the boss, do what I say."
  - *Left-handed power* invites: rather than yelling at the kids to come to dinner, you bake something and let the smell pull them out of their rooms. It is less efficient but better.
- Brent adds that accountability rests on trusting that **God speaks to the whole team, not just to you.**

##### Philippians 2:1–11: Humility as the Shared Ground

- Marty paraphrases: *If there is anything all Jesus followers should agree on, whether conservative or progressive, young or old, it is this: "Do nothing out of selfish ambition or vain conceit."*
- In Western, consumer-driven culture, this is one of the most radical verses we don't take seriously.
- **The Christ hymn:** Jesus is the one person whose ambition *would have been* pure, and he still didn't pursue it. The point is not whether ambition is pure. The point is God's character.
- **"Therefore God exalted him."** Fundamentalist readings often treat "every knee will bow" as a promise that *we* will be proven right. The passage says the opposite: Jesus is exalted *because* he was humiliated and chose to lose on behalf of others.

##### Galatians 1:15–2:10: Paul's Accountability to Community

- Paul received his gospel directly from Jesus. He went to Arabia, not to the apostles.
- After three years he visited Cephas (Peter) and James.
- **Fourteen years later** he went back with Barnabas and Titus and laid out his gospel before the leaders "to be sure I was not running and had not been running my race in vain."
- **The dance:** Paul has a secure identity and a gospel straight from Jesus ("whatever they were makes no difference to me"), and he still submits to the community. He doesn't make the leaders more than they are, and he doesn't think he can do without them.
- The result: the pillars (James, Cephas, John) gave him "the right hand of fellowship," and they asked only that he remember the poor.

#### 5. Element Two: I Am Only a Piece

##### Genesis 1:26–27

- "In the image of God he created them; **male and female** he created them."
- Marty reads this less as a statement about gender and more as a statement about **diversity**. The image of God is reflected in humanity *together*. It is also a striking early claim that men and women have equal value.

##### Genesis 2:18–25

- Adam (*'adam*, "humankind") starts out alone, and God says, "It is not good."
- God takes a piece out of Adam. Marty stresses that this is not a statement of the woman's subservience. Adam goes from whole to less than whole, and he is complete only together with the woman (*ish* and *ishah*, "man" and "woman").
- "Bone of my bones… one flesh" is about **oneness made from distinct parts**. It applies to marriage, but at its root it is about humanity being made of many parts that together form God's image.

##### 1 Corinthians 12:12–27: The Body of Christ

- Many parts, one body. No part can say "I don't belong," and none can say "I don't need you."
- The parts that **seem weaker are indispensable**, and God gives "greater honor to the parts that lacked it."
- It is **not a zero-sum game**, and not everyone's experience is the same. At different times different parts need special care, much like an injured limb.
- Brent connects this to the **manna** in the wilderness: some gathered more and some less, but everyone had what they needed.

#### 6. Element Three: Learning (The Gem with Seventy Faces)

- A Jewish saying holds that every passage is like a **gem with 70 faces**. Turn it in the light and a different facet shows.
- It is arrogant to assume that what I see, even if I'm right, is the *whole* picture.

##### John 9:35–41: Blindness and Sight

- The healed man says, "Who is he, sir? Tell me so that I may believe." That is humble openness.
- Jesus tells the Pharisees, "Now that you claim you can see, your guilt remains."
- **Claiming to see clearly creates blindness. Admitting what you don't see is true sight.** Community gives us the eyes we lack.

##### Why 70? The Number of Community

- **70** = 7 × 10, complete community:
  - 70 went down to Egypt (Genesis 46:27; Exodus 1:5).
  - 70 elders at Sinai.
  - Jesus sends out 70, or 72, depending on the Gospel manuscript (Luke 10).

##### Numbers 11:24–29: Eldad and Medad

- Seventy elders gather at the tent and prophesy. Two men on the list, **Eldad and Medad**, stayed in the camp, and the Spirit rested on them too.
- Joshua says, "Stop them!" Moses answers, "Are you jealous for my sake? I wish all the Lord's people were prophets."
- Marty prefers to say **72** because it reminds him that **community doesn't fit neatly in a box**. Members of God's community often show up where they "aren't supposed to be." Community should not be used to exclude people.

#### 7. Tradition, Liturgy, and Consumerism

- High-church traditions (Catholic, Lutheran, Anglican, Episcopal) have a shared history of belief and practice to lean on.
- Non-denominational traditions often avoid ritual for the sake of freedom and autonomy, which makes intentional community even more important.
- In a consumer culture, even liturgy can stand in for real relationships. Church used to sit inside a shared village life of farming, bartering and living together. Now it is often an event people go to consume.

#### 8. Closing Principles

- **Bonhoeffer (*Life Together*):** "Every human wish and dream that is injected into the Christian community is a hindrance to genuine community and must be banished if genuine community is to survive. He who loves his dream of community more than the Christian community itself becomes a destroyer of the latter…"
- **Community is a gift we must go after.** It doesn't just happen to us. We have to unwrap it.
- **Community is mutual.** "What am I getting out of it?" is only half the question. We are givers as well as receivers, just as in marriage and at work.

### Examples & Applications

- **The workplace team:** Marty leads by consensus instead of title. A manager could set a team norm that big decisions wait for real buy-in, and treat pushback as useful information rather than an obstacle.
- **The graduate cohort:** Marty chooses to engage classmates he likes and ones he doesn't. A student could pick a study partner from a different background or viewpoint.
- **Third spaces:** A regular coffee shop, gym, sports bar or neighborhood association can become a place for real, cross-difference community.
- **Left-handed power at home:** Parents can draw children in by invitation and warmth, the "smell of the cookies," instead of commands and threats.
- **Church frustration:** Before leaving a church because it falls short of an ideal, ask whether you are loving your *dream* of community more than the people in front of you.
- **Paul's 14-year check-in:** Even seasoned leaders, pastors and teachers benefit from bringing their core convictions back to trusted peers for review.
- **The "72" posture:** When someone outside your group or tradition shows real spiritual fruit, respond like Moses ("I wish all were prophets"), not like Joshua ("Stop them!").
- **Starting a BEMA group:** Brent invites listeners to use the BEMA website's groups and contact pages. The goal is not to find people like you. As he puts it, "It doesn't make it easy… but it is simple."

### Potential Areas for Further Exploration

- **The *chavurah* model:** How rabbis formed disciple groups, and how the mix of backgrounds among Jesus' twelve affected their dynamics.
- **Right- and left-handed power:** Reed's chapter in the BEMA hosts' recent book, plus related ideas in Henri Nouwen's and Richard Rohr's writing on power and powerlessness.
- **Philippians 2:6–11 as an early Christian hymn:** Scholarly debate about *kenosis* (self-emptying) and how the passage reverses Roman ideas of honor and status.
- **Galatians 2 and the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15):** How Paul's account relates to Luke's, and what this says about authority and accountability in the early church.
- **The image of God as communal:** Reading Genesis 1–2 alongside Trinitarian theology and Jewish readings of *'adam*.
- **The number 70 in Scripture and Jewish tradition:** The 70 nations of Genesis 10, the 70 elders, the Sanhedrin, the Septuagint, and the "70 faces of Torah" (*shivim panim laTorah*).
- **The 70/72 manuscript question in Luke 10:1:** Textual criticism and possible links to Numbers 11.
- **Bonhoeffer's *Life Together*:** Written for the underground seminary at Finkenwalde, with its strong critique of idealized community.
- **Loneliness and modern isolation:** The Surgeon General's 2023 advisory on loneliness, and how faith communities might respond.
- **Liturgy and consumerism:** How high-church and low-church traditions each help and hinder deep relationships today.

### Comprehension Questions

1. Why does Marty place community *second* among the four pillars, and why does he avoid starting with wrestling? How does Brent's stool metaphor describe the relationship among the pillars?
2. How does Marty tell the *pillar* of community apart from friendship and affinity, and how does the makeup of Jesus' disciples illustrate the difference?
3. In Galatians 1–2, how does Paul show both a secure personal identity and real accountability to community? What does "to be sure I was not running… in vain" show about his posture?
4. How does Marty reinterpret "every knee will bow" in Philippians 2:9–11, and why does this reading matter for how Christians handle disagreement?
5. What do the "gem with 70 faces" saying and the story of Eldad and Medad in Numbers 11 teach about the limits of our own perspective and the boundaries of community?

### Personal Summary

This episode argues that following Jesus can't be done alone, and that the community it requires is harder than friendship. Marty presents community as accountability (I might be wrong), incompleteness (I am only one piece of God's image), and learning (others see facets of the gem that I can't). Jesus gave up even pure ambition and was exalted because he chose to lose for others. Paul, with a gospel straight from Jesus, still went back to check it with people. In the same way, we are called to humbly stay in relationship with people unlike us. The "72" reminds us that God's Spirit doesn't stay inside our boundaries, and Bonhoeffer's warning reminds us that chasing an ideal community can destroy the real one. The practical challenge is to stop waiting for the perfect group, go find the imperfect people nearby, and show up ready to give as well as receive.
