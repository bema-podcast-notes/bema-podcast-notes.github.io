### Title & Source Summary

**Episode:** 498 – The Four Pillars: Text (Session 10, Part 1 of 12)  
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon and Brent Billings  
**Focus:** The first of BEMA's four pillars, the text. Passages: Romans 3:1–4; Isaiah 55:6–13; Ezekiel 40:4; Psalm 40:6 and Jeremiah 23:29 (mentioned); Deuteronomy 17:18–20; Joshua 1:7–9; Hosea 14; Proverbs 2; Romans 11:1–24

This episode opens Session 10 and starts a 12-part series on the four pillars of BEMA: **text, community, discipleship, and wrestling**. The idea came from a listener on Marty's "BEMA request line" YouTube video. Each pillar gets three episodes. Marty opens each one with his own reflections, and two other teachers will share their views without knowing what he said. Marty begins with the text. He tells how mystical mentors, his trips to Israel and Turkey with Ray Vander Laan, and his own Jewish identity came together to make the text his central spiritual practice. He and Brent then read through his favorite passages about the power of God's words. The episode closes with Romans 11, where Marty argues that the text is what the Jewish people bring to the Jew–Gentile relationship. He ends with practical ways to "create space" in the text.

### Key Takeaways

- **The four pillars are text, community, discipleship, and wrestling.** Marty first put them into words after his 2008 and 2010 trips with Ray Vander Laan. His thought was: "If the Greeks could change the world with four pillars, so can we."
- **The pillars are a "pocket-sized" summary** of a large body of teaching. They are like the 30-second version of a trip story that you need ready when you get home.
- **Marty's path to the text was unusual.** Mentors who leaned toward mysticism first taught him prayer and a personal relationship with the risen Jesus. Only later did God bring him "all the nerdy, crazy stuff."
- **The Essenes at Qumran became his model.** They gave their whole lives to knowing, copying, and preserving the text. Ray Vander Laan's question, "Where are the Essenes today?", changed Marty's life.
- **For Marty, studying the text became a form of prayer.** It joined his mysticism, his Jewish identity, his bookish nature, and his commitment to scholarship.
- **God's word never returns void (Isaiah 55).** Marty believes the text has a supernatural power that a blog post or a song we write does not have.
- **God's leaders are called to stay close to the text.** The king had to write out his own copy of Torah (Deuteronomy 17). Joshua was told to meditate on it day and night (Joshua 1).
- **The text is what the Jewish people brought to the Jew–Gentile relationship** (Romans 3; Romans 11). When the church split from Judaism, it lost something important.
- **Honor the text without worshiping it.** The text is not Jesus. The goal is to put Scripture in its proper place, not to make it an idol (sometimes called bibliolatry).
- **There are many ways to engage the text.** You can memorize, read and meditate, study, write it out, listen, or make art from it. Even small seeds bear fruit.

### Main Concepts & Theories

#### 1. Origin and Structure of the Four Pillars

- **Where the idea came from:** After studying with Ray Vander Laan in Israel (2008) and in Israel and Turkey (2010), Marty wanted a short way to sum up what had changed him. Learning about Hellenism had exposed some of his own idolatries. So he deliberately set "four pillars" against Hellenism's four pillars.
- **The four pillars:**
  1. **Text:** the Scriptures as the Word of God.
  2. **Community:** how the early church lived out fellowship together.
  3. **Discipleship:** a particular way of raising up a chosen few as leaders who are set apart.
  4. **Wrestling:** asking questions and struggling with God, inside and out. Marty grew up in a setting that discouraged this. Wrestling runs through all the other pillars.
- **How the series works:** Three episodes per pillar. Marty gives his "2026 perspective" first. Two other voices follow, each starting with a blank slate. Session 7's series on spiritual disciplines used the same approach.
- **Background:** The pillars were first introduced near the end of Session 5. Marty and Brent call those closing episodes some of BEMA's best.

#### 2. Marty's Journey: Mysticism Meets Scholarship

- **Mystical mentors:** Steve Edwards and Bill Westfall took Marty on as a self-confident 22-year-old church leader. They were not scholars. They taught him prayer and contemplation, drawing on the early Christian mystics, and showed him a personal relationship with the *risen* Christ, not just with a historical figure.
- **Prayer felt like a second language:** Contemplation went against Marty's cerebral, bookish nature. Still, he kept at it because he saw what Jesus had done in his mentors' lives.
- **Qumran:** Standing above the ruins where the Dead Sea Scrolls were found, Ray taught about a community (linked to the "sons of Zadok") that committed itself to the text. When people asked for "the ancient paths" (Jeremiah 6:16), these people would be ready with an answer. A friend shouted into the valley, "I want to be an Essene!" Marty sat there in tears, agreeing.
- **A call tied to his Jewish identity:** Ray told Marty that a Gentile should be able to "grab you by your beard and say, tell me about Torah." Marty asked whether this kind of devotion to the text could count as prayer for him. Ray said yes.
- **Where it all met:** The text became the place where Marty's mysticism, Jewish heritage, belief in the Holy Spirit, cerebral nature, practice of contemplative prayer, and scholarship (both "rogue" and formal) came together.

#### 3. Marty's Favorite Passages About the Text

| Passage | Main Idea |
|---|---|
| **Romans 3:1–4** | The Jews were "entrusted with the very words (oracles) of God." For Marty, this is a calling to keep and protect Torah. |
| **Isaiah 55:6–13** | At the center of a poem about repentance and renewal sits God's word, which "will not return to me empty." |
| **Ezekiel 40:4** | The verbs mix intensity: "look" with the eyes but "hear" (*shema*) with the ears. Trust God's word over what you see. |
| **Psalm 40:6 / Jeremiah 23:29** | "My ears you have pierced (chiseled)." The rabbis linked this to God's word as a hammer that breaks rock. The text opens stopped-up ears. |
| **Deuteronomy 17:18–20** | Out of everything a king could do, God requires him to write out his own copy of Torah and read it daily. |
| **Joshua 1:7–9** | Before the conquest begins, Joshua's basic tool is the book of the law, kept on his lips and meditated on day and night. |
| **Hosea 14:1–9** | "Take words with you and return to the Lord." Marty reads these as God's words, not just our own words of confession. |
| **Proverbs 2:1–11** | Search for wisdom like hidden treasure. Marty argues the place to dig is Scripture, not abstract philosophy. |
| **Romans 11:1–24** | The olive tree. Jews and Gentiles belong together, and the text is part of what Israel brings. |

#### 4. People of the Ears (Ezekiel 40:4)

- In Hebrew, **shema** (hear) means much more than "listen." It includes taking something in and responding to it.
- One would expect "look and listen" or "see and hear." Ezekiel 40:4 mixes them: *look* with your eyes, *hear* with your ears.
- Ray taught that in rabbinic discussion, God wants people who use their eyes but whose deepest trust rests on what they *hear*, which is His word.
- **Main point:** People naturally trust what they can see and treat what they hear as an extra. God reverses that order, and the text trains us to be "people of the ears."

#### 5. The Text and Leadership

- Deuteronomy 17 and Joshua 1 both put the text at the center of a leader's life.
- In Ezra and Nehemiah, when the community is in crisis, someone stands up and reads Torah. Ezra reads it all day, even in the rain.
- Marty's warning: our culture pushes leaders toward platform and celebrity. God calls them to humility, to knowing His text, and to leading out of lives changed by it.
- This does not mean memorizing verses makes someone a spiritual giant. It means that God's word, received by a humble seeker, has real power.

#### 6. The Text and the Jew–Gentile Relationship (Romans 11)

- **Context (Romans 9–11):** God works through His covenant people, Israel. He has also spared and saved the Gentiles, "vessels" one might have expected Him to destroy.
- **Elijah and the remnant:** Elijah thought he was the only faithful one left. First-century Jews surrounded by Greco-Roman idolatry may have felt the same way. God had kept 7,000 who had not bowed to Baal. God is often at work in ways we cannot see.
- **Hardening and stumbling:** When God's message is taken over for some other religious agenda, God blinds those who claim to see. What they hold most dear becomes the thing they trip over.
- **The olive tree:** Gentiles are wild branches grafted in. "You do not support the root, but the root supports you." Gentiles should not be arrogant. Jews should remember that their own story was always about including the Gentiles.
- **A new humanity (Ephesians 2):** Jesus tears down the dividing wall and makes one new humanity out of two.
- **Marty's claim:** What the Jewish people brought to this relationship was the text, held in a unique way. When Jews and Gentiles split in the first and second centuries, Gentile Christianity lost much of that.

#### 7. Why "BEMA" Means Text

- The **bema** was the raised platform in the synagogue where the text was read.
- The pillar is **text**, not history or Jewish hermeneutics, though both serve it. The vision is a people who gather around the text.
- **Guardrail:** The text is not Jesus. It should be put in its proper place, not worshiped.

#### 8. Creating Space

- In Session 1, BEMA said that when we make space for God, He fills it.
- Prayer, fasting, feasting, and liturgy all create space. Time in the text is one more way, and that investment "will never return void."

### Examples & Applications

- **Memorize:** Learn one verse a week. With a couple of weeks off, that is about 50 verses a year, which Marty thinks would change anyone's spiritual growth. (Brent plans to start by adding to his memorized portion of Isaiah 55.)
- **Read and meditate:** Use a YouVersion plan or any regular habit. Simply *listen* to the text and sit with it, without turning it into study.
- **Study, but not only study:** Marty studies 40 to 60 minutes every morning. He warns that people tend to default to study and skip the other practices.
- **Write it out:** Marty has typed one page of the Bible a day for almost 18 years and is finishing 2 Chronicles, the last book of the Hebrew Tanakh. His wife, his children, and most of his disciples write it out by hand in notebooks. This follows the king in Deuteronomy 17 and the Essenes.
- **Listen:** Play an audio Bible on a long commute. Marty once listened to Philippians about 50 times in one week.
- **Make art:** Marty's daughter responds to Scripture with painting, collage, and poetry.
- **Read aloud to others:** Brent reads Scripture every week on an Instagram live stream to keep himself accountable. He encourages listeners to read aloud to their families. For most of history, God's people met the text by *hearing* it.
- **A real-world comparison:** Think of a trip story. People who travel are encouraged to have a 30-second version ready. The four pillars do the same job for a large body of teaching.

### Personalized Summary

This episode brings BEMA back to its foundations. Marty starts with the first pillar, **text**, and tells how it became his own. Mentors who leaned toward mysticism taught him to know the living Jesus. Then a lesson above Qumran left him longing to be "an Essene," devoted to knowing God's words. Through his Jewish heritage and Ray Vander Laan's encouragement, he came to see the text as a form of prayer. It is the place where his contemplative side and his scholarly side meet. His favorite passages share one conviction: God's word has a living power that never returns void. That power shows up in Isaiah's renewal, in the king's handwritten Torah, in Joshua's daily meditation, and in Hosea's call to "take words with you." In Romans 11, Marty argues that the text is the gift the Jewish people bring to one shared Jew–Gentile family. That is why the movement is named after the bema, the platform where Scripture was read. The challenge to listeners is simple. Pick one way to make space in the text, such as memorizing a verse a week, writing out a page, listening on the commute, or reading aloud to your family, and trust that even the smallest seed will grow.
