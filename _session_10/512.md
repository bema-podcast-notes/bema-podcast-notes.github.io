### Title & Source Summary

**Episode:** 512, "BEMA 10-Year Anniversary Live Episode" (celebrating 10 years and 500+ episodes)  
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon and Brent Billings, with Reed Dent and Elle Grover Fricks; Zack Dean as unofficial co-host and emcee; a closing prayer from the companion prayer writer; recorded in front of more than 100 live listeners  
**Focus:** Matthew 24:45–51, Psalm 16, Deuteronomy 6:4–5 (the Shema), the boundary-stone teaching (Deuteronomy 19:14; Proverbs 22:28), Samson and the jawbone (Judges 15), Hebrews 11–12, and the ten-year story of the BEMA community

This live episode marks ten years of the BEMA podcast. Part celebration, part reflection and part memorial, it brings together the hosts, their families and a live audience. Marty gives three words for the decade: grateful, honored and determined. Elle shares the BEMA lessons that changed her life. Reed reads an original essay about walking the Camino de Santiago. Brent looks back on the global community that has grown around the podcast. After a light-hearted "truth and a lie" game, the hosts answer audience questions about secondary resources, AI, growing theologically, "trusting the story," and staying in a local church. The evening ends with Hebrews 11–12, the "cloud of witnesses," as Marty remembers his late father and their late friend and colleague Josh. He then sets out a vision for the next ten years, and the evening closes with a prayer.

### Key Takeaways

- **Faithful stewardship is the posture for the future.** Marty uses Jesus's parable of the faithful and wise steward (Matthew 24:45–51) to say BEMA must handle well what God has given: the text, theology and practice.
- **BEMA wants to be a "first word," not the last word.** The goal is to start people on a lifetime of study, not to hand them final answers.
- **"Bless God" redirects the credit.** Following Ray Vander Laan, Marty points the thanks he receives back to God, who is the one at work.
- **Forgiveness is a burden you choose to carry.** From Samson's jawbone, Elle learned that forgiving someone means taking the cost onto yourself, and that God meets and restores the one who does.
- **Don't let your boundary stones be moved.** Elle had let others shrink the calling God gave her, believing women could not teach or lead, until a teacher named her gifts.
- **The Shema's *me'od* ("very-ness") welcomes the whole person.** Loving God "with all your very" means bringing the full, particular self God made, not an edited version.
- **Everyone reaches the end of their own strength before God's grace.** In Reed's Camino essay, every pilgrim arrives at a point their own legs cannot carry them past, and God's grace is wide enough for all of them.
- **Let the primary text be primary.** Commentaries, podcasts and AI should send us deeper into Scripture, not replace it.
- **Growth means looking back on your past self with some embarrassment.** Marty expects to cringe in ten years at what he says today, and treats that as healthy.
- **The cloud of witnesses includes people we have known.** Hebrews 11–12 says the faith of those who came before is "made perfect" only together with us, which gives present faithfulness lasting weight.
- **A vision for the next decade:** presence over celebrity, growth over comfort, joy over leisure, left-handed testimony over right-handed power, Christ's humility over selfish ambition, and trusting the story over the security of certainty.

### Main Concepts & Theories

#### 1. Three Words for Ten Years: Grateful, Honored, Determined

Marty ends each quarterly board report with three words describing where he is. For the anniversary he chose:

1. **Grateful:** for working with friends on something he loves, for supporters who see value in the ministry, and for God inviting people to partner with him. Many listeners tell Marty that BEMA "saved my ability to read the Bible" or "saved my faith." He sees this as God's work, not the team's genius.
2. **Honored:** that people trust the team with deeply personal stories. Twenty years ago Marty stood in a church lobby thanking a teacher himself, so he knows what that moment feels like. Ray Vander Laan taught his students to answer thanks with "Bless God," turning the attention back to God.
3. **Determined:** to steward well whatever God has given. Marty reads **Matthew 24:45–51**. The faithful servant is found doing his job when the master returns. The wicked servant, who assumes the master is delayed, faces judgment.

**What faithful stewardship covers:**

- The words of God (the biblical text)
- Theology (how ideas about God are formed and held)
- Praxis (practice and application)
- A "first word" (opening the conversation, not closing it)

#### 2. Elle's Life-Changing BEMA Lessons

Elle first met BEMA as an undergraduate at Washington State University, where Marty ran a campus ministry. She shares three lessons, each paired with a portion of **Psalm 16**.

##### Lesson One: Dropping the Jawbone (Judges 15)

- On a study trip in Israel, Marty pointed to the field where Samson released the foxes (Judges 15:4–5) and set the Philistine fields on fire.
- The lesson's core: **forgiveness is a burden we choose to take on.** When someone wrongs us and we forgive, the debt does not disappear. We absorb it.
- When we "die to ourselves" this way, God meets us, takes the burden up, and brings us back to life.
- Elle says this lesson kept her from becoming "a bitter and lonely person."

##### Lesson Two: Boundary Stones

- In the ancient world, boundary stones marked the land God had given a family. Moving a neighbor's stone was theft (Deuteronomy 19:14; Proverbs 22:28).
- In a stone amphitheater, Marty told the group that "some of us have allowed our boundary stones to be moved farther than they should be."
- Elle realized she had accepted teaching that women could not teach or lead, which shrank the calling God gave her.
- The moment mirrored what Ray Vander Laan once did for Marty: naming a calling in a student and inviting them to step into it.

##### Lesson Three (Still Learning): *Me'od* in the Shema

- **The Shema** (Deuteronomy 6:4–5) commands loving God with all your heart, soul and *me'od*.
- ***Me'od*** literally means "very." Loving God "with all your very-ness" means bringing everything that makes you you.
- Elle once believed she had to edit herself to fit ministry spaces. She is learning that the particular way God made each person is intended, and enough.

#### 3. Reed's Camino Reflection: Burdens and Grace

Reed read an essay from his 2019 sabbatical, when he and family and friends walked 63 miles of the Camino de Santiago, from Lugo to Santiago de Compostela, over six days.

**Key images and movements:**

- **The pack as burden:** at first awkward and unbalanced, the backpack soon became "a home." Pilgrims unpacked and repacked their burdens every day, like "darkly comedic prayers of the hours."
- **The closed tomb:** the Cathedral of St. James was under renovation, so the group worshiped at another church. Reed confidently said God "does not live in temples made by human hands," then struggled with that idea himself.
- **The church door:** packs had to be left outside. Others dropped theirs and walked in. Reed refused, guarding his "dirty laundry, earplugs, identification" from imagined thieves, "like Gollum with his ring" and like the older brother outside the prodigal son's party (Luke 15).
- **Self-imposed exclusion:** "my judgment was self-imposed. How could I ever again speak of God excluding anyone?"
- **The call to worship:** music from inside drew him in barefoot. A friend whispered, "I'm glad you're here."
- **The end of the world:** at Muxía on the Costa da Morte, a chapel's doors open onto the sea. Reed realized that however far each pilgrim walked or however heavy their pack, everyone reaches a point where their own legs can carry them no farther. Everyone stands on equal ground before God's vast grace.

**Theological point:** we often cling to burdens out of fear, even when grace invites us to set them down. God's grace levels every difference in effort, distance and merit.

#### 4. Brent's Reflection: A Global Community

- In 2012, Brent heard Marty "yelling and jumping up and down" in a church conference room in Moscow, Idaho. He joined the next BEMA class, then took it a second time because he had "absorbed 20% at best."
- The first surprise was September 2018, when a couple from Illinois asked to meet the team in person.

#### 5. Audience Q&A: Key Answers

##### Q1: How do we balance reading the Bible with using secondary resources?

- **Elle:** she works in "input seasons" and "output seasons." During creative work she shelves secondary sources and stays in the primary text so she doesn't absorb other voices without noticing.
- **Marty:** structure. He gives the same daily time to each kind of study, regardless of how he feels, and trusts the Spirit to work over time.
- **Reed:** as he gets older he consults secondary materials less. His community practices reading Scripture aloud for an hour at a time with no analysis or discussion, so the primary text can stay primary.

##### Q2: Should we use AI for PaRDeS (layered Jewish interpretation)?

- **PaRDeS** is a rabbinic acronym for four levels of interpretation: *Peshat* (plain), *Remez* (hint), *Derash* (searching or homiletic) and *Sod* (secret or mystical).
- **Elle's guidance:**
  - If you use AI at all, treat it like a library. Ask it for **primary sources**, then go read them yourself.
  - Tools like Sefaria already link classic commentaries to each verse without AI.
  - The danger: "we don't know what we don't know." For example, AI might say "all Egyptians practiced circumcision" because it is drawing on Herodotus, whose bias against Egyptians a non-specialist wouldn't spot.
- **Reed:** AI can "erase the wrestling," and wrestling is central to BEMA's approach.
- **Marty:** like the Tower of Babel, technology is a tool. The question is whether it serves God's justice and care for creation or selfish ambition. His graduate paper explored how AI could serve the poor, which it mostly isn't being used for.

##### Q3: What does Marty cringe at in the 2016 episodes?

- Mostly language and jokes that now seem demeaning, such as a flippant remark about Sarah's attractiveness at 90.
- Statements made with "way more confidence" than they deserved.
- His principle: we should *hope* our future selves know more and believe differently. God still uses us at every stage.

##### Q4: How does "trusting the story" relate to a fallen creation? (Reed)

- "Trusting the story" means believing the story begins in Genesis 1, where creation is **"very good,"** not with the fall.
- If the story starts with sin, brokenness becomes something we resign ourselves to. If it starts with goodness, brokenness feels wrong, and that tension is the point.
- Repentance is a call back to who we already were, not a call to become someone else.
- **Personal application:** Reed extends trusting the story to people who have hurt him. Instead of "outsourcing wrath to God," he asks with curiosity, "If you're beloved and created good, what happened?" He gives up his claim to judgment and vengeance.

##### Q5: How do I engage a local church that doesn't read Scripture this way?

- **Elle:**
  - Look for partner communities that share these values.
  - Honor other traditions by asking *why* a reading matters to them, since it usually has a long history.
  - Don't be the person who knows more than everyone. "Be nice to preachers."
- **Reed:**
  - The body of Christ is united by "the spirit of unity and the bond of peace" (Ephesians 4:3), not a shared lens.
  - The kingdom grows like yeast: small and hidden.
  - Paraphrasing Pope John Paul II, the right context for theological conversation is love and mutual friendship. Be a friend first.
- **Marty:**
  - The problem was never the content itself but the **posture** we hold toward certainty and curiosity.
  - Using the "good eye" from Season 3, look for what is beautiful. At his own church, contemplative teachers with very different methods enrich him.

##### Q6: What do you admire in your co-hosts?

| Host | What others admire |
| --- | --- |
| **Brent** | Attention to detail, commitment to excellence, hospitality of spirit, never judging people, real friendship with people everywhere, the "companion voice" |
| **Marty** | Finishes everything he starts, a "sparkle in the eye" that leads people beyond the horizon, the driving force that gets things done |

#### 6. The Cloud of Witnesses (Hebrews 11–12)

On study trips Marty teaches this passage in the stadium at **Aphrodisias** (Turkey).

**The flow of the text:**

1. **Hebrews 11:1:** faith is confidence in what we hope for and assurance about what we do not see.
2. **The Hall of Faith:** Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham and Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Moses, Rahab, Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David and Samuel. All of them have messy, human stories.
3. **Hebrews 11:13–16:** they died "without receiving the things promised," seeing them "from a distance," as "foreigners and strangers."
4. **The faceless multitudes:** people who were tortured, imprisoned and stoned. "The world was not worthy of them."
5. **Hebrews 11:39–40:** "none of them received what had been promised, since God had planned something better for us, so that only together with us would they be made perfect."
6. **Hebrews 12:1–3:** surrounded by this great cloud of witnesses, we throw off what hinders and run with perseverance, fixing our eyes on Jesus.

**The image:** the arena stands are full of witnesses, from the patriarchs to the apostles to unnamed believers, cheering the current runners. Following Ray Vander Laan, Marty invites people to picture those *they personally knew* in the stands.

**A personal turn:** for years Marty taught this lesson without anyone specific in his own "stands." This year he taught it after losing his father (October) and Josh (a month later), and pictured them "sitting just right to my left."

#### 7. Remembering Josh: A Vision for the Next Ten Years

Josh's last teaching series was on **Qoheleth (Ecclesiastes)**: mortality, death and what really matters. Drawing on that series and Hebrews, Marty offered a list of what he believes Josh would say about the next decade:

| Not this... | ...but this |
| --- | --- |
| Celebrity | Presence with real people |
| Comfort | Growth (the right kind) |
| Leisure | Joy |
| Right-handed power | Left-handed testimony |
| Selfish ambition | The humility of Christ |
| Listeners, likes and shares | Listening, loving and empathy |
| What we are owed | What we give |
| A flurry of accomplishment | The affirmation of Sabbath |
| The security of certainty | Trusting the story |

**"Left-handed" vs. "right-handed":** a recurring BEMA image. "Right-handed" power is the culturally favored kind: strength, dominance, control. "Left-handed" testimony is the unexpected, humble, subversive way God often works (compare Ehud in Judges 3).

The companion prayer asked God to help the community steward its part in the story with "presence, growth, joy, justice, humility, love, empathy, generosity, Sabbath, and peace," and to "bless our beginnings, our endings, and our epilogues."

### Examples & Applications

- **Stewardship at work:** a manager who inherits a successful team can coast or keep investing. Matthew 24 challenges anyone entrusted with something good (a job, family, ministry or talent) to keep tending it, not assume the "master is delayed."
- **Forgiveness as absorbing cost:** forgiving a friend who borrowed money and never repaid it doesn't make the loss disappear. You choose to carry it rather than demand it back. Elle's lesson says God meets us in exactly that choice.
- **Checking your boundary stones:** many people, especially women in some church settings, have accepted narrower limits on their calling than God intended. One application is to ask a trusted mentor, "What gifts do you see in me that I might be ignoring?"
- **Bringing your "very-ness":** someone who hides their humor, creativity or intellect at church to fit in can practice the *me'od* principle by bringing their whole self into community.
- **Guarding the pack:** like Reed outside the church, people often protect resentments, reputations or control, missing worship and connection because they won't set them down.
- **Responsible AI use:** instead of asking a chatbot, "What does this verse mean?", ask, "What ancient sources discuss this verse?", then read those sources directly (for example on Sefaria) and check who wrote them and what biases they had.
- **Being a friend before a reformer:** a church member eager to share new insights might first host a meal or help a neighbor in a crisis. Trust built in ordinary life opens the door to deeper conversations.
- **Your own cloud of witnesses:** at a funeral or anniversary of a loss, Hebrews 12 invites you to picture that person cheering you on, and to see your faithfulness today as part of completing their story.
- **Auditing your aims:** a church, nonprofit or business could use Josh's list as a self-check. Are we chasing visibility or presence? Comfort or growth? Metrics or real care?

### Potential Areas for Further Exploration

1. **The parables of readiness in Matthew 24–25:** how the faithful servant, the ten virgins and the talents work together as teaching on stewardship and waiting.
2. **Samson and forgiveness:** a closer study of Judges 14–16 and BEMA's "Dropping the Jawbone" lesson.
3. **Boundary stones in the Torah and wisdom literature:** Deuteronomy 19:14, 27:17; Proverbs 22:28, 23:10; and their links to land, inheritance and justice.
4. **The Shema and *me'od*:** how Jewish interpretation has understood "all your strength or very-ness," including rabbinic readings tying *me'od* to material resources.
5. **Women's leadership in Scripture:** Deborah, Huldah, Phoebe, Junia and Priscilla, and how readings of 1 Timothy 2 have shaped church practice.
6. **Pilgrimage theology:** the Camino de Santiago, the Psalms of Ascent (Psalms 120–134) and biblical pilgrimage feasts as spiritual practice.
7. **PaRDeS and Jewish hermeneutics:** the four levels of interpretation and how to use them responsibly.
8. **Ethics of AI and the Tower of Babel:** Genesis 11 as a lens on technology, empire and human ambition.
9. **"Trusting the story" and original goodness:** Genesis 1–3 and different traditions on human nature (Augustinian original sin vs. Eastern Orthodox and Jewish emphases).
10. **Hebrews 11–12 in context:** Hebrews' audience, the stadium imagery of Aphrodisias, and the meaning of being "made perfect" together.
11. **Qoheleth (Ecclesiastes):** Josh's final series on mortality, *hevel* ("vapor") and what truly matters.
12. **Left-handed vs. right-handed power:** Ehud (Judges 3), the Benjaminite slingers (Judges 20:16) and the upside-down power of the cross.
13. **Spiral dynamics and faith development:** Elle's episode on developmental frameworks and how communities grow.

### Personalized Summary

This anniversary episode is a reminder that a ministry is ultimately made of people: their stories, their friendships and their losses. Marty's three words (grateful, honored, determined) frame the decade as a gift to be stewarded, not an achievement to be admired. Elle's testimony shows how one well-timed lesson can change a life: forgiveness as chosen burden, refusing to let your boundary stones be moved, and bringing your whole *me'od* to God. Reed's Camino essay invites you to ask which "packs" you are guarding at the door while grace calls you inside, and promises that every pilgrim, however far they walked, ends up on equal ground before God's vast grace.

The Q&A offers practical wisdom. Let the primary text stay primary. Use tools like AI to go deeper, not to skip the wrestling. Expect your future self to grow beyond you. Trust that the story starts with "very good." Love your local church through friendship before critique. The evening's emotional center is Hebrews 11–12. Those who went before us, including people we have personally loved and lost, are made complete only together with us. As you think about your own next ten years, consider Josh's list: choose presence over celebrity, growth over comfort, joy over leisure, humility over ambition, and trusting the story over the security of certainty. Run your race in a way that would make the witnesses in the stands smile.
