### Title & Source Summary

**Episode:** 496 – Session 10 Introduction: Mourning, Slowing Down, and What Comes Next  
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon and Brent Billings  
**Focus:** The launch of BEMA Session 10; grief and Jewish mourning practices (shiva and the 40-day mourning period, with reference to Job's friends); plans for the season, ministry updates, and BEMA's mission to college students

This episode opens Session 10 (also called Season 10) of the BEMA Discipleship Podcast. It comes right after the death of beloved co-host Josh Bossé, which was shared at the end of Session 9. Marty and Brent explain how the Jewish pattern of mourning has shaped their approach. The new season will move at a slower, every-other-week pace. That pace honors Josh's unhurried posture toward life, and it also fits Marty's busy year and Brent's coming sabbatical. The hosts lay out the season's content: interviews, a long-form teaching series from each host, and a memorial episode for Josh. They also cover the Session 2 reboot, new YouVersion reading plans, the work of team member Amber Miller, website features, a financial update, and a reminder that BEMA's core mission is discipling college students.

### Key Takeaways

- **Grief needs a formal space.** Jewish mourning gives structure to grief: seven days of *shiva* followed by 33 more days, for 40 days in total. When the formal period ends, grief is not finished. Life is re-engaged, but it is never the same as before.
- **Slowing down is an act of honor.** Session 10 will release roughly every other Thursday. This breaks a near-weekly rhythm that has held since 2016, and it reflects Josh's habit of being fully present instead of hurrying.
- **Season content:**
  - Interviews with Brad Gray and Brad Nelson on the Lord's Prayer, and with David Rudolph, timed near Passover
  - A long-form series from each host, including the four pillars of BEMA, the women of Joshua and Judges (about 11 episodes), and the books of Samuel (about 16 episodes)
  - Occasional movie episodes
  - A memorial episode for Josh, possibly the longest episode BEMA has ever released
- **The Session 2 reboot is the top priority.** Like the Session 1 reboot, the new Session 2 episodes replace the originals in the feed but keep the original dates. The old episodes stay available as legacy content.
- **New listeners should start at Episode 0** and go through Sessions 1–5 in order. Don't skip Session 5, which covers church history and ties the whole BEMA "stream of consciousness" together.
- **New tools are available.** YouVersion reading plans now include binge plans, deep-dive plans, the BEMA liturgy plans, and a roughly 450-day "every verse" whole-Bible plan.
- **BEMA is supported by its listeners.** It runs no ads. Its year-end goals were met and exceeded. The average gift is about $22 a month, which shows how much small, consistent giving matters.
- **College ministry is the heartbeat.** BEMA began as a class for college students, and its long-term vision includes a network of mentors for graduates.

### Main Concepts & Theories

#### 1. The Jewish Framework for Mourning

- **Shiva (seven days):** This comes from the account of Job's friends, who sat with him in silence for seven days (Job 2:13). The point is simply to be present: no explanations, no platitudes, no tidy answers.
- **The remaining 33 days (40 days total):** This is a continued formal period of mourning. Practices include covering mirrors and avoiding grooming, makeup, or anointing.
- **After the formal period:** "There's no timeline on grief." When the formal period ends, the mourner is invited to step back into a life that has kept moving, without being expected to "get over it."
- **Application to the podcast:** The hosts treat all of Session 10 as a "season of mourning." The podcast continues, but at a gentler pace.

#### 2. The Prayer Labyrinth Story: Josh and "the Slow Work of God"

- On a leadership retreat, the team walked a prayer labyrinth in cold, rainy weather while Reed read Pierre Teilhard de Chardin's prayer on trusting "the slow work of God."
- Josh walked so slowly that he took twice as long as Brent, who was deliberately going as slowly as he could.
- Marty felt torn. Part of him wanted Josh to "get with the program," and part of him saw that Josh "actually gets it."
- **The lesson:** Josh did not care about convenience or speed. He did what the moment invited. That posture is the model for Session 10.

#### 3. Reasons for the Slower Pace

1. **Honoring Josh** and the way he approached life.
2. **Replanning work Josh had started.** He was working on *BEMA Liturgy Part 2* with Elle, and the team now has to rethink that project without him.
3. **Marty's schedule:** three study tours and a book tour from April to August.
4. **Brent's sabbatical:** about 100 days, possibly in late 2027. Brent carries most of the production work, so he needs to get ahead before stepping away.

#### 4. The Four Pillars of BEMA

- Introduced at the end of Session 5 as a deliberate contrast to the "four pillars of Hellenism" from Session 3.
- The four pillars are **text, wrestling, discipleship, and community**.
- In Session 10, each host takes a focus episode on these pillars. Josh's absence is felt especially here. The hosts also grieve a series Josh had planned that will now never be recorded.

#### 5. The Reboot Philosophy

- The original Session 2 dates from 2017–2018. Since then the world has changed, the team has learned more, and Marty has found things he got wrong.
- **Why replace episodes instead of adding new ones?** Long-time listeners have already made the journey, but dozens or even hundreds of new listeners start every day, and they deserve the best foundation available. Keeping the original dates preserves the order: Genesis to Revelation, then church history.
- **The co-hosts are now included.** The original Sessions 1–5 had only Marty and Brent. The reboot can draw on the larger team, for example by expanding the short (15–20 minute) prophet episodes.
- **The reboot companion** was designed largely by Amber Miller. It includes reflection and discussion points, sometimes a curated Spotify playlist, and a closing prayer read by listeners. Dan Gummel, whom the team met through the Bible Project, suggested adding listener voices.

#### 6. BEMA's Organizational Mission

- BEMA is an arm of **Impact Campus Ministries**. It started as a class for students at the University of Idaho and Washington State University.
- Early seasons assumed a local college audience, so the mission itself was never clearly stated to the wider podcast audience.
- **Josh's role:** After his liver transplant, Josh became a virtual campus minister. He connected isolated college students and helped them begin a discipleship journey. BEMA intends to keep that work going and to staff it.
- **Long-term vision:** Graduates are "flung out into the world." BEMA hopes they will be "caught on the other side" by mentors in their own fields who share BEMA's language and worldview.

### Examples & Applications

- **Being present with grieving friends:** Shiva teaches that showing up and staying quiet is often better than offering explanations. A hospital visit or a meal dropped off without advice can do more than a carefully chosen Bible verse.
- **Sabbath-like pacing in a productivity culture:** A ministry deliberately cutting its output in half goes against the cultural pressure to be productive all the time. Individuals can do the same by keeping margin in their calendars during hard seasons.
- **Small, faithful giving:** Most of BEMA's funding comes from people giving around $10–$22 a month. The same principle of many small, faithful acts adding up applies to church volunteering, community service, and habits of spiritual practice.
- **Finding community through the groups map:**
  - The BEMA website map shows independent local groups. Orange pins mark virtual groups that anyone, anywhere can join.
  - A staff member traveling in London contacted a local group and shared a meal with a listener.
  - Brent uses the map to meet listeners when he travels.
- **Re-engaging Scripture:** The YouVersion plans offer several paths. The binge plan pairs one episode a day with readings, the deep dive follows a weekly rhythm, and the "every verse" plan covers the whole Bible in about 450 days.
- **Learning in public:** Marty openly admits past mistakes ("not as rare as you would think"). He shows that growing in understanding means being willing to revise what you have taught before.

### Potential Areas for Further Exploration

1. **Jewish mourning customs:** Study *shiva*, *sheloshim* (the 30-day period), the Mourner's Kaddish, and how they compare with Christian practices of grief and lament.
2. **The Book of Job:** Look at how the friends' silent presence (Job 2:11–13) contrasts with their later failed attempts to explain Job's suffering.
3. **Teilhard de Chardin's "Patient Trust":** Reflect on what trusting "the slow work of God" means in seasons of loss or waiting.
4. **The four pillars of Hellenism vs. the four pillars of BEMA:** Go back to Sessions 3 and 5 and compare the two worldviews.
5. **The Lord's Prayer in its Jewish context:** Get ready for the Brad Gray and Brad Nelson interview by studying the prayer's Hebraic background.
6. **The women of Joshua and Judges:** Study figures such as Rahab, Achsah, Deborah, Jael, and Jephthah's daughter.
7. **The books of Samuel:** Read ahead to prepare for Reed's series.
8. **Church history:** Revisit Session 5 to trace how the early Jesus movement led to the modern church.
9. **Lament as a spiritual practice:** Explore the lament psalms and Lamentations as resources for grieving with God.

### Personal Summary

This episode is less a teaching than a pastoral word to the BEMA community. Marty and Brent have just lost their friend and co-host Josh Bossé, and instead of pushing ahead with business as usual, they borrow the Jewish practice of mourning. They make room for grief, stay present, and step back into life slowly and honestly. Session 10 will move at "Josh's pace": slower, more attentive, less concerned with efficiency. It will still bring interviews on the Lord's Prayer and Passover, series on the four pillars of BEMA, the women of Joshua and Judges, and Samuel, and a memorial episode fitting for Josh. Along the way, the hosts point new listeners back to Episode 0, encourage everyone to use the reboot companions and reading plans, thank the many small-dollar donors who keep the ministry going, and restate their central purpose: equipping college students to follow Jesus and carry that faith into every part of life. The episode is a reminder that faithfulness sometimes looks like slowing down, and that grief and hope can walk the same labyrinth together.
