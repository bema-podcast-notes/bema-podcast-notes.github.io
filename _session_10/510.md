### Title & Source Summary

**Episode:** BEMA 510: Wrestling (Four Pillars Series Finale)  
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon and Brent Billings, with guest Reed Dent  
**Focus:** Genesis 32:22–30 (Jacob wrestles with God at the Jabbok); the pillar of "wrestling" in discipleship

This episode closes BEMA's Four Pillars series with a reflection on wrestling, the pillar Marty likes to end on. Reed Dent does not walk through Genesis 32 line by line, since the earlier reboot episode on Jacob already did that. Instead he reads it the way Lectio Divina works: he notices phrases that stand out and asks what God brings to mind through them. His main move is to separate **"brain wrestling"** (working through doctrines, interpretations, and ideas about God) from **"soul wrestling"** (a personal struggle with God's presence, with your own identity, and with how the way of Jesus asks you to treat other people). Working through Jacob being left alone, the new name Israel, the unanswered question "What is your name?", and the naming of Peniel, Reed argues that wrestling is less about winning better answers and more about meeting God face to face and being shaped into a person of reconciliation and peace.

### Key Takeaways

- **Wrestling has two levels.** Brain wrestling deals with ideas, doctrines, and interpretations. Soul wrestling deals with God's presence, your identity, and how you love other people. Both matter, but people often try to settle a soul problem with brain work.
- **Being alone isn't the worst thing that can happen.** Jacob sends everyone and everything across the river. Solitude, whether you choose it or the Spirit leads you into it, is sometimes the only place a certain kind of encounter with God can happen.
- **Community still matters.** Reed calls it "the tag team": friends who honor your discomfort without bulldozing it with quick answers or making you feel broken for asking.
- **Some of our wrestling is with people, not God.** Jacob struggled "with God and with humans." Questioning what others say about God is different from wrestling with God himself.
- **Wrestling doesn't always overturn tradition.** Sometimes the truth is hard because it isn't what you were taught. Sometimes it's hard because it *is* what you were taught.
- **The question of soul wrestling is often "Where is God?"** Not "Does God exist?" Job and the Psalmists ask why God is hidden, not whether he is real.
- **You can use God to avoid God.** Intellectual study can become a way to dodge the real presence of God and the hard personal matters it brings up.
- **Wrestling starts with a question about you.** Jacob asks for a blessing, and the man answers, "What is your name?" Knowing yourself may matter more than knowing about God.
- **Overcoming may just mean surviving.** It may look like staying in the ring, limping, and saying "I don't know," rather than walking away clean and victorious.
- **The blessing is intimacy with God, and it prepares us for reconciliation.** The match readies Jacob to meet Esau with an open, softened heart.
- **Tagline:** "You came in looking for better answers but ended up leaving a better person."

### Main Concepts & Theories

#### 1. Lectio Divina vs. Historical-Critical Reading

Reed reads the passage reflectively, which Marty compares to **Lectio Divina**, the old practice of slow, prayerful reading where you pay attention to the words that strike you.

- **Historical-critical / grammatical reading** asks what the text meant to its first audience, with attention to grammar and context.
- **Lectio Divina / imaginative reading** asks what God is saying to *me* through these words today.
- BEMA usually works in the first mode. This episode deliberately uses the second, and both are valid ways to engage scripture.

#### 2. Brain Wrestling vs. Soul Wrestling

| | **Brain Wrestling** | **Soul Wrestling** |
|---|---|---|
| **With humans** | Re-examining interpretations, doctrines, traditions, sermons, and denominational positions | Struggling with Jesus' commands about other people: forgive, love your enemies, "Who is my neighbor?" |
| **With God** | Asking who God is, why God acts or doesn't act, and studying God as a character in the text | Asking *where* God is; longing for presence when God feels absent |
| **With self** | Thinking about your beliefs | Asking "Who am I?" and staying open to change |

- The two levels are connected. Reed says it's all part of the same thing.
- The risk is lopsidedness. BEMA listeners often lean toward brain wrestling and miss the deeper soul work. Marty admits he often tries to satisfy a soul need with brain answers.
- Reed links his own pull toward answers to being an **Enneagram 5**, a personality type driven by knowledge and understanding.

#### 3. "Jacob Was Left Alone": Solitude

- Jacob strips himself of family, servants, and possessions. He isn't physically naked, but he is exposed.
- Reed compares this to going "to the end of yourself," past what feels settled about God, scripture, and life.
- **The pull to retreat:** when hard questions come up, the instinct is to go back to the safety of consensus, to accept everything you were told and put the questions behind a closed door.
- **Marty's counterpoint:** nobody stripped Jacob. He sent them away himself. Sometimes we push ourselves into lonely deserts out of self-pity or self-righteousness, and God says, "You've done this to yourself. I'll meet you here."
- **Biblical pattern:** Jesus, Elijah, and Moses are led by the Spirit into solitary places because some work can only happen there. Jesus regularly "withdrew to lonely places and prayed."
- **Brent's question:** did the wrestling start right away, or after hours alone? He compares it to Marty's "creating space" practice, where things begin to surface only around the fifth hour of sitting quietly.

#### 4. "You Have Struggled with God and with Humans"

- Jacob's whole life is a struggle with people: Esau, Isaac, Laban.
- Reed found it freeing to realize that much of his theological struggle was with *people's representations* of God, not with God.
- **Fear of the ring:** hard questions can feel like falling down a well, or, quoting John Dominic Crossan on parables, like "an earthquake opening up the ground at your feet."
- **The cenote image:** a cenote is a sinkhole that looks like a dangerous pit. Underneath it are beautiful underground pools people swim and dive in. What's behind our questions may be better than we fear.
- Reed often reassures college students that if they keep going with a question, they'll be okay and everything won't collapse.

#### 5. "Please Tell Me Your Name": Wrestling with God

- Jacob wrestles all night without knowing who his opponent is.
- **Insight:** anything that feels like wrestling may be wrestling with God, even if we don't recognize it yet.
- **The biblical wrestling question is "where," not "whether."** Job and the Psalmists never doubt God's existence. They ask why God is hiding his face. Reed calls the question of God's existence a modern one.
- **The tradition's ambiguity:** BEMA has noted before that the text leaves it unclear whether the figure is a man, an angel, or God. What matters is that Jacob met the divine in a way that changed him.
- **Staying in the ring:** Psalm-like persistence means continuing to seek and pray even when the ring feels empty and nobody is in the stands.

#### 6. "What Is Your Name?": Wrestling with Self

- Marty: identity isn't a small part of this passage, it's huge.
- Name changes in the Bible mark identity changes. Jacob ("heel-grabber" or "supplanter") becomes Israel ("one who struggles with God").
- Jacob asks for a blessing and gets a question about himself back. Reed reads this as: maybe what I'm missing isn't knowledge of God but understanding of myself.
- **Staying open to change:** Reed recalls a student who wanted to preach the same sermon at 65 that he preached at 25. Reed's response was essentially, "Why would you want to keep living after that?"

#### 7. Overcoming Redefined

- Reed doesn't always feel like he's prevailing. Sometimes he feels tied into a pretzel, as if God has him in a suplex.
- **Marty:** overcoming isn't coming out washed clean and victorious. It's sitting on the stool between rounds. You come out "even more of a mess, limping."
- **Peniel:** "I saw God face to face, and yet my life was spared." Overcoming may simply mean *I'm still here.*
- **Brent's reading:** Jacob starts the night gripping hard, determined to overcome everything. He ends with clarity and humility: "God spared me. I didn't have anything to do with it."

#### 8. The Blessing and Its Purpose

- **Warning about distortion:** "I won't let you go unless you bless me" can be bent into prosperity-gospel thinking ("until I get a new car").
- **The deeper reading:** it's right to long for real intimacy with God and impact from God, and to insist on it. The wrestling should center on getting *God*, not mastery, knowledge, or being the smartest person in the room.
- **Context:** Jacob is about to meet Esau. The wrestling prepares him for a reconciliation he doesn't expect, which reflects a shift in values away from flocks and possessions and toward family.
- **Marty:** the encounter left Jacob "soft enough, open enough" to receive Esau's forgiveness, something he couldn't have taken in without the grappling match.

#### 9. The Road to Emmaus Parallel

- Reed sees an echo in Luke 24. The disciples don't recognize Jesus, he vanishes the moment they do, and only afterward do they say, "Were not our hearts burning within us?"
- Like Jacob, they understand the divine encounter only *in hindsight*.

### Examples & Applications

1. **The tag-team friend:** Find one or two people who will sit with your questions without fixing them or judging you. Be that friend to others by listening instead of answering right away.
2. **Practicing intentional solitude:** Try Marty's "creating space" idea. Set aside long, unhurried stretches alone with God, and expect it to feel lonely before anything surfaces.
3. **Checking for avoidance:** On BEMA's Israel and Turkey trips, Reed noticed people asking detailed contextual questions to avoid harder topics like their marriage or their kids. Ask yourself whether your Bible study is a way of avoiding God.
4. **Soul wrestling with Jesus' commands:** Pray "forgive us as we forgive those who trespass against us" slowly, and name one specific person. Wrestle with what loving an enemy would actually require this week.
5. **For pastors and teachers:** You don't always need an answer. Saying "I don't know" to a congregation can model honest faith, because leaders have to wrestle too.
6. **Reframing doubt:** When a question feels like the ground giving way, picture a cenote instead of a well. There may be something life-giving below.
7. **Psalm-style prayer when God feels absent:** Use lament psalms (Psalm 13, 22, 88) as templates for staying in the ring and saying "Where are you?" without giving up.
8. **Measuring growth over decades:** Imagine who you hope to be at 50 or 60. Keep growing instead of locking in your current convictions.
9. **Wrestling before reconciliation:** Before a hard conversation with an estranged family member or friend, take time alone with God so your own heart can be softened first.

### Personal Summary

This episode treats wrestling as more than intellectual sparring. Reed Dent reads Jacob's night at the Jabbok as a picture of a struggle on several fronts: with traditions handed down by people, with a God who sometimes feels absent, and with your own identity. Jacob goes out alone, grapples all night without knowing his opponent, gets a question instead of an answer, and leaves limping. Only afterward does he realize he has seen God face to face. The point for anyone who loves digging into the text is that study is good, but it can also become a way to keep God at arm's length. Real wrestling means staying in the ring when it's uncomfortable, saying "I don't know," and insisting on the blessing of God's presence rather than on mastery. The goal isn't to become a sharper theologian. It's to become someone who can cross the river and embrace an estranged brother. As Marty puts it, you come in looking for better answers and leave a better person.
