### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Elle Grover Fricks argued that the Bible is a "purple" book, full of the supernatural, and invited us to "stick your head out the window" at the part of Scripture that feels hardest to believe and to choose the sacred text over the algorithm.[^1]

**This week:** The series moves from the pillar of Text to the pillar of Community. Marty defines biblical community as *radical fellowship with people you don't agree with*, which is harder than friendship.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

Marty begins by explaining his personal order for the four pillars (text, community, discipleship, wrestling) and why community comes second for him: it is the pillar he most needs to be challenged on. He then separates the pillar of community from friendship and affinity, which he values deeply, and describes it as intentionally choosing ongoing relationships with people unlike us. Jesus' own circle of disciples is the model.[^3]

He describes three elements of that community: accountability (respecting others' voices because I might be wrong), being only a piece (no single person carries the whole image of God), and learning (others see facets of truth I can't). Along the way he reads Philippians 2, Galatians 1–2, the creation stories, the body of Christ in 1 Corinthians 12, the man born blind, and Eldad and Medad, and he closes with Dietrich Bonhoeffer's warning about loving our dream of community more than the real community in front of us.[^4]

### Accountability: I Might Be Wrong <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#accountability-i-might-be-wrong}

*Marty's first element of community is respecting other people's voices, because "I think I'm right, but I don't know."*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Marty takes loneliness seriously and affirms that friendship and affinity matter. But he describes the pillar of community as something else: intentionally choosing ongoing relationships with people who differ from us in age, ethnicity, economics, ideology and politics. His model is Jesus' *chavurah* (close group of disciples), which included Pharisees, a tax collector, zealots, probably Herodians, and someone with ties to the priests, people who would never have chosen to spend time together.[^5] What do you imagine daily life was like in that group? Why might Jesus have gathered people who were so likely to clash?

*Read Galatians 1:15–2:10 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Marty calls what Paul does here a "dance." Paul received his gospel directly from Jesus, yet fourteen years later he laid it out before the Jerusalem leaders "to be sure I was not running and had not been running my race in vain." He doesn't make the leaders more than they are, and he doesn't think he can do without them.[^6] As you listen, which phrases show Paul's secure identity, and which show his willingness to be accountable? What do you make of the two sitting side by side?

*Read Philippians 2:1–11 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty calls the call to give up selfish ambition one of the most radical verses Western, consumer-driven culture doesn't take seriously. He notes that fundamentalist readings often treat "every knee will bow" as a promise that *we* will be proven right, while the passage says Jesus is exalted *because* he chose to lose on behalf of others.[^7] How does reading "therefore God exalted him" this way change how Christians might handle disagreement with one another?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Marty leads his team by seeking buy-in rather than relying on his title, and he contrasts right-handed power, which coerces, with left-handed power, which invites (baking something and letting the smell draw the kids to dinner). Brent adds that accountability rests on trusting that God speaks to the whole team, not just to you.[^8] Who in your life has the standing to tell you that you might be wrong, and what has made that trust possible?

</div>

### I Am Only a Piece <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#i-am-only-a-piece}

*Marty's second element is that no single person carries the whole image of God.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 1 Corinthians 12:12–27 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Marty draws out several features of Paul's picture: no part can say "I don't belong," none can say "I don't need you," the parts that seem weaker are indispensable, and God gives greater honor to the parts that lacked it. He adds that it is not a zero-sum game and that, at different times, different parts need special care. Brent connects this to the manna in the wilderness, where some gathered more and some less but everyone had what they needed.[^9] What do you notice in the passage about how honor and need are distributed in the body?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty reads "male and female he created them" (Genesis 1:26–27) less as a statement about gender and more as a statement about diversity: the image of God is reflected in humanity *together*. In Genesis 2, he notes that Adam (*'adam*, "humankind") goes from whole to less than whole when God takes a piece out of him, and is complete only together with the woman (*ish* and *ishah*, "man" and "woman"). He stresses that this is not a statement of the woman's subservience.[^10] What does reading these passages as a picture of "oneness made from distinct parts" highlight for you, and what questions does it raise?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** In your church or in another community you belong to, which "parts" seem weaker or receive less honor? What might it look like, concretely, to give them greater honor?

</div>

### Learning from the Gem with Seventy Faces <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#learning-from-the-gem-with-seventy-faces}

*Marty's third element is learning: other people see facets of truth that I can't.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read John 9:35–41 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** A Jewish saying holds that every passage is like a gem with seventy faces, and Marty says it is arrogant to assume that what I see, even if I'm right, is the *whole* picture. In John 9, he contrasts the healed man's openness with the Pharisees, concluding that claiming to see clearly creates blindness, and admitting what you don't see is true sight.[^11] What do you notice about the difference between the healed man's posture and the Pharisees' posture? How does community supply the eyes we lack?

*Read Numbers 11:24–29 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** Seventy elders prophesy at the tent, but Eldad and Medad, who stayed in the camp, prophesy too. Joshua wants them stopped; Moses wishes all the Lord's people were prophets. Marty prefers to speak of "72" rather than 70 because it reminds him that community doesn't fit neatly in a box: members of God's community often show up where they "aren't supposed to be."[^12] All of our traditions draw boundaries in some way. Without judging anyone's tradition, when have you seen real spiritual fruit in someone outside your own group, and was your first instinct more like Joshua's or Moses'?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty closes with Bonhoeffer's *Life Together*: "He who loves his dream of community more than the Christian community itself becomes a destroyer of the latter…" He adds that community is a gift we have to go and unwrap, and that "What am I getting out of it?" is only half the question, because we are givers as well as receivers.[^13] What "dream" of community might you need to hold more loosely, and what is one thing you could give to the real community in front of you this month?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Marty argues that following Jesus can't be done alone, and that the community it requires is harder than friendship. Community means accountability (I might be wrong), incompleteness (I am only one piece of God's image), and learning (others see facets of the gem that I can't). Jesus gave up even pure ambition and was exalted because he chose to lose for others, and Paul, with a gospel straight from Jesus, still went back to check it with people. The "72" reminds us that God's Spirit doesn't stay inside our boundaries, and Bonhoeffer reminds us that chasing an ideal community can destroy the real one.[^14]
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, name one person or group unlike you that you will intentionally move toward this week.

</div>

#### Looking Ahead {#looking-ahead}

**[Ep. 503 — Japanese Woodworking Joint](/guides/503)** (31:44): Reed Dent, who directs Campus Christian Fellowship, offers his own view of the pillar of community in a short episode. He compares healthy community to Japanese joinery, where hand-cut pieces lock together into one strong whole without glue or screws, and describes three interlocking elements: friendship, conversation and practice. He also speaks about envy, each person's place in the body of Christ, and why a community must keep the ability to criticize itself.[^15]

**What to bring next week:**
- Read 1 Samuel 18:6–16 and notice how Saul's envy of David grows.
- Bring one memory of an ordinary shared activity (a meal, a game, a late-night conversation) that helped a group become a real community for you.
