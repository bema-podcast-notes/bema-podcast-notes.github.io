**Last week:** Kat Armstrong showed us how images like stones, mountains and valleys tie the Bible into one story, and how good study equips people to keep reading on their own.

**This week:** Our final week of Session 7. Marty returns from his 100-day sabbatical, and he and Brent look back over the whole season of rest.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This is the last meeting of Session 7, and the episode is Marty's first time back in the recording booth after a 100-day sabbatical. He is honest about what the time away was not: it brought no dramatic new insight, ministry or vision. It was renewal through stopping, resting, worship, play and a new rhythm of work, along with a 10-week Ignatian prayer retreat in which he met with Jesus in the same place, in the same way, every day. The simplicity, he says, was the profound part.

Marty and Brent then look back at the session's shape. Brent noticed that Session 7 formed a chiasm (a mirrored structure with the key point at the center): guided reflections in the first half, practices shared by different hosts in the second half, and the conversation with Kat Armstrong at the center. Marty's main takeaway is that variety of thought, and even disagreement, is healthy for a faith community. He suggests meeting difference with "of course they see it differently" rather than feeling it must be fixed, and he urges listeners not to rank what God is doing through others. Tonight we do what the episode does: look back over the whole season, name what changed, and ask what comes next.

### Rest That Doesn't Have to Prove Anything <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#rest-that-doesnt-have-to-prove-anything}

*Marty's sabbatical shows that rest does not have to justify itself with results.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Ecclesiastes 3:1–8 aloud slowly, then sit together in silence for one minute.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The capstone nods to Ecclesiastes 3, "a time for this and a time for that," as a frame for the rhythms of rest and work[^1]. Look back over the pairs in the passage. Which ones speak of stopping, holding back or letting go? Which pair best describes the season you are in right now?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty describes sabbatical as a longer form of Sabbath with five parts: stopping, resting, worship, play and working differently. For him, play meant drumming every day and cooking, especially smoking meat and making chili[^2]. Which of the five has been most crowded out of your life, and what is one life-giving thing you usually skip "because life"?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Marty's friend Dave named the pressure to come back from sabbatical with a profound discovery, and reminded him that this is not what sabbatical is for. Marty returned with no big revelation, just refreshed, which he calls "just Jesus." During his retreat, slow and repeated prayer let him stay with his struggles with worry and anxiety instead of praying briefly and moving on[^3]. What is one concern you tend to pray about quickly and then leave? What might it look like to return to it in the same place, in the same way, each day for the next few weeks?

</div>

### Of Course They See It Differently <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#of-course-they-see-it-differently}

*Marty's biggest takeaway from the season is that difference is a feature, not a flaw.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Proverbs 27:17 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Marty names three sources of difference among the session's hosts: personality, real disagreement, and paradox or language. In the third case people see the same thing but, as Marty puts it, "enter through doors on different sides of the room." His example: one host questioned the phrase "creating space," preferred "attentiveness," and then described ways to create space for attentiveness[^4]. Brent ties this to "iron sharpening iron"[^5]. Think back over our own ten weeks. Where did we enter through different doors, and which of the three sources was at work?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Listeners respond very differently to the same content. Some called Josh's Mishkan series "peak BEMA," others found its connections a stretch, and some will love Session 7 while others are glad to get back to the Bible. Marty encourages us not to rank content as good or bad, but to take what we need from what God is doing through the person next to us[^6]. Which week of Session 7 landed least for you? What might God have been doing through it for someone else in this room?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** Our group holds Church of Christ, Baptist, Christian Church and Methodist convictions, and our traditions differ on real things, including how and whether to keep a Sabbath. Marty suggests answering disagreement with "of course they see it differently" instead of feeling it must be fixed or settled, and the notes offer a small-group version: "Of course you see it that way. Tell me more."[^7] The notes add that, for Marty, truth gets clearer as many people add to the conversation and push back on each other[^8]. Where does "of course" come easily for you, and where does it feel risky? Who in your family, friendships or church could you say "Tell me more" to this week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">35 min</span> {#closing}

*Walking back through Session 7.* Read these aloud slowly, one person per line:

- **Week 1, Session 7 Intro:** Sabbath shaped how the session itself was built: six sessions of work, then a session of rest[^9].
- **Week 2, Truth:** God meets people in physical, tangible ways, and objects with a story can become "standing stones" that point us to him[^10].
- **Week 3, Love:** Wise boundaries can be an act of love, and like rain we can move between intimate and public spaces, carrying God's presence to others[^11].
- **Week 4, Community:** We were asked what space our worship makes for raw community, for growth beyond our control, and for giving the seeds of our lives away[^12].
- **Week 5, Teaching:** The most lasting teaching often happens without words, through the way we live and "the way we stop"[^13].
- **Week 6, Place:** Exodus 29 deals with sin for the sake of presence, and the morning and evening *tamid* makes repentance a daily rhythm[^14].
- **Week 7, Wonder:** God's saving work is something to marvel at; holiness spreads outward but always belongs to God[^15].
- **Week 8, Trust:** Sabbath is an act of trust, a way of believing that God, not our effort, keeps the world going[^16].
- **Week 9, Kat Armstrong:** The Bible is one story held together by images we can see and touch, and good study equips us to keep reading on our own[^17]. Her conversation sat at the center of the session's mirrored shape[^18].

> For ten weeks we have practiced a season of rest together, moving from the seven days of creation and the Tabernacle into conversation and practice. We sat with truth, love, community, teaching, place, wonder and trust, and then heard how the Bible's images hold its story together. Marty came back from his own sabbatical with no grand discovery, only renewed and closer to Jesus, and that may be the most honest picture of what rest is for. We have not all rested, prayed or read in the same way, and that is part of the gift. Marty's conclusion is that the goal is not "the one right way," and Brent adds that without disagreement no one grows, iron sharpening iron[^19].
{: .guide-summary}

*Sit together in silence for 90 seconds before the next question.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Looking back across all ten weeks, how has this season changed the way you read the Bible, understand it, or relate to it? Be as honest about what did not change as about what did.

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q8:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Which of the reflections (truth, love, community, teaching, place, wonder or trust) do you want to return to on your own, and what would returning to it look like in an ordinary week?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** Going around the room in one sentence each, with no comments until everyone has spoken: what practice of rest are you carrying out of this season, and who will you tell about it?

*Close by reading together the prayer Marty spoke at the end of the seven reflections:* "May we trust the story enough to find a space to rest. May that rest remind us of who God is and how much He loves us."[^20]

</div>
