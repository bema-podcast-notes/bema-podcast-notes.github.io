### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** At Cana, Jesus answered a crowd that may have wanted fury with an abundance of the best wine, and in the temple he confronted what had gone wrong, while John's word *Ioudaios* pointed to the Judean temple authorities rather than all Jewish people.[^1]

**This week:** Brent Billings and Josh Bossé read Jesus's conversation with Nicodemus as a Passover conversation, full of Exodus, Sinai, and creation imagery.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode sets the conversation on Passover night, during *Leil Shimurim* ("the night of watching," from Exodus 12:42), when Jews stay up watching for salvation. On that night Jesus tells Nicodemus he cannot even see the kingdom coming. Nicodemus's question about returning to the womb is treated as a serious objection, not a foolish one, and being born of water and of spirit is heard as two moments in Israel's story: the Red Sea and Sinai.[^3]

The hosts then hear John 3:16 as Nicodemus might have, alongside Exodus 4:22–23 where Israel is God's firstborn son sent for the sake of the world. They describe judgment as self-administered: light has come, and people either step into it or hide like Adam and Eve. The teaching ends on Mount Moriah, where Abraham discovers a God who provides rather than demands.[^4]

**Opening Question:** Think of a time you had to start over: a move, a new job, a new school, a change in your family. What did you carry with you into the new start, for better or worse?

### Nicodemus on the Night of Watching <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#nicodemus-on-the-night-of-watching}

*The episode hears "born again" as Jesus saying Israel needs another Passover, another Exodus.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Exodus 12:42 and John 3:1–8 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes explain that on *Leil Shimurim* Jews "stay up watching for salvation to come," which makes Jesus's opening words pointed: while they are symbolically watching for God to deliver them, he says they will not be able to see the kingdom.[^3] What words about night, seeing, and birth do you notice in John 3:1–8? Name one and what it suggests in this Passover setting.

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The episode says Nicodemus, a Pharisee and member of the Sanhedrin, was not asking a ridiculous question. His words about the womb may mean: we cannot return to Egypt; how can someone with experience and baggage start from a clean slate; and why should a weary nation start over just when it has found stability?[^3] Which of those three objections sounds most like a question you have asked, or heard someone ask?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Jesus plays on *ruach* (Hebrew) and *pneuma* (Greek), which mean both "wind" and "spirit." The notes hear a hint of Ecclesiastes's "chasing after the wind" and conclude that "You cannot grasp this movement intellectually; you must experience the spiritual transformation yourself."[^5] What is the difference between understanding something about God and experiencing it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes say Israel passed through water at the Red Sea and then spirit at Sinai, and that Torah built on their slavery: "Take care of the alien, orphan, and widow because you were once slaves." In the notes' words, "Their baggage becomes the ground for their rebirth rather than an obstacle to it."[^6] Share only what you are comfortable sharing: how might a hard experience in your life become ground for caring for someone else rather than a disqualification?

</div>

### Lifted Up and Sent Into the World <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#lifted-up-and-sent-into-the-world}

*Jesus turns Nicodemus from ascending toward God to healing that comes down to people and a mission that includes the whole world.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read John 3:9–17 and Numbers 21:4–9 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** In Numbers 21, a plague of snakes comes after Israel complains about a detour, and the episode notes they resolved it by confessing and asking Moses to intercede. The notes say Jesus uses the lifted serpent to "flip the idea of ascending": healing comes down to the people. Combined with Daniel's Son of Man, the conclusion is that "Spiritual experience without justice work is incomplete."[^7] What do you notice in the two passages about who is lifted up, who looks, and what happens next?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> "We speak of what we know" uses a plural, and the hosts hear Numbers 13–14: Jesus (*Yehoshua*, the same name as Joshua) and John the Baptist as the two faithful spies, with John positioned at the Jordan. The ten spies called the land "a land that devours its people," preferring the desert's simplicity.[^8] Where do you see people (including yourself) preferring a comfortable religious desert to the demands of the promised land?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The hosts encourage "hearing this verse as Nicodemus would have heard it, with fresh ears." Exodus 4:22–23 calls Israel God's firstborn son, so John 3:16 can be heard as God giving Israel into the world so that all nations would come to God. The notes put Jesus's challenge this way: "You are at the end of Passover, and yet you're not interested in bringing any Egyptians along with you."[^9] For many of us John 3:16 was one of the first verses we memorized, and our traditions have taught it in particular ways. What has that verse meant to you, and what does hearing it with Nicodemus's ears add to it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes connect this to the temple cleansing: the marketplace sat in the Court of the Gentiles, the only space where non-Jews could worship, so a practical solution was "solved by sacrificing Gentile access to sacred space." They ask what structures in our churches or lives create barriers to "the very people we are meant to serve."[^10] What is one barrier, in a church, an organization, or your own habits, that you could help remove?

</div>

### Stepping Into the Light <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#stepping-into-the-light}

*Judgment, in the episode's reading, is what happens when light arrives and people choose whether to come into it.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read John 3:18–21 and Genesis 3:8–10 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** The notes describe a "self-administered" judgment: light has come into the world as a new creation, and people step into it or stay in darkness. John 3:20 echoes Adam and Eve hiding in Genesis 3. The notes list very human reasons we resist the light: we will mess up, our inadequacies will be revealed, we will hurt people's feelings, and we will discover uncomfortable truths about ourselves.[^11] Which of those reasons do you think keeps people in their "dark little niches" most often, and why?

*Read Genesis 22:9–14 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Abraham had lost many people, and the notes picture God's test as saying, "Lay that fear naked and see what God I am." God did not ask for the ram; Abraham offered it because he understood that God provides, and the saying became "On the mountain of the Lord, it will be provided."[^12] What fear about God does this story invite you to lay down?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q11:** The notes read John 3:21 as radical grace: if you step into the light, everything you do, including failures, "will be revealed as having been part of God working in you." This only works "in a context of genuine grace," where exposure does not lead to being "pounced upon and torn apart."[^13] No one needs to share anything personal here. What would help a group like ours become a place where people could step into the light without fear?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> On the night of watching, Jesus tells a teacher of Israel that watching is not enough: they need another Exodus, through water and through spirit. Nicodemus's objections are real, and Torah answers them by turning slavery into the reason to welcome the stranger. John 3:16 calls Israel back to its mission to bring the nations along. Light has come, and the only judgment is whether we step into it or hide. On Mount Moriah, Abraham learns that God provides, and that is the rebirth Jesus describes.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, what is one place in your life where you want to step out of hiding and into the light?

</div>
