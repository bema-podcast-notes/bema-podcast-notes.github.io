### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Marty began his hundred-day step away, and we heard that real rest needs a plan, firm boundaries, and a deliberate start and finish. After six sessions of work, Session 7 is the rest.[^1]

**This week:** The first of seven guided Sabbath reflections, built on Josh Bossé's Episode 325, links the first day of creation to the theme of truth and to the first furnishings of the *Mishkan* (Tabernacle).[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This episode is a guided meditation rather than a lesson. Brent reads Genesis 1:1–5 four times, and each of the first three readings is followed by a passage from Exodus 25: the Ark, the Table of the Bread of the Presence, and the Lampstand. After each pairing Marty asks one reflection question, and several minutes of silence follow.[^3] On the first day God speaks light into darkness, and the notes explain why truth fits as the theme: "Light reveals what is real."[^4]

Marty's three prompts build on each other: physical objects that remind us God is real, places and practices that fill us up, and weaving the two into a natural and restful relationship with God. He closes by describing the "standing stones" on his bookshelf and by encouraging listeners to experiment with spiritual practices without fear of failing.[^5] This guide follows the same pattern, so expect to hear Genesis 1:1–5 more than once and to sit in silence before answering.

### The Ark: Reminders That God Is Real <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-ark-reminders-that-god-is-real}

*God meets people in physical, tangible ways, beginning with the place where God says, "I will meet with you."*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:1–5 aloud. Then read Exodus 25:10–22 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** On the first day God speaks "Let there be light" into a world that is "formless and empty," and the notes link that moment to truth breaking into chaos. The Ark is where God promises, "There… I will meet with you," and its poles are never removed, which suggests God's presence is always ready to move with the people.[^4] Hearing the two passages side by side, which words or images stand out to you? What do you notice about where and how God chooses to meet people?

*Sit together in silence for two minutes, then take up the next question.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Marty's first prompt: "What things in your life, physical things, material things, remind you of the realness of God? Is there anything that you cling to when you are in darkness that connects you to God?"[^5] Answer as fully or as briefly as you like.

*Read Joshua 4:1–7 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> In Joshua 4, Israel piles up stones from the Jordan so later generations will ask what they mean. Marty keeps "little standing stones" on top of his bookshelves and, in his hardest seasons, sits holding them and remembering their stories. He insists this is not idolatry: "The objects don't hold power. They help us remember."[^6] The traditions in this room are not equally at ease with physical objects in faith. What shaped your sense of where a helpful reminder ends and something that competes with God begins? Given that line, what might you set out as a standing stone of your own?

</div>

### The Table: What Fills You Up <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#the-table-what-fills-you-up}

*The bread kept before God pictures ongoing fellowship and provision.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:1–5 aloud again. Then read Exodus 25:23–30 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes observe that repeating the same text lets familiar words "strike the listener differently depending on what came just before."[^3] The Table holds bread kept "before me at all times," a picture of ongoing fellowship, provision, and a shared meal between God and the people.[^7] What sounded different in Genesis 1:1–5 this second time? What does it suggest to you that the bread is always there, not only on special days?

*Sit together in silence for two minutes.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Marty's second prompt: "What tangible actions or spaces nourish your spirit? Is there somewhere that you go or something that you do that fills you up?" For Marty it is being outdoors and "spending time on the top of the mountain."[^7] Where or what is that for you?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Which place or practice that once filled you up have you let slip? What would make it easy to return to it this month?

</div>

### The Lampstand: Weaving It Together <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#the-lampstand-weaving-it-together}

*The Lampstand gathers the whole sequence into one picture of light.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:1–5 aloud a third time. Then read Exodus 25:31–40 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The Lampstand is hammered from one piece of pure gold, with cups shaped like almond blossoms that recall the Tree of Life, and its light ties back directly to "Let there be light." It is made "according to the pattern shown you on the mountain."[^8] What connections do you notice between this passage and the first day of creation?

*Sit together in silence for two minutes.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Marty's third prompt: "How can you weave these things, these physical things that connect you to God, and these practices or spaces that nourish your spirit, together into a natural and restful relationship with God?" The notes add that objects and practices should fit into a rhythm of life, "not become a list of religious duties."[^9] What might that weaving look like in an ordinary week of yours?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty urges listeners to try things and accept that some ideas won't work. The notes suggest, for example, lighting a candle at dinner for a week to echo the Lampstand, then changing or dropping it if it doesn't help. In Marty's words, "We don't lose anything here."[^10] What small experiment will you try this week, and what would help you treat a failed experiment as part of the rest rather than a failure?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Each time we read Genesis 1:1–5, God spoke light into darkness, and truth broke into chaos. The Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand show a God who has always met people in tangible ways: a place to meet, bread always present, and light filling the space. Our objects can become standing stones that help us remember, and our places and practices can fill us up. The invitation is to weave them into a rhythm that feels restful rather than forced, and to experiment without fear.
{: .guide-summary}

*Read Genesis 1:1–5 aloud one last time, then sit together in silence for one minute.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, name the object, place, or practice you will return to this week.

</div>
