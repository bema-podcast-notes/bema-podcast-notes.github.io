### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** We asked when, where, who and why of the Hebrew Scriptures, and saw the Bible as a table of faithful voices in conversation rather than a single flat account.

**This week:** We turn to the Gospels themselves, starting with the word "gospel" and what it meant to call a book by that name in Caesar's world.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode begins with the Greek word *euangelion*, "good news." Long before the New Testament, it was the word rulers used to announce the arrival of a new king and kingdom, from Alexander the Great to each new Caesar.[^1] An inscription from Priene in Turkey, written decades before Jesus' birth, calls Augustus "savior of the World" and says his birth brought "the gospel of peace."[^2] When Matthew, Mark, Luke and John called their works "gospel," they were making a subversive and politically dangerous claim: there is a new King and a new Kingdom.[^3]

The episode then explains why BEMA does not harmonize the four Gospels into one account. Each writer has a distinct agenda and narrative arc, and the differences between them are invitations to ask why each author made his choices.[^4] Finally, it argues that the gospel is the announcement that the Kingdom of God has arrived. The cross is central and climactic, but the gospel is bigger than that one moment.[^5]

**Opening Question:** Think of an announcement that changed your daily life: a new boss, a new law, a birth, a merger, a diagnosis that turned out well. Was it good news for everyone who heard it? What made it good news, and for whom?

### Caesar's Good News <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#caesars-good-news}

*Before it was a Christian word, "gospel" was an imperial one.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The Priene inscription, as quoted in the notes, speaks of "bestowing on us Augustus Caesar as savior of the World, for he has put an end to war and brought perfect peace," and it ends, "Never will another gospel surpass the gospel that was announced at his birth. He is not only Lord of the Empire but Lord of the Earth and of the calendar and of time itself."[^6] Read those lines aloud slowly. Which words and titles do you recognize from the way Christians talk about Jesus? What do you notice about what Caesar's "gospel" promises?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The notes describe how a town would respond to a new emperor: build a temple, post inscriptions calling him "savior of the world," reorganize the calendar around his birthday.[^7] Against that background, the Gospel writers announced a new King, a new Kingdom, and a Kingdom that had already arrived, and "To proclaim another king besides Caesar was treasonous."[^8] How does hearing "gospel" as a public, political announcement change the way the word sounds to you?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes list modern "gospels" that promise the good life: capitalism ("Free markets bring prosperity and freedom"), technology ("Innovation will solve humanity's problems"), nationalism, and consumerism ("Purchasing and possessing will make you happy").[^9] Which of these makes the most convincing promises to you personally? What exactly does it promise, and how does that compare with the Kingdom Jesus announced?

</div>

### Four Voices, Not One Blend <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#four-voices-not-one-blend}

*The episode asks us to hear each Gospel in its own voice rather than weaving them together.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 20:29–34 aloud, then Mark 10:46–52.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes give several examples of differences between the Gospels: Matthew mentions two angels at the tomb where Mark mentions one, and Matthew has two blind men and two demoniacs where Mark and Luke have one.[^10] Compare the two readings you just heard. What differences do you notice in the number of people, the names, and the details? The notes suggest that instead of asking "What actually happened?" we ask, "Why did Matthew include two angels/blind men/demoniacs?"[^11] What changes in you when you ask the second question instead of the first?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes put it this way: "The diversity of the four Gospels is a feature, not a bug."[^12] In your church background, were the Gospels mostly taught as one combined story (a harmony or "life of Christ") or as four separate books? What did that approach give you, and what might it have hidden? How might you read a Gospel differently this year?

</div>

### The Gospel of the Kingdom <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#the-gospel-of-the-kingdom}

*If "gospel" means a new King and Kingdom, then the gospel is more than a single transaction.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 3:1–2, Matthew 4:17, and Matthew 10:5–8 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes trace the same message through John the Baptist ("Repent, for the Kingdom of Heaven is near/at hand"), Jesus' first message (a "word-for-word continuation of John's message"), the instructions to the Twelve and the Seventy-Two, and the apostles in Acts.[^13] In the passages you just read, who announces the Kingdom, and what actions go with the announcement?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes contrast two contemporary views. Kingdom theology (N. T. Wright and others) sees the gospel as the announcement that God's Kingdom has arrived, encompassing Jesus' whole ministry, death and resurrection. Modern evangelical orthodoxy (the notes name John Piper and Timothy Keller) focuses on salvation through faith in Jesus' substitutionary death.[^14] The episode's synthesis is that the cross is "central and climactic to the Gospel, but the Gospel is BIGGER than just that moment."[^15] If someone asked you to explain "the gospel" in two minutes, what would you have said five years ago? What, if anything, does this episode invite you to add or reorder?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q8:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes describe preaching the gospel as "announcing that God's Kingdom has arrived in Jesus, demonstrating that Kingdom through acts of healing and justice, and inviting people to live under God's reign now."[^16] Where could you demonstrate the Kingdom this week in an ordinary act, at home, at work, or in your neighborhood?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> In the first century, "gospel" was the word heralds used when a new emperor took power and the world was told that life would now be better under his rule. The Gospel writers took that word and gave it to a crucified rabbi from Galilee, announcing a better King and a better Kingdom. Each of them told that story in his own voice for his own audience, and we will listen to each voice on its own terms. The good news is not only that we can be forgiven, but that God's Kingdom has broken into the world and is making all things right.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: how would you state the good news today, in your own words?

</div>
