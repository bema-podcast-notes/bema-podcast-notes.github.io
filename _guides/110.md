**Last week:** We renamed the first parable the Parable of the Soils, heard why Jesus taught in a way that made people dig, and found a prophetic remedy for each poor soil.

**This week:** The episode steps back to show the tools: PaRDeS, the four-level Jewish way of reading, and a single word in the Parable of the Soils that leads all the way back to Isaac.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The notes say Jesus taught in parables "not to make things easier, but intentionally harder," because Eastern teaching prizes discovery over explanation.[^1] The episode then lays out PaRDeS: *p'shat* (the surface reading), *remez* ("hint," a connection to the Hebrew Scriptures), *drash* (the truth hidden in the story, found by following the hint), and *sod* (mystery, revelation only God can give).[^2] It insists that the deeper levels are not more true than the surface, that this is not a Bible code, and that it works best in a *havurah*, a "study group."[^3]

The main example is the word "hundredfold," which the notes say appears in only one other place in Scripture: Isaac's harvest in Genesis 26.[^4] The episode ends by leaving the Parable of the Weeds unexplained so listeners can practice the method themselves, and this session gives you time to do exactly that.[^5]

### Why Make It Hard? <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#why-make-it-hard}

*The disciples ask why Jesus teaches in parables, and the episode argues his answer is deliberate.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 13:10–13 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** What does Jesus say about who receives "the knowledge of the secrets of the kingdom" and who does not? Notice the reason he gives in verse 13.

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The notes describe Western teaching as focused on "explanation and clear communication," and Eastern teaching as one where "Teachers bury truth in the learning process" and "Students must dig to unearth treasures."[^6] They add that the East "Believes discovered truth is more intimately understood than explained truth."[^7] Where have you seen each approach in your own education or church life, and what did each do for you?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say Jesus "catered to the highest common denominator, not the lowest," and ask, "How often do we cater to passive learners rather than challenging people to discover truth?"[^8] Thinking about churches in general rather than any one tradition, what helps or hinders people from becoming active learners rather than passive ones?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes tell of Ray Vander Laan studying at Yeshiva University, unable to answer when an Orthodox rabbi asked about his Rabbi Jesus's teaching on a passage. Ray said, "I would sit in the back of the class and just cry because I didn't really know the teachings of my Jesus."[^9] What feelings does that story stir in you, and what would you like to know about Jesus's teaching that you do not yet know?

</div>

### Four Ways of Reading <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#four-ways-of-reading}

*The episode walks through the four levels of PaRDeS using the Parable of the Soils.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 13:8 and 13:23 aloud, then Genesis 26:12–22.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Look at the numbers in Matthew 13:8 and 13:23. What order are they in, and what seems unusual about it? The notes point out that the numbers run "backwards" and that "hundredfold" appears only one other time in Scripture, in Genesis 26:12.[^10] Now look at Genesis 26:12–22. What does Isaac do when people quarrel over his wells?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes describe the *drash* that emerges once the hint leads to Isaac: he "chose to stay in the land and persevere in his father's mission despite opposition," and "didn't stomp his feet demanding his rights."[^11] For Jesus's audience, the notes put it as: "Stay in the land, persevere with faithfulness in what God has called you to, and God's mission will work."[^12] How does knowing about Isaac change what "good soil" looks like?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> *Read Matthew 16:15–17 aloud.* The notes give Peter's confession as an example of *sod*, revelation that "Cannot be learned or taught by any human teacher, no matter how skilled."[^13] They also stress that "Deeper levels are not more true or more inspired than p'shat" and that "90% of Christian teaching is based on p'shat and that's okay."[^14] Why do you think the notes are so careful to say the deeper levels are not a spiritual upgrade?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Isaac kept digging wells when others quarreled over them. Where in your life is God asking you to stay in the land and keep digging, even when it would be easier to fight for your rights or leave?

</div>

### Practice: The Weeds and the Wheat <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#practice-the-weeds-and-the-wheat}

*The episode leaves the next parable hanging so listeners can try the method for themselves.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 13:24–30 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** Start with the *p'shat*: what happens in the story? Then, following the notes' advice to "Ask 'What seems weird?'", name anything that seems unusual, backwards or surprising.[^15] Choose one or two keywords to search in the Hebrew Scriptures this week.

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes picture Jesus's twelve as a *havurah*, debating his parables while Jesus rolls over "with one eye open and a smirk," listening.[^16] They say that in this kind of reading "The wrestling with text matters more than finding 'the answer.'"[^17] What would it look like for this group to wrestle together this way, rather than looking for one right answer?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q11:** What is one practice that would help you know the Hebrew Scriptures better over the next month, so that you have more hints to notice?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Jesus taught in parables to make his students dig, because truth that is discovered is held more deeply than truth that is simply explained. PaRDeS gives us four ways to read: the plain surface, the hint that points back to the Hebrew Scriptures, the truth found by following that hint, and the mystery only God can give. One unusual word, "hundredfold," leads to Isaac, who stayed in the land and kept digging wells when others quarreled. None of the levels makes anyone more spiritual, and all of them work best in a group that wrestles together.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one thing you noticed today that you want to keep digging into?

</div>
