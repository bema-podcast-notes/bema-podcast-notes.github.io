### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Gathered waters produce dry ground — community. The altar requires uncut local stones. Don't cut people to make them fit.

**This week:** Day 4 fills Day 1's space with specific luminaries. The stars barely get a mention — and they are the ones who matter most in real darkness. The priestly garments tell the same story: hold the whole community on your shoulders while weighing each person's name on your heart.

### Opening <span class="guide-time">10 min</span> {#opening}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-opening" markdown="1">

**Opening Question:** Who in your life has functioned like a star for you — not solving your problem, not lighting everything up, but helping you know what season you were in or where you stood? What did they specifically do or say?

</div>

### Genesis 1:14–19 — Day 4 <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#genesis-11419-day-4}

*Read Genesis 1:14–15 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Day 4 fills Day 1's space with specific luminaries. The stars barely get a mention in the text — yet Josh's point is that in real darkness, stars are the most important lights. You cannot work by starlight, but you can know *where* you are and *what time* it is.[^1] Why does knowing where you are and what season you are in matter so much? Have you ever been in a dark season where you didn't know either — and what happened when someone helped you see?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The stars signal the seasons — when to plant, when to rest, when to migrate. Are you currently in a season you have clearly named? If not — what would change if you could?

</div>

### Exodus 27:20–21 — The Oil <span class="guide-time">5 min</span> {#exodus-272021-the-oil}

*Read Exodus 27:20–21 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Quick Observation:** The people bring clear oil beaten from olives — literally the fruit of the land (Day 3). The community's contribution *becomes* the light the priest tends.[^2] What does your community bring that makes your lamp possible?

</div>

### Exodus 28 — The Garments <span class="guide-time">35 min</span> {#exodus-28-the-garments}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The shoulder stones bear all twelve names of Israel's sons — the whole community — on the priest's shoulders.[^3] The breastpiece bears twelve individual stones, one per tribe, over the priest's heart. The distinction: the shoulders carry the community as a whole; the heart holds each tribe as an individual, ensuring no one is lost in the math. In your own leadership — of a household, a team, a small group, a friendship — do you tend to carry people on your shoulders or on your heart? Where does that distinction matter most?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Reed's practice: after meeting someone new, he writes down their name and one detail, and months later greets them by name.[^4] Brent learns every name in a wedding party. These are small-scale acts of the breastpiece. Whose name have you been meaning to hold more carefully? And is there someone whose name you have let slip?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The gold plate inscribed "Holy to the LORD" carries this function: Aaron bears the people's guilt so they will be accepted before God.[^5] Reed's observation: this challenges the common claim that "God cannot be in the presence of sin." Aaron carries the community's guilt *into* the holy place, not away from it. What does it mean that holiness travels toward sin rather than away from it? How does that change what "being holy" means in practice?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The rabbis read the command to keep the altar fire always burning as advice to keep it *small*.[^6] A fire you tend constantly, even if small, is better than a great fire that burns out. What is your one manageable practice — the small flame you can actually tend daily?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#closing}

> *"The priestly garments are built around the same logic as the stars: not to shine so brightly that no one can look at you, but to help people know where they are and what time it is. To carry the whole community on your shoulders while weighing each person's name on your heart. To be findable. To give away fruit. And to carry the community's guilt toward God rather than away from it. Holiness travels toward people, not away from them."*
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** One sentence —

*"The name I most need to hold over my heart right now is ___."*

</div>

### Looking Ahead — Week 5: Day 5 and the Ordination {#looking-ahead-week-5-day-5-and-the-ordination}

**[Ep. 329 — Sanctuary: Moving the Mishkan](/guides/329)** (1:40:41)

Day 5 — the first blessing in creation, the first *nefesh chayah* — pairs with Exodus 29's ordination of Aaron and his sons. Birds and sea creatures both come from the waters. The birds' blessing is on the *earth*, not in the sky. Their fruitfulness requires landing. The priests are the same: they can enter holy spaces, but their calling is at the doorway, among the people. Three ordination offerings move from sin (humility) to transformation to feast. And Exodus 29 ends with God's thesis: "I brought them out of Egypt so that I might dwell among them."

<div class="guide-homework" markdown="1">

**Before Week 5:** *When have you received care from someone who had clearly "landed" — who was at home in elevated or privileged space but chose to be near you? What did that feel like?*

</div>
