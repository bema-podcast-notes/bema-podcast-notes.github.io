### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Ezra and Nehemiah told one continuous story of a hard, wave-by-wave return from exile. Zerubbabel the pioneer, Ezra the shepherd and Nehemiah the prophet led in very different ways, and God blessed each of them because they worked the way God created them to work.[^1]

**This week:** Haggai, a prophet whose ministry lasted only about nine months, steps into that same rebuilding work in Jerusalem with a blunt message: it is time to get up and build God's house.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

Haggai speaks to the returned remnant during the time of Ezra and Nehemiah, when the people had built comfortable homes for themselves while God's temple still lay in ruins. The episode asks why God, who told David he was content in a tent, now seems to want a house, and it answers that the issue is the heart of God's people more than the building.[^3] Haggai's repeated call to "give careful thought to your ways" invites the people to look honestly at whether their priorities are working.[^4]

The episode also follows the people's morale over those nine months: conviction, work, discouragement, renewed promise, drift and another call to re-center. Along the way God promises that the humble second temple will carry greater glory than Solomon's, and Haggai teaches through a priestly question about purity that impurity spreads more easily than holiness.[^5]

**Opening Question:** Think of a project that started with energy and then got hard partway through: a renovation, a garden, a new job, a ministry, a family goal. What kept it going, or what made it stall?

### Give Careful Thought to Your Ways <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#give-careful-thought-to-your-ways}

*Haggai asks a struggling people to look honestly at their own lives before he asks them to build.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Haggai 1:1–11 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Listen for the phrases that repeat and for the list of things the people are doing. The notes say Haggai uses "give careful thought to your ways" again and again (1:5, 7; 2:15, 18), creating a teaching rhythm, and the episode paraphrases his question as "How's that working out for you?"[^6] What do you notice about the specific examples Haggai gives, and what tone do you hear in the way he asks?

*Read 2 Samuel 7:1–7 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The notes set this scene beside 2 Samuel 7, where God told David he did not need a house. David wanted to build because he had just built himself a palace and felt the contrast; the remnant had built "paneled houses" for themselves and felt no such discomfort.[^7] The notes conclude that "This is more about the heart of God's people than it is about his house."[^8] What is the difference between David's motivation and the remnant's, and why might God answer the two situations so differently?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The episode connects obedience with satisfaction, "not in a prosperity gospel sense, but in terms of genuine fulfillment," and it distinguishes biblical teaching from formulaic "if we do this, God will make it rain" thinking.[^9] Our churches have heard different teaching about how obedience and blessing relate. Without needing to settle that, how would you describe the difference between Haggai's "how's that working out for you?" and a promise that obedience pays off materially?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q4:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes ask how often Christians prioritize personal comfort, career advancement and lifestyle upgrades while giving minimal attention to God's purposes.[^10] If Haggai looked at your week and asked you to give careful thought to your ways, where would he point first, and what would building God's house look like there?

</div>

### The Glory of This Present House <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#the-glory-of-this-present-house}

*About a month into the work, the people are discouraged by how small the new temple looks.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Haggai 2:1–9 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes say some of the older people remembered Solomon's temple, and the second temple would be smaller, less ornate and built with fewer resources. It remained a "weak, tepid memory" of Solomon's temple until Herod's renovation.[^11] The original notes hear "hints of Joshua" in God's words, "Be strong!", and say God "is going to shake things up."[^12] As you read verses 3–5 again, what does God say to discouraged people, and what does God not say?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** God promises, "The glory of this present house will be greater than the glory of the former house" (2:9). The notes argue that "glory" here cannot mean architecture, but "the presence of God and what God will accomplish through this temple."[^13] The episode then turns the question on us: "Is it the impressive nature of our Bible knowledge? Is it the academic credentials that we place behind our names?"[^14] What do churches and Christians tend to count as glory today, and what would it mean to measure by presence instead?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty's final thoughts say, "God is still able to dwell in even the humblest of temples, in fact he seems to prefer it."[^15] Where in your life or your community does something feel too small or too plain to matter, and how does Haggai's promise change the way you see it?

</div>

### Drift Is Easy, Holiness Takes Work <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#drift-is-easy-holiness-takes-work}

*Haggai uses a question about ritual purity to explain why staying faithful takes sustained effort.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Haggai 2:10–19 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Haggai asks the priests two questions from the purity laws of Leviticus. The notes summarize the answers: something consecrated does not make ordinary things holy by touching them, but something defiled does make clean things unclean. "It takes so much more work to remain clean than it is to be unclean and sinful."[^16] What do you notice about the two questions and the priests' answers, and why might Haggai have chosen this example for people in the middle of a long building project?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** The notes trace a cycle across Haggai's nine months:[^17]

- Initial conviction about priorities
- Response, as the people begin to work
- Discouragement about a month in
- Renewed encouragement through God's promise
- Spiritual drift two months later
- A renewed call to give careful thought

The notes call this the real struggle of sustaining obedience "month after month after month," and they borrow Eugene Peterson's phrase "a long obedience in the same direction," suggesting that recognizing the pattern as normal rather than as failure helps people persevere.[^18] Where do you find yourself in this cycle right now, and what helps you move from drift back to careful thought?

*Read Haggai 2:20–23 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> God tells Zerubbabel, "I will make you like my signet ring." The notes explain that a signet ring sealed documents with royal authority and represented the king's own presence, and that the promise says this humble work is "going somewhere positive and redemptive."[^19] What ordinary, unglamorous work in your life might God be sealing with that kind of purpose?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> The remnant came home, built paneled houses and left God's house in ruins, and Haggai asked them to give careful thought to their ways. When they started building, the work looked small next to the memory of Solomon's temple, and God promised that its glory would be greater, because glory is about God's presence. Haggai also told them the truth about faithfulness: drift is easy and holiness takes work, month after month. God still dwells in humble temples and seems to prefer them, if his people keep walking the path.[^20]
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, name one "paneled house" you want to give careful thought to this week.

</div>
