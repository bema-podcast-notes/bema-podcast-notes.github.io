### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** We walked through the Herodian homes of Zippori and asked, without heaping on guilt, what story our own comforts tell about who sits at the center.

**This week:** The Essenes went the opposite direction. Convinced the Temple was corrupt beyond repair, they withdrew to the desert to preserve "the path" through radical devotion to the Text.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The notes describe the Essenes as a movement driven by priests who believed God had abandoned the corrupt Temple system and who saw that corruption as a sign of the end times. They withdrew to Qumran near the Dead Sea, calling themselves "sons of light," and grounded their mission in Isaiah 40 and Jeremiah 6:16.[^1] Their spirituality had two sides: knowing the path, through a meticulous four-person copying process that produced the Dead Sea Scrolls, and walking the path, seen in the dam and canal they built to fill their ritual baths with "living water."[^2]

The episode also makes a case that John the Baptist had Essene connections, while noting the crucial difference: John went out to the people.[^3] The application holds both sides together. The Essenes' devotion is something to imitate; their isolation is something to avoid. As the original notes put it, "The isolation may have been a problem but their dedication was not."[^4] Because the episode is short, this guide spends extra time with the texts the Essenes and John built their lives on.

### Preparing the Way in the Desert <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#preparing-the-way-in-the-desert}

*The Essenes read two prophetic texts as a personal calling.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Isaiah 40:1–5 and Jeremiah 6:16 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes say the Essenes "literally went to the desert believing they were fulfilling this prophecy" from Isaiah 40, and that Jeremiah 6:16 shaped their sense of mission as preserving the "ancient paths."[^5] Read both passages again. What words or images stand out to you? If you took these verses as a personal calling, what would you feel compelled to do?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The original notes capture the Essene motivation this way: "Someday someone will ask about the path and someone better be able to tell them and show them what the path is."[^6] Who in your life has been that person for you, someone who could show you the path when you asked? What made them ready?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The Essenes divided the world into "sons of light" and "sons of darkness," counting the Temple establishment and anyone who compromised with the culture among the darkness.[^7] The notes also describe their "fanatical commitment to a particular version of eschatology," reading the Temple's corruption as a sign that the age was ending.[^8] Our traditions read the end times in different ways, and this question is not asking anyone to settle that. Instead: how have beliefs about the end times, in the church you grew up in or now attend, shaped how people related to the surrounding culture? Where do you notice a "light and darkness" way of sorting people in yourself?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q4:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes admit that much about the Essenes is debated. Scholars long assumed they were an all-male priestly community, yet women's remains have been found in Qumran's cemeteries.[^9] How do you respond when a teaching you assumed was settled turns out to be more complicated?

</div>

### Knowing the Path, Walking the Path <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#knowing-the-path-walking-the-path}

*Two practices at Qumran show what total devotion looked like.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The Essenes copied Scripture with four people for every word: one recited, one verified the recitation, one wrote, and one verified the writing. At God's name, all four stopped, went to the *mikvah* (ritual bath), washed completely, and only then wrote the name.[^10] The notes conclude that "knowing the path wasn't merely intellectual but involved the whole person."[^11] What do you notice about how this process treats the Text? What would it look like for your own reading of Scripture to involve "the whole person"?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The Essenes believed purification required *maim chaim*, "living water" from a spring or from rain, and that water stopped being living once moved by human hands. In the desert, they built a dam in a wadi and a plastered canal so rainwater would flow by gravity into their baths.[^12] The notes call this their commitment to doing "God's thing God's way."[^13] What is your first reaction to that much effort for that purpose? What does it reveal about how seriously they took obedience?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> When scholars compared the Dead Sea Scrolls with medieval manuscripts about a thousand years younger, the notes report they found "less than 2% variance."[^14] How does knowing about this kind of care affect the way you hold the Bible in your hands?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Marty's rabbi, Ray Vander Laan, once told him: "I believe that if God ever finds another group of people that want to be that devoted to knowing the path and walking the path, I think God will show up." The notes add, "This is one of the reasons Marty started Bema."[^15] The episode's discussion questions ask, "What is everyone's relationship to the text?"[^16] Be honest: what is your relationship with Scripture right now, and what is one realistic step that would deepen it?

</div>

### A Voice That Went to the People <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#a-voice-that-went-to-the-people}

*John the Baptist shared much with the Essenes, but he did one thing they would not.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Luke 3:2–6 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** Read the passage again and notice where John is, what he proclaims, and which prophet Luke quotes. The notes list hints of an Essene connection: John's father Zechariah was a priest, John was dedicated to the Lord, and he shared the desert setting, the emphasis on repentance and purification, and the use of Isaiah 40.[^17] Which of these connections seems strongest to you, and what does it add to your picture of John?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes observe that the place where the Jordan enters the Dead Sea, one of the sites where John baptized and where Jesus was baptized, is less than three miles from Qumran. The original notes ask: "Is it coincidence that Jesus begins his ministry so close to a group of people who believed that their devout dedication to the path would reveal the glory of the Lord and that God would return?"[^18] How would you answer that question?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q11:** The notes name the key difference: "Rather than waiting in the desert for people to come seeking the path, John went out to the people."[^19] They also contrast Essene washings, "repeated ritual purifications for maintaining the purity of the separated community," with John's baptism, "a one-time public act of repentance and preparation for the coming kingdom."[^20] Our traditions understand baptism in different ways. What has baptism meant in the church that formed you? What, if anything, does this background add to or complicate in that understanding?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q12:** The original notes sum up the Essenes' problem in two lines: "They aren't talking to anybody" and they have "separated themselves entirely from the world."[^21] Where in your life are you tempted to withdraw: from people who see things differently, from a hard relationship, from your neighborhood? What would it look like to carry your devotion out to them, the way John did?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> The Essenes looked at a corrupt Temple and went to the desert to prepare the way of the Lord. They copied every word four times over, washed before writing God's name, and carved canals through the wilderness so that even their water would come by God's hand. They were right that devotion matters, and the episode suggests God did show up, less than three miles away, at the river where John baptized Jesus. But they were not talking to anybody. John kept their devotion and carried it to the people. That is the invitation in front of us: know the path, walk the path, and stay at the crossroads where others can see it.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one commitment to the Text you want to make, and who is one person you want to stay connected to while you keep it?

</div>
