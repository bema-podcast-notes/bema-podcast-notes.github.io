### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** The Jerusalem Council distinguished the works of the law that made Israel distinct from what was required of Gentiles, and chose not to burden them. Then Paul and Barnabas, agreed on the gospel, parted ways over Mark.

**This week:** Paul's second and third journeys. A surprising circumcision, a speech in Athens, and a quiet shift in how Paul spends his time.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode opens with what looks like a contradiction. Right after the council decides Gentiles need not be circumcised, Paul circumcises Timothy. Marty argues that both actions come from the same conviction: the gospel welcomes "whoever is being pushed out." In Athens, Paul begins with affirmation, quotes the Greeks' own poets, and uses their altar to an unknown god as a bridge, an approach the notes connect to the conviction that truth can be found everywhere.

The title comes from a language problem. The Greek New Testament uses one word, *mathetes*, for everything from a casual student to a committed disciple, while Hebrew distinguishes a *limud* (as the notes describe it, a student who attends and learns) from a *talmid* (a disciple who commits years to imitating a rabbi's whole life). The episode traces Paul's journeys from zero disciples on the first, to three on the second, to "about twelve" on the third, mirroring Jesus, and asks whether disciple-making is more central to God's mission than church planting alone.

### Whoever Is Being Pushed Out <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#whoever-is-being-pushed-out}

*Right after the council, Paul does something that seems to undo it.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Acts 16:1–5 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Notice what Luke tells us about Timothy's family, what Paul does, and what Paul and Timothy then deliver to the churches (verse 4). What makes this scene surprising, coming right after Acts 15?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes describe Timothy as "a mamzer - illegitimate child," excluded from circumcision "due to his mixed heritage." The episode rejects the common explanation that Paul was simply avoiding offense to Jews, calling Timothy's circumcision "not cultural accommodation but radical welcome."[^1] How does that reading change the way you see Paul's decision?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes resolve the apparent contradiction this way: when Gentiles were excluded for being uncircumcised, Paul said they did not need it; when Timothy was excluded from circumcision, Paul circumcised him. "The Gospel reverses whatever forces people to be outsiders," and its message is that "whoever is being pushed out is welcome in."[^2] Think about your church, family or circle of friends. Who is being pushed out right now, and in which direction would welcome have to move to bring them in?

</div>

### Finding Truth in Athens <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#finding-truth-in-athens}

*Paul engages one of the most sophisticated audiences in the ancient world, and he begins by listening.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Acts 17:16–28 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Notice how Paul begins his speech, what he points to in the city, and whom he quotes. The notes say Paul "begins with affirmation rather than confrontation," "studies and quotes their poets and philosophers," and uses "their own religious symbols (altar to unknown god) as conversation bridges."[^3] What do you notice about the tone of this speech compared to the city that distressed him in verse 16?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes hold up the Jews of Berea as a "model of noble character through eager reception and careful Scripture examination."[^4]

*Read Acts 17:10–12 aloud.*

What two qualities does Luke praise in the Bereans? Which of the two comes more naturally to you?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The episode teaches that "truth can be found everywhere and should be utilized wherever discovered." The notes apply this to "secular philosophy, non-Christian religions, popular culture - as all truth belongs to God," while also naming the need to balance "truth recognition with doctrinal discernment."[^5] Our traditions draw these lines in different places. Where have you found something true in an unexpected place? How do you hold that alongside discernment?

</div>

### Students or Disciples? <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#students-or-disciples}

*A single Greek word hides a distinction that shaped how Jesus and Paul made followers.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The episode contrasts three terms:[^6]

- ***Mathetes*** (Greek): a general term covering everything from casual student to committed disciple.
- ***Limud*** (Hebrew, as the notes use it): a student who attends classes and learns but does not commit to imitating the teacher's life.
- ***Talmid*** (Hebrew): a disciple who commits years to following a rabbi, imitating every aspect of the rabbi's life.

It then traces Paul's journeys: on the first, zero disciples and zero house churches; on the second, three disciples (Timothy, Priscilla and Aquila) and one house church; on the third, about twelve disciples and multiple house churches.[^7]

*Read Acts 18:1–4 and Acts 19:1–10 aloud.*

Notice who Paul lives and works alongside, how long he stays, and how many people Luke mentions in Acts 19:7. What do you notice about how Paul's ministry has changed?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q8:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes call attention to "Luke's deliberate paralleling of Paul's ministry with Jesus' ministry," including the "about twelve disciples" detail, and conclude that "disciple-making appears more central to God's mission than church planting alone."[^8] What would change if a church measured its health by the disciples it was forming rather than by its attendance or the number of churches it started?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** The notes end with a challenge: "are we making mathetes or talmidim?"[^9] Where are you a student of Jesus, attending and learning, and where are you a disciple, imitating his life? Who is one person who could follow you closely enough to learn from how you live?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Paul's actions around circumcision only look inconsistent until we see what drives them: whoever is being pushed out is welcome in. In Athens he begins by listening and affirming, trusting that truth found in the culture can point beyond itself. And across his journeys he moves from spreading the message widely to investing in a small number of people who live alongside him, about twelve, like Jesus. The question the episode leaves us with is personal: are we making students, or disciples?
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each: who is one person you will invest in more deeply this month?

</div>
