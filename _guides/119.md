### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Jesus fed 4,000 in Gentile territory to show his provision is enough for the nations, warned his disciples about a yeast that shuts outsiders out, and healed a blind man in two stages as a picture of their partial sight.

**This week:** At Caesarea Philippi, a place the ancient world called the Gates of Hell, Peter names Jesus as Messiah and then, moments later, tries to correct him.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode begins with the setting. Caesarea Philippi sat beside a spring flowing from a cave at the headwaters of the Jordan. It was first a center of Baal worship and later of Pan worship, and its cave was seen as an entrance to the underworld. Its yearly festival drew huge crowds and involved extreme sexual debauchery. The hosts argue that Jesus deliberately brought his disciples to this most pagan and uncomfortable place to teach them about his church.

Peter's confession, the hosts suggest, carries hints of Joshua's conquest and of Israel hearing God's voice at Sinai. The episode lays out three readings of "on this rock" (Catholic, Protestant, and a rabbinic, contextual reading tied to the physical rock face), and describes the Kingdom's strategy as offensive rather than defensive. It ends with Peter's whiplash, from receiving revelation and a new name to being called "Satan," and with Jesus' call to take up the cross, possibly spoken to a pagan crowd.

### Confession at the Gates <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#confession-at-the-gates}

*Jesus asks the most important question of his ministry in the most unlikely place.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 16:13–20 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes say Caesarea Philippi was considered "The Gates of Hell," a center of Baal and later Pan worship, where the spring flowing from a cave was seen as a literal entrance to the underworld. Herod's son Philip built up the city to profit from the huge festival held there.[^1] Imagine walking into this place as one of the disciples. What do you notice about the two questions Jesus asks (verses 13 and 15), and why might he ask them here rather than somewhere quieter?

*Read Joshua 3:10 aloud, then read Deuteronomy 5:22–27 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes observe that the phrase "living God" appears only a few times in Scripture. In Joshua 3:10 the living God will drive out the Canaanite nations; in Deuteronomy 5:26, the first mention, Israel hears the voice of the living God from the fire at Sinai. The episode connects this second text to listening, noting that the name Simon means "one who hears."[^2] What does each passage add when Peter says "living God" in a place like this?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The episode lays out three readings of "on this rock." The Catholic reading sees Peter himself, and the notes grant "validity to recognizing Peter as the leader of the early church." The Protestant reading sees Peter's confession. A rabbinic, contextual reading notes that rabbis point to concrete things, and that Jesus uses two Greek words, *petros* ("a rock or stone," Peter's new name) and *petra* ("bedrock, cliff face"), perhaps pointing at the rock face of the Gates of Hell itself.[^3] Which reading did you grow up with, and what does it protect or emphasize? What does the physical setting add for you?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes describe Jesus' strategy as offensive, not defensive: "Jesus runs into chaos with shalom," and the church is not built in "safe little corners." The gates of hell will not hold against a Kingdom that is advancing.[^4] The notes also raise the need for wisdom and appropriate boundaries.[^5] Where is a place near you that feels like the gates of hell, a place of chaos or brokenness? What might it look like, wisely, to bring shalom there?

</div>

### From Rock to Stumbling Block <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#from-rock-to-stumbling-block}

*Peter's best moment and his worst moment happen in the same conversation.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 16:21–28 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes list Peter's high point (he receives *sod*, the level of meaning that only God can reveal, is praised, renamed, and promised the keys of the kingdom) and his low point (he rebukes his rabbi and is called "Satan," meaning opposer).[^6] Using your notes from this week, compare verses 17–19 with verses 22–23. What changed between Peter's two speeches? What do you think Peter was trying to protect?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode reads "get behind me" as a call for Peter to return to his proper place as a disciple, following rather than leading, and to go back to being Simon, "the one who hears/listens."[^7] What does it suggest that the remedy for Peter's mistake is to go back to listening?

*Read Mark 8:34–38 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes point out that Mark and Luke show a crowd present when Jesus speaks about taking up the cross. Given the location, that crowd would likely have been Pan worshipers. The episode suggests the invitation was spoken to pagans, and that the warning about being ashamed may have come while the disciples shrank back in embarrassment.[^8] Who does Jesus call in Mark 8:34? How does it change the invitation to imagine it spoken to people fresh from the festival at the Gates of Hell?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes warn against "letting spiritual highs lead to pride" and "rebuking God's word when it doesn't match our preferences."[^9] Think of a time when a spiritual high point was followed closely by a misstep. What helps you stay a listener rather than becoming the one who corrects Jesus?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#closing}

> Jesus took his disciples to the most pagan place they could imagine and asked who they said he was. There, at the Gates of Hell, Peter named him Messiah, Son of the living God, and Jesus spoke of building his church on that rock. The episode hears an advancing Kingdom here, one that runs into chaos with shalom instead of hiding in safe corners. Moments later Peter tried to correct his rabbi and was sent back to his place as a disciple who listens. And Jesus' invitation to take up the cross may have gone out even to the crowd at the Gates of Hell.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: where is one place you sense Jesus inviting you to bring shalom, rather than avoiding it?

</div>
