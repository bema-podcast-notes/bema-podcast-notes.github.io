### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** We traced a six-step pattern of order out of chaos from Genesis to the Jordan, and watched Jesus meet Israel's three wilderness tests by knowing the text and walking it.

**This week:** Jesus begins his public ministry. He settles in Capernaum, calls his first disciples, and opens the Sermon on the Mount with the Beatitudes.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode reads Jesus's "withdrawal" to Galilee after John's arrest as anything but a retreat: he set up his base in Capernaum, within walking distance of Herod Antipas's capital.[^1] He called fishermen who had likely already been sent home from the Jewish educational system and gave them a second chance to follow a rabbi.[^2] Crowds came from every corner of the region, religious Jews and pagans and everyone in between, and Matthew presents that mixed crowd as the backdrop for what Jesus says next.[^3]

Following Dallas Willard, the episode reads the Beatitudes as pronouncements rather than prescriptions: God's favor already rests on people the world considers unblessed.[^4] It also traces a mirrored structure in the Beatitudes with hunger for righteousness and mercy at the center, and explains righteousness (*tzedakah*) as generosity toward others rather than rule-keeping.[^5]

### A Bold Move and a Second Chance <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#a-bold-move-and-a-second-chance}

*Jesus moves into Herod's backyard and calls the students no rabbi had chosen.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 4:12–22 and Isaiah 9:1–2 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The original notes ask whether Jesus "withdrew from Herod… or did he setup ship right underneath Herod nose?"[^6] The notes say Capernaum was about an hour's walk from Tiberias, Herod's capital, and that ministering so close "demonstrated chutzpah (audacity)."[^7] They also describe Matthew's quotation of Isaiah as a *remez*, a textual hint that expects the audience to recall the whole passage: judgment on Zebulun and Naphtali, followed by a promise of restoration.[^8] What do you notice in these verses about where Jesus goes, what he announces, and what Isaiah's context adds?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The notes outline the three stages of Jewish education, *Bet Sefer*, *Midrash* and *Talmud*, and say most students (99%) were told to "go back to your father's trade" before finishing.[^9] Peter and Andrew were fishing on their own, likely already released from that path, while James and John were still fishing with their father.[^10] When Jesus said *Lech achari* ("Come, follow me"), "he offered these men a second chance at rabbinical discipleship."[^11] Put yourself in the boat. What would it have meant to hear those words after being sent home?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode presents Bruce Chilton's theory that Jesus was viewed as a *mamzer* (an illegitimate child), which would have kept him out of traditional rabbinic training and helps explain why John, a "rogue priest" trained by the Essenes, might have taken him as a disciple.[^12] The notes describe Jesus as a "ragamuffin rabbi" calling "dropout students."[^13] How does it change your picture of Jesus to imagine him as someone who knew exclusion from the inside?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes say the disciples Jesus called had already "failed out" of the traditional system and were offered "a second chance at significance."[^14] Where have you received a second chance you did not earn, and who in your life might be waiting for you to offer them one?

</div>

### Blessed Are Those <span class="guide-time">35 min</span> {#blessed-are-those}

*Seeing a crowd of every kind of person, Jesus tells his disciples whom God's favor already rests on.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 4:23–5:12 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes list where the crowds came from: Galilee (religious Jews), the Decapolis (pagans), Jerusalem and Judea, Syria, and the region across the Jordan.[^15] They suggest imagining a modern gathering of "Conservative religious fundamentalists," "Secular humanists and atheists," "Academic theologians," political activists from opposing parties and international observers, and say "The disciples' discomfort with this mixed crowd mirrors how uncomfortable we might feel in such a setting today."[^16] Who would be the hardest person in that crowd for you to sit next to, and why?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes, following Dallas Willard's *The Divine Conspiracy*, say "The Beatitudes are pronouncements, not prescriptions," and that "blessed" means "God's favor rests upon" not "happy."[^17] Read Matthew 5:3–10 again slowly, pausing after each line to say, "God's favor rests on you." What changes when you hear each line as an announcement rather than an assignment?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes trace a chiasm, a mirrored structure, in the Beatitudes: the first half about "Experiencing brokenness" and the second half about "Engaging brokenness," with hunger for righteousness and mercy side by side at the center.[^18] They conclude: "True hunger for righteousness (tzedakah) leads to mercy. Righteousness is not about rule-following but about generosity and caring for others."[^19] Look at the two lines at the center. How does placing them together reshape what it means to be righteous?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes contrast two ways of thinking. Traditional religious thinking says, "When I finally get my spiritual life together, then God will bless me." Jesus says, "God's favor is already on you in your brokenness, mourning, and need."[^20] They add that the second half is costly: "The last time you truly showed mercy, how hard was it?"[^21] Which Beatitude do you most need to hear as a pronouncement over your own life right now, and which one are you being asked to live out for someone else?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Jesus did not hide from Herod after John's arrest; he moved into Herod's backyard and began announcing a new day, just as Isaiah had promised for Zebulun and Naphtali. He called fishermen who had been sent home from school and gave them a second chance to follow a rabbi. When a crowd of every kind of person gathered, Jesus pulled his disciples aside and told them whom God's favor rests on: the poor in spirit, the grieving, the meek, the hungry. At the center of his words, righteousness and mercy stand together, and the Sermon on the Mount begins with good news for people who know they do not have it all together.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: complete the line "Blessed are..." with a person or situation in your life where you want to see God's favor this week.

</div>
