### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** The third day, the bronze altar, and the courtyard asked whether we make room for raw community, let it grow beyond our control, and give away the seeds of our lives so others can flourish.[^1]

**This week:** The fourth reflection, built on Josh Bossé's Episode 328, links the fourth day of creation to teaching, to the oil that kept the lamp burning, and to the priests in their sacred garments.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

On the fourth day God sets lights in the sky to "serve as signs to mark sacred times," and they give light and order time simply by being where God placed them. The lamp in the Tabernacle needs steady tending "from evening till morning," with oil supplied by the whole community. Aaron's garments make his role visible and carry the names of the tribes on his shoulders and over his heart.[^3]

The main theme is that teaching happens over time, in community, and most of all through the way a person lives, not only through what they say. Marty's central question is what it would look like to teach the kingdom of God "not with words, but simply with your presence," and he closes by naming five ways a life teaches, ending with "the way we stop."[^4] The episode is longer than recent weeks, but the guide keeps the same unhurried pace.

### Opening <span class="guide-time">10 min</span> {#opening}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-opening" markdown="1">

**Opening Question:** Describe one person who taught you about God more by how they lived than by anything they said. What did you learn from watching them?

### Lights and a Lamp <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#lights-and-a-lamp}

*The heavenly lights and the Tabernacle lamp both give light and mark time without saying a word.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:14–19 aloud. Then read Exodus 27:20–21 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** God makes the lights to separate day from night, to "serve as signs" marking "sacred times, and days and years," and to give light on the earth. The word often translated "sacred times" (Hebrew *mo'adim*) is the same word later used for Israel's appointed feasts. Aaron and his sons tend the lamp "from evening till morning," which matches the creation refrain "there was evening and there was morning."[^5] What do the lights and the lamp have in common in these two readings? What do you notice about time in both passages?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The people bring "clear oil of pressed olives," the purest grade, which comes from crushing. The priests tend the light, but the people supply the fuel, which the notes say makes teaching a shared effort. They compare this to volunteers who show up every week, set up chairs, lead the children, and bring meals.[^6] Who has been "bringing the oil" in a community you have been part of, and what did their faithfulness teach you?

*Sit together in silence for two minutes, then take up the next question.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The lamp is "a lasting ordinance… for the generations to come," and the notes draw the lesson that "Teaching is a long, faithful practice, not a one-time event." The reflection asks how each person can take part in teaching with the community, not just receive it from experts.[^7] Where are you already part of your community's teaching, whether by bringing oil or tending the light? Where might you be invited to take part?

</div>

### Names on the Shoulders and Heart <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#names-on-the-shoulders-and-heart}

*Aaron's garments speak before he says anything.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:14–19 aloud again. Then read Exodus 28:1–12 and 28:29–30 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The garments are made "to give him dignity and honor," in the same materials as the Tabernacle itself, so the priest becomes "a kind of walking sanctuary." Marty asks, "How do you identify someone as a priest?" both literally (by garments and official role) and poetically (by what you can see in a person's life).[^8] Looking at the passage, what details make the priest recognizable? Then answer Marty's question the poetic way: what signals a priestly life to you?

*Sit together in silence for two minutes.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Aaron bears the names of the twelve tribes on two onyx stones on his shoulders and on twelve stones "over his heart." The notes say, "The priest literally carries the people." Marty's central prompt follows: "What could it look like to teach the kingdom of God? Not with words, but simply with your presence."[^9] Whose names do you carry, on your shoulders or over your heart, and what might your presence be teaching them?

*Read Exodus 19:6 and 1 Peter 2:9 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes connect this reflection to the priesthood of all of God's people (Exodus 19:6 and 1 Peter 2:9) and conclude that "Teaching is not limited to official teachers."[^10] The traditions in this room hold different convictions about who teaches, who leads, and what ordination means. How did your church background shape your sense of who is a teacher? How does that sit with the idea that a whole life can teach, and what would it change for you this week?

</div>

### The Way We Stop <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#the-way-we-stop}

*The bells on the robe and the plate on the turban make the priest's presence known.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Exodus 28:31–38 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Golden bells and pomegranates alternate around the hem, so the priest's movement can be heard: "you hear him before you see him." The golden plate engraved "Holy to the Lord" shows his identity in the most visible place on his body.[^11] Think of someone who brings a noticeable change into a room, such as calm, joy, or attention. What is it about them that others feel?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Marty's closing: "the way we live, the things we do, the postures we take, the energy we give and the way we stop. These are priestly roles in our world." The notes suggest "the way we stop" likely refers to Sabbath and keeping sacred rhythms of rest, tying back to the "sacred times" of the fourth day.[^12] Which of these five is your life teaching most clearly right now? Which would you most like to teach differently, especially the way you stop?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> The sun and moon never give a lecture, yet they order every day and season. The lamp burned from evening till morning because a whole community brought the oil and the priests kept tending it. Aaron's garments spoke before he did: he carried the people on his shoulders and over his heart, and his whole life was marked "Holy to the Lord." How we live, what we do, the postures we take, the energy we give, and the way we stop are all priestly work, and all of it teaches.
{: .guide-summary}

*Read Genesis 1:14–19 aloud one last time, then sit together in silence for one minute.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, what would you want someone to learn about God just by watching your life this week?

</div>
