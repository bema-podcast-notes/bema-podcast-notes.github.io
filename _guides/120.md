### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** At Caesarea Philippi, the Gates of Hell, Peter confessed Jesus as Messiah, Jesus spoke of a church that advances into chaos with shalom, and Peter went from receiving revelation to being sent back to his place as a listening disciple.

**This week:** Six days later, on a high mountain, Jesus is transfigured before Peter, James and John, and the Gospel story turns toward Jerusalem.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode reads the Transfiguration as a deliberate retelling of Moses at Mount Sinai, listing seven parallels between the two mountain scenes. It argues that Peter's offer to build tabernacles was not foolish blurting but sharp Torah thinking, since building the Tabernacle is exactly what comes after Sinai. When God speaks from the cloud, the hosts hear a quotation from each of the three parts of the Hebrew Scriptures. They also connect the scene to an ancient Jewish midrash on Psalm 43 in which Moses and Elijah are God's "truth" and the Messiah is God's "light." The episode suggests the mountain was Mount Hermon rather than the traditional Mount Tabor.

Coming down the mountain, Jesus finds his disciples unable to heal a boy, a scene the episode compares to Moses coming down to the golden calf. The hosts suggest the problem was trying to use spiritual authority from a posture of pride. The Transfiguration also marks a turning point: from here, Jesus sets his sights on Jerusalem.

### A New Sinai <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#a-new-sinai}

*On a high mountain, Jesus' story echoes Moses' story, and Peter recognizes it.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 17:1–8 aloud, then read Exodus 24:15–18 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes list seven parallels between the Transfiguration and Moses at Sinai: a high mountain, three companions, a cloud covering, the glory present, a changed appearance, the glory settling (*shakan*, "to dwell" or "settle"), and the timing "after six days."[^1] Using the list you brought, which of the seven can you find in the two passages? Which seem clearest to you, and what might the echo have signaled to Jewish readers?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Peter is often pictured as an impulsive fool here. The episode disagrees: after Sinai, the next major movement in Exodus is building the Tabernacle; the word Peter uses is literally "tabernacles" (*sukkot*); and Peter, only six days after being rebuked at Caesarea Philippi, is trying to get things right. God's response, the notes say, is not chastisement but affirmation.[^2] How have you usually heard Peter described in this scene? What changes if he is reading the Sinai pattern correctly?

*Read Psalm 43:3–4 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> When God speaks from the cloud, the notes hear one quotation from each part of the Hebrew Scriptures: Psalm 2:7 from the *Ketuvim* (Writings), Isaiah 42:1 from the *Nevi'im* (Prophets), and Deuteronomy 18:15 from the Torah. The episode also points to a midrash on Psalm 43, which the notes date to about eighty years before Jesus, in which Moses and Elijah are "truth" and the Messiah is "light": "When you are in trouble and need vindication, pray for Moses, pray for Elijah, and pray for Messiah."[^3] What does it add to the scene to hear Moses and Elijah as truth and Jesus as light? Which words in Psalm 43:3–4 echo the mountain story?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes draw an application from Peter: "Recovering the original Jewish context often transforms apparent weaknesses into sophisticated theological affirmations."[^4] They also note that Peter's growth was not linear: a great confession, a rebuke, and then this moment of insight.[^5] Who in your life have you been quick to write off as impulsive or foolish? Or where are you, like Peter six days after a rebuke, trying to get it right again? What would a more generous reading look like this week?

</div>

### Coming Down the Mountain <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#coming-down-the-mountain}

*Coming down from the glory, Jesus finds a crisis, and the road turns toward Jerusalem.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 17:14–20 aloud, then read Exodus 32:15–20 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes set two descents side by side. Moses comes down from meeting God, finds the people worshiping the golden calf, burns it in fire and mixes it with water, and the generation is called wicked and perverse. Jesus comes down, finds the disciples unable to cast out a demon from a boy who is thrown into fire and water, and calls the generation "unbelieving and perverse," echoing the Song of Moses in Deuteronomy 32.[^6] Which shared details do you notice when you hear the two passages together?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The disciples had cast out demons before, and they had the authority Jesus gave them. The episode suggests the problem this time was the posture of their hearts: using spiritual authority from pride rather than humility, perhaps treating miracle-working as proof of position, a kind of spiritual idolatry. The notes connect fasting with humility.[^7] Why might authority that worked before fail now? What does it suggest that the episode links this failure to the golden calf?

*Read Matthew 17:22–23 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes describe the Transfiguration as a turning point near the center of Matthew, Mark and Luke. Before it, the focus is on teaching, the feedings and the widening mission to the Gentiles; after it, Jesus "resolutely sets His sights on Jerusalem," with repeated predictions of his death and resurrection.[^8] How does the mood shift in verses 22–23? What do you expect the coming weeks of our study to feel like?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes warn: "When we begin to use spiritual practices to establish our own importance or position, we practice a form of idolatry that renders us ineffective."[^9] Where are you tempted to treat a gift, a role, or your knowledge of Scripture as proof of your standing? What practice of humility (prayer, fasting, or something else) might reset your posture this week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#closing}

> On a high mountain, after six days, a cloud covered Jesus and his three companions, and his face shone like Moses' face had at Sinai. Peter saw the pattern and offered to build tabernacles, and God answered by quoting the Writings, the Prophets and the Torah. Moses and Elijah, truth, stood with the Messiah, light, just as an old Jewish reading of Psalm 43 had hoped. Then, coming down the mountain, Jesus found disciples trying to use power without humility. From here on, Jesus' face is set toward Jerusalem.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one way you want to listen to Jesus more closely as the story turns toward Jerusalem?

</div>
