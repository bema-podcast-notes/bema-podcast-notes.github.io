### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Paul defended God's freedom to show mercy to outsiders: the potter shows patience to vessels others wrote off, and Gentiles are wild branches grafted into Israel's tree, whose calling is irrevocable.

**This week:** Eleven chapters about God's mercy to everyone turn on one word, "therefore," and become a call to live as one body whose defining mark is love.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This is a short episode, so the session spends more time in the text itself. The episode lays out the shape of Romans: three groups with one problem (chapters 1–3), one solution for all (4–8), God choosing to bless and redeem everyone (9–11), and then the "therefore" of chapters 12–16, where the only logical response to God's inclusive grace is to become "people of love."[^1] It notes that Paul urges the believers to offer their bodies as "a living sacrifice," singular: one body serving together.[^2]

The episode then follows that love in three directions: toward fellow believers with different gifts, toward enemies (including a fresh reading of "heaping burning coals"), and toward governing authorities, where submission flows from love rather than blind obedience.[^3]

### The Hinge: Therefore <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-hinge-therefore}

*Paul turns from what God has done to how God's people should live.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Romans 12:1–2 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** What does Paul base his appeal on in verse 1? Look closely at the words "bodies" and "sacrifice": which is plural and which is singular? What does Paul set in contrast in verse 2?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The episode summarizes Romans in four movements, ending with "Therefore, become people of love," and says Paul's "therefore" "connects God's mercy (chapters 1-11) to our response of sacrificial love."[^4] It calls the offering "a living sacrifice" (singular), "one unified body serving together."[^5] Why might love, rather than better doctrine or stricter practice, be the "only logical response" for a church that had been arguing over who belonged?

*Read Romans 12:3–8 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> List the gifts Paul names. The notes draw out that "No group should think themselves superior to others" and that "Each member belongs to all others and serves for the benefit of the community."[^6] Why do you think Paul warns against thinking of ourselves more highly than we ought just before he lists the gifts?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes describe Paul's vision of unity as "not uniformity, but diverse gifts working together as one living sacrifice."[^7] What gift do you see in someone in this group, and what is one way you could offer your own gift for the good of the whole this month?

</div>

### Overcoming Evil with Good <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#overcoming-evil-with-good}

*Love reaches past the church to strangers, persecutors and enemies.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Romans 12:9–21 aloud, then Proverbs 25:21–22.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Go through the commands in Romans 12:9–21 and sort them: which are mostly about fellow believers, and which are about outsiders, persecutors or enemies? Which group of commands takes up more space? What does Paul quote in verse 20?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes say the image of heaping burning coals "is not about passive-aggressive revenge." Instead, "In Hebrew scripture, burning coals always connect to God's presence. Responding to evil with good brings God's presence into the situation."[^8] How have you heard verse 20 explained before, and how does this reading change what Paul is asking of someone who has been wronged?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes connect this to the *sarx* ("flesh"), our animal appetites, saying "Believers must learn to master the sarx and lay down their lives for others, including neighbors, leaders, and enemies."[^9] What does the sarx want to do when someone wrongs us, and what helps you master it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes list practical shapes of a living sacrifice, including "Blessing those who persecute you instead of cursing them" and "Feeding your enemy when hungry, giving drink when thirsty."[^10] Think of one person who has wronged you or who opposes you. What is one concrete good you could do for them, bringing God's presence into that situation?

</div>

### Love and the Authorities <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#love-and-the-authorities}

*Paul applies the same love to life under Roman rule.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Romans 13:1–10 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** What reasons does Paul give in verses 1–7 for submitting to governing authorities? Now read verses 8–10. How do they frame everything that came before? The episode says submission "flows from the broader call to love, not blind obedience that contradicts God's commands."[^11] How does that framing help you read verses 1–7?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes include "Paying taxes and showing respect even to leaders you disagree with" alongside "Maintaining civil behavior while still obeying God when human laws conflict with divine commands," and point to examples such as Daniel, the Hebrew midwives and the apostles.[^12] People of good faith draw this line in different places. How do you discern when love means submitting and when faithfulness to God means refusing?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q11:** The notes call this "a radical alternative to both passive compliance and aggressive resistance."[^13] Think of a leader or authority you find hard to respect. What would it look like to treat them with love this week, without giving up your convictions?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> After eleven chapters showing that God has shown mercy to everyone, Paul says "therefore": the only logical response is to become people of love. We offer our many bodies as one living sacrifice, diverse gifts with no group above another. That love reaches enemies too; heaping burning coals is not revenge but bringing God's presence into a hard place. Even submission to authorities flows from love, not blind obedience. Theology that is true leads to lives that are changed.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: who is one person you will try to overcome evil with good toward this week?

</div>
