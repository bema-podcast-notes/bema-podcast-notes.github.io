### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Abram left his father's household, built altars and pitched tents, and learned in Egypt that he could not control every outcome. He carried that lesson into his conflict with Lot, letting Lot go twice.

**This week:** A frustrated, childless Abram demands answers. God responds with an ancient covenant ceremony and walks the path between the pieces himself, and later gives Abraham a sign to keep.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This episode covers Genesis 15–17. It opens with Abram's frustration: he speaks to God, gets no answer, and speaks again. The hosts suggest that God's silence was protective, and that the partial answer Abram did get led him and Sarai into the Hagar story.[^1] The center of the episode is the blood path covenant, an ancient betrothal ceremony in which both parties walked between halved animals and pledged their lives. In Genesis 15, God walks through on behalf of both parties.[^2]

The episode also traces the long shadow of Abram's trip to Egypt, which he left with wealth, servants, and Hagar, and it reads Genesis 17 as a chiasm with circumcision at its center.[^3] The hosts see a progression from Noah's covenant, where God kept the sign in the sky, to Abraham's, where God's people keep the sign themselves.[^4] The Hagar story involves an enslaved woman and an abuse of power, so expect some hard conversation alongside the good news.

**Opening Question:** Think of a promise someone made to you, or that you made to someone, that was sealed with a ceremony, a handshake, a ring, or a signature. Why did that outward act matter, and what did it change?

### When God Is Silent <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#when-god-is-silent}

*Abram speaks twice before God answers, and the gap between the two says a lot.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 15:1–6 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes point to a small detail: "But Avram said ... And Avram said..." In Hebrew, this repetition implies that the first question got no response and that time passed before the second.[^5] We cannot know how long the gap lasted. Listen to verses 2 and 3 again. What changes between Abram's first statement and his second, and what do you imagine happening in the silence between them?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The notes say that God gave Abram "a *little* insight," but Abram still misunderstood, and he and Sarai brought Hagar into the story and "mess the story up." Marty's reflection: "Sometimes I think I want answers but G-d knows I don't need them."[^6] Another summary adds that God meets us in our frustration and doubt, "responding to our questions even when we're cranky with Him."[^7] Why might more information have been more dangerous for Abram than less? What does God's response to a cranky Abram suggest about how God treats our frustration?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes suggest that sometimes God withholds information because premature answers lead to poor decisions, like giving a teenager complete freedom before they have developed wisdom.[^8] Think of a time you demanded an answer, from God or from life, and acted on a partial one. Looking back, what would you tell yourself in that moment?

</div>

### The Blood Path <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-blood-path}

*An ancient betrothal ceremony shows Abram what kind of partner God is.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 15:7–21 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Before explaining the ceremony, look at the scene itself. The notes point out that birds of prey came down and Abram drove them away, which implies the carcasses had been lying there a while with Abram doing nothing.[^9] Then Abram falls into a deep, terrifying sleep, and God passes between the pieces as a smoking firepot and a blazing torch.[^10] The Hebrew word for this deep sleep, *tardema*, is the same kind of sleep God brought on Adam when Eve was formed.[^11] What do you notice about what Abram does, what he does not do, and who ends up walking the path?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes explain that this was a common covenant used in engagements between the father of the bride and the groom. The lesser party, the groom, walked first, stomping through the blood in a white robe and pledging that if he failed to take care of the bride, the father could do in his blood what he was doing in the blood of the animals. Then the greater party made the same pledge.[^12] Abram, as the lesser party, should have gone first. The notes suggest he realized who he was dealing with and that he could not deliver on his own end.[^13] God walks through on behalf of both, saying in effect, "I know you're going to make a mistake. So, when that happens, I'll be the one to pay the price."[^14] What does this reveal about God's character, and what do you think it was like for Abram to watch it happen?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes link God's announcement of slavery in Egypt (verses 13–14) to Abram's earlier trip there. He left Egypt with goods, wealth, and slaves, including Hagar, Sarai's Egyptian slave, and later he sleeps with her.[^15] One summary also names the power imbalance in the Hagar story: unchecked power leads to abuse.[^16] The notes quote a line from RVL: "The biggest challenge will not be getting Avram and his people out of Egypt, it will be getting Egypt out of his people."[^17] How does a past compromise keep shaping a family's story, and where do you see who pays the price for it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say Abram had put his trust in Egyptian wealth instead of in God and God's story, and that his descendants would carry "that seed of Egypt" and have to struggle to get it out of them.[^18] Without naming more than you want to, what is one piece of "Egypt" that you have carried out with you: a habit of trusting something other than God? What would it look like to let God start getting it out of you?

</div>

### A Sign to Keep <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#a-sign-to-keep}

*The covenant grows, and so does what God asks of his partner.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 17:1–14 aloud, and if time allows, continue through verse 21.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes lay out Genesis 17 as a chiasm:[^19]

- Abram falls facedown (v. 3) and Abraham falls facedown (v. 17)
- Abram's new name (vv. 5–6) and Sarai's new name (vv. 15–16)
- "everlasting covenant" (vv. 7 and 13)
- "foreigner" (vv. 8 and 12)
- "for the generations to come" (vv. 9 and 12)
- Center: every male among you shall be circumcised (v. 10)

As you read, what do you notice about what sits at the edges and what sits at the center? One summary reads the name changes as removing hierarchy and possession: Avram, "exalted father," becomes Abraham, "father of many," and Sarai, "my princess," becomes Sarah, "princess."[^20] What do the new names suggest about the kind of family God is building?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes connect this covenant deliberately to Noah's. In Noah's covenant, God put the bow in the sky, where it could not be lost. Now God gives the sign to his people to keep. "This sign will require a step of obedience and by this we see G-d extending his expectations and responsibility of his people."[^21] Our traditions think about covenant signs, and about what God asks of his people, in different ways. Setting those debates aside for a moment, what does it mean to you that God's first covenant sign was one God kept, and the next was one his people had to keep?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** Marty reasons *kal v'chomer* ("how much more") from Abraham to us: we are thousands of years past Abraham's covenant and the coming of Jesus, so how much more does God expect of us today?[^22] He concludes, "He meets us where we are at but expects us to continue to be better and calls us forward to take our next step of faith."[^23] What is one next step of faith you sense God calling you toward right now?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Abram comes to God frustrated and childless, and God's first answer is silence. When God does act, it is through a ceremony Abram understands: a blood path where each partner pledges his life. Abram cannot bring himself to walk it, and God walks it for both of them, promising to pay the price when Abram fails. Abram's failures still have consequences, and getting Egypt out of his people will take generations. Yet God keeps calling him forward, giving him a new name and a sign to keep, meeting him where he is without leaving him there.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, what is one place where you need to trust God without having all the answers?

</div>

#### Looking Ahead {#looking-ahead}

The episode sets the story of Hagar and Ishmael beside the story of Abraham and Isaac and centers on one Hebrew phrase, *hineni* ("here I am"), as a picture of presence and faithfulness.

**What to bring next week:** Read Genesis 21:8–21 and Genesis 22:1–19 side by side. Make a short list of the details the two stories share, and notice where they differ.
