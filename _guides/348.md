### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** The sixth day and Exodus 30 invited us to wonder at God's saving work: free offerings, an equal ransom, a new start, and a holiness that spreads but always belongs to God.

**This week:** The seventh reflection closes the series where creation itself closes, with rest, and asks whether we trust God enough to stop working.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This final guided reflection sets three readings side by side: God finishing creation and resting on the seventh day, God filling Bezalel and Oholiab with his Spirit and skill to build the Tabernacle, and the Sabbath command given as a lasting covenant sign. As the creation account ends in rest, the Tabernacle instructions end in Sabbath. Building sacred space and resting in God's presence belong to the same story.

The episode's center is trust. Stopping work exposes what we depend on, and holiness comes from "the Lord who makes you holy," not from how much we produce. Marty's prompts move from our gifts, to our own Sabbath space, to whether we know the practice well enough to pass it on. The notes add that Marty was on sabbatical when the episode aired, so the hosts were practicing the rest they were describing. The episode ends by pointing ahead to a conversation with Kat Armstrong and to a second half of the session focused on spiritual practice.

### Spirit-Filled Hands <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#spirit-filled-hands}

*The seventh day and the craftsmen of the Tabernacle show that making things and resting belong together.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 2:1–3 aloud, then sit together in silence for one minute.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Exodus 31:1–11 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes point out that God fills Bezalel "with his Spirit, wisdom, understanding, knowledge and skill," and that the filling shows up as metalwork, stone cutting, woodworking and design[^1]. Look back over the passage. What do you notice about the kinds of gifts named, and about who else receives ability besides Bezalel and Oholiab? Share one thing that stood out.

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say Bezalel's story widens what "spiritual" gifts can be: practical and artistic talents can be expressions of God's Spirit, and building the Mishkan was a shared, community effort[^2]. What gifts in your church or family rarely get called "spiritual," and what might change if they were?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Marty's first prompt asks whether we have the faith and faithfulness to use our abilities to create spaces for God's kingdom, and whether we see setting those gifts down as part of creating that space[^3]. Think of one ability you use most in an ordinary week. What kind of space for God's kingdom could it make, and what would it cost you to set it down for a day?

</div>

### A Sign Between Me and You <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#a-sign-between-me-and-you}

*Right after the building instructions comes the command to stop.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Exodus 31:12–18 aloud, then sit together in silence for one minute.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The Sabbath is "a sign between me and you for the generations to come," given "so you may know that I am the Lord who makes you holy." The notes draw out the point that holiness comes from God, not from how much we produce[^4]. Why might the Tabernacle instructions end with a command to stop rather than with a final building task? What does that placement say about the work that came before?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Exodus 31:17 says God rested "and was refreshed." The notes call rest something God himself models, "not a weakness to put up with"[^5]. How does picturing God refreshed change the way you think about your own need for rest?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes frame Sabbath as an act of trust: "If we cannot stop, we may be trusting our own productivity more than God's provision."[^6] Our traditions have taught very different things about a day of rest, including which day, what belongs in it and how strictly to keep it. Without deciding who has it right, what has your tradition taught you a day of rest is for? And when you try to stop, what does the stopping expose about what you actually depend on?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Marty's second prompt asks whether our Sabbath has room for heart-level connection with God, "or is it all action and spiritual production"[^7]. The notes give the example of ministry leaders and volunteers who fill their weeks with church activity and still have no unhurried time with God[^8]. Where in your week is there space that is not production of any kind, spiritual or otherwise? If you cannot find one, what is the smallest space you could guard this coming week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#closing}

> The creation story ends with God resting and blessing the seventh day, and the Tabernacle instructions end the same way, with Sabbath. God gave Bezalel, Oholiab and the other workers real skill to build a place for his presence, and then he told his people to stop. Our gifts matter, and setting them down matters too, because it shows we believe God holds the world together and that our holiness comes from him. Marty closes the series with a prayer: "May we trust the story enough to find a space to rest. May that rest remind us of who God is and how much He loves us."[^9]
{: .guide-summary}

*Sit together in silence for two minutes before the closing question.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** Marty's last prompt asks whether we know the practice of Sabbath well enough to explain it to the generations after us[^10]. Going around the room in one sentence each, with no comments until everyone has spoken: how would you explain Sabbath to someone a generation younger than you?

</div>
