### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** We finished Session 4 with the capstone, walking the whole Bible as a Tale of Two Kingdoms (Empire and Shalom) and practicing one question at every stage: "who were God's people and what were they doing at this point?"[^1]

**This week:** Session 5 begins. Before stepping into church history, Marty and Brent compress all four sessions into a set of short frameworks, from Torah as partnership to Revelation, so we carry the whole story with us.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

Session 5 sets out to answer one question: "Where are we today and how did we get here?"[^2] Before answering it, this episode reviews the full biblical arc in memorable phrases: Torah as partnership, the historical books as a redemption cycle showing God's patience, wisdom as tools for the journey, the prophets' warnings, woes and hope, and the remnant's returning, yearning and learning.[^3]

The review continues through the so-called silent years (the synagogue and Hellenism, with five Jewish responses to Hellenistic culture), the Gospels as "the story in flesh," Acts as the epilogue of the early church, the letters as "text to context," and Revelation as using the past to illuminate the present.[^4] Marty also compares starting Session 5 without Sessions 1 to 4 to watching only the last 20 minutes of a movie, and he invites listeners to draw the whole story on a whiteboard.[^5]

### Torah to Remnant: A Story of Partnership <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#torah-to-remnant-a-story-of-partnership}

*The episode frames the first half of the Bible as one story of God choosing a partner and staying patient with that partner.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 12:1–3 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes call Torah "partnership," with Genesis as the "basis of the partnership," and they describe Chronicles as the moment injustice takes root "when people forget to bless all nations and focus only on blessing themselves."[^3] As you listen to Genesis 12:1–3, what does God ask, what does God promise, and who is the blessing finally meant to reach? Name one word or phrase you noticed.

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode walks through Torah book by book: Exodus as "the formal choosing," Leviticus as "defining the partnership," Numbers as "shaping the partner," and Deuteronomy as "remembering the partnership."[^3] What changes for you when you hear the word "partnership" in places where you have usually heard the word "law"?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The prophets move through three phases: warnings while the people are comfortable, woes and judgment, and then, in exile, prophets sent "not to condemn but to strengthen."[^3] Why might God's way of speaking change with the season his people are in? Think of a season in your own life: when did you need a warning, and when did you need hope?

</div>

### Silent Years to Revelation: Text to Context <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#silent-years-to-revelation-text-to-context}

*From the synagogue to Revelation, the episode shows God's people trying to live the story in new and pressured settings.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes describe Hellenism as the culture that proclaimed "man is the measure of all things," favoring self-preservation over self-sacrifice, and they name five Jewish responses to it: Sadducees, Herodians, Essenes, Pharisees and Zealots, each with a real strength and a real flaw.[^4] Looking at that list, which group's strength do you most admire, and which group's flaw is easiest to understand?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes highlight the idea that Jesus "IS the story in flesh" rather than simply someone who fulfilled Scripture.[^6] What difference does it make to read the Gospels as the whole Tanakh story lived out in a person, rather than as a list of fulfilled predictions?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The letters show the early church applying the way of Jesus to each setting: Philippi, Ephesus, Corinth and Thessalonica each received something different.[^4] The notes also ask which of the five responses to Hellenism show up in Christianity's engagement with culture today, and what dangers lie in either ignoring context or over-contextualizing.[^7] Without pointing at anyone else's church, which response do you think your own tradition is most tempted toward, and what strength comes with that temptation?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** If your own street, workplace or family were one more "context" for the letters, what would applying the way of Jesus faithfully there require of you this month?

</div>

### Carrying the Story Forward <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#carrying-the-story-forward}

*Session 5 assumes we know the story well enough to notice where the church later kept it and where it lost the plot.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Marty says about half the listeners he meets skipped Session 2, even though it holds ideas Jesus draws on throughout Session 3, and he compares joining at Session 5 to watching the last 20 minutes of a movie.[^5] What part of the biblical story do you feel you know least well, and what would it take to fill that gap?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes suggest carrying three questions into church history: how well each era stayed faithful to the partnership, how it moved through the redemption cycle, and how it applied text to context.[^8] Which of those three do you expect will be the most revealing lens, and why?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** Marty's whiteboard exercise asks us to draw the whole biblical story on one surface so we can see its movement.[^5] What would it look like for you to actually do this before next week, and where would you put it so you would keep seeing it?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> This week we stood at the edge of 2,000 years of church history and looked back over the road behind us. The story is one of partnership: God choosing a people to bless all nations, staying patient through their failures, sending warnings and hope, and finally living the story in the flesh in Jesus. The early church took that story into one new setting after another. As we begin asking "where are we today and how did we get here?", we carry the whole story with us so we can notice where God's people kept the plot and where they lost it.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, which part of the biblical story do you most hope the church remembered as it moved into the centuries after the apostles?

</div>
