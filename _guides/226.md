### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Jesus touched a man with leprosy and honored the faith of friends who lowered a paralyzed man through a roof, while Nicodemus told his student Shmuel, "We are still students."[^1]

**This week:** Nicodemus finally meets Jesus at night, and the next morning Jesus calls the most unlikely disciple yet.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode opens with Moses making a bronze serpent and lifting it on a pole (Numbers 21), and the hosts are candid about how hard that story is to interpret. It then moves to a rooftop at night, where Nicodemus has the conversation recorded in John 3: being born again (or "from above"), the wind that blows where it will, and Jesus's words about being lifted up like the serpent. Nicodemus leaves knowing he has stood on holy ground, but held back by his position, his family and his fear.[^2]

The second thread is Matthew. Unsettled, estranged from his family and questioning whether impossible things can happen, he hears Jesus say "Follow me" at his tax booth and walks away from everything. Simon is horrified, and Jesus answers with a line the hosts see as the heart of his ministry: "Get used to different." Along the way the hosts notice John writing down Jesus's words on the stairs and Jesus telling his followers to leave food for travelers who come after them.[^3]

**Opening Question:** When have you been invited into something (a job, a friendship, a move, a ministry) that did not fully make sense at the time? What helped you decide?

### Looking at a Bronze Serpent <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#looking-at-a-bronze-serpent}

*The episode begins with one of the strangest healings in the Torah, and the hosts do not pretend it is easy.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Numbers 21:4–9 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Notice what the people do, what God commands, and what actually heals them. The notes say the story "appears to contradict typical biblical patterns (looking at an image for healing)." In the show, Joshua questions the logic, and Moses answers that it is not about reasoning but about faith.[^4] What strikes you as strange or surprising in this passage? What do you make of Moses's answer to Joshua?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The hosts caution that reading Numbers 21 through John 3 "creates circular interpretation," using the New Testament to interpret the Old. They mention the idea of a banner (*Adonai Nissi*, "the Lord is my banner"), in which the pole points beyond itself to God as the true source of healing, and they note that the popular idea of "looking at what you're most ashamed of" is poetically appealing but "may not be exegetically sound."[^5] Why might it matter to let Numbers 21 speak on its own terms before connecting it to Jesus? What do we gain, and what might we lose, if we skip that step?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes sum up the episode's thread on faith and reason this way: "Faith doesn't require abandoning intellect," but "intellectual mastery cannot substitute for trust." In the Moses story, "Obedience precedes understanding."[^6] Is there a place in your life right now where you sense trust being asked of you before understanding arrives? What makes that hard, or what makes it possible?

</div>

### Nicodemus on the Rooftop <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#nicodemus-on-the-rooftop}

*A teacher of Israel comes as a genuine seeker, and leaves with more questions and more fear.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read John 3:1–17 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** In the episode, the meeting happens at night on a rooftop, after Mary Magdalene vouches for Nicodemus's sincerity. Nicodemus takes "born again" literally, and the show adds that his mother has died, suggesting he would believe it possible if she were alive. Jesus uses touch (hands, heart) to illustrate flesh and spirit, and the wind image to describe a Spirit whose effects can be seen even when the mechanism cannot be understood.[^7] As you listen to the passage, what do you notice about how Jesus answers Nicodemus's questions? Where does the conversation turn?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The show's Jesus connects being lifted up to the bronze serpent and says, "It's not about Rome. It's about sin," pointing to "something bigger than political liberation." Meanwhile Andrew and John listen from the stairs; Andrew whispers, "Have you ever heard anything like this?" and John shushes him and keeps writing. The notes see in this a "plausible mechanism for Gospel preservation."[^8] What do you think of the show's picture of how Jesus's words were remembered? How does it affect the way you read John 3:16–17?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** Nicodemus is ready intellectually but held back by his position in the Sanhedrin, his family (a new grandson), his age, pressure from Rome and his wife Zohara's suspicion. His final words: "My heart is swollen with fear. It could tell me nothing except that I am standing on holy ground." The invitation stays open: meet at the well on the fifth day.[^9] What kinds of obstacles stand between knowing and following for people you know, or for you? Share only what you are comfortable sharing. What would it take to walk to the well?

</div>

### Get Used to Different <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#get-used-to-different}

*Jesus's second invitation goes to the man everyone in the group despises.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 9:9–13 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The show builds slowly toward this moment. Matthew questions his mother about whether impossible things can happen, and she refuses his "blood money" on behalf of his father. When Jesus says "Follow me," Matthew leaves immediately. The Roman soldier Gaius tells him, "Your brains have taken you far," and Matthew answers, "I thought they had too." He brings his writing tablet without thinking, and Jesus says, "There may be some use for that yet."[^10] What does the Gospel text emphasize about Matthew's call? What does the show's backstory add to how you hear it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q8:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Before moving on, Jesus tells his followers to leave food for travelers coming after them. The notes describe a community "not doing things only for themselves," one "thinking beyond immediate needs to care for future travelers."[^11] What would it look like for a church or small group to "leave food" for people who will come after them?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** Simon's reaction is immediate: "We can't call Matthew!" Jesus reminds him, "You thought it was crazy when I called you too," and then says, "Get used to different." Matthew will even host the next dinner party.[^12] Without naming anyone, who would be your Matthew: the person whose welcome into your circle would most unsettle you? What would "get used to different" ask of you if Jesus called them?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> This episode is built around two invitations. Nicodemus hears Jesus explain new birth, the wind of the Spirit and a serpent lifted up, and he knows he is standing on holy ground, yet fear and obligation keep him on the rooftop. Matthew hears two words at his tax booth and leaves everything, carrying the tablet Jesus says may yet be useful. Between them stands Simon, learning that Jesus's community will include the very people he resents. The notes leave the invitation with us: each person "must decide whether the gain outweighs what would be lost."[^13]
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, what is one "different" you sense Jesus asking you to get used to?

</div>
