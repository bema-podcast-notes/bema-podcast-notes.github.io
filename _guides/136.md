### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Luke told Jesus' ascension as a rival to Caesar's, and Pentecost arrived at Shavuot as a second Sinai. The test of a true celebration was whether it left uncut corners for the alien, the orphan and the widow.

**This week:** The new community heals, shares, and organizes care, and each act of good draws opposition. The episode asks who carries the work of the Messiah: one person, or many.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode traces a pattern through Acts 3–6: the believers do something that shows the Kingdom (healing a lame man, sharing possessions, caring for widows), opposition follows, and the work continues. It also suggests that the Spirit's work in the disciples was gradual, pointing to the moment in John 20 when Jesus breathed on them before Pentecost.

The center of the episode is a claim about Jewish hope. Marty explains that Jewish messianic expectation focused more on the arrival of an age, *olam haba* ("the age to come"), than on a single person. Isaiah 32 speaks of a king but also of rulers, each one a shelter. Luke seems to draw on this when Peter (whose name means "rock") heals people with his shadow, recalling Isaiah's "shadow of a great rock." The episode closes with a hard question about what would actually convince Jewish neighbors that the age to come has begun.

### Doing Good, Facing Opposition <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#doing-good-facing-opposition}

*In Acts 3–6, every demonstration of the Kingdom seems to provoke resistance.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Acts 3:1–10 and Acts 4:1–3 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes identify a repeating pattern in these chapters: "Kingdom demonstration → persecution → continued Kingdom work." The lame man is healed, then the Sanhedrin confronts the apostles; more healings lead to jealousy and imprisonment; organized care for widows is followed by Stephen's arrest.[^1] What do you notice about how quickly opposition follows the healing? Who is upset, and what seems to bother them?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode argues that the disciples' empowerment by the Spirit was gradual "rather than instantaneous," noting that Jesus had already breathed on them and said, "Receive the Holy Spirit."[^2]

*Read John 20:21–22 aloud.*

Our traditions describe the Spirit's work in different ways, some emphasizing a decisive moment and others steady growth. However your tradition frames it, what has the Spirit's work looked like in your own life so far?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes suggest that "genuine Kingdom living disrupts status quo systems, whether religious or political."[^3] Where have you seen doing good provoke resistance, in your church, workplace or community? What helped people keep going?

</div>

### A King and His Rulers <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#a-king-and-his-rulers}

*Jewish hope looked for an age to come, and Isaiah pictures many people sharing the Messiah's work.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The episode explains that Jewish messianic expectation centered on *olam haba* ("the age to come") rather than a single individual.[^4]

*Read Isaiah 32:1–2 aloud.*

Notice how many leaders are described, and what each of them provides. What picture of the coming age does this passage paint?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes argue that Luke "deliberately connects Peter's healing shadow (Acts 5:15) with Isaiah 32:2's 'shadow of a great rock.'" Since Jesus named Peter "rock" (*Petros*), Luke presents Peter's healings as messianic prophecy coming true through a follower of Jesus.[^5]

*Read Acts 5:12–16 aloud.*

What do you think Luke wants his readers to understand by making that connection? What does it suggest about the relationship between Jesus the king and the people who follow him?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes explain that Jewish communities "seek evidence of Kingdom transformation, not just individual salvation," and suggest that Jewish skepticism toward Christian claims stems from "the absence of visible Kingdom transformation in Christian communities throughout history."[^6] That is a hard thing for Christians to hear. What kind of evidence have Christians usually offered for Jesus? What evidence would your own community be able to show?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode claims that the Spirit's power available to the early believers is the same power available today, and "the difference lies in willingness to act."[^7] Where do you sense a gap between what you believe God could do through you and what you are actually willing to try?

</div>

### When the Widows Were Overlooked <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#when-the-widows-were-overlooked}

*The community faces its first internal conflict, and the way it is handled becomes part of its witness.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Acts 6:1–7 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** When Hellenistic Jews complained that their widows were being neglected, the apostles "created a systematic solution by appointing seven deacons rather than engaging in divisive arguments."[^8] What do you notice about how the complaint was received and what the apostles did with it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Barnabas, a Levite from Cyprus, sold a field and gave the proceeds to the community. The notes call this sharing "not mere charity but embodiment of Kingdom economics where 'there were no needy persons among them.'"[^9] What is the difference between charity and a community where no one is in need?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** Think of a group you belong to (a church, a family, a team). Who in it is most likely to be overlooked right now? What is one practical step you could take, as one of the "each one," to make sure they are not?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> In Acts 3–6 the Kingdom becomes visible in healed bodies, shared money, and widows who are fed, and each time opposition follows. Jewish hope looked for an age to come, and Isaiah pictures a king whose rulers are each a shelter in a dry land. Luke shows Peter, the rock, casting that shade. The episode leaves us with a hard and hopeful claim: the strongest evidence for Jesus is a community where the age to come is already visible.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each: for whom could you be a little shade this week?

</div>
