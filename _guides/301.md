### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** John the Baptist, the friend of the bridegroom, knew his part ("He must become greater; I must become less"), and the episode invited us to see "from above" and certify that God is truthful by trusting God's story.[^1]

**This week:** We jump ahead to John 20, where Brent Billings and Reed Dent look at Thomas and at the disciples who stayed behind locked doors.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode reads Thomas as a man of unvarnished honesty who says what comes to mind, admits when he does not understand, and may voice what others are thinking but will not say. It distinguishes two kinds of doubt and belief: cognitive (in the head) and lived (in the gut, shaping how we actually live).[^3] The surprise is that the other disciples, who saw Jesus and were overjoyed, were still behind locked doors a week later, while Thomas moves from doubt to the confession "My Lord and my God."[^4]

Jesus meets Thomas without condemnation, gives him what he asked for, and only then calls him to believe. The episode draws on Frederick Buechner's distinction between believing in God and believing God, and on a poem by Denise Levertov.[^5] Questions about doubt can be personal, so share only what you want to share.

**Opening Question:** Think of a time you said out loud what everyone in the room was thinking but no one would say. How did people react?

### Thomas, the Unvarnished Disciple <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#thomas-the-unvarnished-disciple}

*The episode reads Thomas as an honest speaker rather than a failed believer.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read John 11:16, John 14:5, and John 20:24–25 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes describe Thomas as an "Unvarnished speaker" with a "Skeptical tendency" who is "Honest about limitations," and they suggest he "Possibly speaks for others." One line from the episode puts it simply: "Thomas is just saying what they're not saying."[^3] Listening to his three statements side by side, what kind of person do you hear? Name one word and the line that suggests it.

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes observe that Thomas was absent from the locked room the first week and wonder whether that means he was not hiding.[^4] What might it mean that the one disciple known for doubt was not the one hiding?

*Read John 20:26–29 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes say Jesus offers "No condemnation," meets Thomas where he is by providing exactly what he requested, and only "After provision, then challenge" says "Stop doubting and believe." They compare this to the woman caught in adultery: grace first, no condemnation, then direction.[^6] What would it look like for a group like ours to respond to someone voicing doubt in that order, and what tends to happen in churches when the order is reversed?

</div>

### Locked Doors and Lived Belief <span class="guide-time">35 min</span> {#locked-doors-and-lived-belief}

*The episode asks what it means that people who believed were still hiding.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read John 20:19–23 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes trace the other disciples: in week one they are behind locked doors, see Jesus's hands and side, become "overjoyed," receive the Holy Spirit and a commission, and in week two they are still behind locked doors. The notes call this "Cognitive belief with lived doubt."[^4] Comparing John 20:19 with John 20:26, what stays the same and what changes?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Buechner writes, "Believing in God is an intellectual position," but believing God "affects who you are and what you do with your life like believing your house is on fire." The notes contrast faith as position with faith as relationship.[^7] Where have you seen the difference between those two kinds of believing, in yourself or in someone you admire?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The episode says, "We can have cognitive doubt and still have lived belief," and also notes that the disciples could say "yeah, we accept, we see, we're glad" while "we're not going out and doing."[^8] The notes' reflection questions ask whether we have cognitive doubt with lived belief, or cognitive belief with lived doubt.[^9] Which combination do you think is more common among churchgoing people, and why might it be easier to notice in others than in ourselves?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes suggest a "Locked Door Inventory": what are we hiding from despite believing? They also offer the prayer from Levertov's poem, "Lord, I believe, help thou mine unbelief," borrowed from Mark 9:24.[^10] You do not need to say this aloud. What is one locked door you find yourself behind, and what might one step outside it look like this week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Thomas says what others will not, and Jesus does not shame him for it. He gives Thomas what he asked for and then invites him to believe, and Thomas answers, "My Lord and my God." The disciples who believed were still behind locked doors, which reminds us that saying the right things is not the same as living them. The goal is not a faith without questions but a lived belief that walks out the door, held in a community where doubts can be spoken safely.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, what would it look like for you to move one step from cognitive belief toward lived belief this week?

</div>
