### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** We finished Romans: "weak" and "strong" believers learning to live together, Paul's mission shared with women and men alike, and a letter bracketed by the obedience of faith and centered on no condemnation.

**This week:** We move from Rome to Corinth, where a church in a diverse, sex-obsessed port city struggled with two problems at once: selfish division and compromise with the culture around it.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode sets 1 Corinthians in its city: a port and cultural melting pot, full of goddess worship (especially Aphrodite), trade guilds with their own patron gods, and sharp divisions between rich and poor.[^1] It names two main problems in the church, selfish division and cultural compromise, and reads Paul's famous temple passage as being about the whole community, not individual bodies.[^2]

The episode presents love, in 1 Corinthians 13, as Paul's answer to both problems, and it notes that even the Lord's Supper had been corrupted by selfishness and class divisions.[^3] It also suggests that modern Western Christianity looks a good deal like Corinth.[^4] Our traditions practice the Lord's Supper and read 1 Corinthians 7 differently, so this session invites us to hear one another as well as the text.

### A City of Many Gods, a Church of Many Factions <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#a-city-of-many-gods-a-church-of-many-factions}

*Paul begins by confronting a church that has divided itself around leaders.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 1 Corinthians 1:10–17 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** What four slogans does Paul quote in verse 12? What questions does he ask in verse 13, and what does he say about his own role in verse 17? The notes observe that the "Christ group" was "particularly self-righteous."[^5] Why might claiming to follow only Christ become its own kind of faction?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes describe Corinth as an "extremely diverse port city serving as a cultural melting pot," where worship of Aphrodite made its sexual culture more extreme than typical Roman cities, like "Las Vegas multiplied several times over."[^6] How might living in that kind of city create "identity challenges for believers," as the notes put it?[^7]

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes say "Current denominational splits, theological factionalism, and social media arguments mirror the Corinthian tendency to divide over leaders and secondary issues."[^8] Each of our traditions has its own story of how it came to be, and none of us is being singled out here. What are the "I follow ___" statements Christians make today, and which one are you most tempted to make?

</div>

### Y'all Are a Temple <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#yall-are-a-temple}

*Paul's temple image addresses the community as a whole, and the guilds tested how far believers would blend in.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 1 Corinthians 3:16–17 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Look carefully at the "you" in these verses. The notes point out a "Plural Subject, Singular Temple": "Y'all are a temple."[^9] If your translation does not show the plural, read it aloud with "you all." What changes when you hear it that way?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes say this passage "is frequently misinterpreted" as being about individual bodies, and draw out a "Unity Requirement": "If they cannot find unity, they destroy God's temple," because "God's Spirit dwells in the unified community, not just in individuals."[^10] How have you usually heard this passage applied? What does it mean that division, not only private sin, can damage God's temple?

*Read 1 Corinthians 7:6–12 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say that "In a sex-obsessed culture, Paul recommends abstinence as counter-cultural witness, while clearly distinguishing between his opinions and divine commands."[^11] Notice where Paul says he speaks and where he says the Lord speaks. Our traditions apply 1 Corinthians 7 in different ways. What do you make of Paul marking the difference so carefully?

*Read 1 Corinthians 6:12 and 10:23–24 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes explain that the guilds functioned "like fraternities combined with labor unions," that each worshiped its own deities, and that "Monthly guild feasts involved drunken debauchery, sexual immorality, and consumption of meat sacrificed to idols."[^12] Paul answers with the refrain the notes render "I have the right to do anything, but...," reframing the question as "Is this beneficial for my community?"[^13] Where in your work or social life do you face a "guild feast," and how would that question change your choices?

</div>

### Love Heals the Broken Body <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#love-heals-the-broken-body}

*Even the Lord's table had become a place of division, and Paul offers love as the remedy.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 1 Corinthians 11:17–22 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** What is happening when the Corinthians gather to eat? Who goes hungry, and who has plenty? What does Paul say in verse 22 about what they are doing to the church? The notes describe the scene as the "wealthy feasting while poor go hungry."[^14]

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** The notes say "Even the Lord's Supper had become corrupted by the Corinthians' selfishness and class divisions," and that the Supper "serves as both indictment of our divisions and invitation to the table of grace where all come equally needy."[^15] Our traditions practice the Lord's Supper with different frequency, words and understanding. What does your tradition emphasize at the table, and how might the table expose or heal divisions in a church today, including economic ones?

*Read 1 Corinthians 13:1–7 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say Paul places the "love chapter" at the center of his discussion of spiritual gifts, in a chiastic (mirror-shaped) structure, and that "Love is not sentiment but action that seeks the good of others rather than self-promotion."[^16] We often hear this chapter at weddings. How does it sound different when you hear it addressed to a divided church?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q11:** The notes say Paul "invites the Corinthians to tell a different story with their lives than the surrounding culture" and that "Churches must prioritize what unites believers over what makes them distinct or unique."[^17] Choose one line from 13:4–7. Where could you practice it this week toward a believer who is different from you?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Corinth was a crowded, diverse port city, and its church brought the city's habits inside: factions around favorite leaders, compromise with guild feasts and goddess worship, and a Lord's Supper where the rich ate while the poor went hungry. Paul reminds them that together they are God's temple, and that division destroys it. His answer to both division and compromise is love, not as a feeling but as action that seeks the good of others. The question shifts from "Is this permissible?" to "Is this beneficial for my community?" and the broken body is healed at a table where all come equally needy.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one way you will choose what builds up the body over what you have a right to do?

</div>
