### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** The trumpets sounded over a world where shalom was being torn into thirds, but seven promised the suffering would end. John framed Revelation's opening as the games, where believers run their race to show the watching world what their God is like.

**This week:** Between the sixth and seventh trumpets, John is recommissioned as a prophet. He eats a scroll that is sweet and bitter, and he watches two witnesses testify, die and rise again.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode shows how heavily Revelation 10–11 leans on Ezekiel and the other prophets. The mighty angel echoes Ezekiel's vision of God's glory, and the little scroll that is sweet in the mouth and bitter in the stomach repeats Ezekiel's commission: God's word is sweet to receive and hard to proclaim.[^1] John is told he must "prophesy again," this time about many peoples, nations, languages and kings.[^2]

In chapter 11 the hosts identify the two witnesses as Moses and Elijah, the Law and the Prophets, and read the doubled images (two olive trees, two lampstands, twenty-four elders) as the unified body of Jews and Gentiles bearing witness together.[^3] Their death, public humiliation and resurrection after three and a half days told a weary church that apparent defeat is not the end.[^4]

### The Little Scroll <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-little-scroll}

*John's commission is modeled on Ezekiel's, with one striking difference.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Ezekiel 3:1–6 aloud, then Revelation 10:8–11.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Compare the two passages. What is the same about how each prophet receives God's word, and what is different about the people each is sent to? The notes point out that Ezekiel was told he was not being sent to people of "obscure speech and strange language" but to Israel, while John must prophesy "about many peoples, nations, languages, and kings."[^5] What do you make of that change?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The notes explain the two tastes. The sweetness is "the privilege and truth of God's message"; the bitterness is "the difficulty of delivering hard truths to suffering people."[^6] God's words, they say, are "hard to hear and digest when you are sitting in the heat of oppression and fear."[^7] Why might a message that is true and good still turn bitter once a prophet has to carry it to people who are suffering?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The mighty angel's rainbow, the notes suggest, recalls God's covenant with Noah: even amid apocalyptic destruction, God remembers his promises.[^8] When has a word from God been sweet for you to receive but heavy to live out or pass on?

</div>

### The Two Witnesses <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-two-witnesses}

*Two witnesses prophesy for a limited time, are killed, and are raised.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Revelation 11:3–12 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** List the powers the witnesses have in verses 5–6. Then read 1 Kings 17:1. The notes say any first-century Jew would immediately think of Moses and Elijah: Elijah shut up the heavens so no rain fell, and Moses turned water to blood and struck the earth with plagues. The two also appeared together at the Transfiguration.[^9] What does it add that Moses stands for the Torah and Elijah for the Prophets, so that "together they represent the entire witness of the Hebrew scriptures"?[^10]

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The witnesses are called "the two olive trees and the two lampstands," drawing on Zechariah 4, where olive trees and lampstands represent God's anointed ones.[^11] Read Zechariah 4:1–6 and 4:11–14. What do you notice about how the olive trees and the lampstand relate to each other, and what does that suggest about the witnesses in Revelation 11?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes observe that John keeps doubling where we would expect one: twenty-four elders (twelve tribes and twelve apostles), two olive trees, two lampstands. This doubling "points to the unified body of Christ composed of both Jews AND Gentiles," so the witnesses represent the whole community of believers.[^12] Many of us were taught to look for two literal individuals in a future end-times scenario, and the notes acknowledge that reading.[^13] What were you taught about the two witnesses, and what does reading them as the whole Jew-and-Gentile community add, or cost, for you? If the witnesses are the whole community, what does that say about the witness of the congregation you belong to?

</div>

### Prophesy Again <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#prophesy-again}

*The command to John becomes a command to the whole church.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Revelation 10:11 and 11:15–19 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes point to the Greek word *palin*, "again" or "anew," in "You must prophesy again." It shows that this is "not a new message but a renewal of ancient prophetic truth." It needs saying again because people are suffering, the words are hard to digest, and the community needs to be reminded their struggle is worth it.[^14] The episode itself says that reading Revelation this way takes "redundant training" because it is "such a radically different way of reading Revelation that most of us were handed."[^15] Why do you think old truths have to be spoken again and again, both to the first readers and to us?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q8:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The witnesses are killed and left unburied, then raised after three and a half days, half of seven: significant but limited.[^16] The notes say this mirrors believers "who felt their witness was useless," and that the message is: "Your witness may seem defeated, but God will vindicate you."[^17] Then the seventh trumpet announces that "the kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Messiah," which the notes describe as the contest between Empire and Shalom.[^18] Where does your own witness feel defeated or useless right now, and what old, true word do you need to hear spoken again there?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> John eats the scroll as Ezekiel did: sweet to receive, bitter to carry to people living in the heat of oppression and fear. But his commission reaches further than Ezekiel's, to many peoples, nations, languages and kings. The two witnesses, Moses and Elijah, the Law and the Prophets, picture a whole community of Jews and Gentiles testifying together, and when they are killed and mocked, God breathes life into them again. The time is limited, the vindication is certain, and the call is to prophesy again.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one place where you sense a call to "prophesy again" this week?

</div>
