### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Brent and Josh heard Jesus's words to Nicodemus as a call to another Exodus on the night of watching, read John 3:16 alongside Israel as God's firstborn son, and described judgment as choosing to step into the light.[^1]

**This week:** Marty and Brent stay in John 3 with John the Baptist, who ratifies Jesus's identity and ministry and shows what it looks like to know your part in God's story.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode begins with geography: the five zones of the land and the Judean wilderness near the Jordan, possibly near Qumran, where John and Jesus were baptizing. Drawing on Ray Vander Laan, Marty connects John's baptism sites to Elijah's stories and suggests John may have served as Jesus's rabbi.[^3] When John's disciples worry that Jesus is baptizing more people, John answers with the image of the friend of the bridegroom: "He must become greater; I must become less."[^4]

The second half builds on Josh's teaching from the previous episode about two paradigms: those "from above," who see through God's eyes, and those "from the earth," limited to earthly and religious logic. Accepting the testimony of the one from above "certifies" that God is truthful, and the hosts wrestle with whether "Son of God" here means Jesus, Israel as God's firstborn, or both.[^5]

### Where John Baptized <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#where-john-baptized}

*The episode grounds John's testimony in real places that carried real memories.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read John 3:22–26 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes walk through five zones of the land (coastal plain, Shephelah, Judah mountains, Judean wilderness, and Rift Valley) and place these baptisms in the desert near the Jordan, possibly near Qumran. They also note an argument between John's disciples and "a certain Jew" over ceremonial washing, which may reflect differences between Pharisaic, Essene, northern and southern practice.[^3] What do you notice in John 3:22–26 about the places named, the people involved, and what is worrying John's disciples?

*Read 2 Kings 2:7–14 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Following Ray Vander Laan, the notes say John chose baptism sites tied to Elijah's stories, including the place where Elijah "put his cloak on the water and parted the Jordan before being taken up in chariots of fire." John also resembled Elijah in clothing, diet, and mannerisms.[^3] Why might a prophet choose a place with that kind of memory to deliver his message?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Marty proposes that John may have been Jesus's rabbi: Jesus adopts John's practice of baptizing with disciples and proclaims John's message verbatim, "Repent, for the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand," beginning after John is beheaded.[^6] What does it change for you to picture Jesus learning from and echoing a teacher? Who taught you the faith, and whose words do you find yourself repeating?

</div>

### He Must Become Greater <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#he-must-become-greater}

*John the Baptist answers his disciples' competitive worry with a wedding image and a settled sense of who he is.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read John 3:27–30 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes explain that the friend of the bridegroom (the best man) waits outside, listens for the announcement that the marriage is consummated, and then announces it so the celebration intensifies. "His role is crucial but limited - the wedding is not about him."[^4] What does this image tell you about how John understands his role, and why might he choose a joyful image rather than a sad one?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes apply John's answer to ministry: when another church plants nearby and seems more successful, "the response should be celebration rather than jealousy."[^7] Each of our traditions has its own history of comparing itself with other churches. Without criticizing anyone, what do you think makes that kind of comparison so tempting for churches, and what does John's answer offer instead?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes describe John as free from imposter syndrome: he "Knows exactly who he is and who he is not," knows the part he is and is not called to play, and declares, "A person can receive only what is given them from heaven."[^8] Where in your life right now is it hardest to accept your part without comparing it to someone else's, and what would John's words change there?

</div>

### From Above, From the Earth <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#from-above-from-the-earth}

*Building on last week, the episode contrasts two ways of seeing and asks what it means to trust God's story.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read John 3:31–36 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes contrast those "from the earth," who are "Stuck in religious paradigms" and cannot see what God is doing, with those "from above," who are fluent in the "second language" of Kingdom perspective, where hope and resurrection become second nature. They point to Nicodemus as someone who moves from one to the other by the end of the Gospels.[^5] What contrasts do you notice in John 3:31–36, and where do you see Nicodemus's struggle from last week in them?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q8:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The hosts wrestle with "Son of God." Exodus 4:22–23 calls Israel God's firstborn, and John 3 is full of Passover imagery, yet lines like "The Father loves the Son and has placed everything in his hands" have "a Jesus-specific flair." One possible synthesis is "Jesus as Israel's firstborn incarnate."[^9] What does it add to hold both meanings together rather than choosing one?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** The word translated "certified" (*sfragizo*, Greek, "to seal, prove, confirm") means that accepting the testimony of the one from above proves true what God says is true. The notes describe this as choosing "self-sacrificial love over self-preservation" and becoming "people of the ears" (trusting God's word) rather than "people of the eyes."[^10] Where are you facing a situation this week in which trusting God's story would mean acting differently than your circumstances suggest?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> John the Baptist stands in Elijah's places, speaks Elijah's message, and still knows the story is not about him. When his disciples worry about losing ground, he talks about a wedding and a best man whose joy is complete when the groom arrives. He knows that a person can receive only what is given from heaven. The episode invites us to see from above rather than from the earth, and to certify that God is truthful by trusting God's story over our fears.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, where do you want to practice "He must become greater; I must become less" this week?

</div>
