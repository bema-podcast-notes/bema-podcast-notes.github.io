### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** James wrote to Jewish believers scattered among the nations, calling them to *mitzvoth*, deeds that show faith is real. His concern with works was a different conversation from Paul's, and his warnings about the tongue may have had Jewish and Gentile believers in view.

**This week:** Another letter to scattered believers, this time from Peter to communities under pressure. The episode is short, so we will spend more time in the text itself.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode reads 1 Peter as a letter from the leader of the apostolic movement to believers in remote regions like Cappadocia, Galatia, and Bithynia, places where many fled during early Roman persecution, possibly under Nero. Peter's central image is believers as "living stones" being built into a "spiritual house," a Jewish way of speaking about the Temple. Faith here is something we live out together, not alone.

The hosts also trace Peter's own story, from the disciple who drew a sword to the restored shepherd of John 21, and they hear that change in his call to repay evil with blessing. Finally, they make a pointed claim about today: American Christians are losing a default cultural position, which is not the same as persecution. That is a claim we may weigh differently, and the handout invites honest conversation about it.

### From Sword to Shepherd <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#from-sword-to-shepherd}

*The man writing this letter has a history, and the episode hears that history in what he now teaches.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read John 18:10–11 aloud, then John 21:15–17.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes describe "Early Peter" as impulsive, willing to fight with a sword, and sure he would never deny Jesus, and "Mature Peter" as someone who advocates grace, peace, submission to authority, and persistent good works after his restoration on the beach.[^1] Compare the two scenes. What is Peter doing with his hands, his words, and his loyalty in each? What has changed between them?

*Read 1 Peter 1:1 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode explains that Peter likely focused on remote regions lacking strong apostolic leadership. Thousands fled to places like Cappadocia, known for underground cities carved into tufa rock. Some followed Jesus' teaching, "When persecuted in one place, flee to another," and others stayed and often died.[^2] What does it tell you that both fleeing and staying were faithful options? How would you have decided?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes say Peter's transformation "demonstrates the possibility of change through resurrection hope."[^3] Where in your own life do you still reach for the sword (defending, fighting back, protecting yourself)? What would "feeding Jesus's sheep" look like in that same situation?

</div>

### Living Stones <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#living-stones}

*Peter gives scattered, frightened believers a new picture of who they are together.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 1 Peter 2:4–10 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes explain that each believer is a "living stone," chosen and quarried by God, and that many stones are built into a single "spiritual house," which was Jewish terminology for the Temple, "avoiding the direct use of the holy word 'Temple.'"[^4] Read the passage again slowly. Which words are singular and which are plural? What does Peter say the stones are being built into, and what is the house for?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The episode stresses that believers do not function as isolated individuals; "the community together tells a story and shows the world what God is like," and perseverance happens together, not alone.[^5] How would this letter sound to a believer hiding in Cappadocia? How does it sound to you, in a culture that tends to think of faith as personal and private?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes compare Peter's image to Paul's teaching that "we" (plural) are "the temple" (singular) of the Holy Spirit.[^6] Several of our weeks in Paul's letters touched this idea. What does it add to hear Peter, the leader of the whole movement, say the same thing in his own words?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** If your church is a house built of living stones, which stones around you hold you up, and which ones are you helping to carry? Name one person you could strengthen this week simply by showing up.

</div>

### Blessing Instead of Retaliation <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#blessing-instead-of-retaliation}

*Peter teaches a way of responding to hostility that looks very different from his younger self.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 1 Peter 3:8–17 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes summarize Peter's approach: do not repay evil with evil or insult with insult; repay evil with blessing; keep your tongue from evil; seek peace and pursue it; and always be prepared to give an answer for your hope "with gentleness and respect."[^7] Which of these instructions stands out to you, and which seems hardest for Christians today?

*Read 1 Peter 4:12–16 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode notes Peter's call not to be surprised at fiery ordeals, and to see insult for Christ's name as blessing, so that "malicious speakers may be ashamed of their slander."[^8] How does expecting hardship, rather than being shocked by it, change the way a person responds when it comes?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** The hosts make a strong claim: "American Christianity is not experiencing true persecution despite cultural changes." They describe true persecution as "dying, hiding, fleeing, and living in constant physical danger," and they say the loss of an assumed Christian worldview should be engaged "missionally" rather than fought through political power.[^9] People in our room may see this differently. What in the hosts' claim rings true to you, and what would you push back on? How might your answer change the way you respond to cultural opposition?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q11:** The episode's alternative to fighting back is "persistent good works, forgiveness, love, and compassion at personal expense."[^10] Think of one person or group you feel opposed by right now. What would repaying them with blessing look like this week, in a specific act?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Peter, the man who once swung a sword in the garden, writes to scattered and frightened believers with a very different instinct. He tells them they are living stones, built together into God's house, telling the world together what God is like. He calls them to bless rather than retaliate, to do good persistently, and to give a reason for their hope with gentleness. His own life is evidence that resurrection hope can change a person.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each: where is one place this week you want to answer hostility with blessing?

</div>
