### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Stars help you know where and when you are. The priest holds the whole community on the shoulders and each name over the heart. Holiness travels toward people, not away from them.

**This week:** Day 5 brings the first blessing in creation. Birds are creatures of the sky — but their blessing is on the earth. The priests are birds: they can enter holy places, but their calling is at the doorway. God's thesis for the whole Exodus: "I brought them out so that I might dwell among them."

<div class="guide-q guide-q-opening" markdown="1">

**Opening Question:** When have you received care from someone who had clearly "landed" — who was at home in elevated or privileged space but chose to be near you? What did that landing feel like?

### Genesis 1:20–23 — Day 5 <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#genesis-12023-day-5}

*Read Genesis 1:20–23 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Day 5 brings birds who can reach the sky — but their blessing from God is to be fruitful "on the earth," not in the sky.[^1] Josh's application: people with elevated callings are only blessed when they land near the people they serve. Elevated space is not a place to settle. Where are you currently circling — staying in the sky — rather than landing near people who need you on the ground?

</div>

### Exodus 29 — The Three Offerings <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#exodus-29-the-three-offerings}

*Read Exodus 29:1, 10–22 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The ordination moves through three offerings: the sin offering (preventive humility — the priest acknowledges before starting that they will make mistakes); the burnt offering (transformation — the only one with no atonement and nobody eats it; pure worship); and the peace/ordination offering (celebration — the priest shares in the meal).[^2] These are not a sequence to get past. They are a rhythm to inhabit — like morning and evening. Where are you currently living — in the sin offering, the burnt offering, or the peace offering? What would it take to hold all three simultaneously?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Blood is placed on the priests' right earlobe, right thumb, and right big toe — in that order.[^3] First what you hear. Then what you do. Then where you go. Not the other way around. In a situation you are currently navigating, are you waiting to "be in the right place" before acting? What would it look like to start with the ear (what am I hearing?) and the thumb (what am I doing with my hands right now?)?

</div>

### The *Tamid*, the Altar, and God's Thesis <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-tamid-the-altar-and-gods-thesis}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q4:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The *tamid* — the burnt offering burning morning and evening, every day — is always already on the altar when you arrive.[^4] You don't have to bring your own. Repentance is a standing invitation, not a gate you must open. How does the image of an offering already burning — already waiting for you — change how you approach repentance?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Exodus 29:46 — "I brought them out of Egypt so that I might *dwell* (*shakan*) among them."[^5] The word *shakan* shares its root with *mishkan*. Every week of this series exists for this: not so God can be worshipped from a distance, but so God can be *with* the people. Not "so that they could serve me" — but "so that I might dwell among them." What does it mean to receive the whole tabernacle as God's desire for proximity rather than God's demand for performance?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

### Looking Ahead — Week 6: Day 6 and the Anointing Oil {#looking-ahead-week-6-day-6-and-the-anointing-oil}

**[Ep. 330 — Sanctuary: Traversing the Mishkan](/guides/330)** (1:58:10)

Day 6 pairs with Exodus 30's golden altar of incense, the census half-shekel, the bronze basin, anointing oil, and sacred incense. The central distinction: *tselem* (image) is given and cannot be counterfeited; *demut* (likeness) is worked out over time. The anointing oil = *tselem* (God's image, covering everything, already present); the incense = *demut* (God's likeness, kept holy for God, not our brand). The half-shekel: you hold only half. No single person carries the full image of God. The bronze basin: are you willing to be washed by raw community?

<div class="guide-homework" markdown="1">

**Before Week 6:** *What is one quality of God that you see most clearly not in yourself but in another person — someone whose half-shekel completes yours?*

</div>
