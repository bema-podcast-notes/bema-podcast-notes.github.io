### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** In two Sabbath controversies, Jesus read Torah through the yoke of love for God and neighbor and chose mercy as the weightier command, then withdrew as Isaiah's gentle servant.

**This week:** The conflict sharpens. The Pharisees say Jesus's power comes from Beelzebul, and Jesus answers with logic, a warning, a sign, and a new definition of family.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

After Jesus heals a man who was demon-possessed, blind and mute, some Pharisees attribute his power to Beelzebul, a name the notes trace to the Philistine god Baal-Zebub, "Lord of the Flies."[^1] Jesus answers that a divided kingdom cannot stand, and then gives a warning about blasphemy against the Holy Spirit that the episode carefully explains: not a single unforgivable mistake, but the stance of opposing what God is clearly doing.[^2]

The episode then takes up Jesus's teaching about trees and fruit, the sign of Jonah as a story of outsiders responding while insiders miss it, and the principle that "What you look for, you will find."[^3] It closes with Jesus's mother and brothers standing outside, and his words about who his family really is.[^4]

### A Kingdom Divided <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#a-kingdom-divided}

*Jesus heals a man and is accused of working for the enemy.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 12:22–37 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Jesus gives several replies in verses 25–29. Find them and put each in your own words. The notes name three: internal contradiction, unique authority, and the strong man principle.[^5] They add that "driving out demons was understood in Second Temple Jewish thought as something only God could do."[^6] Which of Jesus's arguments do you find most convincing, and why?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Jesus repeats his tree-and-fruit image from the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 7:15–20). The notes stress the direction of the argument: you identify a good tree by finding good fruit, and Jesus's fruit is healing, sight, walking and cleansing.[^7] The notes say "The Pharisees want to assign evil motives to obviously good outcomes."[^8] Why do you think it is so tempting to explain away good fruit when it comes from someone we have already decided against?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Verses 31–32 have worried many people. The notes say blasphemy against the Spirit is "Standing in opposition to what God is clearly doing," and that it is not "A single mistake that permanently damns someone," nor "Doubting or questioning."[^9] They conclude: "The sin isn't unforgivable because God won't forgive it; it's unforgivable because you cannot receive forgiveness while actively standing in that place of opposition."[^10] How have you heard this passage taught in your tradition, and how does the notes' reading compare?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q4:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say many Christians are taught that non-believers cannot truly do good, and that "This runs contrary to everything Jesus teaches."[^11] They invite us to look for the Kingdom "in people who don't wear Christian T-shirts."[^12] Where have you seen good fruit this year from someone outside your circle, and what would it mean to recognize it as God at work?

</div>

### The Sign of Jonah <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#the-sign-of-jonah}

*The same leaders now ask Jesus for a sign.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 12:38–45 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Look at the two Old Testament examples Jesus names in verses 41–42. Who responded to God's message in each case, and what do they have in common? The notes sum up the pattern: "Gentiles responded to God's message while God's people resisted."[^13] Why might that be a sign the religious leaders could have recognized in their own day?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty quotes one of his favorite teachers: "What you look for, you will find."[^14] If we look for everything that is broken, there is plenty; if we look for the Kingdom "bursting forth in unexpected places," we may find God more than ever.[^15] What are you most often looking for when you read the news, scroll your phone, or walk into church?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** In verses 43–45, a house is swept clean and left empty. The notes draw a principle from it: "Darkness is not a thing - it's the absence of light," so "The only way to overcome evil in our lives is to fill the void with the good things we were created to do."[^16] Marty shares that an addiction he had fought for years lost its grip not through more effort to resist but when he gave himself to studying the Text, the thing he was made to do.[^17] What is one good thing you were made for that could fill space in your life where you have mostly been trying to subtract?

</div>

### Who Is My Mother? <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#who-is-my-mother}

*Jesus's family arrives and stands outside.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 12:46–50 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Notice where Jesus's family is standing and what they want. The notes point out that his family stands outside, "not sitting at His feet," wanting him to come out to them.[^18] What does Jesus do with that moment?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes hold a tension: honoring father and mother remains a command Jesus kept, yet the notes paraphrase his priority this way: "I'm not going to miss out on the thing that God is doing in God's household (beit av) just to keep this other beit av happy." (*Beit av* is the household, the family unit.)[^19] The notes add that the rift heals: Mary is at the cross, and Jesus entrusts her to John.[^20] How do you hold honoring family together with following a calling they may not understand?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** "Whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother." Who has been that kind of family to you, and who might need you to be that kind of family to them?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Jesus healed a man who could not see or speak, and some of the most religiously trained people in the room called it the work of Beelzebul. Jesus answered that a kingdom divided cannot stand, and that a tree is known by its fruit. The danger he warned of is not doubt or honest disagreement but standing against what God is clearly doing. Meanwhile, outsiders like Nineveh and the Queen of Sheba responded while insiders asked for signs. What we look for, we will find, and Jesus invites us into a family made of everyone who does the Father's will.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: where will you look for good fruit this week?

</div>
