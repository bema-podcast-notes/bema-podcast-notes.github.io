### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** We met the Pharisees, whose answer to Rome was "You're asking the wrong question. Let God deal with Rome." Jesus spent most of his ministry among them because they had nearly everything God's people needed except compassion.[^1]

**This week:** Marty and Brent finish the Silent Years by reviewing all five responses to Hellenism and turning the question on us: not "Which group am I?" but "How do I associate with each of them?"

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode begins with Galatians 4:4 and the "set time" of Jesus' coming. Marty proposes that the timing was perfect because Judaism was in the middle of a living argument about how to respond to Hellenism, with five different answers in dialogue with one another.[^2] The hosts then walk back through the Sadducees, Herodians, Essenes, Zealots and Pharisees, naming a real strength and a real danger in each, and insisting that every group has something God wants to use.[^3]

The tone is personal. Marty and Brent are candid about their own habits (screens, entertainment, judgment), and Marty offers a sharp critique of American evangelicalism as "the worst mix" of Herodian culture and Pharisaic religion.[^4] The goal is self-awareness rather than labels: embracing each group's strengths while guarding against each group's weaknesses.[^5]

### Opening <span class="guide-time">10 min</span> {#opening}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-opening" markdown="1">

**Opening Question:** On his first Israel trip, Marty met a group of Amish travelers carrying MP3 players and laptops. When he asked about it, they laughed: "There's three orders of Amish. There's the New Order, there's the Old Order, and we are what's called the Out-of-Order."[^6] Where in your life are you a little "out of order," holding a conviction you don't quite live consistently? Keep it light; a small example is perfect.

</div>

### The Set Time <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#the-set-time}

*Before looking at the five groups again, the episode asks why Jesus came when he did.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Galatians 4:4–7 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes compare three translations of verse 4: "when the set time had fully come" (NIV), "when the fullness of time had come" (ESV), and "when the appointed time arrived" (CJB).[^7] Read the verse in the translations people brought. What does each wording emphasize, and what else in verses 4–7 tells you this moment was deliberate?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Marty argues the "perfect timing" was the cultural moment when Judaism was actively wrestling with how to respond to Hellenism, with all five responses in full dialogue.[^8] Thinking back over the last eight weeks, what about that argument made it a fitting stage for Jesus' ministry and message?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The original notes say the moment mattered because of Hellenism's effect on people's worldview, "Especially how they interact with others who believe differently than they do."[^9] Where in your own life is the question of how to stay faithful among people who believe differently most alive right now?

</div>

### Five Responses, Two Sides Each <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#five-responses-two-sides-each}

*Every group gets both a strength and a danger, and the episode refuses to let us pick a team.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes give each group a positive contribution and a negative tendency:[^10]

- Sadducees: living out the priestly role / complete corruption
- Herodians: fluency in the culture, positioned to redeem it / compromise and idolatry
- Essenes: "Complete and utter devotion to the Text" / isolation and separatism
- Zealots: passionate action that moves from learning to doing / grabbing the wrong tools and weapons
- Pharisees: passionate devotion to God's commandments / lacking compassion

Which positive contribution surprised you most, given how these groups are usually talked about? What would the body of Christ lose without it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Marty says he has shifted how he teaches this material. Instead of asking which single group we belong to, he asks, "How do I associate with each of these groups in different areas of my life?"[^11] Marty associates most with the Essenes yet admits strong Herodian habits; Brent identifies as a Pharisee who struggles to see potential in people.[^12] Choose one area of your life (work, money, screens, politics, church) and describe which group's strength and which group's weakness show up there.

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> *Read Hebrews 13:17 aloud.* The notes observe that many Protestants reject the idea of priesthood while more liturgical traditions embrace it, and that Israel was a kingdom of priests that also had a whole tribe set apart for priestly work.[^13] How has your own tradition taught you to think about formal leaders (elders, ministers, pastors, priests) alongside the priesthood of all believers? The episode's question for any leader is, "In what places am I using what God has set up for my own personal gain?"[^14] What would honest attention to that question look like in a church you know?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Brent names the particular Zealot danger: "They stopped seeing potential in people because they see the Roman soldier, and they just say, 'Well, that is a foregone conclusion. There is nothing good there. I'm going to get rid of that to meet my goal.'"[^15] For the Pharisee side, the episode borrows a question from Brené Brown: "Do we really believe that everybody everywhere is trying to do their best?"[^16] Without naming them, think of someone you have quietly written off as a foregone conclusion. What would it look like this week to look for potential in that person?

</div>

### The Worst Mix <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#the-worst-mix}

*Marty turns the lens on the modern church, and the historical Pharisees come off better than we might expect.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Marty's critique is that American evangelicalism takes the worst of the Herodians and the worst of the Pharisees and combines them: spiritually Pharisaic (rigid about who is in and out, lacking compassion) and culturally Herodian (consuming culture, living comfortably).[^17] He adds that the historical Pharisees were at least consistent; their villages had no marble streets, no paved roads, no ornate city gates.[^18] Where does this assessment ring true in your experience of church, and where would you want to push back or add nuance?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> When Brent asked Marty, "Do you spend more time in scripture or more time in movies and TV?" Marty had to pause, which led to the insight: "We create space for the things we want to create space for."[^19] If someone looked at your last month's calendar and screen time, what would they conclude you have made space for? Name one small space you could create this week for the Text, prayer or service.

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Paul says God sent his Son when the set time had fully come, and this episode suggests that part of what made the time right was a people arguing about how to live faithfully under a powerful, seductive culture. Each of the five answers carried something God could use and something that could ruin it. We are not asked to choose one team. We are asked to notice where each of these voices lives in us, to keep the devotion, the leadership, the cultural fluency and the zeal, and to remember that the whole project exists for people who are trying to do their best with what they have.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: which group's strength do you most want to borrow this week, and which group's weakness do you most need to watch?

</div>
