### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** We met the God-fearing Gentiles of Galatia, pressed by Shammai's school and by Rome to convert fully, and heard Paul insist that his gospel came directly from Jesus.[^1]

**This week:** Galatians 2 shows the early church's leaders welcoming Paul as an equal, and then shows how hard it was to live out that welcome at the table.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This episode walks through Galatians 2. After fourteen years of ministry, Paul returns to Jerusalem with Titus, a Gentile, as a test case. Peter, James and John, the "pillars," decide Titus does not need circumcision and give Paul and Barnabas the "right hand of fellowship," a sign of full acceptance and partnership in the Roman world. Later, in Antioch, Peter eats with Gentiles until "men from James" arrive and then withdraws, and Paul confronts him publicly.[^2]

Along the way, the notes clarify two terms. *Halakhah* (literally "how you walk") is the oral interpretation of how to live out Torah, and many restrictions behind the conflict, such as Jews not eating with Gentiles, were matters of oral tradition rather than written Scripture. The notes also stress that in Jewish thought salvation comes entirely by God's grace; the real debate was over justification, why God declares some people righteous.[^3]

### Fourteen Years and a Handshake <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#fourteen-years-and-a-handshake}

*We watch the Jerusalem leaders decide whether Gentiles must become Jewish to belong.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Galatians 2:1–10 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Look closely at the passage. Who goes up to Jerusalem with Paul, what does Paul do once he gets there, and what do the leaders decide? What do they ask of Paul at the end?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The notes explain that Paul brought Titus, "a Gentile from Crete (considered particularly pagan)," as a test case, and that the decision not to circumcise him set a precedent for Gentile inclusion without conversion. They add that the "pillars" (Peter, James and John) parallel a Jewish Midrash that calls Abraham, Isaac and Jacob the pillars of the Jewish people, and that the "right hand of fellowship" signified full acceptance as an equal partner.[^4] What does it mean that this decision came from the pillars, and not only from Paul?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes observe that Paul submitted to church leadership, "willing to abandon his ministry if they didn't approve," and that the early church had "squeaky wheels" and people angry about change, just as churches do today.[^5] Think about a disagreement your church has worked through (or failed to). What helped people stay together, and what would you want to do differently next time?

</div>

### Peter at the Table in Antioch <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#peter-at-the-table-in-antioch}

*We look at what happened when the handshake had to become a meal.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Galatians 2:11–14 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Trace Peter's behavior step by step. What was he doing before the "men from James" arrived, what changed after, and who followed his lead? What reason does Paul give for the change?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** According to the notes, the restriction on Jews eating with Gentiles was a matter of *halakhah*, human interpretation of how to remain distinct and clean, rather than written Scripture. Peter's withdrawal "threatened the Gospel message by suggesting that Gentile converts were second-class members of the community."[^6] Why would Paul treat a seating arrangement as a gospel issue rather than a matter of manners? The episode uses a playful "Pint o' Bacon" to stand for forbidden food at a shared table; what would be on the table in your setting?[^7]

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes call Peter's behavior an example of "the human tendency to modify behavior based on social pressure."[^8] Where are you most tempted to withdraw from someone because of who is watching? What would the courage to stay at the table look like there this week?

</div>

### Justification Inside a Jewish Debate <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#justification-inside-a-jewish-debate}

*We read Paul's summary of his argument in light of the debate the notes describe.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Galatians 2:15–16 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes put it plainly: "no Jew ever taught that salvation comes through works." The debate was about justification. Shammai held that God declares people righteous through the "works of the law" (*Miqsat Ma'ase haTorah*), while Hillel held it was through faith, like Abraham.[^9] How does knowing this change the way you hear "works of the law" and "faith" in these verses? What caricatures of Judaism does it help us avoid?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q8:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Many of us learned "faith versus works" as an argument between Christian groups, or as a way to describe the difference between Christianity and Judaism. How was it taught in your tradition? If Paul is taking a side inside a Jewish debate about who belongs, what does that shift about the way you talk about faith with others?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> In Jerusalem, the pillars of the church shook Paul's hand and agreed that Titus could belong without becoming Jewish. In Antioch, even Peter found it hard to live that out when the wrong people were watching. Paul saw that who eats with whom is not a side issue; it tells the world who belongs in God's family. The question Galatians 2 hands us is not whether we believe in welcome, but whether we stay at the table when it costs us something.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: whose table do you want to stay at, or invite to yours, this week?

</div>
