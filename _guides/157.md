### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** In Corinth, a diverse port city, the church had divided around leaders and compromised with its culture, and Paul offered love as the way to heal the broken body.

**This week:** Paul writes to Corinth again, this time defending his own authority against critics who found him unimpressive, and he does it by boasting in weakness rather than strength.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This is a short episode, so the session spends more time in the passages. The episode points out that 2 Corinthians is actually at least Paul's third letter to the church, since 1 Corinthians itself refers to an earlier letter, and that the Corinthians had grown and repented since the problems of 1 Corinthians.[^1] Its central theme is Paul's defense of his apostleship against critics, including the so-called "super-apostles," and his claim that true authority is shown through suffering and self-sacrifice rather than impressive speech or credentials.[^2]

The episode explores theories about Paul's "thorn in the flesh" and closes with reflections on spiritual authority today, taking seriously both the abuse many have experienced and the need for leaders who look like Jesus.[^3] It also touches on spiritual gifts, a topic on which our traditions differ.[^4] Some of this may touch painful experiences; share only what you choose to share.

**Opening Question:** Think of someone whose influence on your life was far greater than their outward impressiveness. What was it about them that earned your trust?

### An Ongoing Conversation <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#an-ongoing-conversation}

*Paul's letters to Corinth are part of a long relationship, and his critics have been comparing him to others.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 1 Corinthians 5:9 aloud, then 2 Corinthians 10:1–11.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** In 1 Corinthians 5:9, what earlier letter does Paul mention? In 2 Corinthians 10:10, what do Paul's critics say about him? In verse 8, what does Paul say his authority is for? The notes say his critics "dismissed him as unimpressive in person despite his forceful letters."[^5]

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say this "missing correspondence highlights how much of the apostolic writings we don't possess and reminds us that our canonical letters are part of larger pastoral relationships."[^6] How does it change your reading of Paul's letters to think of them as one side of a long, unfinished conversation with real people?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes observe that "The Corinthians had grown and repented from their earlier issues, showing that even difficult church situations can change."[^7] Think of a hard situation in a church, family or friendship that you have been tempted to write off. What would it look like to hold on to hope for it?

</div>

### Boasting in Weakness <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#boasting-in-weakness}

*Instead of matching his rivals' credentials, Paul boasts in what makes him look weak.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 2 Corinthians 11:5–9 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** What does Paul call his rivals in verse 5? What does he say about his speaking ability, and what did he refuse to take from the Corinthians? The notes say Paul "sarcastically refers to his opponents as 'super-apostles,'" and that his refusal of financial support "may have been interpreted as a sign of his inferiority." He "robbed other churches" to serve Corinth for free.[^8] Why might refusing payment have made Paul look weaker in Corinth, not stronger?

*Read 2 Corinthians 11:21–30 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Look at the list of what Paul "boasts" about. What kinds of things are on it, and what kinds of things are missing? The notes call his defense "counterintuitive: rather than boasting in credentials or supernatural experiences, he boasts in his weaknesses and sufferings."[^9] What does it say about Paul's idea of leadership that this is his résumé?

*Read 2 Corinthians 12:7–10 aloud, then Galatians 4:15.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes list theories about the thorn: a physical ailment such as blindness (pointing to Galatians 4:15), a deserting wife (since rabbinical students were expected to be married), or his hard calling to the Gentiles (since "thorn in the side" refers to pagan nations in the Old Testament).[^10] They suggest "The mystery itself may be the point - Paul's weakness becomes a source of strength and testimony to God's sufficient grace."[^11] Why might it be a gift that we do not know exactly what the thorn was?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Paul says "when I am weak, then I am strong," and the notes apply this to "any form of leadership or service where vulnerability and dependence on God can become sources of authentic strength."[^12] What is a weakness or limit in your life that you usually hide? What might it look like to let God's grace be sufficient there?

</div>

### Authority That Builds Up <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#authority-that-builds-up}

*The episode brings Paul's defense into a world that is wary of authority.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 2 Corinthians 13:10 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Paul says twice that his authority is "for building you up and not for tearing you down," a principle the notes say challenges leaders "to evaluate their use of authority by its fruit in people's lives."[^13] The episode "acknowledges the legitimate skepticism toward spiritual authority in contemporary culture, especially given widespread abuse by religious leaders," but calls "for discernment rather than wholesale rejection."[^14] Without naming anyone, how have your experiences with leaders shaped how you hear the word "authority," and what helps you tell authority that builds up from authority that tears down?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say the episode ends by acknowledging that "something special was happening in Corinth with miraculous gifts (not mentioned in other Pauline letters)," while saying "this doesn't mean God isn't working differently in different places today." This "models how to navigate theological differences within the body of Christ without dismissing other believers' experiences."[^15] Our traditions hold different views on the miraculous gifts. How can we disagree on a question like this without dismissing one another?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** The notes say "we need leaders who look like Jesus - humble, merciful, and willing to suffer for others - and when God provides such leadership, we should be willing to follow."[^16] Who is a leader in your life you could pray for this week, and what would it look like for you to lead someone in that same way?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> 2 Corinthians is one part of a long conversation between Paul and a church that had grown and changed. His critics compared him to impressive "super-apostles," but Paul would not compete on their terms. Instead he boasted in beatings, shipwrecks, sleepless nights and a thorn he begged God to remove, and he heard that grace was enough. True authority, Paul says, is for building people up, not tearing them down. In a world rightly wary of abused authority, we are invited to discern, and to look for leaders who look like Jesus.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: where is one place you will let weakness, rather than strength, be the way God works through you this week?

</div>

It sets the letter in Ephesus, a center of Artemis worship, and reads Paul's instructions about marriage and his vision of "one new humanity" against that background.

**What to bring next week:** Read Ephesians 1:3–14 and 2:11–22, and circle every "we," "us" and "you." Notice where the pronouns change and come ready to say what you think it means.
