### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** In 2 Thessalonians, the "man of lawlessness" turned out to be a type the first readers knew in Caesar, and the letter called a frightened church to "stand firm" in the overlap between the ages.[^1]

**This week:** We meet the two men Paul wrote the Pastoral Epistles to, and we ask why Paul sent each one where he did.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode introduces 1 Timothy, 2 Timothy and Titus through the people who received them. Timothy came from Lystra, the son of a Jewish mother and a Greek father, and the notes describe him as a *mamzer* ("a child born from an illegitimate union"), a status that excluded him from full participation in the religious community. Paul nevertheless took him on and called him "my true son in the faith."[^2]

The heart of the episode is a surprising pair of assignments. Paul stationed "Timid Timothy" in Ephesus, a huge and strategic city, and "Tough Titus" in Crete, a smaller place with a hard reputation. The episode uses Robert Farrar Capon's idea of "left-handed power" to explain why Kingdom deployment often runs against worldly wisdom.[^3] The notes also mention the letters' guidance on church order, and since our traditions organize leadership differently, any conversation about that is a chance to learn from one another.

### Timothy, the Outsider Paul Called Son <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#timothy-the-outsider-paul-called-son}

*Timothy's story begins in an unimpressive town with a family background that closed doors.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Acts 16:1–3 and 2 Timothy 1:5 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** What do these passages tell you about Timothy's family, his hometown and his reputation? The notes add that Lystra was an "unimpressive town" whose ruins "remain largely unexcavated," and that Paul's repeated returns there "demonstrate divine guidance over strategic planning."[^4] What do you notice about the people in Timothy's life who shaped his faith?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The notes explain that a *mamzer* was "prohibited from entering the assembly of God's people" and "excluded from community religious participation," a status that was especially hard in "Shammai country" regions like Galatia, where strict Torah observance was emphasized.[^5] *Read 1 Timothy 1:2 aloud.* What would it have meant to someone with Timothy's background to be addressed as "my true son in the faith"?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say Paul's approach "mirrors Jesus's methodology": just as Jesus chose disciples who "weren't good enough" for other rabbis, Paul selects "those whom the world rejects." They point to David's anointing and Gideon's army as further examples of God's "consistent divine preference for the overlooked."[^6] Which other biblical stories come to mind where God chooses the person everyone else passed over?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes call Timothy's story "profoundly encouraging for anyone who has ever felt inadequate or excluded."[^7] Where have you felt like a *mamzer*, on the outside of something you longed to belong to? Or, if that is too personal to share, who around you might feel that way, and how could you be a Paul to them?

</div>

### Two Disciples, Two Assignments <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#two-disciples-two-assignments}

*Paul did not send each man where we might expect.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 1 Timothy 4:12 and 2 Timothy 1:6–7 aloud. Then read Titus 1:5 and 1:10–13 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Compare how Paul speaks to Timothy with how he speaks to Titus. What words of encouragement does Timothy receive, and what instructions does Titus receive about the people of Crete? What do these passages suggest about each man's personality and the challenges each faced?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes describe the two places. Ephesus was the "second largest city in the Roman Empire," the "headquarters of the early church movement in Asia Minor," a "high-risk, high-reward situation" to which Timothy was assigned "despite Timothy's timidity and confidence struggles." Crete was a "small island" with a difficult population, a "lower risk, moderate reward situation" suited to a "tough personality."[^8] If you were Paul's strategy consultant, where would you have sent each man? Why do you think Paul did the opposite?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say the letters also address church order, with elder qualifications "focusing on character over competency."[^9] Our churches choose and recognize leaders in different ways. In your tradition, what qualities are most looked for in a leader, and how does that compare with the weight these letters give to character?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Timothy was sent into a hard place in spite of his timidity, and Paul told him to "fan into flame" his gift and not let anyone look down on him.[^10] Where do you sense you are being asked to step into something that feels bigger than your confidence, and what would "fanning into flame" look like for you this week?

</div>

### Left-Handed Power <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#left-handed-power}

*The episode names the principle behind Paul's surprising choices.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** The notes contrast "Right-Handed Power," conventional wisdom that "matches strength to challenge, experience to opportunity, and confidence to leadership roles," with "Left-Handed Power," Kingdom methodology "where losing becomes winning, weakness becomes strength, and death brings resurrection."[^11] A few weeks ago we heard Paul say in 2 Corinthians that "when I am weak, then I am strong."[^12] Where have you seen left-handed power at work in a church, a family or your own life?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes warn that modern churches can end up "only promoting those with obvious qualifications or confidence."[^13] What might our churches, or this group, miss if we only listen to the most confident voices?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q11:** Name one weakness or limitation you usually try to hide. How might God use it, rather than despite it, in the place he has put you?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Timothy began as an outsider, shut out of the assembly and raised in an unimpressive town, and Paul called him "my true son in the faith." Then Paul did something no strategist would do: he sent the timid disciple to the great city of Ephesus and the tough one to the smaller island of Crete. The episode calls this left-handed power, the Kingdom way in which weakness becomes strength and losing becomes winning. The Pastoral Epistles are not only manuals for church order. They are letters to real people whom God chose to use in their weakness.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: who is one overlooked person you will encourage this week, the way Paul encouraged Timothy?

</div>
