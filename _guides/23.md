### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** We read Sinai as a wedding: vows given as a *ketubah*, a bride unfaithful in the middle of the ceremony, and a groom who writes the vows again.

**This week:** The rest of Exodus is about the Tabernacle, and Marty shows how its long building instructions retell the creation story and end in joy.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The entire rest of Exodus ends up being about the Tabernacle, and many readers skim it. Marty slows down and finds a pattern: seven times the text says "the LORD said to Moses," and the seventh speech is about the Sabbath, just as Genesis 1 builds to a seventh day of rest. When the work is done, Moses finishes it, inspects it, and blesses it, in language that echoes God finishing and blessing creation. Inside the Tabernacle, the Torah rests in the Ark behind a curtain embroidered with cherubim, like the Tree of Life guarded in Eden. The Tabernacle becomes a mobile Genesis that travels in the center of the camp.

Marty also traces an inverted structure (a chiasm) across these chapters, with the golden calf at its center and God's promise of presence and rest at its heart. The episode closes with two grand openings, the Tabernacle and Solomon's temple, where fire falls and the people fall on their faces in joy. A challenge from Ray Vander Laan asks what that means for us if we are the new temple.

### A Mobile Genesis <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#a-mobile-genesis}

*The Tabernacle instructions are built to remind Israel of the creation story.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes list seven verses in the Tabernacle instructions that begin with "The LORD said to Moses" or "Then the LORD said to Moses."[^1] Have seven different people each read one aloud, in order, and have the seventh person keep reading to the end of the chapter.

*Read Exodus 25:1; 30:11; 30:17; 30:22; 30:34; 31:1; and 31:12–18 aloud.*

What do you notice about the seventh speech? Where have we heard a pattern of seven that ends in rest before?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Marty compares the end of creation in Genesis 2, where God finishes his work, blesses, and rests, with the end of the Tabernacle work.[^2]

*Read Exodus 39:43 and Exodus 40:33 aloud.*

What words do you hear repeated from the creation story? Why might the writer want a people living in tents in the desert to hear creation every time they set up the Tabernacle?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Inside the Holy of Holies sat the Ark, and inside the Ark the tablets of the Law. The notes explain that the Torah was called the Tree of Life, and that it sat behind a curtain depicting cherubim, who guarded the center of the Garden after the fall. The Tabernacle was also called the Tent of Meeting, and the word for "meeting," *moed*, is the same word for "seasons" in Genesis 1.[^3] What does it suggest that the way back to the Tree of Life was placed right in the middle of Israel's camp?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes describe the Tabernacle as "a constant reminder to call you back to the love of G-d, to the forgiveness of G-d, the acceptance of G-d, and the rest that trusting the story provides you."[^4] Israel carried that reminder with them wherever they went. What reminds you of God's love and acceptance in the middle of an ordinary week? What would you want such a reminder to look like?

</div>

### Presence at the Center <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#presence-at-the-center}

*The golden calf sits at the very center of the Tabernacle chapters.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Marty lays out the Tabernacle section as a mirror: Glory of the LORD, Tabernacle and garments, Bezalel and Oholiab, Sabbath, and then the same four in reverse, ending with the Glory of the LORD filling the Tabernacle. In the center sit the golden calf, the broken tablets, and God's promise in Exodus 33:14 that his presence will go with them and give them rest.[^5]

*Read Exodus 33:12–17 aloud.*

What does Moses ask for, and what does God promise? What do you make of the fact that this promise sits right beside Israel's worst failure, wrapped on both sides by plans for God to live among them?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes compare the detailed Tabernacle instructions to a couple choosing paint colors, fixtures, and furniture for a new home, because God delights in creating a beautiful dwelling place with his people.[^6] Marty also describes his "skeleton practice" of memorizing the structure of biblical books so that patterns like this become visible.[^7] How does it change your reading of these long, detailed chapters to imagine God taking that kind of care over a home he wants to share?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The center of the chiasm reminds Israel that God's presence goes with them and gives them rest, even after the calf.[^8] Think of a season when you failed badly. In that season, did you experience God as moving toward you or away from you? What would it take for you to hear "my presence will go with you" as meant for you now?

</div>

### Falling on Joyful Faces <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#falling-on-joyful-faces}

*When God's house opens, the people fall on their faces in joy.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes describe two grand openings. At the Tabernacle, fire came from God's presence and consumed the offering; "Everyone shouted for joy and fell on their faces." At Solomon's temple, fire came down again, the glory filled the house, and the people knelt and worshiped.[^9]

*Read Leviticus 9:23–24 and 2 Chronicles 7:1–3 aloud.*

Notice what the people do and what they say. Many of us picture falling on our faces before God as an act of terror. What in these texts suggests something else?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say that at Pentecost, tongues of fire came to rest on believers, and God opened a new temple made of his people.[^10] How does it change your picture of Pentecost to see it as the next grand opening in this story?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** Ray Vander Laan's challenge, as the notes record it, ends with this question: "then what in the world should people do when they meet you? The new temple of G-d."[^11] The notes frame this as a call away from fear or judgment and toward joy.[^12] Each of our churches has its own habits for how we show God to outsiders, and each has strengths. When people meet you, or meet our churches, what do you think they mostly come away feeling? What is one thing you could do this week so that someone meets the joy of God's presence in you?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> The Tabernacle is creation retold: seven speeches, a Sabbath, a finished work that is blessed, and a Tree of Life guarded by cherubim in the middle of the camp. At the center of the whole section stands the golden calf, and right beside it God's promise that his presence will go with his people and give them rest. When the Tabernacle and the temple opened, fire fell and the people fell on their faces, shouting for joy. If we are now God's temple, our lives are meant to carry that same presence, rest, and joy into every place we go.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: where will you carry God's presence this week, and to whom?

</div>

Marty builds on a lesson from Ray: in creation God made a space and asked people to fill it, but with the Tabernacle God asks his people to create the space and he will fill it.
