### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** At Cana, Mary's "Please" set Jesus's public ministry in motion, a point of no return like a stone cutter's first strike, and his first followers turned out to be a "rogue outfit" of fishermen and tradesmen.[^1]

**This week:** Jesus touches a man with leprosy, friends dig through a roof to bring a paralyzed man to him, and two Pharisees argue about whether God can be bigger than their reading of Torah.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode opens in a pawnshop, where a man with leprosy tries to sell his tools, and moves to the healing of that man and then of a paralyzed man lowered through the roof of Zebedee's house. The hosts dwell on Jesus's willingness to touch someone considered unclean and on the faith of the friends who carried the paralytic. They also note a sobering detail: Pharisees literally pushing people out of a window to get closer with their questions.[^2]

Running through the episode is a conversation between Nicodemus and his student Shmuel about whether God is confined by Torah or Torah is subject to God. The hosts turn the question toward Christians: are our interpretations of the Bible underneath God, or have we put God underneath them? Meanwhile the disciples, Simon especially, are still trying to work out what "catching men" means, and Matthew's eye for detail hints at the Gospel he will one day write.[^3]

### Don't Turn Away from Me <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#dont-turn-away-from-me}

*The first healing restores far more than skin.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Leviticus 13:45–46 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q1:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes point to Leviticus 13–14 as the background for leprosy in the Bible and say the condition "created complete social isolation; touching a leper was shocking." The episode opens with the man trying to sell his tools in a pawnshop, showing the economic devastation as well.[^4] What does the passage require of the person with the disease? What would an ordinary day have looked like for him?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Jesus touches the man and tells him, "Don't turn away from me." He then sends him to "go to the temple, make the sacrifice that Moses required, and then go on your way." The healed man's joy quickly shifts from his disease to ordinary concerns like clothing, which the notes read as "complete restoration to community life."[^5] What do you notice about the different kinds of restoration in this scene: physical, social and religious? Why do you think Jesus sends him to the temple rather than simply sending him home?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes see in Jesus's touch "acceptance and inclusion" for someone society had rejected.[^6] Who around you might be waiting for someone not to turn away? What would the equivalent of that touch look like in your setting this week?

</div>

### Faith That Comes Through the Roof <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#faith-that-comes-through-the-roof}

*Jesus is moved by people who refuse to let a crowd keep their friend from him.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Mark 2:1–12 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Notice whose faith Jesus sees in verse 5. The notes stress that Jesus "specifically notices and commends the faith of the friends, not just the paralytic," and in the episode he tells them, "Your faith is beautiful."[^7] What do you notice about what the friends actually do in this passage? What kind of faith is that?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> In the episode, Pharisees literally push people out of the window to insert themselves with their questions. The notes call them the "theology police" and ask: "How often do churches create barriers to Jesus in the name of protecting truth?"[^8] Thinking diagnostically about churches you have known, including your own, what are some ways a concern for truth can, without anyone intending it, crowd out people who need to get close to Jesus?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The disciples are still unsure what they are for. Simon wants to "do something" and protect Jesus, and James had been about to join the 288, the Jerusalem choir referenced in 1 Chronicles 25. John offers simple advice: "If he needed you to know what that meant, he would've told you. Just be you, okay?"[^9] The notes connect the friends' faith to "intercessory prayer and practical advocacy for others."[^10] Who is someone you could carry toward Jesus right now, in prayer or in practical help? What would "opening the roof" look like for them, given who you are?

</div>

### We Are Still Students <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#we-are-still-students}

*Two Pharisees debate how big God is allowed to be, and the hosts hand the question to us.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Shmuel argues that certain things are impossible because Deuteronomy and Exodus say God cannot take human form. Nicodemus asks whether Shmuel is limiting God to his own interpretation and insists: "We are still students. All of us, our understanding will never be complete." The notes put the parallel question to modern believers: "Is God subject to the Bible, or is the Bible subject to God?"[^11] Both men are serious teachers within the same Jewish tradition, so this is a debate among the faithful, not a story of good guys and bad guys. In your own words, what is the difference between trusting Scripture and trusting your interpretation of Scripture?

*Read Isaiah 40:3–5 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q8:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> In the episode Nicodemus studies Isaiah 40 with Shmuel and connects it to John the Baptist's ministry. Nicodemus also says, "We can walk the ancient path but still be open to new things," referencing Jeremiah.[^12] What in this passage might lead a careful teacher like Nicodemus to wonder about John? What does it look like to walk an ancient path and still stay open?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** The notes observe that "Conservative evangelical Christianity often struggles with this tension between confidence in Scripture and humility about interpretation," and that "We frequently put God underneath our interpretations rather than our interpretations underneath God." They add that this "doesn't mean abandoning Scripture but maintaining teachability and openness to correction."[^13] Every tradition in this room takes the Bible seriously, and each has its own ways of holding conviction and humility together. Where have you seen that balance held well? What is one conviction you hold firmly, and one place where you would honestly say, "I am still a student"?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> In this episode Jesus touches a man everyone else avoided and tells him not to turn away. He looks at four friends on a roof and calls their faith beautiful, while some religious leaders push people out of the way to get their questions in. And in a quiet study, Nicodemus reminds his student that none of us has finished learning. The notes describe the goal as remaining students whose "finite, fallible interpretations must remain subject to the infinite God."[^14] That humility frees us to carry others to Jesus instead of guarding the door.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, finish this: "I am still a student of..."

</div>
