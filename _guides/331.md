### The Full Map — Series Complete {#the-full-map-series-complete}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** *Tselem* is given; *demut* is worked out. We hold only half a shekel. Good ruling draws out rather than tramples. God's face is not our brand.

**Today:** The seventh day has no "God saw that it was good," no evening and morning, no partner in the creation sequence. What was missing from God's sight? Josh's answer: *us* — resting with God. That is Shabbat's partner. Rest completes the work.

### Opening <span class="guide-time">10 min</span> {#opening}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-opening" markdown="1">

**Opening Question:** What is one part of your life — a project, a relationship, a season of growth, a season of faith — that you have not been able to rest in because it doesn't feel finished? What would it look like to rest in it anyway?

</div>

### Genesis 2:1–3 — Day 7 <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#genesis-213-day-7}

*Read Genesis 2:1–3 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Verse 1 says creation "was finished." Verse 2 says God *finished* it on the seventh day.[^1] Josh's reading: the rest itself completes creation. Something can be finished and still unfinished — and rest is what makes it whole. Where else in life is this true? What does it mean that rest can *complete* something rather than just pause it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Genesis 2:3 ends with: God created (*bara*) "in order to make" (*la'asot*) — to keep working and shaping.[^2] God did not create in order to reach a finished product. God created in order to keep engaging. What does it mean for your understanding of faith — and of God's ongoing work in you — that creation was designed to be a continuing process rather than a completed state?

</div>

### Exodus 31:1–18 — Bezalel and the Sabbath <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#exodus-31118-bezalel-and-the-sabbath}

*Read Exodus 31:1–3 and 12–14 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** God says: "I have called Bezalel *by name*."[^3] Bezalel means "in the shadow/image of God." His father is Uri — "my light." His grandfather is Hur, who held up Moses's arms. Bezalel is *filled* with the Spirit of God to do the creative work of the tabernacle. What does it mean for your own work — creative, relational, vocational, spiritual — that the Spirit *fills* rather than merely *instructs*? And what happens to what you've been filled with when you stop long enough to rest?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Josh speaks in this episode directly to people whose faith is in a season of deconstruction or reconstruction — where things that seemed fixed are in flux.[^4] His argument: God created *in order to keep shaping*. The spiritual body built through years of practice can be trusted to keep working even when you are not actively maintaining it. And Shabbat is specifically the practice of trusting that. For anyone in this room in that kind of season: what would it mean to take one day where you stop picking at the structure — and trust that the body is still working?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Shabbat must be "guarded" (*shamar*), and profaning it — *chalal* — means to pierce, wound, or bore a hole in something whole.[^5] What is the difference between *resting in* Shabbat and merely *stopping work*? In your experience, what has produced genuine Sabbath rest — not just a day off, but the restoration of the *nefesh* (soul)?

</div>

### Closing — Series Integration <span class="guide-time">35 min</span> {#closing-series-integration}

> *"Seven weeks ago, we started with the Ark holding testimonies in the dark — pieces of a broken covenant that God kept anyway. We moved through a door built for love, dry ground where community grows, priestly garments that carry names over the heart, offerings that end in a feast, oil that covers everything, and incense kept holy for God. And today we arrive at the day with no partner — the day that was waiting for us. The seventh day doesn't call us to do one more thing. It calls us to stop, to trust, and to discover what was already whole."*
{: .guide-summary}

*90 seconds of silence before the first closing question.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Series Integration 1:** Looking back over the seven weeks — what has changed in how you hold one of Josh's four reflection questions from Week 1: testimonies, bread, lamp, or dance? What is different in your Ark, at your Table, in front of your Menorah, or in the rhythm of the dance?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Series Integration 2:** Which pairing across the series changed you most — which day of creation and its corresponding tabernacle section will you carry longest, and why?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Series Integration 3 — Commissioning:** Josh closes the series by arguing that Shabbat is an act of faith: you rest *inside* an unfinished creation and an unfinished faith, and that rest is what completes the work.[^6] Not when everything is whole — *now*. The four questions from Week 1 are still the questions: *What are your testimonies? What nourishes you? Where does your light face? And how do you rest?*

Name one specific practice — from this series or new to you — that you intend to begin or sustain as your way of answering one of those four questions.

*One sentence. Name the practice.*

</div>

**Closing:**

> *"The tabernacle ends with this: God rested. God was refreshed — literally, ensouled. *Vayinafash*. And then the instructions say: build it. Fill it. Move it. Let me dwell among you. The invitation has never stopped. Go rest."*
{: .guide-summary}
