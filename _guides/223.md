### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Jesus spent his days with curious children, honoring their teacher while warning that "smart men lack wisdom," and naming a timid boy "Joshua the brave" at his first act of courage.[^1]

**This week:** Simon's schemes run out, his nets come up empty, and the miraculous catch of Luke 5 turns a desperate fisherman into a disciple.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode follows Simon to the end of his own resources. Deep in debt to Rome, he justifies fishing on the Sabbath, makes deals with Romans, and prays a raw prayer about God's seemingly inconsistent promises. His wife Eden refuses to let him drift, and the hosts see in her the *ezer kenegdo* of Genesis 2. When Zebedee and his sons arrive to help, Jesus teaches from Simon's boat, and the nets fill.[^2]

The hosts also note how the show weaves John's account (Andrew meets Jesus first through John the Baptist) together with the fishing call in the other Gospels, how Jesus teaches with a net in his hands, and his instruction to gather "all kinds" and let him do the sorting. Around the edges, Matthew's isolation deepens and the Pharisees divide over how to handle John the Baptist.[^3]

### Simon at the End of Himself <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#simon-at-the-end-of-himself}

*Simon's crisis is financial, but the episode shows it becoming spiritual.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes describe Simon's desperate prayer as revealing "the authentic human struggle with God's seemingly inconsistent promises throughout Israel's history," a story of "deliverance followed by wilderness, promised land followed by exile, return followed by oppression."[^4] What do you hear in a prayer like that? Why might the hosts treat its raw honesty as authentic faith rather than a lack of it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Simon invokes *pikuach nefesh* ("preserving life"), "the Jewish teaching that almost any commandment can be broken to save a life," arguing that his debt to Rome puts lives at stake. Eden challenges whether the principle really applies, or whether he is excusing "a longer pattern of faithlessness and self-reliance."[^5] How would you describe the difference between a genuine emergency and a rationalization? Why is it so hard to tell from the inside?

*Read Genesis 2:18 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes translate *ezer kenegdo* as "helper who opposes" and see it embodied in Eden, who "is not passively submissive but actively opposes Peter when he strays from faith and tries to fix everything himself." They credit her with *chutzpah* (bold courage) and say the portrayal challenges both patriarchal readings and readings that ignore first-century context.[^6] Notice the words of the verse itself. What does "helper who opposes" add to the way you have heard this verse taught? Then think about your own life: who has loved you enough to push back when you drifted, and how did you receive it?

</div>

### The Catch and the Call <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-catch-and-the-call}

*The show pulls several Gospel accounts into one story of failure, help and recognition.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Luke 5:1–11 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Listen for what Simon says and does from the moment Jesus asks to use his boat to the moment he falls at Jesus's knees. The notes trace Simon's arc in the episode from "I don't need a lamb, I need fish" to "Depart from me, I'm a sinful man."[^7] What changes in Simon between verse 5 and verse 8? What do you think he sees that makes him respond the way he does?

*Read John 1:35–42 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> In John's Gospel, Andrew meets Jesus through John the Baptist and then brings his brother; in Matthew, Mark and Luke, Jesus calls the brothers while they are fishing. The show has Andrew meet Jesus first and then has Jesus call both brothers at the catch, which the notes call "creative faithfulness to the Text."[^8] What is gained by honoring both accounts? Does it raise any questions for you about how the Gospels tell the same story differently?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes see a "divine setup" in the episode: Simon's debt, the failed nights, Andrew's encounter, Zebedee's sons arriving, and Jesus teaching from the boat. They observe that "when Simon works alone he fails, but when others arrive to help, breakthrough becomes possible," and that "what feels like meaningless struggle may be preparation for significant encounter."[^9] Not every struggle resolves the way Simon's did, so answer as honestly as you like. Looking back, where has the help of other people been part of how God met you? Is there a place where you are still trying to fish alone?

</div>

### Gather All Kinds <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#gather-all-kinds}

*Jesus gives his new disciples a job, and it is not sorting.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes stress that the Pharisees were not one block. They were divided between the schools of Hillel and Shammai and over how to handle John the Baptist, whose *tavilat t'shuvah* (baptism of repentance) did not fit the usual ritual washings. Nicodemus questions and investigates, while his student Shmuel may go so far as to give John's location to the Romans.[^10] What changes for you when the religious leaders in the story are divided rather than uniform?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Jesus tells his new disciples to gather "all kinds" of fish and he will sort them out later. The notes observe that "Christians often spend enormous energy on gatekeeping," and that Jesus "removes this burden." Meanwhile Matthew cannot even enter the bar where the fishermen gather, hated "more than Romans because he chose betrayal."[^11] Who would your community find hardest to picture in the net? What would it feel like to let go of the sorting and simply keep gathering?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Simon spends this episode trying to save himself, with deals, rationalizations and lonely nights on the water. Eden will not let him drift, his partners arrive to help, and Jesus meets him in the boat with more fish than the nets can hold. The notes describe the journey as moving "from self-sufficiency to surrender, from practical concerns to recognition of who Jesus truly is."[^12] And the first job Jesus gives the new disciples is not to decide who belongs, but to gather all kinds and trust him with the rest.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, where do you need to stop fixing it yourself and let someone into the boat?

</div>
