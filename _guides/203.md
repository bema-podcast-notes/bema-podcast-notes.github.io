### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** The history reached our own lifetimes, from fundamentalism and liberalism to the Jesus Movement and Jacob Neusner, and Marty insisted that "we find ourselves - as every generation has - perfectly placed for this future."[^1]

**This week:** With the timeline complete, Marty marks the "you are here" spot on the map and names four pillars the church lost and can recover.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This episode gathers up the entire BEMA journey, from creation to the present. Marty names four pillars that were present throughout Scripture and especially in the first-century church rooted in Judaism: Text, Community, Discipleship and Wrestling. When the church rejected its Jewish heritage, he argues, it lost not only relationships with Jewish people but each of these pillars, and it took on empire narratives of consumerism, comfort and doctrinal control instead.[^3]

The episode is hopeful, not only critical. Marty points to the tools, connectivity and cultural openness to complexity that make these pillars more recoverable today than ever, and he frames the whole discussion with *t'shuvah* ("return" or "repentance"): asking what to remove, what to keep and what ancient practices to recover. He also insists that change starts small. After a trip to Israel in 2008, he began by memorizing one paragraph of the Bible each week.[^4]

### Text and Community <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#text-and-community}

*The first two pillars are a Bible carried inside us and a community that includes people we disagree with.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Isaiah 55:10–11 and Ephesians 6:17 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Marty says first-century Jewish children memorized the entire Torah and much of the *Tanakh* (the Hebrew Bible), and that there is a crucial difference between having a Bible on the shelf and having the Bible within you. He connects Isaiah 55 to the power of God's word to accomplish its purpose, and Paul's description of the word as "the sword of the Spirit," suggesting that losing our connection to the Text means losing connection to the power of the Holy Spirit.[^5] What images do these two passages use for God's word? What do they suggest the word does, and where does each passage picture that word being located?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** When the church abandoned its Jewish identity and practice, Marty argues, it reverted to Roman empire values: self-preservation, luxury, comfort and leisure. The Kingdom's most effective tool is an inclusive community that shows the world a better way to live, not merely friendship with the like-minded but radical fellowship with people we disagree with. That, the notes say, is what makes the church truly Eucharistic.[^6] Our own group brings together several traditions. Where have you experienced real fellowship across disagreement, here or elsewhere, and what made it hold?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty lists modern tools for the first two pillars: BibleGateway.com and online lexicons that allow word searches in seconds, and Zoom, social networks and email that make community possible in new ways. The challenge, the notes say, is not to settle for pseudo-community that allows continued idolatry of consumerism.[^7] Where do these tools genuinely help your engagement with the Text and with community, and where do they let you settle for less?

</div>

### Discipleship and Wrestling <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#discipleship-and-wrestling}

*Jesus formed people by sharing life with them, and the faith he inherited welcomed hard questions.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 28:19–20 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Jesus spent three years with twelve young men (and women), all day, every day. Marty notes that he started no churches and went on no missionary journeys; he said "follow me" and showed people how to live. The Great Commission calls us to make disciples, not merely to run programs or attend events.[^8] Notice what the passage actually tells the disciples to do. How does that compare with the way you were discipled, and what is the closest you have come to that kind of life-on-life formation?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say modern churches have replaced immersive discipleship with Bible studies and conferences, which have value but are not substitutes. Marty points to on-the-job training as proof that immersive learning still works and calls for faith communities restructured around total immersion rather than programs.[^9] What would one realistic step toward that kind of discipleship look like in your church, or in this group?

*Read Genesis 32:22–32 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The early church, Marty says, was devoted to the same wrestling that characterized Jacob. They believed the text was meant to be wrestled with, rejected formulas, accepted many shades of gray, and lived with a culture of *chutzpah* (bold engagement and questioning). When the church lost the Text, questions and doubts became threats to doctrines and creeds; it began discouraging dissent and has historically even killed people for it. Marty sees an opening today, because younger generations value life's complexities and questions can become doorways to discovery.[^10] What does Jacob do in this story, and what does he receive? How did the churches you grew up in treat questions and doubts, and how has this BEMA journey changed the way you handle your own?

</div>

### Living in Babylon, Returning Home <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#living-in-babylon-returning-home}

*Marty names our cultural captivity and calls us to a return that starts with small steps.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty uses Emmylou Harris's song "Time in Babylon" and Walter Brueggemann's *Out of Babylon* to describe modern captivity to empire. His examples include five-lane highways and SUVs ("you need that chrome to get you home"), cluster mansions, brand-name obsession, and prioritizing results and wealth over conscience and generosity. The kingdom narrative, by contrast, calls for subversive community, generosity, hospitality, Sabbath and care for the marginalized.[^11] Which of Marty's Babylon examples hits closest to home for you, and which kingdom practice is its best antidote?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** *T'shuvah* asks three questions: what needs to be removed from our faith practice, what needs to be kept, and what ancient practices need to be recovered. The notes stress that this means returning, not inventing new things. Marty adds a Talmudic principle: "Just as rain falls in drops and forms mighty rivers, so it is with Torah. A man studies a little here and a little there, until understanding comes like a rushing spring."[^12] Of those three questions (remove, keep, recover), which feels most pressing in your faith right now? What is one small, realistic and attainable "drop" you could begin with?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Marty marks the spot on the map: you are here. The church began as a Jewish movement that carried the Text in its memory, lived as an inclusive community, made disciples by sharing life, and wrestled boldly with God and with hard questions. When it rejected its Jewish heritage, it lost each of these pillars and drifted into the comforts and controls of empire. Yet we live with more tools, more access and more openness to complexity than any generation before us. The way forward is *t'shuvah*: a return that starts not with dramatic overnight change but with small, faithful drops that, over time, become a river.[^13]
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, name the one pillar you will take a small step toward this week, and what the step is.

</div>
