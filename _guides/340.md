### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** The second day, the curtains clasped into one tent, and the veil showed boundaries and unity working together in love, and Marty challenged us to choose one or two relationships and ask what from our story could refresh them.[^1]

**This week:** The third reflection, built on Josh's Episode 327, connects the third day of creation to shared community life, the bronze altar, the courtyard, and what Josh called "the Dance of Life."[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

On the third day God gathers the waters so dry land can appear, and the land produces plants that carry their own seed: space is made, and life spreads beyond itself. The bronze altar, the first object a worshiper meets inside the gate, ties the worship of God to real, physical, shared life. The courtyard has clear edges and a wide entrance facing the sunrise.[^3]

Marty's three questions build on each other: what kind of space we make in our worship for raw community, how we can open that space to growth beyond our control, and how we can give the seeds of our lives away so they can grow for others. He also asks listeners who would rather skip these slower episodes to stay with them, because there may be "treasures buried here."[^4]

### An Altar for Raw Community <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#an-altar-for-raw-community}

*The altar ties worship to tangible, shared, sometimes messy life.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:9–13 aloud. Then read Exodus 27:1–8 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** On the third day God gathers the waters "to one place" so dry ground can appear, and the land produces plants and trees with seed in them; it is one of the days where "God saw that it was good" is said twice. The altar is made of acacia wood overlaid with bronze, "hollow, out of boards," and carried on poles, so it travels with the people.[^5] Hearing the two passages together, what do you notice? Which detail would you want to stay with longer?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes explain that offerings (*korbanot*) come from a root meaning "to draw near," and that the altar is where ordinary life (grain, animals, the produce of the land) is brought before God, often as a shared meal.[^6] How does hearing an offering as "drawing near" change the way you picture this altar?

*Sit together in silence for two minutes, then take up the next question.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Marty's first question asks what kind of space you make in your worship for raw community, which he describes as "community that isn't predetermined or required to fit some kind of mold, but meeting people where they're at with real vulnerability." The notes set it against faith that is "performative, tightly scripted or exclusive."[^7] Where do you find community like that, and where in your life is it missing?

</div>

### A Courtyard Facing the Sunrise <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#a-courtyard-facing-the-sunrise}

*The courtyard is a defined space with an open, beautiful way in.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:9–13 aloud again. Then read Exodus 27:9–19 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The courtyard is 100 by 50 cubits, enclosed by linen curtains, with its gate on the east side "toward the sunrise": a 20-cubit curtain of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine linen. The notes call it "a space for people, not just for priests."[^8] What do you notice about how this space is bounded and how it is entered?

*Sit together in silence for two minutes.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Marty's second question asks how our community spaces can be opened for "greater growth beyond your control." The notes compare this to gardening: you can water, weed, and provide structure, "but you can't make the seed grow."[^9] Church life is ordered differently across the traditions in this room. In the church you know best, which structures help community grow in ways no one could plan, and which tend to hold it tightly? What do you think shapes that difference?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes imagine a Bible study that makes room for doubt, grief, or disagreement "instead of requiring everyone to reach the same tidy conclusion," and a leader who hands a project to someone else and lets it grow in unexpected directions.[^9] What would it look like for this group, or another group you belong to, to leave more room for what none of us can control?

</div>

### Seeds for Others <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#seeds-for-others}

*Seed-bearing life exists partly to pass life on.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes suggest that mentoring is like sowing: you invest time, skills, or wisdom in someone younger, knowing the fruit "may show up years later and somewhere else."[^10] Who sowed something in you whose fruit they may never have seen?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Marty's third question asks how we can "give the seeds of your life away so that they can grow for others." The notes put it simply: "Fruit is for eating, and seed is for the next generation," and they suggest this is what Josh meant by "the Dance of Life."[^11] What seed of your life (time, skill, wisdom, or story) could you give away this month, and to whom?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> The third day shows God making room and filling it with life that carries seed for the future. The bronze altar ties worship to real, shared, unpolished life. The courtyard shows a community with structure and a wide-open door facing the sunrise. Together they ask whether we make room for honest, vulnerable community, whether we will let it grow beyond our control, and whether we are giving away the seeds of our lives so that others can flourish.
{: .guide-summary}

*Read Genesis 1:9–13 aloud one last time, then sit together in silence for one minute.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, name one shared space in your week and one generous thing you will do there toward someone's healing or flourishing.

</div>
