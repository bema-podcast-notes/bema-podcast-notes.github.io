### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** We finished Session 5 with the capstone episode, where Sarah Bessey's "Benediction" and "Commissioning" sent us out as ministers in our ordinary lives: "Stop waiting for someone else to validate your created self. That is done."[^1]

**This week:** Session 6 begins. Marty and Brent look back on how BEMA's tone has changed, tell some personal stories of transformation, and describe Session 6 as "Postscripts," an open-ended season of deeper dives.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This is a reflective, story-driven episode rather than a teaching episode. Marty and Brent describe how early BEMA carried anger at church traditions and a heavy-handed style of deconstruction (the "Christmas tree controversy" is their main example), and how it grew into a more grace-filled, constructive posture.[^3] Brent tells his own story of moving from an internal "Bible thumper" to a grace-filled believer, and of how shared BEMA language helps him and his wife Maggi stay on the same page.[^4]

The hosts then describe Session 6 as "Postscripts": an indefinite continuation with guest interviews, Midrash studies, character studies and deeper looks at the prophets, compared to the seventh day of Genesis 1 that "goes on indefinitely."[^5] The episode closes with Brent's young son Darius reciting Genesis 1, a picture of a child learning God's goodness before learning about sinfulness.[^6]

### A Teacher's Tone <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#a-teachers-tone}

*Before Session 6 starts anything new, the hosts look honestly at how they used to teach.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes say BEMA's early tone included anger at church traditions and "a confrontational posture that inadvertently created division in families," and that the change was intentional, "shaped by observing how students adopted their teacher's tone."[^3] Where have you seen a group pick up its leader's tone, for good or for ill? What do you think makes tone so contagious?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> In the Christmas tree story, students went home convicted about pagan origins but could not explain a nuanced position to their families. Maggi Billings' pushback helped Marty see the need to present information "without unnecessarily pulling the rug out from under people."[^7] The notes call the story "a parable for how we can wound even when we're technically right."[^8] What is the difference between being right and being helpful? When have you seen the two pull apart?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Over Sessions 1–5, most of us met ideas that unsettled something we grew up with in our own tradition. When that happened, how did you carry it home to family, friends or church? Looking back, what helped, and what would you do differently? (Share as much or as little as you like.)

</div>

### Goodness Before Sinfulness <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#goodness-before-sinfulness}

*The episode ends with a toddler reciting Genesis 1, and that choice says something about where formation starts.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:1–2:3 aloud (take turns, a few verses each).*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** As you listen, keep a count of every time the text says something is "good," and notice anything that is said differently near the end of the chapter. What did you notice about where Genesis 1 puts its weight?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes describe the Billings family's choice to teach Darius Genesis 1 first as a "focus on creation goodness rather than fall narratives" and "learning goodness before sinfulness."[^6] What do you think it does for a person's sense of identity to start with "very good" rather than with sin?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes call this "a deliberate counter-narrative to evangelical traditions that emphasize human sinfulness as the starting point for understanding God and self."[^9] In the tradition you were raised in (or came to faith in), which came first: God's goodness or human sinfulness? What did that starting point give you, and what, if anything, did it make harder?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** If a child, a grandchild or a new believer in your life asked you "Who am I?", what would you most want them to hear first, and how might you say it this week?

</div>

### Postscripts and Shared Language <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#postscripts-and-shared-language}

*Session 6 has no single storyline, so the hosts reach for a picture from the first week of creation.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Brent describes how BEMA gave him and Maggi a shared vocabulary: "alien, orphan, and widow" as shorthand for missional values, "trusting the story" for uncertainty, and *ayin tovah* ("good eye") for extending grace.[^4] Which BEMA phrases have become part of your own vocabulary? Describe a time one of them helped you say something you could not have said before.

*Read Genesis 2:1–3 aloud again.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** The hosts compare Session 6 to the seventh day of Genesis 1 that "goes on indefinitely."[^5] Compare how the seventh day is described with the six days before it. What do you notice, and why might that make a good picture for a season of study with no fixed endpoint?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes mention "hundreds" of discussion groups around the world as part of how BEMA spreads, and describe that model as "sustainable" because it "doesn't depend on constant teacher presence."[^10] As our group starts a season without a set storyline, name one thing you hope we keep from Sessions 1–5 and one thing you hope we try.

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Before Session 6 teaches anything new, it asks us to look at how we hold what we already know. Marty and Brent tell the story of a teaching voice that learned grace, of a marriage given a shared language, and of a child learning that creation is good before learning that it is broken. Session 6 is a postscript, and like the seventh day it stays open. We can carry what we have learned with the same conviction and with a gentler tone.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one thing from Sessions 1–5 you want to carry into this new season, and how do you want to carry it?

</div>
