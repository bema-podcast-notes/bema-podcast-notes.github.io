### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Smyrna's believers were poor because they refused to call Caesar a god, and some in the local synagogue declared them outsiders. Jesus told them he knew, that they were rich, and that the faithful would receive a crown of life and never be hurt by the second death.

**This week:** Pergamum was crowded with thrones: Caesar's, Zeus's, Dionysius's, and the healing god Asclepius's. John's letter speaks to each, and at the same time grounds every image in the Hebrew Scriptures.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The notes describe Pergamum as Caesar Augustus's capital in the province of Asia, holding the *potestas gladii*, the power of the sword, which let its courts decide matters of life and death with Caesar's authority.[^1] The city hosted a temple to Caesar, a towering altar to Zeus, the center of Dionysian worship with its annual festival of excess, and the premier Asclepian (healing complex) of the ancient world.[^2] "Where Satan has his throne," the notes say, refers to this overwhelming concentration of idolatry, not to a single temple.[^3]

Some in the church held to "the teaching of Balaam," the claim that one could serve God and still take part in pagan festivals.[^4] Against all this, Jesus comes as the one with the sharp double-edged sword and promises a white stone with a new name, echoing the white stones on which the Asclepian recorded the names of those it healed.[^5] The notes highlight John's "dual source" method: every cultural reference is also rooted in Scripture through *remez*, a hint that calls a whole passage to mind.[^6]

### Where Satan Has His Throne <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#where-satan-has-his-throne}

*Pergamum was not just another city: it was a seat of imperial authority and a crowd of temples.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Revelation 2:12–13 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes describe the "thrones" of Pergamum: a temple to Caesar Augustus on the acropolis, visible from miles out to sea, with an altar shaped like a throne; an altar to Zeus 44 feet tall and covered in gold leaf; the temple to Dionysius that began the Sacred Way and its annual festival; and the Asclepian healing complex at the foot of the city.[^7] The Sacred Way, the notes add, was lined with seventeen more temples, so that simply crossing town meant traveling through "layer upon layer of pagan worship."[^8] Read verse 13 again. What does Jesus acknowledge about where these believers live, and what does he praise them for?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Rome granted Pergamum the *potestas gladii* (Latin, "the power of the sword"), letting the city make legal decisions "with the full authority of Caesar himself," including real power of life and death.[^9] Jesus introduces himself as "Him who has the sharp, double-edged sword," and the notes say "The sword of his mouth, his word, carries ultimate power over both the sword of Rome and the sword of Pergamum."[^10] What does it mean for a believer who could be sentenced by the city's courts to hear Jesus claim that sword?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes mention Antipas, "a faithful witness put to death in Pergamum," and observe that the letter honors both the church's corporate faithfulness and one person's martyrdom.[^11] Why might Jesus name one faithful person in a letter to a whole church?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes argue that "where Satan has his throne" points to the overwhelming concentration of idolatry in Pergamum rather than one building.[^12] If Jesus were writing to your town, what would he name as its "thrones," the things that demand allegiance there, and where do you feel their pull?

</div>

### The Teaching of Balaam <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-teaching-of-balaam}

*Some in the church had found a way to make peace with the city, and John answers by reaching back to Numbers.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Revelation 2:14–16 aloud, then Numbers 25:1–3 and Numbers 31:16.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes explain that the biblical text does not say outright that Balaam advised Balak to use sexual temptation, but Jewish tradition consistently taught it, and Jude, 2 Peter and Revelation reference it "without hesitation."[^13] Compare Revelation 2:14 with Numbers 25:1–3 and 31:16. What details from Numbers does John bring into his warning, and what does he add?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes summarize the teaching of Balaam as the compromise position: "You can serve God and still participate in pagan culture. You can have both."[^14] In Pergamum, they say, the Dionysian festival involved idol meat, excessive drinking and sexual immorality, and local law made participation part of civic life, so "This wasn't optional participation in a minor cultural event."[^15] The notes add that only "some" in the church had accepted this teaching, not all.[^16] Why do you think the compromise position found traction with some believers and not others?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes describe John's "dual source" method: a cultural reference his audience met daily, and at the same time a *remez* (Hebrew, "hint" or allusion) to the Hebrew Scriptures. A Jewish believer hearing "the teaching of Balaam" would recall the whole story from Numbers and recognize their own situation in it, showing "that God has already spoken to this exact issue."[^17] What difference does it make to face a modern pressure knowing that Scripture has already addressed something like it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes ask readers to notice that believers then, as now, faced cultural pressure unequally and "respond with varying levels of compromise."[^18] Where is the "you can have both" argument most persuasive in your own life right now, and what would it look like to answer it honestly?

</div>

### A White Stone and a New Name <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#a-white-stone-and-a-new-name}

*The letter ends with a promise drawn straight from the city's famous hospital.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Revelation 2:17 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** The notes describe the Asclepian as the premier medical facility of its world, with registration and payment much like modern insurance, ritual cleansing pools, an incense altar where patients worshiped Asclepius to receive treatment, a gateway inscribed "Death may not enter here," acoustic tunnels meant to replicate "the voice of Asclepius like rushing water," and white stone monuments inscribed with the names of those healed.[^19] The notes call avoiding it "a significant sacrifice, not merely a minor inconvenience."[^20] How does the promise of a white stone and a new name answer a believer who had given up the best healthcare in the world to stay faithful?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes observe that the Asclepian imitated the "voice like rushing waters" John hears in Revelation 1, and that John subverts this pagan imitation of divine imagery.[^21] Where do you see something in our culture borrowing the language of healing, salvation or a new identity, and what does it actually offer?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Pergamum was a city of thrones, and every throne asked for worship: Caesar's altar on the acropolis, Zeus's golden altar, the festival of Dionysius, and the incense of the Asclepian. Some believers decided they could have both. John answers with the story of Balaam and Peor, and with Jesus holding a sword sharper than Rome's. To those who stay faithful, even when it costs them their health and their standing, he promises a white stone bearing a new name that only he can give.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one place this week where you will need to choose between "having both" and staying true to his name?

</div>
