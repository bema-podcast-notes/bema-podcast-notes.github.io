### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Our Session 6 guides ran through the Parables series (to Ep. 316) and then the Sanctuary series, whose final week (Ep. 331) looked at the seventh day and argued that rest completes creation. Resting in the middle of an unfinished life, the notes concluded, "is itself an act of faith."[^1]

**This week:** Marty and Brent open Session 7, a Sabbath season timed with Marty's "hundred day step away" from Memorial Day to Labor Day, and explain why the session itself will be light on content.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

Marty's sabbatical closes a two-year stretch he has kept as a personal Sabbath year followed by a personal Jubilee year, drawing on the seven-year rhythms of Leviticus 25 and Deuteronomy 15. He holds those rhythms as principles for worship and growth rather than strict rules.[^3] Marty and Brent then describe how a planned sabbatical differs from a long vacation: it needs a plan, firm boundaries, and a deliberate start and finish, and it can reset unhealthy patterns for good.[^4]

The episode also sets out the shape of Session 7. Sessions 1 to 6 were the work, Session 7 is the rest, and Session 8 returns to work. The first seven episodes will be reflective, with guided prayer, prompts, music, and silence, and listeners and groups are invited to create their own expression of a Sabbath season rather than simply consume one.[^5] This guide is shaped to match: fewer questions, more room to think.

### The Rhythm of Seven <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-rhythm-of-seven}

*Marty's sabbatical grows out of Torah's seven-year rhythms, held with an open hand.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Leviticus 25:1–12 aloud, slowly. Then sit together in silence for one minute.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes summarize the biblical basis of the Sabbath year and the Jubilee: every seventh year the land lies fallow and debts are released, and in the fiftieth year land returns to its original family owners and enslaved people go free.[^3] As you listened to Leviticus 25:1–12, who or what gets to rest in this passage? Name one detail that surprised you or that you had never noticed before.

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> For roughly 1,800 years, Rabbinic Judaism has held that *Shmitah* (the Sabbath year) is tied to the land of Israel, so only those living in the Land are bound to keep it. Marty is not legally obligated, yet he timed his sabbatical rhythm by the Jewish calendar's *Shmitah* cycle. He also picked a personal Jubilee (*Yovel*) year fifteen to twenty years ago and admits the choice was "completely arbitrary."[^6] What do you make of someone choosing to keep a rhythm no one requires of them? What might be gained by doing so?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Marty's key principle is that "If you hold these with clenched fists," these rhythms become irrelevant; the real question is whether a principle exists that can guide one's worship and growth.[^6] Sabbath is taught and practised differently across the traditions in this room. What did your church background teach you, spoken or unspoken, about Sabbath and rest? Where do you find yourself holding rest with a clenched fist today, and where with an open hand?

</div>

### Rest Does Not Happen by Accident <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#rest-does-not-happen-by-accident}

*Marty and Brent explain why real rest needs a plan, firm boundaries, and a deliberate way in and out.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes contrast a common sabbatical ("Undefined time off, like a big vacation," often filled with household chores) with Impact Campus Ministries' "working sabbatical," planned months ahead with a written proposal. Marty's 100-day plan includes working with a spiritual director through the 19th Annotation of the Ignatian Spiritual Exercises, planned family vacations, a structured journal, and a full break from email, social media, meetings, and Zoom.[^7] Looking at the two approaches, what seems to separate rest that restores from time off that can leave a person "feeling worse"?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode compares a sabbatical to a physical fast: easing in and easing out changes how it goes. The same idea applies to a weekly Sabbath, where using Friday afternoon to wind down makes Saturday's rest deeper. Brent adds that the first two or three weeks of a sabbatical can be lost to simply decompressing.[^8] How do you usually enter and leave a day off, a vacation, or a Sunday? What would a deliberate "Friday afternoon" look like for you?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** During the sabbatical Marty's emails will be deleted, not saved, and personal messages get an auto-reply. Brent says Marty must be "cut off entirely," and Marty jokes that this is a good Hebrew phrase, a play on the Torah penalty of being "cut off from one's people" (*karet*). The notes add that this matters even more for someone with disciplined habits, "because those habits will pull them back into work."[^9] Which of your habits, including good ones, pull you back into work when you try to rest? What would it take to be cut off in a healthy way for one day this month?

</div>

### Designing Our Own Session 7 <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#designing-our-own-session-7}

*The episode invites listeners and groups to take part in the Sabbath season, not just listen to it.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty knows guided-reflection episodes will feel odd while driving or on a treadmill, so he invites listeners to choose a time and place where they can actually pause, journal, and pray.[^10] Where and when could you listen to next week's episode so that you can stop while you do it? Name a specific time and place.

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Session 7 is built on the pattern "six days you work, and on the seventh you rest," and its episodes are light on content because piling on information "would not be Sabbath." The notes suggest that discussion groups might hold a shared media fast, practise silence together, or keep reflective journals instead of studying new material.[^5] What would help this group's next nine weeks feel like rest rather than one more assignment? Name one thing you would like us to try together.

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Session 7 begins by practising what it teaches. Marty's hundred days away grow out of Torah's rhythms of Sabbath year and Jubilee, held as principles rather than rules. Real rest does not happen by accident: it needs a plan, firm boundaries, and a deliberate start and finish. After six sessions of work, this is a season of rest, and we are invited to take part in it rather than simply hear about it.
{: .guide-summary}

*Sit together in silence for one minute before the closing question.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, what is one thing you will set down during this Sabbath season?

</div>
