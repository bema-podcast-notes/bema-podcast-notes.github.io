### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** The four horses came from Zechariah and carried the real costs of Roman rule, and the martyrs under the altar were people the first readers knew by name. The 144,000 and the countless multitude pictured God's complete people, with no one missing.

**This week:** The seventh seal opens onto seven trumpets. The episode teaches us to count like a first-century reader and then steps back to show that the whole opening of Revelation follows the shape of the ancient games' opening ceremony.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode starts with a definition: "Apocalyptic literature uses symbols and images to bring and communicate a message of hope to the audience's present day."[^1] It then reads the trumpets through their numbers. Seven speaks of completion and also of finiteness, so the suffering has an end; the repeated "thirds" picture shalom, or wholeness, being torn apart; and the enormous enemy army expresses how outnumbered believers felt.[^2] The plagues of Exodus and the locust army of Joel stand behind the imagery.[^3]

The second half introduces the episode's big proposal. John structures Revelation 1–9 to mirror the seven-step opening ceremony of the games, from the presentation of the emperor to the sounding of the trumpets.[^4] At the games, athletes competed to show the greatness of the god or city they represented. John's point is that how believers run their race under persecution shows the watching world what their God is like.[^5]

### Counting Like a First-Century Reader <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#counting-like-a-first-century-reader}

*The trumpet judgments are full of numbers, and the episode insists they describe experience rather than measurements.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Revelation 8:1–13 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Count how many times "a third" appears in verses 7–12, and list what is affected each time. Brent noticed that "a third of the sun was struck" followed by "a third of the day was without light" does not work scientifically, and the notes agree: these images express "the experience of chaos and darkness, not astronomical predictions."[^6] What picture of the world emerges when you hear the list as an experience rather than a forecast?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Marty refines BEMA's earlier description of three as "community" into the broader idea of shalom. In Jewish thought, the notes explain, relationship exists on three levels: with God, with other humans and with creation. A "third" being struck pictures "the systematic disruption of shalom," order turning to chaos and community pulled apart.[^7] Seven, meanwhile, communicates completion and also finiteness: the suffering "will end."[^8] Which of these two ideas do you think would have meant more to believers under persecution, and why?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes read the army of two hundred million in Revelation 9:16 as qualitative, not quantitative: it expresses how vastly outnumbered the first audience felt.[^9] When have you felt outnumbered for doing what you believed was right, and what helped you keep going?

</div>

### The Opening Ceremony <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-opening-ceremony}

*The episode's central claim is that Revelation's first nine chapters follow a pattern the first readers would have recognized from the arena.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Revelation 4:1–4 and 6:1–2 aloud as reminders of what we have already read.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes lay out seven steps in the opening ceremony of the games and match each to Revelation:[^10]

- The emperor is presented to acclaim; God is presented as Alpha and Omega (Revelation 1).
- A herald announces Caesar's mighty deeds; John hears a voice telling him to write (Revelation 1).
- Caesar addresses cities: "I've heard your positive traits, but I have this against you"; the seven letters (Revelation 2–3).
- Twenty-four priests lead a crowd in white robes in praise; twenty-four elders and the white-robed crowd (Revelation 4 and 7).
- A herald reads a scroll to open the games; the Lamb opens the sealed scroll (Revelation 5).
- Chariot races with horses in consistent colors; the four horsemen (Revelation 6).
- Trumpets signal the competition; the seven trumpets (Revelation 8–9).

Marty acknowledges he takes some liberty in reconstructing the ceremony.[^11] Looking back over the weeks we have spent in Revelation, which parallel strikes you as most convincing, and which would you want to test further?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** In the Roman world, the notes explain, an athlete's victory at the games proved the power of the god his city honored; individual talent was secondary to what the win said about the god.[^12] John's message, in the notes' words, is that "how you conduct yourselves... that is how you show the watching world what your God is like."[^13] What does this framework suggest about why John's readers were not in "a random, meaningless struggle"?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> If the people who watched your life most closely judged God only by how you handled your hardest week this year, what would they conclude about him?

</div>

### Running the Race <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#running-the-race}

*A familiar passage from Hebrews sounds different once the arena is in view.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Hebrews 12:1–3 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes say this passage "is not generic athletic metaphor but specific Olympic imagery." The cloud of witnesses are spectators in the arena, the race is the great cosmic competition, and Jesus is both the pioneer who ran before us and the perfecter who will bring our race to completion.[^14] We studied Hebrews earlier in this session. How does hearing these verses next to Revelation 8–9 change what "run with perseverance" means to you?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q8:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes list what a modern "race" might involve: maintaining integrity in corrupt systems, showing love to enemies, enduring illness or loss with hope, serving others when it costs us, and speaking truth when it is unpopular.[^15] Which of these is the race you are running right now, and what would running it well look like this week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> The trumpets sound over a world where shalom is being torn into thirds and God's people feel hopelessly outnumbered. Yet the number seven promises that this season has an end. John frames it all as the opening of the games, where every athlete ran to show the greatness of a god. His readers are in that arena, surrounded by a cloud of witnesses, and the way they run, with faithfulness, endurance and love, is how the world will see what their God is like.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one way you hope people will see what God is like through how you run this coming week?

</div>
