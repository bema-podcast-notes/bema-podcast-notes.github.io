### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Dieula Previlon taught us that lament is an act of faith, that safety comes before storytelling, and that God, like a good parent, will "connect first, lesson later."

**This week:** A two-hour live anniversary episode looks back on ten years, remembers those who have died, and asks what faithful stewardship looks like for the next ten.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This live episode, recorded in Boise in front of more than a hundred listeners, is part celebration, part reflection and part memorial[^1]. It is long, so this guide follows three of its strongest threads. Marty gives three words for the decade (grateful, honored, determined) and reads the parable of the faithful steward. Elle shares the BEMA lessons that changed her life, and Reed reads an essay about refusing to set down his pack at a church door on the Camino. The evening closes with Hebrews 11–12, the "cloud of witnesses," as Marty remembers his late father and his late friend and colleague Josh, and offers a list of what he believes Josh would say about the next decade.

Session 10 began in a season of mourning, and this episode returns to that grief. Every personal question tonight is an invitation, never a requirement. You are always free to pass.

**Opening Question:** Marty ends each quarterly board report with three words describing where he is[^2]. Think of something you have been part of for a long time: a job, a friendship, a group, a practice. What one word describes where you are with it right now, and why that word?

### Grateful, Honored, Determined <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#grateful-honored-determined}

*Marty frames ten years of BEMA as a gift to be stewarded rather than an achievement to admire.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 24:45–51 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Marty's third word, "determined," comes from this parable: he wants BEMA to steward well what God has given it, which he names as the biblical text, theology, practice, and being a "first word" rather than the last word[^3]. Look back at the passage. What is the faithful servant doing when the master returns, and what assumption leads the wicked servant astray?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Asked what makes him cringe in the 2016 episodes, Marty named jokes that now seem demeaning and statements made with "way more confidence" than they deserved. His principle is that we should *hope* our future selves know more and believe differently, and that God uses us at every stage[^4]. How would it change the way you hold what you are learning in this group if you thought of it as a "first word"?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** When listeners thank him, Marty follows Ray Vander Laan's habit of answering "Bless God," pointing the credit back to God, who is the one at work[^5]. The notes give the example of a manager who inherits a successful team and can either coast or keep investing[^6]. What good thing has been entrusted to you right now that is at risk of drifting because it feels like "the master is delayed"? What would tending it look like this month?

</div>

### The Burdens We Carry <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-burdens-we-carry}

*Elle and Reed tell stories about what we let others take from us, what we choose to carry, and what we refuse to set down.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Deuteronomy 19:14 and Proverbs 22:28 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** In the ancient world, boundary stones marked the land God had given a family, and moving a neighbor's stone was theft. Standing in a stone amphitheater, Marty told Elle's group that "some of us have allowed our boundary stones to be moved farther than they should be." For Elle, that meant realizing she had accepted teaching that women could not teach or lead, which shrank the calling God gave her[^7]. Our traditions hold different convictions about women's roles, so set aside for a moment where you land on that question. From these two verses, what does a boundary stone protect, and who is doing the moving? What kinds of voices or experiences can move a person's stones inward?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> From the Samson story (Judges 15), Elle learned that forgiveness is "a burden we choose to take on." When someone wrongs us and we forgive, the debt does not disappear; we absorb it. When we die to ourselves this way, God meets us, takes the burden up, and brings us back to life. Elle says this lesson kept her from becoming "a bitter and lonely person"[^8]. How does describing forgiveness as absorbing a cost compare with other ways you have heard forgiveness taught? Share only what you are comfortable sharing.

*Read Deuteronomy 6:4–5 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The Shema commands loving God with all your heart, soul and *me'od*, a word that literally means "very." Elle hears it as loving God "with all your very-ness": bringing everything that makes you you. She once believed she had to edit herself to fit ministry spaces, and is learning that the particular way God made each person is intended, and enough[^9]. What part of yourself are you most tempted to edit out when you are with other believers?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** On the Camino, Reed's backpack quickly became "a home." At a church door where packs had to be left outside, others dropped theirs and walked in. Reed refused, guarding his "dirty laundry, earplugs, identification" from imagined thieves "like Gollum with his ring," like the older brother outside the prodigal son's party. He later wrote, "my judgment was self-imposed. How could I ever again speak of God excluding anyone?" Music drew him in barefoot, and a friend whispered, "I'm glad you're here"[^10]. The notes suggest people often guard resentments, reputations or control in the same way[^11]. What "pack" do you find yourself guarding at the door, and what do you think it is costing you?

</div>

### The Cloud of Witnesses <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-cloud-of-witnesses}

*The evening's emotional center: those who went before us, including people we have loved and lost, are made complete only together with us.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Hebrews 11:39–12:3 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Marty teaches this passage in the stadium at Aphrodisias, picturing the stands full of witnesses, from the patriarchs to unnamed believers, cheering the current runners. He notes that everyone in the Hall of Faith has a messy, human story[^12]. Look closely at 11:39–40. What does the writer say about those who came before, and what does it mean that they are "made perfect" only together with us? How does that change the way the runners in 12:1–3 run?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Following Ray Vander Laan, Marty invites people to picture in the stands those *they personally knew*. For years he taught the lesson without anyone specific in his own stands. This year, after losing his father and then Josh a month later, he pictured them "sitting just right to my left"[^13]. If you would like, describe someone (living or gone) whose faith you picture cheering you on, or simply what it is like to imagine those stands. Passing is always welcome here.

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** Drawing on Josh's final teaching series on Ecclesiastes, Marty offered a list of what he believes Josh would say about the next decade: presence with real people over celebrity, growth over comfort, joy over leisure, left-handed testimony over right-handed power, the humility of Christ over selfish ambition, listening and loving over likes and shares, what we give over what we are owed, the affirmation of Sabbath over a flurry of accomplishment, and trusting the story over the security of certainty[^14]. Which one pair names a real pull in your own life right now? What would choosing the second half look like this week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Ten years of BEMA turn out to be a story about people: their friendships, their burdens and their losses. Marty calls the decade a gift to steward, not an achievement to admire. Elle and Reed remind us that we can choose which burdens to carry, refuse to let our boundary stones be moved, and set down the packs we guard at the door. Hebrews tells us we run surrounded by witnesses whose faith is completed only together with ours. That gives our ordinary faithfulness this week more weight than we might think.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room, finish this: "For the next season, I want to choose ___ over ___."

</div>

#### Looking Ahead {#looking-ahead}

**[Ep. 513 — Women in Joshua & Judges: Introduction](/guides/513)** (54:11): Elle Grover Fricks opens a new series on lesser-known women in Joshua and Judges, such as Rahab. First she takes on the "elephant in the room," the conquest of Canaan, by reading Exodus 23 and Joshua 5–7 closely and studying the Hebrew word *ḥerem*, usually translated "destroy." It is a careful look at one of the hardest parts of Scripture, and it sets up every story to come.

**What to bring next week:** Read Exodus 23:20–33 and make two short lists: what God says God will do, and what Israel is told to do. If you have time, read Joshua 6:15–21 and note the words your translation uses for what happens to the city.
