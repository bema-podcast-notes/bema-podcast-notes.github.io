### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** We saw Paul tell his Damascus road story in the words of Ezekiel, Jeremiah, Isaiah and Jonah, presenting it as a prophet's calling rather than a change of religion.[^1]

**This week:** We step back from Acts to see the whole biblical story and ask where Acts, and the letters still ahead of us, fit inside it.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This episode closes the study of Acts by placing it in the Bible's larger story. Marty describes Scripture's structure in literary terms: a preface (Genesis 1–11), an introduction (Genesis 12–50), a long narrative arc (Exodus through the Gospels), and Acts as the "epilogue." The thread running through it all is a "tale of two kingdoms," Empire versus Shalom. Jesus is the climax, "the Author of the narrative jumping directly into the story," and Acts shows a Spirit-empowered community carrying the restoration forward while the story stays open.[^2]

The episode also prepares us for the letters. Marty calls them "inspired midrash": authoritative applications of the same story to very different places and problems, not a systematic theology. He warns that modern readers sometimes make "Paul our Lord and Jesus our Savior."[^3] The notes name few specific verses, so this guide gives time to reading the end of Acts and to mapping the story together.

### The Shape of the Whole Story <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-shape-of-the-whole-story}

*We map the Bible as one story with a preface, an introduction, a narrative arc and an epilogue.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes lay out Scripture's structure this way: preface (Genesis 1–11), introduction (Genesis 12–50), narrative arc (Exodus through the Gospels), and epilogue (Acts).[^4] If you brought a short summary of the Bible's story, share it now. Then, as a group, name one or two familiar stories that belong in each part. What do you notice when they are laid out in this order?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The episode calls the central theme a "tale of two kingdoms": Empire, meaning "systems of domination, oppression, and self-serving power," and Shalom, "God's vision of flourishing, justice, wholeness, and restoration."[^5] Pick one story from your map. Where do you see Empire and Shalom pulling against each other in it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes suggest that personal formation benefits from "seeing one's life as part of the continuing narrative rather than isolated from biblical history."[^6] Where did you see Empire and Shalom competing in your own week, at work, at home, or in your own heart?

</div>

### An Epilogue Left Open <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#an-epilogue-left-open}

*We read how Luke ends Acts and ask what kind of ending it is.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Acts 28:23–31 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes say Acts shows "the potential for restoration through Spirit-empowered community, while showing the story remains unfinished."[^7] What do you notice about how Luke ends his book? What questions does he leave unanswered?

*Read John 14:12 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Marty describes Jesus as "the living expression of the complete realized potential of humanity" and "the perfect example of the kind of man Abraham was on his best days." The notes add that the Spirit empowers the early church to do "greater works" than Jesus, continuing the restoration mission.[^8] How do those two claims fit together? If Jesus is the climax, what does it mean for the community after him to do "greater works"?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> According to the notes, the biblical story "uniquely leaves the ending 'open' for readers to enter the story as active participants."[^9] If the epilogue is still being written, what part of it feels like yours to carry right now?

</div>

### Reading the Letters as Inspired Midrash <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#reading-the-letters-as-inspired-midrash}

*We look ahead to the letters and ask how to read them well.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes describe the letters as "inspired midrash," that is, "authoritative applications of the same narrative to different specific contexts." Paul writes differently to Corinth than to Ephesus "not because the gospel changes, but because the contexts require different applications of the same truth."[^10] Why might reading a letter this way resolve some of Paul's apparent contradictions? What does it ask of us before we apply a verse?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The episode lists ways modern readers misplace their lenses, including over-systematizing Paul's letters into universal doctrine and making "Paul our Lord and Jesus our Savior" by preferring Paul's clearer commands over Jesus's harder parables.[^11] Each of our traditions has passages it reaches for most often when deciding how church should work. Without judging anyone's tradition, which passages does yours lean on, and what would it look like to read them with Jesus at the center?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes suggest that churches in different settings "need different applications of the same gospel."[^12] Think of your own setting (your town, your congregation, your household). What would a faithful application of the same story look like there that might look different somewhere else?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> The Bible is one story: a preface, an introduction, a long struggle between Empire and Shalom, and a climax when the Author enters the story in Jesus. Acts is the epilogue, and Luke ends it with the door still open. The letters ahead of us are that same story applied to real communities with real problems, so we will need to ask about each place before we ask what each letter means for us. The ending is still being written, and we are invited to step into it.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: if your life is part of the epilogue, what is one way you hope to live Shalom this week?

</div>
