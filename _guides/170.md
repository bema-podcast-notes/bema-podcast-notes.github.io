### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** 2 Peter warned believers against escaping pressure by blending in. The false teachers were selling wrong practice, not wrong doctrine, and Balaam's pattern of idolatry, greed, and immorality still names the pressure points of ordinary life.

**This week:** We begin John's three letters. John also cares about how people live, and he puts love at the very center of what counts as true.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode places 1 John among the churches of Asia Minor, where John served as pastor, and notes that it likely answers Docetic teachers who said Jesus never truly became flesh. John borrows language familiar from the Essenes and the Dead Sea Scrolls, such as walking in light and walking in darkness, and he applies the rabbi-disciple pattern: whoever claims to live in Jesus must walk as Jesus walked.

The headline of the episode is a challenge to the familiar idea of "balancing love and truth." The hosts argue that for John love is not truth's equal partner but the thing that makes something true: if it lacks love, it cannot be true, however accurate it sounds. John also says no one can love an unseen God while refusing to love the brother or sister they can see. This is a short episode with a big claim, so we will give plenty of time to the text.

### Light, Darkness, and Flesh <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#light-darkness-and-flesh}

*John opens his letter with the senses and with an old Jewish picture of two ways to walk.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 1 John 1:1–7 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes explain that Docetism was "a Gnostic belief that Jesus never truly became human flesh," and that John responds with "multiple statements in 1 John" emphasizing Jesus' physical incarnation and tangible reality.[^1] What senses does John mention in the opening verses? Why would that matter so much to a church hearing that Jesus only seemed to be human?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode notes that "Sons of Light vs. Sons of Darkness" are binary categories found throughout the Dead Sea Scrolls, where walking in light means obedience to God's ways. John's background may have exposed him to Essene thought through Jesus and possibly John the Baptist.[^2] How does it help to hear John's light and darkness language as a Jewish way of describing how a person lives, rather than only how they feel or think?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** John's letter insists that faith is lived in a real body, in real fellowship. What is one ordinary, physical way (meals, presence, touch, time) that you could walk in the light with someone this week?

</div>

### Walk as Jesus Walked <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#walk-as-jesus-walked}

*John uses the rabbi-disciple relationship to ask what defined Jesus' own way of walking.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 1 John 2:3–11 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes explain that in Jewish education "disciples were expected to become exactly like their rabbi in every way," not only learning teachings but replicating the rabbi's whole approach to life. John applies this directly: "Whoever claims to live in Him must live as Jesus did."[^3] What do you notice in this passage about what walking as Jesus walked looks like? What does John contrast it with?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The episode's central question is, "If Christians must walk as Jesus walked, what defined Jesus' walk?"[^4] The notes answer that it was love, connecting John to Jesus' greatest commandments and his teaching on good trees and good fruit.[^5] If someone followed you around for a week the way a disciple followed a rabbi, what would they conclude defined your walk?

*Read 1 Corinthians 13:1–3 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes connect John to Paul's "clanging gong," where even perfect theological understanding is worthless without love; "Both apostles conclude that love validates all other spiritual qualities."[^6] What do you notice when you put John and Paul side by side? Why might two very different apostles land in the same place?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** John says that anyone who claims to be in the light but hates a brother or sister is still in the darkness. Without naming anyone, who is the person in your life it would be hardest to "walk as Jesus walked" toward right now? What is one step toward them you could take?

</div>

### Love Makes It True <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#love-makes-it-true}

*The episode argues that John reverses the way many of us were taught to think about love and truth.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 1 John 4:7–21 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes highlight John's statement: "Whoever does not love their brother and sister, whom they have seen, cannot love God, whom they have not seen." They call this a "logical impossibility rather than merely difficult," with no "learning to love" exception.[^7] Read the passage again. Where does John use words like "liar" and "cannot"? What do those strong words tell you about how seriously he takes this?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** The hosts argue that the common call to balance love and truth "is backwards according to John's theology." Their claim is directional: "If something lacks love, it cannot be true, regardless of its factual accuracy," and the Bible never states the reverse.[^8] Our traditions all care deeply about getting doctrine right. What does this claim protect that matters? What does it risk? How would it change the way your church handles disagreement?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode describes a "Cosmic Policeman Mentality," feeling responsible to correct error and protect divine truth, and offers John's alternative: "Focus on love, trusting that God can defend Himself."[^9] Where have you felt the pull to be God's policeman? What would trusting God to defend himself free you to do?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q11:** Think of one conversation you expect to have soon where you care about being right. What would it look like to let love lead that conversation, trusting that love is what will make it true?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> John writes to churches where some taught that Jesus never really became flesh, and he answers with what was seen, heard, and touched. He calls disciples to walk as their rabbi walked, and he says plainly what defined that walk: love. In the episode's reading, love is not truth's rival or its equal partner; it is what makes truth true. And no one can love the God they cannot see while refusing to love the person right in front of them.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each: who is the brother or sister you can see that God is asking you to love this week?

</div>
