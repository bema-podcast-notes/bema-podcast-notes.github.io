### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** The fourth day's lights, the ever-burning lamp and Aaron's garments all taught without words, and Marty named the way we live, the things we do, the postures we take, the energy we give and the way we stop as priestly work.

**This week:** The fifth day fills the seas and skies with creatures that each receive a place, and Exodus 29 prepares a priesthood and an altar so that God can make a place among his people.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

Like the reflections before it, this episode is a meditation rather than a lecture. Brent reads Genesis 1:20–23 again and again, each time followed by a section of Exodus 29, and Marty offers a question followed by long silence. Exodus 29 is easy to skip: washing, garments, blood, fire and daily lambs. The reflection shows that every step of it serves one goal, a place where God and people live together.

The thread running through the episode is two postures toward sin. Condemnation focuses on guilt, exclusion and punishment. Presence takes sin just as seriously, but deals with it so that a table, a priesthood or a community can become holy and God can dwell there. Along the way, blood on Aaron's ear, thumb and toe raises three questions about who we listen to, what we hold on to and where we go, and the *tamid* (the continual morning and evening offering) pictures repentance as a daily rhythm rather than a single event.

### Ear, Thumb and Toe <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#ear-thumb-and-toe}

*The fifth day gives every creature a place, and Exodus 29 begins preparing people to serve in God's place.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:20–23 aloud, then sit together in silence for one minute.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Exodus 29:1–21 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The episode pairs the fifth day, when every creature receives its place and is blessed to "be fruitful and increase" there, with the ordination of Aaron and his sons[^1]. As you listened to Exodus 29:1–21, what did you notice being done to the people, and what was done with the blood? Share one detail that surprised you or that you had never noticed before.

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes connect the three places the blood was put to the whole person: the ear stands for what we hear and obey (in Hebrew, *shema* means both "hear" and "obey"), the thumb for what we grasp and hold on to, and the big toe for where we walk[^2]. Why might the text mark consecration on these three parts of the body? What does it suggest about how much of a life is meant to be set apart?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Marty's first reflection question asks: "How would you approach sin, yours or others', if the purpose is to make your table or your community holy?"[^3] The notes contrast condemnation (guilt, exclusion, punishment) with presence, which deals with sin seriously but so that people and places can be made holy[^4]. Our traditions have handled wrongdoing in different ways, from church discipline to confession to accountability relationships, and each grew out of real convictions. Without ranking them, where have you seen a family or church deal with sin in a way that aimed at restoring presence? And when you face your own sin, which posture tends to show up first?

</div>

### Morning, Evening, and a God Who Moves In <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#morning-evening-and-a-god-who-moves-in}

*Exodus 29 ends at a shared meal, a daily rhythm and a promise that God will live among his people.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Exodus 29:31–46 aloud, then sit together in silence for one minute.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** After the sacrifices, Aaron and his sons eat the ordination meat and bread at the entrance to the tent of meeting; the notes put it simply: "Atonement ends at a table." Ordination then takes seven days, and the *tamid* (Hebrew for "continually" or "regularly") calls for one lamb every morning and one at twilight[^5]. What changes when repentance is pictured as something done morning and evening, and ending at a table, rather than once and for all?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> In Exodus 29:46 God says he brought Israel out of Egypt "so that I might dwell among them." The notes draw the conclusion that the Exodus itself served presence[^6]. Look back over verses 42–46. What do the verbs God uses about himself tell you about what the whole sacrificial system is for?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** Marty asks whether the gospel we communicate ends "with simple presence: God dwelling among us, Sabbath, or the like." The notes give an example: instead of ending with "your sins are forgiven so you can go to heaven," consider going further to "God wants to live with you, here and now"[^7]. Each of us learned to tell the good news in a particular way from our own tradition. Where does your version usually end? What would shift in how you talk about faith if it ended in presence?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Marty's third question asks: "Where would you, where could you, where should you place a rhythm of repentance and change in your daily life?"[^8] The notes suggest a short morning prayer and an evening examen (a review of the day like the one Ignatius of Loyola taught) as a modern *tamid*[^9]. What is one specific time and place in your ordinary day where a small rhythm like that could actually live?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#closing}

> Exodus 29 is full of blood and fire, but every step points toward meeting, eating together and God's dwelling. The fifth day gave every creature a place to live, and the Tabernacle was prepared so that God could have a place among his people. Sin is dealt with seriously, but for the sake of presence rather than condemnation. The ear, thumb and toe ask who we listen to, what we hold on to and where we go, and the morning and evening lambs remind us that this is daily work. Marty's challenge is modest on purpose: if we accomplish it, "it would be enough and it would be significant."[^10]
{: .guide-summary}

*Sit together in silence for one minute before the closing question.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** Going around the room in one sentence each, with no comments until everyone has spoken: which of the three, ear, thumb or toe, will you look at most honestly this week?

</div>
