### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Akhsah, offered as a prize and married off without being asked or blessed, rode to her father and asked plainly for springs of water. Caleb answered with a double portion and no lecture, a picture of generous, non-defensive leadership.[^1]

**This week:** The Women in Joshua & Judges series pauses for a behind-the-scenes conversation about how Marty Solomon and Reed Dent wrote *The Gospel of Being Human*, and why careful critique works like "iron sharpening iron."[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This episode is not a teaching episode on a biblical text. Marty has already given many interviews about what the book says, so Brent asks him and Reed how it came to be: their very different writing habits, the book's start in a driveway reading of Barbara Brown Taylor's *An Altar in the World*, how two authors kept one voice, and the role of their editor, Caitlin Carlson.[^3]

The heart of the conversation is the heavy revision that theological writing goes through, especially the chapters on Eve and the "Romans Road." Reed describes the long review threads as "iron sharpening iron," with no bad blood, only people checking whether the claims were true.[^4] The episode also touches on who the book is for, how it got its title, and a tribute to Wendell Berry's call to put down roots.[^5] Since many of us have learned or used the Romans Road, this week is a chance to look at a familiar summary with gratitude and curiosity at the same time.

### How a Book Becomes a Book <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#how-a-book-becomes-a-book}

*Two authors with opposite habits turned a friendship and a shared idea into a book.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Reed writes in bits spread across his normal week, much like writing sermons, in his woodworking shop, at home or at a local copy shop. Marty goes away for about eight days of "sleep, eat, write" and drafts a whole chapter in one sitting. The notes' main idea is that no single method is right; what matters is knowing how you work best.[^6] Which approach is closer to how you do your best focused or creative work, whether at a job, at home, or in prayer and study? What does that way of working need in order to happen?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The book started when Marty and Reed read *An Altar in the World* aloud together, then took a walk talking about curiosity, attention and wonder. Reed calls reading aloud "almost a sacred spiritual act," and Marty fell back in love with the book while recording the audiobook.[^7] What happens for you when a text is read aloud in a group rather than silently alone? What might this group gain by reading aloud more often?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Marty says revision "sucks the life out of the thing," leaving it over-dissected. Yet after reading the finished book aloud he called revision "this beautiful part of the thing becoming a thing."[^8] The authors also had to cut about 15,000 words, including material both of them loved.[^9] Where in your life is something being reworked or cut back right now (a project, a plan, a relationship, a belief)? What helps you stay with it until it becomes what it is meant to be? Share only what you are comfortable sharing.

</div>

### Iron Sharpening Iron <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#iron-sharpening-iron}

*Theological claims drew far more scrutiny than method, and the authors welcomed it.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Proverbs 27:17 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Brent used this proverb to describe the long comment thread on the Eve chapter, and Reed agreed it was the right picture: people with something at stake asking, "Is this legit? Is this actually true?"[^10] Look closely at the image in the verse. What does it suggest about what has to happen between two people for sharpening to take place, and what would turn it into something other than sharpening?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Their first book, on Jewish hermeneutics (ways of reading the Bible), got through review easily, while this one drew much more scrutiny. Reed's response: "What did you think a hermeneutic was for?" Once you learn to read the Bible differently, theological questions follow.[^11] Since you started reading the Bible in the way BEMA encourages, what theological questions have followed that you didn't expect?

*Read Romans 3:23; 6:23; 5:8; and 10:9–10 aloud, in that order.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** These are verses commonly strung together as the "Romans Road," a step-by-step way of sharing the gospel.[^12] Marty says he is not trying to "undo every truth" it points to, but he wanted statements with "just enough teeth" to make readers stop and think again; remove all the teeth and a statement becomes "safe" but no longer says anything.[^13] Reed calls the Romans Road chapter "a package of assumptions," and Brent says Romans is reduced to "six simple steps" when it is "a deeper and richer thing."[^14] Many of us came to faith through, or have shared, a summary like this one. What has it given you or people you know? Hearing the verses read in a row, what questions arise about what sits between them in the letter?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Reed says there was no bad blood in the long theological threads, only people checking whether the claims were true; disagreement in good faith sharpened the ideas.[^15] Think of one person whose honest pushback has sharpened you. What made it feel like sharpening rather than an attack, and how might you offer that kind of pushback to someone else this month?

</div>

### Featherless Bipeds and the Doorway <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#featherless-bipeds-and-the-doorway}

*The authors describe who the book is for and what it hopes readers will notice.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Asked about his audience, Rob Bell once answered, "humans, featherless bipeds," and Marty thinks of that line whenever the question comes up. Marty pictures someone holding a Bible and sensing "there's so much more going on here," standing at a "doorway" between something bigger and their everyday life. He wants to tell them: "Don't look away, don't run away, don't give up. It is here."[^16] Reed, for his part, writes "something universal, something human."[^17] When have you had that sense of a doorway, whether while reading Scripture or somewhere else?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode closes with a tribute to Wendell Berry, whose advice was to "stop some place and stay there, learn the history of it and the names of the plants and animals and the neighbors." The notes point to Reed's contented life in Kirksville, Missouri, and to thriving local book-tour gatherings hosted by churches.[^18] What would it look like for you, or for this group, to be more deeply rooted in the place where you live?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** Marty's words to the person at the doorway were "Don't look away, don't run away, don't give up."[^19] Some seasons make that doorway feel close and others make it feel shut, and both are honest. Where in your ordinary week could you slow down and pay attention long enough to notice it? What tends to make you look away?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> This conversation showed the human side of making a book about being human. Reed writes in bursts woven through ordinary life, Marty writes in solitary marathons, and together they turned a driveway reading and a walk into a book. The hardest chapters, on Adam, Eve and the Romans Road, needed both reverence and teeth, and both authors saw the scrutiny as iron sharpening iron rather than conflict. Honest questions, careful critique and community make our understanding of God and ourselves richer.[^20]
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, name one place this week where you will either offer or welcome "iron sharpening iron."

</div>

#### Looking Ahead {#looking-ahead}

**[Ep. 518 — Women in Joshua & Judges — Deborah](/guides/518)** (56:46): The series resumes as Elle Grover Fricks, with Brent and Reed, walks through Judges 4:1–16 in her own translation, which renders the meaning of every Hebrew name. Read that way, "Unity" is dead, Israel is held down by a king called "Understanding," Deborah ("Honeybee") summons Barak ("Lightning"), and her mysterious title "woman of torches" comes into focus. The hosts also reconsider whether Barak deserves his reputation as a coward.[^21]

**What to bring next week:** Read Judges 4:1–16 and list every personal and place name you find. If you can, look up the meaning of one or two of them with a lexicon or a free tool like Blue Letter Bible, and notice whether the meaning changes how you read the verse.
