### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Peter stepped out onto the water like a true disciple, and a toddler learning to walk up a slope showed us a faith that falls, giggles, and gets back up, secure in a Father who is watching with delight.

**This week:** Two encounters in Matthew 15: a dispute with Pharisees from Jerusalem about handwashing and tradition, and a Canaanite woman who will not take no for an answer.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The first half of the episode explains *halakha*, the developing body of rulings about the way one "walks out" faith in daily life, and the practice of building a "fence around Torah" with extra rules so that no one comes close to breaking a commandment. The notes are clear that this protective system had good intentions. Jesus' critique comes where a tradition ends up cancelling the command it was meant to protect, as in his example of Corban. Dr. Gambino shows that the verses Jesus quotes from Exodus 21 sit in a section all about how people are treated.

The second half takes Jesus to the region of Tyre and Sidon, near Zarephath, where Elijah once stayed with a Gentile widow. A Canaanite woman presses Jesus three times, and her answer about crumbs from the master's table draws on that very story. The episode suggests this may be a moment when Jesus' understanding of his mission grew, a claim our traditions may hold in different ways.

### The Heart of Torah <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#the-heart-of-torah}

*An argument about handwashing becomes a question about what Torah is really for.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 15:1–9 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The episode explains that *halakha* (from the Hebrew word for "walk") was being formed in Jesus' day through rabbinic rulings, and that the Pharisees and teachers of the law were building a "fence around Torah," extra rules meant to keep people from even coming close to breaking a commandment.[^1] What exactly is the complaint brought to Jesus? What example does Jesus give in response, and how does the Corban practice work in his description?

*Read Exodus 21:12–17 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Dr. Gambino describes the laws that follow the Ten Commandments in Exodus 21–23 as commentary that unpacks those commandments through real situations. The two verses Jesus quotes about parents (21:15, 17) surround a law about kidnapping (21:16), which in Hebrew thought means robbing someone of their essence, dignity and identity.[^2] What do you notice about the neighborhood of the verses Jesus quotes? What does it suggest Torah is mostly concerned with?

*Read Matthew 15:10–20 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Jesus locates defilement in the heart, and the notes point out that every item on his list concerns how we treat other people, not ritual purity.[^3] The notes then turn the lens on us: church procedures, membership requirements or theological gatekeeping can sometimes work like Corban, "technically 'righteous'" by their own logic while missing the heart of loving God and neighbor.[^4] Every tradition, including those in this room, builds fences. Name one fence in your own tradition that protects something good. What would it take for that fence to start cancelling the thing it protects, and how would you notice?

</div>

### Crumbs from the Master's Table <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#crumbs-from-the-masters-table}

*In the land of Elijah's widow, an outsider shows she knows Israel's story.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 15:21–28 aloud, then read 1 Kings 17:7–16 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes point out that Matthew calls her a "Canaanite woman" (Mark uses "Syrophoenician") to mark her as the ultimate outsider, since Canaanites were Israel's archetypal enemies.[^5] Trace the exchange: what are Jesus' three responses to her, and what does she do after each one?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The episode places this encounter in the region of Tyre and Sidon, near Zarephath, where Elijah (whom Jewish tradition regards as the first "master" or rabbi) stayed with a Gentile widow during a famine. Her reply about dogs eating crumbs from their master's table, the notes say, recalls that widow, who survived on crumbs, a "starvation ration," through God's provision.[^6] Using the list you brought, what details link the two stories? What is the woman claiming when she points back to Elijah's widow?

*Read Philippians 2:5–8 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode suggests that Jesus, while fully divine, lived primarily in his human nature during his ministry, citing Philippians 2 (he emptied himself) and Hebrews (he learned obedience). On this view, the encounter may be a moment when Jesus' understanding of his mission expanded to include Gentiles directly.[^7] Christian traditions describe how Jesus' divinity and humanity relate in different ways. How were you taught to think about Jesus learning or growing? What in these two passages do you find yourself weighing?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes describe her *chutzpah* (bold audacity and persistence) and suggest that her great faith combined persistence, knowledge of Scripture, and recognition of God's character across ethnic lines.[^8] They apply this to recognizing real faith in people "outside traditional church structures, different denominations, or even other religious backgrounds."[^9] Who has surprised you with faith from a place you did not expect it? How might you honor what you saw in them this week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#closing}

> Jesus does not dismiss tradition; he asks what it is for. When a fence around Torah ends up cancelling the command to honor father and mother, the heart of Torah has been missed, and that heart is how we treat people. Then, in the land where Elijah fed a Gentile widow, a Canaanite woman reminds Jesus of that very story and asks for her crumbs. Her persistence and her knowledge of Israel's Scriptures astonish Jesus, and he calls her faith great. The episode invites us to hold our traditions with care and to watch for faith outside our expected boundaries.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one tradition you want to hold more loosely, or one outsider you want to listen to more closely?

</div>
