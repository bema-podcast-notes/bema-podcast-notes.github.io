### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Psalm 137 gave us words for communal grief and even for hate, and the hosts argued that praying our hate honestly may be a first step on the long road toward "Father, forgive them."[^1]

**This week:** The Psalms series pauses for an interview with Derek Vreeland, discipleship pastor and author of *Centering Jesus*, about what changes when the slain Lamb sits at the center of Christian life.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

Derek's book grew out of his devotional reading of Revelation 7:17 during the unsettled fall of 2020, when he found the picture of the Lamb "at the center of the throne" shepherding a multitude from every nation. From there the conversation ranges widely: Christianity as a way of living rather than a compartment of life, Christian masculinity, nationalism as idolatry, the "spiritual but not religious" trend, and Paul's use of the word "spiritual."[^3]

Derek also describes faith as a square with four connected sides: belief, confidence, trust and allegiance. Throughout, he stresses that the Christian life is above all a way of living shaped by the cross and resurrection.[^4] Some of the episode touches politics and gender. The questions below are meant to help each of us examine our own loyalties, whatever our tradition or political leanings, not to argue about anyone else's.

### Opening <span class="guide-time">10 min</span> {#opening}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-opening" markdown="1">

**Opening Question:** Derek's book began when a phrase from his morning reading "stuck" with him all day.[^5] Think of a phrase, from Scripture, a song or a conversation, that stayed with you for a whole day. What was it, and why do you think it stuck?

### The Lamb at the Center <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#the-lamb-at-the-center}

*Revelation 7:17 places a slain Lamb, not a lion or a warrior, at the center of God's throne.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Revelation 7:9–17 aloud. Then read Revelation 5:5–6 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Derek was reading the NRSV in 2020, a season he describes as having "rage in the air." In Revelation 7:17 he found what he thought the church needed: "a fresh imagination of Jesus as the Lamb of God" at the very center of Christian life. The notes ask how John redefines power by showing a slain Lamb where the reader expects a Lion.[^6] What do you notice about who is gathered in Revelation 7, where the Lamb is, and what the Lamb does? What surprises you about the move from Lion to Lamb in Revelation 5?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Derek says theology and formation are necessary but are not the heart of Christian experience. The heart is "a way of living shaped by the cross and resurrection." He calls compartmentalization a real malady: faith becomes one "cabinet" among many, and politics, hobbies and ideologies in the other cabinets end up shaping our worship.[^7] If someone watched your ordinary week, which compartments would they see? Where does your faith most easily get kept in its own cabinet?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> When people tell Derek, "I don't interpret the Bible, I just take it the way it is," he asks why they didn't greet him with a holy kiss, as Romans 16:16 instructs. Their shift to a "holy handshake" shows that everyone interprets Scripture culturally.[^8] Where have you noticed your own tradition interpreting a command culturally without saying so? How might noticing that make conversations across traditions easier?

</div>

### When Other Loyalties Move In <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#when-other-loyalties-move-in}

*When the Lamb is not at the center, something else will be.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q4:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Brent describes men's ministries built on a narrow template that left many men feeling they did not belong, and accountability groups that assumed struggles he did not have. Derek, building on Nate Pyle's *Man Enough*, says, "Jesus didn't come to make men more masculine and women more feminine." Jesus came to make us more human. Derek affirms that humans are made in God's image and that bodies matter, but he notes the Gospels show no emphasis on gender performance, and he asks instead, "How can we be men like Jesus?" and "What is it like to be a woman in the image of Jesus?"[^9] The churches in this room have taught about men and women in different ways. Whatever your view, what changes when the question becomes *How can I be like Jesus?* rather than *How can I be a better man or woman?*

*Read Acts 1:3 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Derek points out that the kingdom of God was Jesus' main teaching theme, and was what he taught between his resurrection and ascension. When churches reduce the gospel to its internal side, inner peace and personal salvation, a void opens, and political ideologies from the left and the right "swoop in" and disciple people. Derek names one movement's grip on white evangelicalism as his most striking example. He paraphrases Eugene Peterson: the gospel is more political than we imagined, but "political in a way that no one guesses."[^10] Without naming parties, where have you seen people, including yourself, being discipled more by a political story than by Jesus' teaching about the kingdom? What would a church need in order to fill that void well?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** Derek defines nationalism as loving one's nation at a superior level, and says that when love of nation becomes the highest of one's loves, it is an idol. He offers a pastoral diagnostic: when someone reacts viscerally to a sermon or post, he thinks, "I think your idol is getting messed with here." The notes suggest applying that test to any strong pushback against critiques of nation, party or ideology.[^11] Think of a recent moment when you reacted strongly to something said about your country, party or cause. What was being touched in you? What would it look like to give King Jesus full allegiance in that area?

</div>

### The Four Sides of Faith <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#the-four-sides-of-faith}

*Faith is more than agreeing with the right ideas.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Following Gordon Fee, Derek says that every time Paul uses *pneuma* (spirit) or *pneumatikos* (spiritual), he means the Holy Spirit. So Christian spirituality is "spiritual" because of our experience of the Spirit and "Christian" because it is centered on Christ. Derek sees "spiritual but not religious" people as having a "crack in secularism," and he asks people about their "spiritual journey" rather than their religious background. He tells of a baptism candidate who found God through the 12 steps after struggling with drug abuse.[^12] How does Paul's meaning of "spiritual" change the way you hear that word? How might asking about someone's spiritual journey change a conversation with a friend outside the church?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Derek pictures faith as a square. Belief is necessary but elementary, and most people stop there. Confidence answers the idea that faith means certainty; he quotes Greg Boyd: "Faith is simply enough confidence to act on what you believe." Trust is action, putting your life into God's hands. Allegiance draws on Matthew Bates's point that the Greek *pistis* often means loyalty, so King Jesus has our full allegiance in every area of life.[^13] Which side of the square is strongest in you right now, and which is least developed? For anyone raised where "everything's first-tier doctrine," what difference does it make to think of faith as confidence rather than certainty?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Derek compiled his own Daily Office lectionary, a two-year plan with Old Testament, Epistle and Gospel readings every day, following the Christian calendar. The notes suggest such a plan lets Christ's life, not the election cycle or news cycle, set the rhythm of your year.[^14] Our traditions relate to the church calendar very differently, from close familiarity to almost none. What currently sets the rhythm of your year? What is one small step that would let Jesus' life shape it more?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> This episode keeps returning to one image: a slain Lamb at the center of the throne, shepherding people from every nation. Derek asks what would change if that image, rather than cultural masculinity, partisan politics, vague spirituality or doctrinal certainty, sat at the center of our lives. Christianity is not one compartment among many but a whole way of living shaped by the cross and resurrection. Faith grows from belief into confidence, trust and full allegiance, and the hardest question may be his diagnostic one: when something makes me react strongly, is the Lamb being challenged, or is one of my idols?
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, name one area of your life where you want the Lamb to move closer to the center this week.

</div>
