### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Session 3 ended with a panoramic review of the whole story from Genesis through the four Gospels, told as a tale of two kingdoms, Empire and Shalom. We carried forward the claim that people may fail, but faith does not when they keep returning to trust God's story.

**This week:** Session 4 begins. The hosts review Sessions 1–3 once more and lay out the road ahead: Acts, Galatians, Romans, the other letters, and Revelation.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This short episode works as a bridge. Marty and Brent insist that BEMA is a linear journey, comparing Session 4 to a film that only makes sense if you have seen the ones before it. They retrace the story from Torah through the Gospels and name the question Session 4 will ask: how do God's people live out the reality of Christ in their own cultural setting?

The hosts also explain how they will handle each part of the New Testament. Acts will be covered in selected episodes, Galatians and Romans verse by verse, and Revelation at length. They are candid about why: Romans has become a lens through which many Christians read everything else, and Revelation shapes how believers live today. Because the episode is brief, this session spends extra time in the opening lines of three books we will study this year.

### Why Start at the Beginning <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#why-start-at-the-beginning}

*Session 4 assumes the story we have already walked through, and so does the book of Acts.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The hosts compare Session 4 to *Avengers: Endgame*, which only makes sense if you have watched the films that came before it. They argue that jumping into Session 4 without Sessions 1–3 "would be disorienting and ineffective."[^1]

*Read Acts 1:1–5 aloud.*

What do you notice about how Luke opens this book? What does he assume his reader already knows?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The episode retraces the story in seven movements: Torah as partnership, History as the redemption cycle, Wisdom as tools for the journey, the Prophets as warning, woe and hope, the Remnant as returning, yearning and learning, the Silent Years, and the Gospels as the story in flesh.[^2] Which of these movements has changed the way you read the Bible most since we started, and how?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The review recalls five Jewish responses to Hellenism during the Silent Years: Sadducees, Herodians, Essenes, Pharisees and Zealots.[^3] The notes suggest comparing these to the way religious communities respond to cultural change today.[^4] Which of those responses do you recognize most readily in churches today, or in yourself? What makes it attractive?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes describe Session 4 as the phase where "God's people learn to apply Christ's reality to their specific cultural contexts."[^5] Name one specific situation in your own life (work, family, neighborhood, politics) where you would most like help knowing how to live out the way of Jesus. What makes it hard?

</div>

### Two Books That Shape Everything <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#two-books-that-shape-everything}

*The hosts explain why Romans and Revelation will get the most careful treatment of the session.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The roadmap is uneven on purpose: eight or nine selected episodes from Acts, Galatians verse by verse "as potentially the earliest New Testament writing," Romans in an exhaustive verse-by-verse treatment, and Revelation at length.[^6]

*Read Romans 1:1–6 aloud.*

Before we ever reach the famous arguments of Romans, what do you notice about how Paul introduces himself and the gospel? What does he connect it to?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes describe Romans as a "Bible of Bibles" in contemporary evangelical culture, the book through which all Scripture, including the Gospels, gets interpreted. They warn that texts used this way can become interpretive lenses "that potentially distort understanding of other biblical materials," and Marty plans a verse-by-verse study so no one can accuse him of selective reading.[^7] Every tradition in the room has books or passages it leans on most. In your own tradition, which texts serve as the lens for the rest? What has that lens helped you see, and what might it make harder to see?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The hosts call Revelation one of the most significant and most misunderstood parts of Scripture, because understanding "how the story ends profoundly impacts how believers live today." BEMA's trips to Turkey study Revelation partly in the places its first readers lived.[^8]

*Read Revelation 1:1–3 aloud.*

Notice who receives a blessing and what they are asked to do. Our traditions have taught different things about the end of the story. Whatever you were taught, how has it shaped the way you live now?

</div>

### A Group for the Long Road <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#a-group-for-the-long-road}

*BEMA is meant to be processed in community, and this group has 57 weeks ahead of it.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The hosts describe BEMA groups around the world as grassroots rather than programmatic: each develops "according to local needs and personalities."[^9] The notes see in this an echo of how the earliest Christian communities spread.[^10] What do you need this group to be over the coming year in order to keep learning, and what can you offer it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes describe BEMA as introducing new material "at a sustainable rate, meeting learners where they are," which mirrors ancient Jewish methods of building understanding step by step.[^11] What pace or rhythm actually helps you absorb something, rather than just hear it? What is one habit that would help you carry this session week to week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Session 4 picks up a story already in progress, just as Luke does when he opens Acts by pointing back to his first book. The hosts have walked us from Torah to the Gospels so that we can now ask how God's people live out Christ's reality in their own setting. The road ahead includes Romans, read carefully and in order, and Revelation, read as a book about how the story ends and therefore how we live now. We will travel it together, at a pace we can sustain.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each: what is one question you hope this session will help you answer?

</div>
