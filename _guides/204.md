### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Marty marked the "you are here" spot on the map and named four pillars to recover (Text, Community, Discipleship and Wrestling) through *t'shuvah*, a return that begins with small, realistic steps.[^1]

**This week:** This is our finale: the last week of Session 5 and of BEMA's five foundational sessions. We receive a benediction and a commissioning, walk back through the whole story, and decide together what comes next.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This episode offers no new history. Marty and Brent describe it as the conclusion of BEMA's foundational core, not the end of the podcast, and they reflect on the journey of reading Scripture in its historical and cultural context. At its heart are two readings from Sarah Bessey: a "Benediction" from *Out of Sorts: Making Peace With an Evolving Faith*, which pictures faith as a wilderness journey of unpacking and sorting what we carry, and "The Commissioning" from *Jesus Feminist*, which declares every believer a minister of the gospel right where they are.[^3]

The hosts also look ahead. They describe the next season, tentatively titled "Postscripts," as more topical and varied than the first five sessions, with miniseries, single-topic episodes and guests. Because this is our last meeting of the five-session journey, the closing segment is longer than usual. It includes a walk back through the whole story, a time of silence, and three questions to help our group decide how to continue.[^4]

**Opening Question:** Tonight we finish the five foundational sessions of BEMA. Think of another long journey you have completed (a degree, a long hike, a move, a season of caregiving). What did the last day feel like, and what did you carry home with you?

### Leaving Things on the Trail <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#leaving-things-on-the-trail}

*Sarah Bessey's benediction pictures faith as a wilderness journey where we sort what we carry and let fear give way to love.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Bessey's benediction describes leaving "poisonous, lovely things" along the trail. Read this list aloud, slowly. According to the notes, the items left behind include:[^5]

- cynicism, hypocrisy and lies;
- numbing techniques;
- apologetics used as weapons;
- rigid doctrinal statements;
- pomposity and carefully constructed personas;
- even our own righteousness.

What do you notice about this list? Which items surprise you, and why might something be both "poisonous" and "lovely" at the same time?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The benediction also names unexpected attachments: some things we thought we would discard become precious, and some things we held tightly turn out to be unnecessary burdens. It describes a stripping that removes everything standing between us and God, and a call that comes again to venture "further west," because growth in faith is a lifelong journey rather than a destination.[^6] Over these five sessions, what is one thing you expected to let go of that you have kept, or one thing you held tightly that you have set down?

*Read 1 John 4:18 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The benediction contrasts fear-based faith (fear of hell, of punishment, of the "slippery slope," of being wrong, of asking questions) with faith motivated by love, and it draws on this passage. Rather than avoiding the slippery slope of questions and growth, Bessey suggests it "may very well be your saving grace, sending you careening into truth and love, newness and beauty."[^7] What does the verse say fear "has to do with"? Looking back over your life of faith, which fears have motivated you, and where has love begun to replace them?

</div>

### Commissioned Where You Are <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#commissioned-where-you-are}

*The benediction sends us to plant gardens in exile, and the commissioning declares that every one of us is already a minister.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Jeremiah 29:4–7 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The benediction speaks to believers who feel like exiles, not fully at home in the world's systems or in traditional religious structures, and calls them, like the exiles in Babylon, to "plant gardens in your exile, work for the good of your city." It warns against being "so caught up in the 'not yet' of the Kingdom of God that you forget it's also now."[^8] What does Jeremiah tell the exiles to do? What do you notice about how ordinary these instructions are, and how does that connect with last week's picture of living in a modern Babylon?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The benediction prays for "community so rich, so deep, so diverse, that you will disagree and fight and remain in fellowship together anyway," and for a commitment to keep telling one another, "You belong here. There's room for you. There's room for all of us."[^9] Our group has studied across Church of Christ, Baptist, Christian Church and Methodist traditions. What has it been like to read Scripture together across those differences, and what has helped us stay in fellowship?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** "The Commissioning" declares that every believer is "a minister of Jesus Christ, to live in your world as an ambassador of the kingdom," whether surrounded by toddlers or thousands, in a hospital or a kitchen. Our worth does not come from accomplishments, gender, marital status, correct doctrine or the approval of gatekeepers: "Stop waiting for someone else to validate your created self. That is done." The benediction adds, "Don't outsource the work of the Holy Spirit in your life to someone else."[^10] Where have you been waiting for someone's permission? Name the actual places (a kitchen, an office, a classroom, a hospital room) where you are already a minister.

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">35 min</span> {#closing}

> We have walked a long road. In church history we watched Gentile believers separate from their Jewish brothers and sisters and lose the "walking libraries" who carried Scripture; Constantine turn a persecuted movement into Christendom; monastics keep space for God, who fills whatever space we create; East and West divide in 1054; the Crusades kill in Jesus's name; a remnant like Wycliffe and Hus resist the sale of grace; the church struggle with Copernicus; Wesley call people to holiness as culture stopped blinking Christian; America's revivals and restoration movements chase the church of Acts; and Jacob Neusner reopen a conversation closed for 1,800 years. Last week Marty gathered it all into four pillars to recover.[^11]
>
> Behind church history stands the whole story we have studied together. In Torah, God invited a people into partnership as a kingdom of priests, in a world shaped by the conflict between Empire and Shalom. In the Prophets and Writings, God's partners heard warning, woe and hope. In the Gospels, Jesus embodied the story and called disciples to follow him as a rabbi, all day, every day. In the first-century church, the apostles applied the living Text of Jesus to new contexts. And in church history, we asked, "Where are we today and how did we get here?"[^12]
{: .guide-summary}

*Sit together in silence for 90 seconds before anyone speaks. Let the whole journey come to mind.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Across all five sessions, from Torah to today, how has this journey changed the way you read the Bible, the way you understand it, and the way you relate to God through it? Name one specific passage or idea that now reads differently to you.

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** As we finish this stretch of study, take a few minutes for three questions that will shape what our group does next:

- What mattered most to you across these five sessions?
- What was missing, or what do you still need?
- What should we do next, as a group and as individuals?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** Going around the room, complete this sentence in one sentence: "Right where I am, I am commissioned to..."

*To close, one person reads aloud the words from "The Commissioning": "Stop waiting for someone else to validate your created self. That is done."*

</div>
