### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Leviticus showed us what it means to be a kingdom of priests, with the Day of Atonement at its center and commands to celebrate and to care for the oppressed.

**This week:** Israel stands at the edge of forty years in the desert, and Marty begins a series of desert images with the shepherd and Psalm 23.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

Israel has been rescued, married at Sinai, and given the Tabernacle and its manual. Now comes the honeymoon: forty years in the desert, where the people must be shaped. Marty admits the desert is hard to teach from a microphone, and says most of what he teaches in this series comes from his time in the desert with Ray Vander Laan. He begins with a Hebrew word picture. The word for desert, *midbar*, grows from the root *dabar*, "word," and shares that root with the words for "to speak," "shepherd," "pasture," and "sheepfold." The desert is where you learn to speak and to listen.

From there Marty walks through Psalm 23 as a desert shepherd would hear it: green pastures that are tufts of grass around rocks, quiet waters that are muddy pools in a wadi, paths of righteousness worn by thousands of years of flocks, and a valley of the shadow of death where the shepherd moves to the center. He describes mixed flocks of sheep and goats, and notes that desert shepherds are almost always young women. The thread through it all: the desert teaches God's people to become "people of the ears and not people of the eyes."

### The Place of the Word <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#the-place-of-the-word}

*In Hebrew, the desert is tied to the word for "word."*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The Hebrew word for desert is *midbar*. Its root is *dabar*, "word," and the Ten Commandments are also called the Ten Words. The same root produces *diber* ("to speak"), *madbir* ("shepherd"), *dober* ("pasture"), and *divir* ("sheepfold"). Marty explains: "You go to the desert to learn how to speak and to learn how to listen to word. Desert is where you finally learn how to become a people of the ears."[^1]

*Read Deuteronomy 8:2–3 aloud.*

What does Moses say God was doing in the desert? How does the connection between desert and word help you hear this passage?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> One of Marty's favorite quotes about the desert comes from Bruce Feiler's *Walking the Bible*: "Because the place is demanding, it builds character. Because it's destructive, it builds interdependence. Because it's isolating, it builds community. And because it's the desert, it builds nations."[^2] Which line of that quote rings true to a hard season you have lived through?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Marty warns that "This life becomes pretty difficult if we become people of the eyes because it looks like there are lots of things to be worried about and afraid of." The notes add that God uses our deserts to teach us to be people of the ears instead.[^3] What are you looking at right now that makes you anxious? What would it mean to listen instead of look?

</div>

### Psalm 23 in the Desert <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#psalm-23-in-the-desert}

*Every image in Psalm 23 comes from the desert, not from lush green fields.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Marty walks through the psalm line by line. Green pastures are tiny tufts of grass growing around rocks. Quiet waters are what is left after the rainy season, gathered in muddy pools at the bottom of a wadi. The paths of righteousness are real paths, worn over thousands of years of shepherds leading flocks, and shepherds still call them by that name. The valley of the shadow of death is where a flock gets stuck if it is not home by nightfall.[^4]

*Read Psalm 23 aloud, slowly.*

As you listen, replace the picture you described in the opening with the desert picture. What changes? Which line sounds most different to you now?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The desert, Marty says, is not a place of abundance but of "just enough." He puts the sheep's experience this way: "Do I have enough right now, yes. Will I have enough in ten minutes? I don't know. I need to trust the shepherd." Or more simply: "Put your head down and wait for the next tuft of grass."[^5] Many of us carry an unspoken expectation that God's care will look like plenty. How does "just enough" sit with you? Is it comfort, challenge, or both?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> In the valley at night, the shepherd leads from the center of the flock rather than from behind. Marty tells a story from 2006 of a shepherd who got ahead of his flock on the other side of a chasm; when he turned and called, the sheep walked off the edge of a cliff because they trusted his voice so completely.[^6] What does this unsettling story say about the weight of a shepherd's voice, and about the responsibility of anyone who leads?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Marty hopes listeners will see that this is about literal deserts but also about the figurative deserts we all go through. We may never ask for our deserts, yet many of us look back on them fondly from the other side.[^7] Think of a desert season in your own life. What did you learn to hear there that you could not have heard anywhere else?

</div>

### Sheep, Goats, and the Shepherd <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#sheep-goats-and-the-shepherd}

*Desert flocks are mixed, and the shepherd leads by voice.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Shepherds lead with their voice, never with the stick. Their staff is for defense, predators, or walking, not for prodding or beating. Marty describes several flocks penned together overnight, and in the morning only one shepherd's sheep leave the pen when he calls.[^8] Almost every desert flock also includes goats. The sheep huddle together following the shepherd's voice, while the goats scatter across the mountain and do what they want, though they still get where they need to go.[^9]

*Read Matthew 25:31–46 aloud.*

Notice what the sheep did and what the goats did not do. How does the picture of a mixed flock, with both groups traveling in the same direction, change the way you hear this parable?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty says the shepherds in this culture are almost always female, and that in all his years he has never seen a male shepherd. Tending the flock is one of the roles of the younger girls in the family. That would have made Moses and David unusual, and David was likely out helping his older sisters. His sling may have come from throwing rocks to the side of the sheep to guide them.[^10] Our traditions hold a range of convictions about women and leadership, and this is a description of a culture rather than a ruling on those questions. What does it change in how you picture the shepherds in these stories, or the shepherds in Luke 2?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** Marty asks, "Which am I? Which are you?" He wants to belong to the part of the flock that knows how to listen and hear the shepherd's voice, which is what *shema* means: to listen and to obey.[^11] Be honest with yourself: in this season, are you closer to the sheep or the goats? What is one practice that would help you hear the shepherd's voice this week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> The desert, *midbar*, is the place of the word. It is where God took his people for their honeymoon, not to harm them but to teach them to listen. Psalm 23 is a desert psalm: a tuft of grass at a time, a muddy pool that is enough, a path worn by generations, and a shepherd who moves to the center when night falls. Sheep and goats travel in the same flock, but only one group follows the voice. God still uses our deserts to make us people of the ears and not people of the eyes.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is the "just enough" God has given you for today?

</div>
