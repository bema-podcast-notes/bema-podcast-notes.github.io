### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** The Sermon on the Mount ended with a choice: the narrow gate, good fruit, and building on the rock. The Kingdom comes when God moves, people call him Lord, and people actually do what he says.

**This week:** Jesus comes down the mountain and immediately does what he just taught. Three outsiders meet him in quick succession, and then two would-be followers learn what following will cost.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode opens with a twelve-minute review of the whole biblical story, from Torah through the Silent Years to the four Gospels, before landing on Matthew 8. The hosts point out that a rabbi would put his teaching into practice right after a sermon like this one, so what comes next is a clue. Jesus touches a leper, honors the faith of a Roman centurion, and heals Peter's mother-in-law and the crowds.

Along the way the episode makes an important distinction: in Torah, being ritually unclean is a temporary, ordinary state, not the same thing as being sinful. It also notices that Matthew applies Isaiah 53 to Jesus' healing ministry, not his death. The session ends with Jesus' hard words to two would-be disciples, including "let the dead bury their own dead," which the episode explains through Jewish burial customs of the time. Matthew's "*mamzer* agenda" (his focus on outsiders) runs through it all.

### Touching the Untouchable <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#touching-the-untouchable}

*Right after the sermon, Jesus reaches out to a man everyone else kept at a distance.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q1:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode's opening review walks through the whole story so far, and the original notes record Marty saying, "All the setup was to get us to Jesus," and, more provocatively, "The scriptures don't point to Jesus. Jesus is the scriptures."[^1] Brent recalls an earlier phrase: "the story in flesh."[^2] As you look back over what this group has studied, which part of the story helps you most to understand Jesus? What do you make of Marty's distinction?

*Read Matthew 8:1–4 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The notes explain that "mamzer" technically means a child of an illegitimate Torah marriage, but Marty uses it to describe anyone considered an outsider. Matthew's genealogy, birth story, and crowds all lean toward outsiders.[^3] The hosts add that a Jewish rabbi "would immediately put his teaching into practice after something like the sermon on the Mount."[^4] What do you notice about who approaches Jesus first, and about what Jesus does before he says anything?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The episode stresses that uncleanness in Torah is a temporary state, not a moral condition. Menstruation, touching a dead animal, and bodily emissions all made people unclean, often for six or seven days, and everyone took turns being unclean.[^5] The law, the notes say, tells you what to do "WHEN (not IF)" you touch a leper. Jesus touches him, accepts the inconvenience, and then sends him to the priest as Leviticus commands.[^6] How does separating "unclean" from "sinful" change the way you picture this scene? What does it say about how Jesus treated Torah?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes put the modern question plainly: "Who are the 'lepers' in our communities - those we avoid for fear of contamination or inconvenience?"[^7] Without naming individuals, what kinds of people do you or your church find easiest to keep at a distance? What would "touching" look like, given the real cost of it?

</div>

### Outsiders at the Feast <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#outsiders-at-the-feast}

*A Roman officer shows a faith Jesus calls unmatched, and Matthew reads Isaiah 53 in Jesus' daily work.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 8:5–17 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes describe the centurion as a pagan officer of the occupying power who knows enough about Jewish purity to say, "I do not deserve to have you come under my roof," and who understands authority: "I myself am a man under authority." Jesus is amazed and says he has "not found anyone in Israel with such great faith."[^8] The original notes add that "East and West is gentile talk."[^9] What do you notice in the centurion's words that Jesus calls faith?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The episode hears in Jesus' words a reversal: "Those you don't think are in the kingdom are in and those you do think are in the kingdom are not."[^10] That warning is aimed at anyone who assumes they are inside, and it is easy to aim it at someone else instead. Who are the people today whose faith might surprise you? What "cultural markers" does your community tend to use to decide who is in?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Matthew quotes Isaiah 53, "He took up our infirmities and bore our diseases," about an evening of healing at Peter's house, not about the cross. The notes conclude that "Isaiah 53 is fulfilled in how Jesus lives, not just how He dies."[^11] How have you usually heard Isaiah 53 used? What changes if bearing other people's burdens is part of how that prophecy is fulfilled, in Jesus and in those who follow him?

</div>

### The Cost of Following <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#the-cost-of-following}

*Two would-be disciples meet the price of following a rabbi who has nowhere to lay his head.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 8:18–22 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** To the teacher of the law who promises to follow anywhere, Jesus answers that foxes and birds have homes "but the Son of Man has no place to lay His head."[^12] The notes explain that "let me first bury my father" likely refers to second burial: a year after death, the family returned to place the bones in an ossuary, and a superstition had developed that being present was necessary for the person's resurrection.[^13] How does this background change the way Jesus' reply, "Let the dead bury their own dead," sounds to you?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** Marty describes campus students who wanted discipleship for "only a couple hours a day," while biblical discipleship meant sharing life fully, from breakfast to dinner, like the twelve leaving their nets.[^14] What does a convenient, compartmentalized version of following Jesus look like in your life? What is one specific cost that full discipleship would ask of you right now?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Jesus does not just teach mercy on the mountain; he walks down and touches a leper, honors a Roman officer, and exhausts himself healing until evening. Being unclean was never the same as being sinful, and Jesus willingly takes on the inconvenience so that outsiders can come in. Matthew sees Isaiah 53 in this daily, costly love. And when people offer to follow, Jesus is honest: there is no guaranteed home, and Kingdom life comes before even the most sacred customs.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each: who is one person you could move toward this week, even if it costs you something?

</div>
