### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** This is our first week together in Session 10.

**This week:** Session 10 opens just after the death of co-host Josh Bossé. Marty and Brent borrow the Jewish practice of mourning to make room for grief, and they set a slower pace for the whole season in Josh's honor.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This episode is less a teaching than a pastoral word. Marty and Brent describe the Jewish pattern of mourning (seven days of *shiva*, then the rest of a 40-day period) and explain why they are treating all of Session 10 as a "season of mourning." They tell a story about Josh walking a prayer labyrinth far more slowly than anyone else, and they let that story set the pace of the season: episodes every other Thursday instead of every week.

The episode also looks ahead. The hosts name the season's interviews and teaching series (including the four pillars of BEMA), explain why they are rebooting Session 2, and restate BEMA's core mission of discipling college students. Some of us may come to this conversation carrying losses of our own. Share only what you want to share; listening is a full way to take part tonight.

### Sitting With Grief <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#sitting-with-grief}

*Jewish mourning gives grief a shape without putting it on a clock.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Job 2:11–13 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes say the seven days of *shiva* (the first week of formal mourning) come from Job's friends sitting with him for seven days, and that the point is simply to be present: "no explanations, no platitudes, no tidy answers."[^1] Look back at the passage. What do the friends do, what do they hold back from doing, and what detail stands out to you most?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** After *shiva* come 33 more days of formal mourning, for 40 days in all, with practices like covering mirrors and setting aside grooming and makeup.[^2] Yet the notes also say plainly, "There's no timeline on grief," and that when the formal period ends, life is re-engaged but never the same as before.[^3] How can a set period of mourning and "no timeline on grief" both be true? What does each one protect?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes put it this way: "A hospital visit or a meal dropped off without advice can do more than a carefully chosen Bible verse."[^4] Many of us were formed in churches that value having the right word ready for a hurting person. What do you think that instinct is trying to accomplish, and in what moments might quiet presence serve the grieving person better?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The hosts chose to keep the podcast going "at a gentler pace" rather than stopping or pushing on as usual.[^5] Think of someone you know who is grieving, or of your own losses if that feels right. What might showing up and staying quiet look like in the next month? You are welcome to answer this one privately.

</div>

### The Slow Work of God <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#the-slow-work-of-god}

*A story about Josh in a prayer labyrinth becomes the model for the whole season.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** On a leadership retreat, the team walked a prayer labyrinth in the cold rain while a prayer about trusting "the slow work of God" was read. Josh walked so slowly that he took twice as long as Brent, who was going as slowly as he could. Marty felt torn: part of him wanted Josh to "get with the program," and part of him saw that Josh "actually gets it."[^6] Which side of Marty's reaction do you recognize in yourself, and where does it come from?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes sum up Josh's posture in one line: "He did what the moment invited."[^7] What do you think separates doing what the moment invites from simply being slow or inefficient?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Session 10 will release roughly every other Thursday, breaking a near-weekly rhythm that has held since 2016. The hosts give four reasons: honoring Josh, replanning work he had started, Marty's travel schedule, and Brent's coming sabbatical.[^8] The notes observe that a ministry cutting its output in half "goes against the cultural pressure to be productive all the time," and that individuals can do the same "by keeping margin in their calendars during hard seasons."[^9] Where in the coming weeks could you leave some margin, and what would you hope to notice in it?

</div>

### Starting Again at the Beginning <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#starting-again-at-the-beginning}

*The season ahead includes revisiting old material, naming BEMA's pillars, and recommitting to its mission.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The hosts are replacing the original Session 2 episodes with new ones. Since 2017–2018, the team has learned more, and Marty has found things he got wrong. He admits that his mistakes are "not as rare as you would think." New listeners start every day, and "they deserve the best foundation available."[^10] What does it take for a teacher, or a church, to revise what it has taught before? When a teacher you trust changes their mind, how do you tend to respond?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The four pillars of BEMA are text, wrestling, discipleship, and community, first named at the end of Session 5 as a contrast to the "four pillars of Hellenism."[^11] We will spend much of this season with them. Which of the four feels most like home to you right now, and which feels least familiar?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** BEMA began as a class for college students. After his liver transplant, Josh became a virtual campus minister who connected isolated students and helped them begin a discipleship journey. The long-term hope is that graduates who are "flung out into the world" will be "caught on the other side" by mentors in their own fields.[^12] Who caught you at a moment of transition in your life? Who might you be in a position to catch?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Session 10 begins where grief begins: with people sitting together and not rushing to explain. Marty and Brent chose to slow down, not because the work stopped mattering, but because Josh showed them that the moment often asks for presence more than speed. The season ahead is full, with interviews, teaching series, and a memorial for Josh, but it will move at a gentler pace. We can let our own group take that posture too: unhurried, honest, and willing to trust the slow work of God.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, what pace do you hope to keep, with God and with this group, during this season?

</div>
