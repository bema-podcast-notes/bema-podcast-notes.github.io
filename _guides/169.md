### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** In 1 Peter, scattered believers under persecution heard that they were living stones built together into God's house, called to answer hostility with blessing rather than retaliation.

**This week:** 2 Peter takes up the other side of that conversation. When pressure is hard to bear, one way out is to stop being different.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode presents 2 Peter as the companion to 1 Peter. The first letter says, in effect, keep walking faithfully under persecution; the second says, do not lose your distinctiveness by giving in to the culture around you. Peter's opening line suggests he is writing mainly to Gentile believers, and the false teachers he confronts are pushing wrong practice rather than wrong doctrine.

The hosts describe Greco-Roman guild feasts, where professional life, idol worship, and sexual immorality were bound together, and they show Peter using Jewish interpretive tradition (Midrash) about Balaam to name three kinds of compromise: idolatry, the hunger for power and wealth, and sexual immorality. They connect all of this to the *shephelah* from Joshua and Judges, God's people living at the crossroads of cultures.

### Two Letters, Two Temptations <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#two-letters-two-temptations}

*Peter's two letters address two opposite dangers facing the same kind of community.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 2 Peter 1:1 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes point to Peter's greeting, "To those who... have received a faith as precious as ours," and explain that "ours" refers to Jewish believers, so the recipients are Gentiles whose faith is equal to Jewish faith, likely educated Gentiles familiar with Jewish teaching.[^1] What do you notice in this greeting about how Peter sees these Gentile believers? What does it tell you about the relationship between Jewish and Gentile believers that he assumes?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode links 2 Peter to the *shephelah*, the lowland "tension zone between coastal plain and Judah mountains" from Joshua and Judges. God's people are called to live at the crossroads as a kingdom of priests, "positioned between cultures without fully adopting either."[^2] If you remember the *shephelah* from Joshua and Judges, why might that be the place God wants his people?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes summarize the two letters this way: 1 Peter says, "Keep walking in faithfulness despite persecution and suffering," and 2 Peter says, "Don't lose your distinctiveness by giving in to cultural norms."[^3] Which of these two messages do you think you, or your church, most need to hear right now? Why that one?

</div>

### Guild Feasts and Practical Denial <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#guild-feasts-and-practical-denial}

*The false teachers in 2 Peter are concerned less with what people believe than with how they live.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 2 Peter 2:1–3 and 2:12–19 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The episode argues that Peter's concern throughout chapter 2 "focuses on conduct, not beliefs," and that "denying the Lord" here means practical denial through behavior rather than theological denial.[^4] Read through the passage again. What are the false teachers doing, and what are they promising? How many of Peter's charges are about belief, and how many about behavior?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The hosts describe guild feasts as a mix of labor union and college fraternity, organized by trade. They met monthly, each guild honored a patron god, members ate raw meat "to ingest the god's blood and receive divine power," and the evening ended with prostitutes and drunkenness. Job security and advancement depended on membership.[^5] What would refusing the guild have cost a Gentile believer who worked as a blacksmith or potter? Why might a teacher who said "go ahead" have sounded so reasonable?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes observe that contemporary Christianity tends "to prioritize doctrinal correctness while sometimes overlooking behavioral compromise."[^6] Our traditions have each drawn lines around right belief, sometimes carefully. Where has your tradition been strongest at guarding right practice, and where might it have been more watchful about doctrine than about how its people actually live?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The episode names modern parallels: business practices that conflict with Christian values for the sake of career advancement, adopting cultural habits for acceptance, and relaxing standards to avoid social exclusion.[^7] What is the "guild feast" in your field or neighborhood, the place where belonging quietly asks you to compromise? What would faithful participation, rather than withdrawal or surrender, look like there?

</div>

### Balaam and the Midrash <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#balaam-and-the-midrash}

*Peter reaches for stories his readers knew, including what Jewish tradition said about them.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read 2 Peter 2:4–9 and 2:15–16 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes list Peter's examples: angels in chains, Noah "and seven others," Sodom and Gomorrah, and Balaam. They explain that Midrash is a vast library of Jewish oral teaching and interpretation, and that Peter's use of it suggests his audience was well trained in Jewish thought.[^8] What do you notice about the examples Peter chooses? What do they have in common?

*Read Numbers 22:1–6 and Numbers 25:1–3 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> According to the episode, the Midrash adds that after failing to curse Israel, Balaam advised Balak to seduce the Israelites through Moabite women. That makes Balaam a pattern for all three compromises: idolatry, greed (payment for prophecy), and sexual immorality, a reading the notes say 2 Peter, Jude, and Revelation all affirm.[^9] The hosts also note that the 2014 *Noah* film drew its extra-biblical details from Midrash, and that many Christians called it "unbiblical" without recognizing that tradition.[^10] How does it affect you to learn that New Testament writers drew on Jewish interpretive tradition beyond the written text?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** Balaam's three-fold pattern (worship of something other than God, hunger for money and influence, and sexual compromise) still describes the pressure points of ordinary life. Which of the three does your culture make easiest to excuse? What is one practice that could help you stay distinct without pulling away from the people around you?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> 1 Peter told believers to keep walking faithfully under persecution; 2 Peter warns against escaping that pressure by blending in. The false teachers were not mainly selling bad doctrine but bad practice: join the guild, eat at the feast, and the hardship goes away. Peter answers with Israel's own stories, especially Balaam, whose pattern of idolatry, greed, and immorality never goes out of date. God's people are still called to live at the crossroads, engaged with their culture without being absorbed by it.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each: where is the crossroads in your life this week, and what will help you stay distinct there?

</div>
