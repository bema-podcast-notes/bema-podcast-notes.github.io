### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Jesus was rejected in Nazareth for pointing to outsiders, grieved the death of John the Baptist, and fed the 5,000 as a lesson for his disciples, presenting himself as a second Moses whose provision is more than enough.

**This week:** Peter steps out of the boat, and a toddler on a grassy slope helps us hear Jesus' words to him in a new way.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This episode is unusual. Instead of a traditional teaching, Marty reads a chapter from a book he wrote for his daughter Abigail, titled "When Two-Year-Olds Preach." In it he describes watching her fall again and again while learning to walk up a gentle slope in a park, giggling each time and getting back up.

That picture becomes a lens for Peter walking on water. Drawing on the first-century relationship between a rabbi and his *talmid* (disciple), the episode asks in whom Peter actually lost faith when he began to sink, and suggests that Jesus' words to him may be more affirmation than rebuke. It also imagines Jesus' teaching about becoming like children as a whole day spent with children rather than a brief aside. Marty is careful to say this is not about trivializing sin or trusting ourselves instead of Christ.

### Out of the Boat <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#out-of-the-boat}

*Peter does what a disciple does: he tries to do what his rabbi is doing.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 14:22–33 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Read the passage a second time, slowly. Track what Peter says, sees and does, and what Jesus says and does. At what exact moment does the story turn? What is Peter looking at when it turns?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode explains that when a rabbi accepted a disciple, the message was: "I believe you have what it takes to become just like me." Disciples memorized their rabbi's teaching and imitated his every action, and a rabbi would never call a disciple to something they could not do.[^1] With that background, how does Peter's request to come out on the water sound to you?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes ask a pointed question: in whom did Peter lose faith? Jesus was doing just fine on the water, so the episode suggests Peter lost faith in himself, not in Jesus. On this reading, Jesus' question in verse 31 becomes something like: "Peter, if you didn't have what it takes to walk on water, I never would have called you out."[^2] How have you usually heard Jesus' words to Peter, as rebuke or as encouragement? What in the text supports each way of hearing them?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Marty describes a "boat perspective": we try, we sink, we are not surprised, we climb back in, stare at the floor and take the scolding we expected, deciding next time that "it's safer in the boat." He contrasts it with God on a park bench, watching his child learn to walk.[^3] Think of a time you stepped out toward something you believed God wanted and then sank. What did you tell yourself afterward? Which perspective were you living from?

</div>

### Faith Like a Child <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#faith-like-a-child}

*A toddler on a slope becomes a sermon about faith.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 18:1–4 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The disciples ask who is greatest, and Jesus answers with a child. In the park, Abigail fell backward perhaps twenty times, never cried or reached for help, giggled, got up, and adjusted her weight and footing until, ten minutes and twelve feet later, she reached the top. The notes add that she had been perfectly happy at the bottom too.[^4] What words does Jesus use in Matthew 18:3–4 to describe what must happen to us? Which details from the park story help you understand those words?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty imagines the teaching about children not as a twenty-second moment or a "silly children sermon before the real message," but as a whole day the disciples spent playing with children, with Jesus saying only, "Watch the children," before finally drawing one child close to teach.[^5] What might the disciples have learned from that day that they could not have learned from a sentence?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The episode holds two things together. Marty rejects humanistic views that locate the solution to our problems within ourselves, and affirms that hope lies in the resurrected Christ. Yet he argues that faith includes "believing that there must be something worth loving and worth saving if God was willing to sacrifice His Son for it": we believe in Jesus, and Jesus believes in us.[^6] Our traditions may weigh human sinfulness and human worth differently. Where does this both-and ring true for you, and where does it make you uneasy?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Learning to "giggle," the notes say, is not treating grace as a "get out of jail free card." It means refusing to be immobilized by failure: joy in the process, confidence that we are loved, and willingness to keep getting up.[^7] What is one "slope" you keep falling on? What would it look like to fall, giggle, adjust, and try again this week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#closing}

> A rabbi calls only those he believes can become like him, so when Peter steps onto the water, he is acting like a true disciple. When he sinks, the episode suggests, it is not Jesus he stops trusting but himself. A toddler on a grassy slope shows what Jesus may have meant by becoming like a child: falling, giggling, getting up, adjusting, and trying again, secure in being loved. God is pictured less as a voice of rebuke from the boat and more as a Father on a park bench, delighted to watch his children grow.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one place you want to step out of the boat, or get back up, this week?

</div>
