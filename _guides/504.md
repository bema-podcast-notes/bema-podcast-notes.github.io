### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Reed Dent compared healthy community to Japanese woodworking joints, where hand-cut pieces lock together without glue or screws, and named its three parts as friendship, conversation and practice. We carried forward his reminder that community is a craft, learned by staying through the awkward parts.

**This week:** Elle Grover Fricks gives her view of the same pillar. She roots community in an old Hebrew word for witness and asks what keeps people from coming home to it.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

Elle opens with a "found poem" built from news headlines about the loneliness epidemic, then argues that people are made for real, embodied belonging because they bear the image of a relational God. Her key word is the Hebrew root *'ud* ("again"), which sits behind the biblical language of witness: God's people are witnesses who tell the stories of God's work over and over. She pictures Christian community as a bride sharing "letters from the groom" while she waits for him, and she reads Revelation 19 as heaven full of repeated testimony.

She then speaks honestly about three things that keep people out of community: hurt done by the church, the intellectual superiority that can come with new learning, and the lone-wolf ideal of self-sufficiency. Some of tonight's questions touch on church hurt and on loss. Share only what you want to, answer in general terms if that is easier, and feel free to pass at any point.

### Opening <span class="guide-time">10 min</span> {#opening}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-opening" markdown="1">

**Opening Question:** Think of a story about someone you know (a friend, a relative, a coworker) that gets told again whenever the people who know them get together. What keeps that story worth hearing one more time? Any story will do, light or serious.

</div>

### Witnesses Who Tell It Again <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#witnesses-who-tell-it-again}

*Elle grounds community in the biblical idea of witness: telling what God has done, again and again.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Revelation 19:5–10 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Brent reads this passage in the episode, and Marty points out how often Revelation shows multitudes in white robes singing[^1]. As it is read, notice who is gathered, what they keep saying and doing, and how the angel responds at the end. What stands out to you? What do you make of the angel's refusal to be worshiped?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Elle's key word is the Hebrew root *'ud*, meaning "again" or "repeat." On her reading, to bear witness is to retell encounters with God many times over[^2]. She compares this to a wake, where people tell stories of someone they loved: some new ones that make everyone say "That's so them!", and familiar ones people love hearing again because they love the person[^3]. What does that picture capture about Christian community that words like "accountability" or "Bible study" leave out?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Elle says that even though we have the Spirit, a healthy community still feels sorrow and eagerness for the groom's presence[^4]. Where have you seen room for that kind of longing in the gatherings you know, and what tends to crowd it out?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Elle borrows the image of a "letter from the front," the family in *Little Women* piling onto each other over a letter from a father far away[^5]. She adds that at any time some people will be in a "dark night of the soul" and unable to believe right now, while others are full of encounters with God, and both are helped by hearing someone else's story[^6]. Think of a time someone else's story carried you, or a time yours seemed to help someone. What is one of your "letters from the groom," new or often told, that you could share with this group?

</div>

### What Keeps Us Away <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#what-keeps-us-away}

*Elle names three barriers to community, and she speaks about each one from her own experience.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The first barrier Elle names is intellectual superiority. People excited by Hebrew thought and *peshat* (the plain sense of the text) can get frustrated with congregations that "don't know what I know." Elle admits she was once "the insufferable spiritual Gestapo," and Marty says he loves people and community more than his way of reading the text[^7].

*Read Philippians 1:15–18 aloud.*

What is Paul glad about here, and what is he willing to leave alone? Elle suggests answering a verse used "out of context" with "Tell me what you've heard!"[^8] Since many of us are learning this way of reading together, what might that sound like in the churches and families we come from?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The third barrier is the lone-wolf ideal: "It's just me and Jesus." Elle calls it a reasonable coping strategy after leaving or being pushed out of a church. At best it makes us like the Essenes, who were often right about the Temple's corruption but left the people God had placed them among[^9]. At worst it leads to Matthew 7:23, because the last day is about *knowing* the groom, not about having the best doctrines[^10].

*Read Matthew 7:21–23 aloud.*

What do you notice about what the people in this passage point to, and what Jesus points to instead? How does Elle's link between witness and knowing shape the way you hear it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The second barrier is church hurt. Elle speaks about it from experience, and she does not ask anyone to return to places where harm goes on without repentance. She does gently urge people, in time, to look for safe places with different values: "Don't let that harm be the end of your story." Marty adds that solitude can look like the safest option, but full healing usually comes through safe people who bear witness[^11]. Without naming any person or church, what makes a community safe enough for you to let it bear witness to your life? (Answer in general terms if you prefer, or pass.)

</div>

### Coming Home <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#coming-home}

*Elle and Marty close with the picture of the scattered being gathered home to each other.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read John 14:2–3 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes point out that the "you" in Jesus' promise is plural, and that the picture is something like an *insula*, a shared family dwelling of the period. Marty says that individualistic Christianity is just another kind of escape, and that God's dream is a safe family to belong to[^12]. Elle warns that a Christian life spent "camping out in a little shed" with a few favorite preachers poorly prepares us for heaven's "every tribe and tongue"[^13]. What changes when you hear this promise as spoken to a household rather than to individuals?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Elle reasons that a bride takes on her husband's loves: "If Jesus loves this person, obviously I do too." She notes that spending free time scoffing at people online contradicts devotion to Jesus[^14]. Who is hardest for you to love among the people the groom loves, and what is one small step toward them this week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Elle answers a lonely culture with an old word, *'ud*: "again." To be a witness is to keep telling what God has done, the way people at a wake keep telling stories of someone they love. Community is where those stories get passed around, like letters from an absent groom read aloud to the whole family. Church can hurt people, learning can make us proud, and going it alone can feel safe. Still, she invites us not to be "too self-sufficient, too good, too busy, or too right" to come home to the feast[^15].
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, which of Elle's four, "too self-sufficient, too good, too busy, or too right," do you most need to hear this week?

</div>

#### Looking Ahead {#looking-ahead}

**[Ep. 505 — Discipleship](/guides/505)** (51:15): Marty turns to the third pillar, discipleship as Jesus practiced it in its Second Temple Jewish setting. He begins with honest disclaimers about how much we actually know, traces a pattern of imitation and growth through five Gospel passages, and admits that he is not doing this kind of discipleship right now. It matters because it separates the rare calling of intensive discipleship from principles any of us can practice.

**What to bring next week:** Read Matthew 14:22–33 and notice exactly what Jesus says to Peter when he begins to sink. Also bring the name (kept private if you like) of one person who is a step or two ahead of you in following Jesus.
