### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Genesis 1:1–5 and the Ark, Table, and Lampstand asked us to name the objects that remind us God is real, the places and practices that fill us up, and how to weave them into a restful rhythm.[^1]

**This week:** The second reflection, built on Josh Bossé's Episode 326, ties the second day of creation to love, to the curtains and veil of the *Mishkan* (Tabernacle), and to the image of rain.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

On the second day God makes a "vault" between the waters, and the notes read this separation as one that serves life: ordered space where life can grow. The Tabernacle curtains are clasped together "so that the tabernacle is a unit," the frames give the tent structure, and the veil marks degrees of nearness to God's presence.[^3] Together these suggest that boundaries and unity work together in love.

The episode builds on last week's reflection, which gathered our experiences of God, and asks what to do with them: how to bring them to others wisely and lovingly. Marty's prompts ask how to make God's love real for others, how to discern what to keep private and what to share, and how to move between sacred spaces like rain. He closes with a concrete challenge to choose one or two specific relationships this week or month.[^4]

<div class="guide-q guide-q-opening" markdown="1">

**Opening Question:** Think of someone who shared a piece of their own story with you at just the right time and in just the right amount. What made it a gift?

### Clasped Into One <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#clasped-into-one}

*On the second day, and in the Tabernacle curtains, separating and joining work together.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:6–8 aloud. Then read Exodus 26:1–14 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** God makes a "vault" (Hebrew *raqia*, often translated "firmament" or "expanse") to separate water from water, and the notes say the separation creates a habitable space: "Separating things is not rejection. It is how space for life gets made." The ten curtains are joined by 50 blue loops and 50 gold clasps, with the stated purpose "so that the tabernacle is a unit."[^3] What do you notice about separating and joining in these two passages? Which details caught your attention?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> One example in the notes compares the gold clasps to connecting two people who share a similar story so they can encourage each other, turning separate experiences into a shared community.[^5] When has someone made that kind of connection for you, or when have you made it for someone else?

*Sit together in silence for two minutes, then take up the next question.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** Marty's first prompt: "How can we make God's love real for others so that there's unity and access to the presence of God?"[^3] Think of one person or setting in your life as you answer.

</div>

### Frames and a Veil <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#frames-and-a-veil}

*The frames and the veil give the dwelling structure and mark layers of nearness.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:6–8 aloud again. Then read Exodus 26:15–37 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The frames give the tent "structure and definition," marking what is inside and outside. The veil, woven with cherubim, separates the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place, so access to God's presence comes in layers, and the cherubim echo those who guarded the way back to Eden in Genesis 3:24.[^6] What does this layered arrangement suggest to you about how people come near to God, and to one another?

*Sit together in silence for two minutes.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Marty's second prompt: "How can you discern what to keep private and what to share generously? How and where do we draw boundaries that ease the burden on others?" The notes offer an example: someone who has come through grief may tell a close friend the full story, doubts and anger included, and in a church small group share only how God met them. "Both are honest."[^7] How do you decide how much of your story belongs in a given setting?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Churches differ in how much personal sharing they expect or welcome, from public testimony to keeping struggles private. What did your church background teach you, spoken or unspoken, about sharing struggles and stories of God's help? What from that would you like to keep, and what would you like to change in how you share now?

</div>

### Moving Like Rain <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#moving-like-rain}

*Rain moves between the waters above and the waters below and never stays in one place.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Marty's third prompt: "How do we flow between these sacred spaces seen in the Mishkan like water falling like rain, nourishing, slowly re-entering God's presence and taking that presence to others?" The notes picture this as time alone in prayer followed by a return to family life, work, or service with renewed patience.[^8] Where in your ordinary week do you go up into God's presence, and where do you come back down to others?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** Marty's challenge is to pick one or two specific relationships this week or month, and to ask what from your own story could be refreshing and life-giving to that person, and how deep or shallow that sharing should be.[^9] Without naming anyone unless you wish, describe the relationship that comes to mind and what you might bring to it.

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> God creates by separating and brings people near by joining. The waters above and below, the curtains clasped into one tent, the frames that define the space, and the veil that guards the Most Holy Place all show that boundaries and unity work together in love. Our stories of meeting God, including those from dark seasons, are not meant to stay locked away, and they are not meant to be poured out the same way on everyone. Like rain, we keep moving: into God's presence for renewal, then out to the people around us.
{: .guide-summary}

*Read Genesis 1:6–8 aloud one last time, then sit together in silence for one minute.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, name one person you will be intentional with this week (a first name, or simply someone at work, is enough).

</div>
