**This week:** Gathered waters produce dry ground — community. The altar is hollow until the people gather. The stones must come uncut. The feast is spontaneous. The seed is scattered without a brand.

### Opening <span class="guide-time">10 min</span> {#opening}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-opening" markdown="1">

**Opening Question:** Think of a community — a family, a church, a team, a neighborhood — that made you feel like you belonged without asking you to change first. What did it feel like? And what specifically made that belonging possible?

</div>

### Genesis 1:9–13 — Day 3 <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#genesis-1913-day-3}

*Read Genesis 1:9–13 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Day 3 has two creative acts: waters gather, dry land appears; then the land produces vegetation. Josh reads the gathering of chaotic waters as community forming, and dry ground as something qualitatively new — stability where varied life can take root.[^1] What is the difference between a collection of friendships and an actual community? Have you experienced both? What made one produce dry ground and the other not?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> Day 3 is the first time creation takes part in its own creation — God tells the land to produce, and the land does the work.[^2] Josh connects this to rabbinic discipleship: disciples often do things their teacher never imagined. Who have you "produced" that surprised you by surpassing what you thought you gave them?

</div>

### Exodus 27 — The Altar and Courtyard <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#exodus-27-the-altar-and-courtyard}

*Read Exodus 27:1–5, 9, 19 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The bronze altar is hollow and portable.[^3] At each campsite it was set over a heap of local earth and uncut local stones. The altar only exists where the people gather, and it is made of the earth of *that* place. What does that suggest about the relationship between the people and the worship space? What is the "local earth" that your community brings to God's table?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Exodus 20:25 commands that altar stones must not be cut with tools. Josh's application: communities that require people to reshape themselves before they can belong are "raising a sword" against those people.[^4] Where in your own community are you cutting stones to make people fit before they can belong?

</div>

### The Seed Principle and the Feast <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#the-seed-principle-and-the-feast}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The peace offering must be eaten within one or two days.[^5] One family has more meat than it can eat; others in the courtyard have nothing. God doesn't command the feast — God sets up conditions where people choose to start it. Is there a surplus in your life right now — food, time, attention, wisdom — that the conditions of your life are pushing you to share? What would the spontaneous feast look like?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** Plants scatter seed and do not brand it.[^6] Josh's goal is that someone far away starts a BEMA discussion group and nobody knows Marty's name. Where in your current season are you holding your seed too tightly? What would it look like to scatter it without needing to watch it grow?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> *"Day 3 gives us community as the place where dry ground appears — stable, capable of supporting varied life that reproduces beyond what any one person planned. The altar is hollow until you gather. The stones must come uncut. The feast happens not by command but by proximity and surplus. The seed scatters without a label."*

### Looking Ahead — Week 4: Day 4 and the Priestly Garments {#looking-ahead-week-4-day-4-and-the-priestly-garments}

**[Ep. 328 — Sanctuary: Listening by the Mishkan](/guides/328)** (2:00:54) — *Note: This is the longest episode in the series.*

Day 4 places the sun, moon, and stars in the sky. The stars are barely mentioned — yet they are the lights that matter most in real darkness. You cannot work by starlight, but you can know where you are and what season you are in. The priests are like the stars: not blinding, but navigational. Each of their garments communicates something about what a priest does: shoulder stones carry the whole community; the breastpiece holds each tribe's name over the heart; bells make the priest findable; pomegranates promise fruit given away; the gold plate carries the people's guilt into God's presence.

<div class="guide-homework" markdown="1">

**Before Week 4:** *Who in your life has functioned like a star for you — not solving your problem, but helping you know what season you were in? What did they do, specifically?*

</div>
