### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Jesus crossed to the pagan side of the lake, freed a man no society wanted, and sent him home with nothing but his story. That one story was enough to change a region.

**This week:** Back in Capernaum, four friends dig through a roof, and Jesus forgives before he heals. The title he uses for himself, "Son of Man," opens a startling idea about what the Messiah came to do.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode notices that Jesus responds when he sees "their faith," the faith of the four friends, which suggests faith is something visible. It argues that Jesus' healings were usually teaching tools, and that here the lesson is forgiveness. The title "Son of Man" can simply mean "a human being," but it can also point to Daniel's figure coming on the clouds. Marty passes on a teaching from his own teacher: the expectation that the one who suffered unjustly would return to bring justice, and Jesus' shocking reversal, that he comes to forgive. Marty openly admits this may be "too large of a leap," so treat it as a reading to weigh, not a settled fact.

The session then follows Jesus to Matthew's dinner with tax collectors and sinners, where Jesus quotes Hosea, "I desire mercy, not sacrifice," and to his sayings about fasting and new wine in old wineskins. The episode uses these to introduce three layers of Jewish interpretation: *p'shat* (the plain meaning), *remez* (a hint to another text), and *drash* (the deeper meaning). Forgiveness is tender ground, so the closing questions leave room to pass.

### Faith Through the Roof <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#faith-through-the-roof}

*Four friends refuse to accept "the house is full," and Jesus sees their faith.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Mark 2:1–5 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes explain that Jesus was teaching in an *insula*, a multi-story residential building in Capernaum that could hold many people, and that digging through a roof of mud, branches, and tiles would have taken hours.[^1] Then Jesus forgives the man "BECAUSE HE SAW THEIR FAITH." The original notes conclude: "Faith is visible. It is seen. It influences action."[^2] Whose faith does the text say Jesus saw? What does that suggest about faith that our individualistic habits might miss?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The episode says healing was not Jesus' primary mission but a teaching tool, since once Jesus heals one person, the question becomes "what about everyone else?" The paralytic becomes, in the hosts' words, "a prop" in a larger lesson about forgiveness and authority.[^3] The original notes invite the group to wrestle with this: "Do you agree or disagree with Marty? Why? Why not?"[^4] Where do you land, and why?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes ask, "Are we willing to go to uncomfortable, inconvenient, or even socially awkward lengths to bring people to Jesus?"[^5] Think of someone in your life who needs wholeness they cannot reach on their own. What would carrying them to Jesus look like in practice, and what roof might you have to dig through?

</div>

### The Son of Man Who Forgives <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#the-son-of-man-who-forgives}

*A title with two meanings, and a reversal of what people expected the Son of Man to bring.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Mark 2:6–12 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes say the teachers of the law correctly recognized that only God can forgive sins in the cosmic sense Jesus was claiming. Jesus does not dispute that; instead he heals the man to show he has the authority he claims.[^6] Notice the question Jesus asks in verse 9. What do you think he wants the onlookers to understand by answering it with a healing?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes describe two meanings of "Son of Man." In Ezekiel, God calls the prophet "son of man," meaning simply "you're just a human being." In Daniel 7, "one like a son of man" comes on the clouds to establish justice.[^7]

*Read Daniel 7:13–14 aloud.*

What does Daniel's figure come to do? How would someone who knew this passage have heard Jesus claim the title while forgiving a man's sins?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The episode passes on a teaching from Marty's teacher, Ray: the expectation that the Son of Man would be Abel, the first person to suffer unjustly, returning to bring justice. Jesus' claim turns that around. Ray called it "the only time in Jewish history where anybody has had the audacity to suggest that when Abel comes back, he's going to look at Cain, he's going to look him in the eyeballs, and he's going to say, 'I forgive you,' rather than, 'Here's what you got coming.'"[^8] Marty admits "he might be making too large of a leap," yet the notes insist that "forgiveness plays a key part in this lesson and story" either way.[^9] What persuades you about this reading, and what makes you cautious? What changes if the one with every right to vengeance chooses to forgive?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The original notes say the crowd was in awe when they realized "God has given them the authority to forgive," which takes us "back to the sermon on the Mount and engaging in the act of forgiveness."[^10] The notes also admit that "Discussing forgiveness is always a potential landmine," and ask, "What does it look like to forgive and hold boundaries?"[^11] Without naming anyone, think about the person you brought to mind this week. What makes forgiving them hard, and what would it mean to forgive while still keeping wise boundaries?

</div>

### Mercy, Not Sacrifice <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#mercy-not-sacrifice}

*At Matthew's table and in a saying about wineskins, Jesus points back to the prophets.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 9:9–17 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes point out that Matthew, a tax collector, was himself an outsider, and that after Jesus calls him he immediately invites his tax collector and sinner friends to dinner. "Sharing a meal signifies right relationship and acceptance."[^12] The notes explain that when Jesus quotes Hosea 6:6, he is using a *remez*, a deliberate quotation meant to call the whole surrounding passage to mind, and that the drash "never contradicts the p'shat."[^13] Following the prophets' own critique, the original notes sum up the deeper meaning: "If you're not looking out for the mamzer, you're missing the point."[^14] What do you notice about who is at the table and who is asking questions about it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> On new wine and old wineskins, the notes give the *p'shat* plainly: "You cannot force new ideas into old, rigid worldviews." The hosts admit BEMA itself works like new wine: refreshing to those yearning for more, rejected by those content with their current system, which "isn't about intelligence or sincerity, but about readiness for transformation."[^15] Where have you had to find a new wineskin for something you were learning? Where might you be resisting one now?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> Four friends showed their faith with their hands, and Jesus saw it. He forgave before he healed, because forgiveness was the lesson. In claiming the title "Son of Man," Jesus may have been saying something astonishing: that the one with every right to justice comes to forgive. Then he sat at a table full of outsiders and reminded everyone of the prophet's words, "I desire mercy, not sacrifice." The forgiveness at the center of the Sermon on the Mount has walked off the page and into a crowded house in Capernaum.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, or simply pass: what is one small step toward forgiveness you could take this week?

</div>
