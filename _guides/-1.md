### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This episode is an introduction to the BEMA way of reading. The name comes from the raised platform in ancient synagogues where the Torah was read. That platform stood in the center of the room with the community gathered around it, rather than at the front like a stage. The hosts describe BEMA's starting point: the Bible is "a Jewish book written by Jewish authors to a Jewish audience," and reading it well means asking the questions its first hearers would have asked.

The episode also tells the story behind the method. Marty describes his own crisis of faith, the trips to Israel that made him start his understanding over, and the scholarship that reconnected Jewish and Christian study after a long separation. We will also hear the plan for the road ahead: five sessions from Genesis to Revelation and on into church history, beginning with the Torah. Tonight is mostly about how we want to learn together.

### A Platform in the Center <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#a-platform-in-the-center}

*The name BEMA describes a room arranged around the text.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** In ancient synagogues the bema stood in the center, not at the front, with the community gathered around it. The notes say this placement symbolized that Scripture, not human opinion, "should be at the center of study and discussion."[^1] Picture the room where you usually hear the Bible taught. Where does the text sit, where does the teacher stand, and where are you? What does that arrangement teach without saying a word?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The 2024 version of the episode puts it this way: "Only God's words are elevated; human interpretation remains secondary." Yet after the text was read, the community "wrestled together with its meaning and application."[^2] How do you hold those two together: a text above everyone, and a room full of voices working out what it means?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** BEMA starts from the conviction that the Bible is "a Jewish book written by Jewish authors to a Jewish audience."[^3] The notes add that BEMA tries to ask "Jewish questions" rather than impose later theological frameworks on the text.[^4] Each of our traditions has its own inherited questions that we bring to Scripture. What is one question your church taught you to ask of every passage? What might be gained, and what might feel risky, in setting it aside long enough to hear a different one?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The notes describe BEMA as a model where content is delivered and then "local discussion groups" process it together, so the community aspect travels with the material.[^5] We are one of those groups now. What would you need from this group over the coming months to keep the text, not any one of us, at the center?

</div>

### Dumping Out the File Cabinet <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#dumping-out-the-file-cabinet}

*Marty's own journey began with a crisis and a willingness to start over.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** Marty describes a "crisis of faith" and a church environment that lacked space for processing difficult questions. His trips to Israel in 2008 and 2010 required "dumping out" his mental "file cabinet" of biblical images and starting over.[^6] What do you think is in your own file cabinet: images of Bible stories you formed early and have rarely examined? Name one.

*Read Genesis 1:1–5 aloud, slowly, as if you had never heard it.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** We will spend next week in this chapter, so this is only a practice run. As you listen, notice the details: what happens, in what order, and what words repeat. Then compare that with the picture of this passage you already carry. What did you notice tonight that your mental picture leaves out or rearranges?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes explain that for about 1,800 years Jewish and Christian scholarship worked separately, until scholars like Jacob Neusner began bridging them in the 1970s. Later discoveries such as the Dead Sea Scrolls also shaped this recovery.[^7] Why might it matter for ordinary readers like us that this conversation was missing for so long and has only recently resumed?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes observe that "authentic questions and intellectual honesty can strengthen rather than weaken faith when pursued within supportive community structures."[^8] Some of us were taught that doubt is dangerous; others found doubt was where faith finally became our own. What has your own experience been? What would help you stay in the room when a session unsettles something you were sure of?

</div>

### Four Pillars for the Road <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#four-pillars-for-the-road}

*Text, Community, Discipleship, and Wrestling hold up the whole study.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** The four pillars are Text (better biblical understanding), Community (the importance of relationship), Discipleship (pursuing Jesus's model), and Wrestling (engaging difficult questions).[^9] The notes say the pillars together guard against "purely academic study" on one side and "purely experiential faith" on the other.[^10] Which pillar comes most naturally to you, and which one do you tend to neglect?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q10:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> BEMA began as a class for college students through Impact Campus Ministries on the Palouse, because students are independent thinkers who "haven't yet become entrenched in established patterns."[^11] Whatever our age, what patterns of reading the Bible are you open to having loosened this year?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> BEMA takes its name from a platform at the center of a room, with people gathered around the text rather than lined up in front of a speaker. Marty's journey began when his old pictures of the Bible stopped holding, and he had to dump out the file cabinet and start again. The four pillars give us a way to walk that road together: read the text closely, stay in community, follow Jesus, and welcome the wrestling. We start next week at the very beginning, with a poem about creation and rest.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence, what is one hope you bring to this group for the months ahead?

</div>
