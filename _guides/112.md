### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** We followed the mustard seed to Ezekiel's tree that shelters every kind of bird, and the leaven to Sarah baking for strangers, and saw a Kingdom that grows through small acts of welcome to outsiders.

**This week:** The disciples ask Jesus to explain, and he answers by telling the story again in a new way. The notes connect his parables back to the four soils and draw one clear instruction from the Parable of the Weeds.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The notes for this episode are a short outline, so this guide stays close to what they record. Their first observation is that Jesus seems to answer the disciples' request for an explanation "NOT with answers but by retelling the parables in a new way."[^1] The notes suggest this means there is more to dig for, and that most of the parables in question tie back to the four soils of Matthew 13:1–23.[^2]

The second half of the outline asks what the Kingdom is like and what our role in it is. The notes' answer is direct: "DO NOT GO WEEDING!" The weeding belongs to Jesus and takes place at the harvest, and our task is to engage the work of the Kingdom.[^3]

### Opening <span class="guide-time">10 min</span> {#opening}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-opening" markdown="1">

**Opening Question:** Think of a time you asked someone for a straight answer and got a story instead. How did you react at first, and what did the story eventually teach you?

</div>

### An Explanation That Retells <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#an-explanation-that-retells}

*The disciples finally get Jesus alone and ask him to explain the Parable of the Weeds.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 13:24–30 aloud, then Matthew 13:36–43.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Put the two passages side by side. What does Jesus's explanation identify, and what does it leave unexplained? The notes observe that the explanation contains "no reference to any of the elements of PaRDeS that we've learned about," which "likely means there are more pashat, remez and drash teachings."[^4] What questions are you still left with after hearing the explanation?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q2:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes suggest several ways to keep digging: "What keywords in the passages could you search for in the rest of the Text?" and "What translations can you use that hint at unique words you may not find in other translations?"[^5] Choose two or three keywords from these passages as a group. If you searched last week, share what you found. Where else in Scripture might these words lead?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q3:** The notes wonder about Jesus's purpose in tying these parables back to the soils: "Is he preparing his disciples for an upcoming challenge?"[^6] When has God left you with a story to live into rather than an explanation to file away, and how did that shape you?

</div>

### Which Soil Are You Now? <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#which-soil-are-you-now}

*The notes return to the four soils and give each one a voice.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 13:18–23 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Read Jesus's explanation slowly. For each soil, what happens to the word, and what causes it? Report the verb Jesus uses for each.

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes put each soil into everyday words. The path sounds like "Whatever, whatever. Who cares?" The rocks sound like "Oh, man. I love learning these things. I love listening to your guys' podcast. It's so great," from people who never build deep roots. The thorns are people who "understand the message," and even "know what God's calling them to do," but are choked by degrees, jobs, to-do lists, wealth and security. The good soil belongs to people who are "doing the work, they're learning how to dive into the Text."[^7] Which description surprised you, and why?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The notes ask each of us directly: "What kind of soil are each of you?"[^8] Thinking about the past few months, not your whole life, which voice sounds most like yours, and what would it take to move one step toward good soil?

</div>

### No Weeding Allowed <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#no-weeding-allowed}

*Jesus's story ends with an owner who refuses to let his servants pull the weeds.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 13:28–30 aloud again, and then Matthew 13:40–43.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** In the story, who wants to pull the weeds, who refuses, and when does the weeding finally happen? Who does it? The notes summarize: "Jesus says that is HIS responsibility," and "The weeding will take place at the Harvest by his Angels."[^9]

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes ask, "What are examples of what it looks like to us to 'weed the Kingdom?' Are we guilty of that?"[^10] Every tradition, including the ones in this room, has its own ways of deciding who belongs. Without pointing at any particular group, what does weeding look like in church life generally, and why is it so tempting?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes also ask, "What is the risk if we spend our time weeding before the Harvest?"[^11] In the parable, what is the owner afraid will happen to the wheat? What might that look like among real people?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** The notes close with a charge: "Be about the task of engaging the work of the Kingdom."[^12] If you set down the job of weeding, what Kingdom work would you have more time and energy for this week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> The disciples asked for an explanation, and Jesus gave them the story again, which means there is still more to dig for. His parables keep returning to the soils and to the question of what kind of ground we are. In the Parable of the Weeds, the servants want to clean up the field, and the owner tells them to wait. The weeding belongs to him and comes at the harvest. Our task is not to sort the field but to engage the work of the Kingdom.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one piece of "weeding" you want to hand back to Jesus?

</div>
