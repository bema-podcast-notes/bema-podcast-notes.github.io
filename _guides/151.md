### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Righteousness comes from God as a gift, not a wage, and Abraham, counted righteous before he was circumcised, is the father of everyone who trusts, Jew and Gentile alike.

**This week:** Paul keeps going through Romans 5–6, arguing that grace is far stronger than sin and that baptism pictures dying to a life driven by fear and rising to a life of trust.

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode reads Romans 5–6 verse by verse, through a Jewish lens rather than through later Augustinian theology.[^1] It explains that *pistis*, "faith," means trust in God's promises, and it notes that many scholars now prefer to translate *pistis Christou* as "faith of Christ" rather than "faith in Christ."[^2] It points out Paul's use of a rabbinic teaching tool, *kal va'chomer* ("lesser to greater"), signalled by the phrase "how much more," to show that grace always outpaces sin.[^3]

The episode also makes two claims that may land differently across our traditions. It argues that our identity as God's image-bearers is more fundamental than our fallen condition, and it describes baptism as a picture of dying to fear-based living and being raised to new life.[^4] This session gives room to read the passages closely and to hear how each of us has been taught to understand them.

### How Much More <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#how-much-more}

*Paul argues from the lesser to the greater: if sin could do so much, grace can do far more.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Romans 5:6–11 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Read verses 9 and 10 again slowly. What phrase repeats, and what two things is Paul comparing each time? The notes explain that this is *kal va'chomer*, a "lesser to greater" argument, which you can spot by the phrase "how much more."[^5]

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** Look at verse 7. The notes say Paul is contrasting rule-keeping with worth: people "rarely die for someone merely because they're righteous (follow rules), but they will sacrifice for someone whose life is inherently valuable and 'good' (worth preserving)."[^6] What do you make of Paul saying Christ died for us "while we were still sinners"? What does that suggest about how God values people?

*Read Romans 5:15–21 aloud.*

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say Paul uses Adam as a "pattern," a representative figure for humanity's tendency toward sin, with Jesus representing God's greater work.[^7] However your tradition understands Adam, how does the comparison between the "one man" Adam and the "one man" Jesus work as an argument in this passage?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** The episode sums up the passage: "Grace always outpaces sin - where sin increases, grace increases all the more," and says grace is "exponentially more powerful than sin."[^8] Where in your life, or in the world, does sin feel bigger than grace right now? What would it look like to apply "how much more" to that situation?

</div>

### Image-Bearers First <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#image-bearers-first}

*The episode asks which is more basic to who we are: the image of God or our fallenness.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Genesis 1:26–31 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** What does God say about humanity in this passage, and what word does God use to describe everything at the end? The notes say God declares creation "good" and "never revokes this assessment."[^9] Where in the Bible, or in your church experience, have you seen that assessment affirmed or questioned?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The episode acknowledges that sin is "real and destructive," but argues that "our identity as image-bearers of God is more fundamentally true than our fallen condition."[^10] Our traditions have taught different things about human nature and depravity. What were you taught to believe about who you are at the deepest level, and how does the episode's emphasis sit with that teaching?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes explain that *pistis* from a Hebrew perspective means "trust," specifically "trusting in the promises of God rather than creedal affirmation alone."[^11] They add that "faith of Christ" makes sense because "Abraham could demonstrate the same faith as Christ without having creedal knowledge of Jesus," and that "Christians are called to demonstrate the same trust that Abraham and Jesus showed."[^12] What changes when faith is something we share with Jesus, not only something we direct toward him?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** If you truly believed that being God's image-bearer is more basic to you than your failures, what is one way you would treat yourself, or someone else, differently this week?

</div>

### Buried Alive <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#buried-alive}

*Paul turns to baptism as the picture of a death and a new life.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Romans 6:1–11 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q9:** List the words in this passage connected with death and burial, and the words connected with life. What does Paul say has died, and what does he say we now walk in?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** The notes say "Baptism serves as a physical picture of dying to the old self and being raised to new life," and that the Didache shows early Christian baptism "involved forsaking pagan idolatry and embracing a new way of life based on trust rather than fear."[^13] Our traditions practice and explain baptism in different ways. What has your tradition taught you that baptism is and does, and what in Romans 6 does your tradition especially emphasize?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q11:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say "Eternal life" refers "not to duration but to quality - the kind of life God intended for creation," and that "This life begins now, not just after death."[^14] How would you describe that kind of life to someone who has only heard "eternal life" as life after death?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q12:** The episode says sin stems from fear: "fear of not having enough, not being enough, or insecurity about our place in the world." Grasping grace, we "die to this fear-based living and embrace a life of trust, generosity, and self-sacrifice."[^15] Which of those fears do you most need to bury, and what would one act of trust look like in its place?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> In Romans 5, Paul argues from the lesser to the greater: if sin entered through one man and brought death, how much more does grace through Jesus bring life. Christ died for us not because we kept the rules but because God counts us worth saving, image-bearers whose goodness God has never revoked. Faith is trust in God's promises, the same trust Abraham and Jesus showed. Baptism pictures a burial: the old life driven by fear goes into the water, and we rise to a life of trust that begins now.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one fear you want to leave in the water?

</div>
