### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Noah the insulator, Lot the assimilator and Abraham the engager left us with a diagnostic question: which one am I?[^1]

**This week:** A guest conversation with Dr. John Whittaker about making the Bible accessible for everyday life, about Psalm 1, and about stepping out in faith at any stage of life.[^2]

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

Dr. John Whittaker taught at Boise Bible College for nineteen years (Marty was one of his students), then joined a church plant called The Pursuit, and later launched an online ministry of audio teaching and commentary.[^3] His phrase for his approach is "blue jeans theology": theology dressed in everyday clothes, refusing to choose between scholarly depth and practical accessibility.[^4]

Whittaker keeps returning to Psalm 1, the tree planted by streams of water, as his picture of what Bible study is for. He tells stories of small moments that changed his life (two books handed to him by a coworker at a Safeway bakery) and of resources reaching believers in Nigeria and the Philippines.[^5] He also talks honestly about leaving secure positions with no guaranteed income, and about his conviction that God is more interested in what He does in us than through us.[^6]

### Theology in Blue Jeans <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#theology-in-blue-jeans}

*Whittaker's ministry grew out of watching people hungry for the Bible and unable to get at it.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** As a teenager working cleanup in a Safeway bakery, Whittaker was given two books by a Christian coworker who sold donuts: Tozer's *The Pursuit of God* and Brother Lawrence's *The Practice of the Presence of God*. The notes say those books "set the trajectory for his entire life and ministry."[^5] Why do you think such small, unplanned acts can shape a whole life? What does that suggest about the ordinary moments in your own week?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** After first hearing Whittaker preach, a woman at The Pursuit walked out in tears saying she felt "like a kid in a candy store," because for the first time someone was helping her understand the Bible.[^7] What do you think had kept her from understanding for so long? What first helped you begin to understand the Bible?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes describe blue jeans theology as "a rejection of ivory tower academics disconnected from real-world application" and "a commitment to translation work," taking complex ideas and making them understandable "without losing integrity."[^4] Where have you seen depth without accessibility, or accessibility without depth? Who in your life needs the Bible "in blue jeans" right now, and what could you offer them?

</div>

### A Tree by Streams of Water <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#a-tree-by-streams-of-water}

*Psalm 1 is the picture Whittaker keeps coming back to for what discipleship produces.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Psalm 1 aloud twice, with two different readers.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** Share the image or phrase you noticed this week, or one that stands out now. Look closely at the first verse: what verbs describe the person's relationship to the wicked, and what movement do you notice from one to the next? What does the psalm set against each other in the rest of the poem?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q5:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes summarize what Psalm 1 shapes for Whittaker: the source of wisdom (the word of the Lord), the goal of discipleship ("flourishing life"), the means of transformation ("meditation on Scripture day and night") and the outcome ("fruitfulness in every season").[^8] The psalm says the tree yields fruit "in season." What does that suggest about flourishing, and how is it different from expecting constant, visible results?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** Whittaker builds his resources around how people actually live: listening "while commuting, exercising, doing laundry," using otherwise "dead" time.[^9] What would delighting in and meditating on Scripture realistically look like in your rhythm right now? Name one stretch of "dead" time that could become root time.

</div>

### Stepping Out at Any Age <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#stepping-out-at-any-age}

*Whittaker's own story is a series of risks taken without a safety net.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** Whittaker left a nineteen-year teaching position to join a church that "couldn't afford him for at least a year," during his children's expensive teenage years, and later launched his current ministry "with no guaranteed income."[^6] Through it he holds that "God is more interested in what He does in us than what He does through us."[^2] What do you make of that statement? How would it change the way you measure a season of your own life, including a season that did not go the way you planned?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q8:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes record his conviction that five-year plans "are fine but must be held loosely" and that "Failure isn't fatal - you can pick yourself up, dust yourself off, and figure out the next step."[^10] Where are you holding a plan tightly right now? What would holding it more loosely look like, while still being faithful? (Share as much as you like.)

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> John Whittaker's life was redirected by two books from a coworker at a bakery, and he has spent his ministry trying to hand the Bible to people in a form they can actually use. Blue jeans theology refuses to choose between depth and accessibility. Psalm 1 gives the goal: people rooted in God's word like trees by streams of water, fruitful in their season. Whittaker's own risks remind us that plans can be held loosely, because God is more interested in what He does in us than in what He does through us.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one small way you could hand Scripture to someone in "blue jeans" this week?

</div>
