### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Jesus turned to his disciples and called them salt, light and a city on a hill, a gate where the poor outside the walls could find provision and hope.

**This week:** Jesus pauses to answer the question his blessing on outsiders raises: is he throwing out the Bible?

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

This episode takes on Jesus's statement that he came not to abolish the Law and the Prophets but to fulfill them. Many Christians were taught that this means Jesus completed the law so we no longer need it. The episode instead explains the words in their rabbinic setting: to "fulfill" Torah is to interpret it correctly and walk it out in life, and to "abolish" it is to misinterpret it or fail to live it.[^1] In effect, Jesus is inviting people to watch him to see how the Bible should be read and lived.

The episode then introduces *s'micha*, the authority to offer new interpretations of Torah, and argues that Jesus received it at his baptism from John and from the voice of God.[^2] That authority is what lets him say, "You have heard it said, but I say to you." The episode closes with a challenge to read all of Scripture, including its hardest passages, through Jesus's own lens of loving God and loving others.[^3]

### What Fulfill Means <span class="guide-time">25 min</span> {#what-fulfill-means}

*In Jesus's world, to fulfill Torah was to read it rightly and live it rightly.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 5:17–20 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** Read the passage slowly, perhaps twice. What do you notice about how Jesus talks about the Law: how long it lasts, how small a commandment he cares about, and what kind of righteousness he expects? Which line surprises you most?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The notes explain that in the rabbinic world fulfillment requires "Correct intellectual/theological interpretation of Torah" and "Walking out that interpretation through behavior and obedience (halach - one's walk)."[^4] Abolishment happens when Torah is interpreted incorrectly, or interpreted correctly but not lived out.[^5] The notes put Jesus's meaning simply: "If you want to see how to interpret and live your Bible correctly, watch me."[^6] How does reading the passage this way change what Jesus is claiming?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes describe a common teaching that Jesus fulfilled the law by "Accomplishing all 613 commandments perfectly," like "Beating all the 'levels' of the Torah 'video game,'" so that we "no longer need to follow Old Testament law."[^7] Our traditions have taught the relationship between the Old Testament and the New in different ways, and each has reasons. What has the way you were taught helped you see, and what does the rabbinic reading add or challenge?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** If "fulfill" means interpreting rightly and then walking it out, think about the last week of your life. If someone had only watched how you lived, without hearing a word you said, what would they conclude you believe the Bible is about?

</div>

### Authority to Interpret <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#authority-to-interpret}

*The crowds noticed that Jesus taught as someone with authority, and the episode explains why that mattered.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 3:16–17 and Matthew 7:28–29 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes explain that *s'micha* is "the rabbinic term for authority to teach and interpret Torah in new ways," and that "S'micha was conferred by two rabbis who already possessed it, through public laying on of hands."[^8] The episode argues Jesus received it at his baptism from two sources: John the Baptist and "God's voice from heaven declaring Jesus as his beloved Son."[^9] Look at the crowd's reaction at the end of the sermon. What do you think they were wrestling with, and how does the baptism scene answer their question?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q6:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes say fulfilling the Prophets means "living out the prophetic vision with his life," pointing to Isaiah 53's suffering servant and Amos's call to "let justice roll."[^10] They also note that Jesus says "the Law and the Prophets" here, while Luke 24:44 adds the Psalms.[^11] Read Luke 24:44 aloud. What difference does it make to think of prophecy as something Jesus lived out rather than only something he checked off?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q7:** The notes say that at the heart of the Beatitudes, Jesus connects hunger for righteousness with mercy, and that this was "counterintuitive to a community living under Roman oppression who might have expected a message of righteous vengeance."[^12] Think of someone who has wronged you or people you love. What would hungering for righteousness look like there if it came out as mercy instead of vengeance?

</div>

### Reading Everything Through Jesus <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#reading-everything-through-jesus}

*If all authority belongs to Jesus, then his lens governs how we read the rest of the Bible.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 28:18 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The notes draw out the implication: "followers of Jesus do not have s'micha," so "Christians must read all of scripture through the lens Jesus provides: love God and love others."[^13] They cite Brian McLaren's observation: "Christians have made Jesus their savior, but Paul their Lord," meaning we often let Paul interpret Jesus rather than letting Jesus interpret Paul.[^14] In the traditions you have known, which way has the interpreting usually gone, and what difference would it make to reverse it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q9:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes apply this lens to the conquest stories in Joshua: whatever that book teaches, it must "Be aligned with Jesus's command to love God and love others," "Make us more loving toward people," and "Teach something about loving people, not justify violence."[^15] How might this lens help you with a passage of Scripture that troubles you, and what questions does it leave open?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q10:** The notes close by saying "every part of scripture - including difficult passages about violence, conquest, or exclusion - must be read through Jesus's framework of loving God and loving others."[^16] Name one passage you have avoided or struggled with. What would it look like to reread it this week, asking how it teaches you to love God and love people?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">15 min</span> {#closing}

> When Jesus announced God's favor on outsiders, his disciples wondered whether he was throwing out the Bible. His answer was the opposite. In the language of his day, to fulfill Torah was to interpret it correctly and walk it out faithfully, and Jesus was inviting everyone to watch him. With the authority affirmed at his baptism, he would go on to teach as no other teacher did, with mercy at the center of righteousness. All authority belongs to him, so every part of Scripture is read through his lens: love God and love others.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: what is one part of the Bible you want to "watch Jesus" interpret for you in the weeks ahead?

</div>

#### Looking Ahead {#looking-ahead}

**[Ep. 96 — But I Say Unto You](/guides/96)** (38:15): The episode works through the rest of Matthew 5, where Jesus gives six examples of what fulfilling Torah looks like: murder, adultery, divorce, oaths, retaliation and love for enemies. It argues that Jesus is not raising the bar but revealing what the commandments were always meant to do, and it rereads "be perfect" as a call to be set apart by love.

**What to bring next week:** Read Matthew 5:21–48. Each time Jesus contrasts what people have heard with what he says, write down one word that names what that teaching is really about.
