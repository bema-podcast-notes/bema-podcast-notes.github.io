### Where We Are {#where-we-are}

{% include guide-series.html %}

**Last week:** Jesus challenged a tradition that had cancelled the command to honor parents, and a Canaanite woman near Zarephath pointed him to Elijah's widow and asked for her crumbs, showing great faith from the ultimate outsider.

**This week:** Jesus feeds a second crowd, this time in Gentile territory, and then confronts his disciples with a question: do you have eyes and still not see?

### About This Session {#about-this-session}

The episode sets the feeding of the 4,000 beside the feeding of the 5,000. The first took place in Jewish territory; the second takes place in the Decapolis, a Gentile region, right after the encounter with the Canaanite woman. The hosts read the different numbers in the two stories as Jesus' way of teaching that his provision is enough for the nations too.

The disciples, worried about bread, miss the point, and Jesus warns them about "the yeast of the Pharisees and Sadducees." The episode reads that warning as aimed at a teaching that shuts out outsiders, and it immediately applies the warning to religious communities today, including our own. The session ends with the blind man at Bethsaida, whom Jesus heals in two stages, a living picture of disciples who see only partly.

### Bread for the Nations <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#bread-for-the-nations}

*The second feeding repeats the first, with different numbers in a different place.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 15:29–39 aloud, then read Matthew 14:13–21 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q1:** The notes set out the two feedings side by side: 5,000 people, 5 loaves, 2 fish and 12 baskets in Jewish territory; 4,000 people, 7 loaves, an unspecified number of fish and 7 baskets in the Decapolis.[^1] Using the list you brought, what differences do you see between the two stories, in numbers, in places, and in who is there?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q2:** The episode reads these differences symbolically. Four stands for the Gentiles (the four corners of the earth); seven stands for completion and also for the seven pagan nations named in Joshua 3:10; the fish go uncounted because the Gentiles do not have the two tablets of Moses. The message, in the notes' words: "Jesus is enough not just for Jews but for Gentile nations."[^2] The notes add that Jesus goes straight from the Canaanite woman into Gentile territory, so his travels act out what he is teaching.[^3] Which part of this reading do you find most persuasive, and which part do you want to test further?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q3:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes point out that the disciples worry about food in both stories, and that in the Decapolis they could not buy food from pagan cities, yet they still do not see that Jesus provides whatever the place or the people.[^4] Why do you think the disciples seem to have forgotten the first feeding so quickly? What makes past provision so easy to forget?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q4:** If Jesus' provision is enough for the nations, the question turns to us. Where do you find yourself rationing grace, quietly assuming there may not be enough for "them"? What would it look like to set a table for someone outside your circle this week?

</div>

### Eyes That Cannot See <span class="guide-time">30 min</span> {#eyes-that-cannot-see}

*Jesus warns about yeast, then heals a man in two stages to show his disciples what they are missing.*
{: .guide-note}

*Read Matthew 16:5–12 aloud, then read Mark 8:14–26 aloud.*
{: .guide-note}

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q5:** The notes describe the healing at Bethsaida as a teaching prop: Jesus takes the man outside the village (a private lesson), heals him partially so that he "has eyes but cannot see," makes his point, then completes the healing.[^5] Look at Mark 8:17–21 and list the questions Jesus asks his disciples. Then look at 8:22–26. What connections do you notice between the questions and the healing?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q6:** The episode explains that yeast is a common biblical image for sin, and reads "the yeast of the Pharisees and Sadducees" as a teaching that excludes outsiders and *mumzers* (outcasts), against Matthew's steady theme of Jesus including the outsider.[^6] The notes do not leave the warning in the first century; they say "modern religious communities can fall into similar patterns of excluding those deemed unworthy, unclean, or outside the boundaries."[^7] Why might a teaching like that be compared to yeast? How does it spread in a community without anyone deciding to spread it?

</div>

<div class="guide-q guide-q-optional" markdown="1">

**Q7:** <span class="guide-tag">Optional</span> The notes trace a progression in Matthew: the Sermon on the Mount, a Roman centurion's faith, the first visit to the Decapolis, the feeding of the 5,000, Peter on the water, the Canaanite woman, the feeding of the 4,000, the warning about yeast, and the blind man.[^8] Looking back over the past several weeks of our study, where have you watched the circle of Jesus' mission widening?

</div>

<div class="guide-q" markdown="1">

**Q8:** The blind man's first sight is blurry before it is clear. Where might you be like him: seeing something of Jesus' mission, but still not seeing certain people clearly? What would a "second touch" look like for you this week?

</div>

### Closing <span class="guide-time">20 min</span> {#closing}

> Jesus fed two crowds. In Jewish territory the numbers spoke of Moses, Torah and the twelve tribes; in the Decapolis they spoke of the nations, and there were baskets left over there too. The disciples, worried about bread, missed the lesson both times. So Jesus warned them about a yeast that shuts outsiders out, and then healed a man in two stages to show them their own partial sight. The episode invites us to admit where our own eyes still see blurry, and to let Jesus touch them again.
{: .guide-summary}

<div class="guide-q guide-q-closing" markdown="1">

**Closing Question:** In one sentence each, going around the room: who is one person or group you want to see more clearly this week?

</div>
