### Title & Source Summary

**Episode:** BEMA 340: Session 7 Sabbath Reflection on the Third Day, the Altar and the Courtyard  
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon and Brent Billings  
**Focus:** Genesis 1:9–13 (the third day of creation); Exodus 27:1–8 (the bronze altar); Exodus 27:9–19 (the Tabernacle courtyard and its entrance)

This episode belongs to Session 7, a slower, more reflective season built around Sabbath. It doesn't teach the way most BEMA episodes do. Brent reads Scripture aloud, Marty asks a question for reflection, and long stretches of silence follow. The episode follows the notes of Episode 327, in which Josh Bales connected the third day of creation to the theme of shared community life. Josh linked that day to the next items of the Mishkan (the Tabernacle): the bronze altar, the courtyard, and what he called "the Dance of Life." Three readings of the third day are paired with three Tabernacle passages. Each pairing leads to a question about raw community, growth we can't control, and giving our lives away so others can flourish.

### Key Takeaways

- Session 7 is meant to be slow and contemplative. Marty asks listeners who would rather skip it to stay with it, because there may be "treasures buried here."
- The episode is built on Episode 327 from Josh Bales's Mishkan series at the end of Session 6. Listeners who want the teaching behind it should go back to that episode.
- The **third day of creation** is when the gathered waters make room for dry land, and the land produces plants that carry their own seed. It speaks of space being made and of life that spreads beyond itself.
- The **bronze altar** is the first object a worshiper meets inside the Tabernacle gate. It ties the worship of God to real, physical, shared life.
- The **courtyard** is a defined space with an entrance that faces the sunrise. It shows a community with structure that still has an open door.
- The three reflection questions build on each other:
  1. What kind of space do you make in your worship for **raw community**?
  2. How can you open that space to **growth beyond your control**?
  3. How can you **give the seeds of your life away** so they can grow for others?
- The closing charge asks listeners to engage on purpose with the spaces they share with others, and to give of themselves generously for the healing and flourishing of other people.

### Main Concepts & Theories

#### 1. The Purpose of Session 7

- Session 7 is a "different kind of content." It is a Sabbath season of reading, reflection and silence.
- The format is simple:
  1. Brent reads a creation passage and a Tabernacle passage.
  2. Marty asks a question for reflection.
  3. Silence follows so listeners can think, pray and respond.
- **Note on the transcript:** The repeated "Thank you" and "Do you like it?" lines are almost certainly errors the transcription software made during the silences. They are not spoken content.

#### 2. The Third Day of Creation (Genesis 1:9–13)

- **Making room:** God gathers the waters "to one place" so dry ground can appear. Space is opened up where life can take root.
- **Life that reproduces:** The land produces "seed-bearing plants and trees… that bear fruit with seed in it." This life is built to spread and to feed others.
- **"Good" twice:** In the Genesis account, the third day is one of the days where "God saw that it was good" is said twice. Once is for the dry land and once is for the vegetation.
- **Link to community:** Following Josh's framework, the third day stands for our life together. Space is made, things grow, and the fruit carries seed for the people who come next.

#### 3. The Bronze Altar (Exodus 27:1–8)

- **Construction:** The altar is made of acacia wood overlaid with bronze. It is 5 cubits square and 3 cubits high, with a horn at each corner, bronze utensils and a bronze grating.
- **Hollow and portable:** It is "hollow, out of boards," and carried on poles. It travels with the people rather than staying in one fixed place.
- **Place of drawing near:** In the Hebrew tradition, offerings (*korbanot*) come from a root meaning "to draw near." The altar is where ordinary life (grain, animals, the produce of the land) is brought before God, often as a shared meal.
- **Link to the reflection:** The altar ties worship to tangible, shared, sometimes messy life. That is why Marty's first question is about **raw community**.

#### 4. The Courtyard (Exodus 27:9–19)

- **Dimensions:** The courtyard is 100 cubits by 50 cubits, enclosed by linen curtains 5 cubits high on bronze bases with silver hooks and bands.
- **The entrance:** The gate is on the east side, "toward the sunrise." It is a 20-cubit curtain of blue, purple and scarlet yarn and fine linen, embroidered by a skilled worker.
- **Structure with openness:** The courtyard has clear edges, and it also has a wide, beautiful way in. It is a space for people, not just for priests.
- **Link to the reflection:** Marty's second question asks how our community spaces can be opened for "greater growth beyond your control." Like the plants of the third day, a healthy community grows in ways no one can fully manage.

#### 5. Raw Community

- Marty defines it as "community that isn't predetermined or required to fit some kind of mold, but meeting people where they're at with real vulnerability."
- Raw community stands against faith that is performative, tightly scripted or exclusive.
- It takes a willingness to let go of control, which is the subject of the second question.

#### 6. Seeds and Generosity ("The Dance of Life")

- Seed-bearing plants exist partly to pass life on. Fruit is for eating, and seed is for the next generation.
- The third question asks how we can "give the seeds of your life away so that they can grow for others."
- This seems to be what Josh meant by "the Dance of Life": life given, received and passed on within a community.

### Examples & Applications

- **Hospitality without an agenda:** You could have someone over for a meal without planning the conversation. Let it be honest and unpolished, which is a modern picture of the shared meal at the altar.
- **Opening up a small group:** A Bible study could make room for doubt, grief or disagreement instead of requiring everyone to reach the same tidy conclusion.
- **Releasing control in ministry:** A leader could hand a project or a group to someone else and let it grow in directions they wouldn't have picked.
- **Mentoring:** You could invest time, skills or wisdom in a younger person, knowing the "fruit" may show up years later and somewhere else.
- **Gardening as a parable:** Anyone who has planted a garden knows you can water, weed and provide structure, but you can't make the seed grow. Community works the same way.
- **This week's practice:** As the closing charge suggests, pick one shared space (work, home, church or neighborhood) and do one intentional, generous thing in it toward someone's healing or flourishing.

### Potential Areas for Further Exploration

- **BEMA Episode 327:** Josh Bales's original teaching on the third day, the altar, the courtyard and the Dance of Life.
- **The other Mishkan episodes and their Session 7 reflections:** How the days of creation line up with the furnishings of the Tabernacle.
- **The Hebrew idea of *korban* (offering):** The link between sacrifice, drawing near and the shared meal in Leviticus 1–7.
- **Bronze versus gold in the Tabernacle:** Why the outer court uses bronze while the inner furnishings use gold, and what that change says about drawing near.
- **The Tabernacle and creation:** Scholarly work that reads the building of the Tabernacle (Exodus 25–40) as an echo of the creation account.
- **Seed imagery across Scripture:** Genesis 1, the parable of the sower (Matthew 13), John 12:24 ("unless a kernel of wheat falls to the ground and dies…") and 1 Corinthians 3:6–7.
- **Sabbath as a practice of letting go of control:** How rest, community and trust are connected.

### Personal Summary

This episode is an invitation to slow down and think about the people around you. The third day of creation shows God making room and filling it with life that carries seed for the future. The bronze altar shows worship tied to real, shared, unpolished life. The courtyard shows a community with structure and a wide-open door facing the sunrise. Together they lead to three questions: Do I make room for honest, vulnerable community? Am I willing to let it grow beyond my control? Am I giving away the seeds of my life so others can flourish? The charge for the week is practical: engage on purpose with the spaces you share, and give of yourself generously so others can heal and flourish.
