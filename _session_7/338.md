### Title & Source Summary

**Episode:** 338: Sabbath Reflection (Session 7): The Second Day: Love, Curtains, and Rain  
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon and Brent Billings  
**Focus:** Genesis 1:6–8 (the second day of creation); Exodus 26:1–14 (the tabernacle curtains and coverings); Exodus 26:15–30 (the upright frames); Exodus 26:31–37 (the veil and the entrance curtain)

This is the second of seven Sabbath reflections that open Session 7 of the BEMA podcast. These episodes are guided meditations, not teaching lectures. Each one is built on Josh Bosse's Mishkan series from the end of Session 6. This reflection draws on Josh's second episode (episode 326), which tied the second day of creation, when God separated the waters above from the waters below, to the theme of love. Josh linked that day to the curtains and the veil of the Mishkan (the tabernacle) and to the image of rain. Brent reads Scripture aloud and Marty gives reflection prompts, with silence left between them. Together they ask how believers can make God's love real for others. That means bringing unity and access to God's presence, knowing what to keep private and what to share, and moving between sacred spaces the way rain moves between heaven and earth. The episode builds on the previous reflection, which centered on personal testimonies and experiences of God.

### Key Takeaways

- **This is a guided reflection, not a lecture.** The episode is mostly Scripture reading, prompts, and silence. It is meant to be practiced, not just heard.
- **The second day is about separation that serves life.** God puts a "vault" (the firmament, or sky) between the waters. The separation creates ordered space where life can grow.
- **Curtains join as well as divide.** The tabernacle curtains are fastened with loops and gold clasps "so that the tabernacle is a unit." The structure that sets boundaries also creates unity.
- **The veil marks degrees of nearness.** The curtain separates the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place. Access to God's presence comes in layers.
- **Boundaries can be an act of love.** Marty asks how wise boundaries "ease the burden on others." Not everything has to be shared with everyone at once.
- **Rain is the picture of faithful movement.** Water moves between the waters above and the waters below. In the same way, believers can move between intimate and public spaces, taking in God's presence and carrying it to others.
- **Our testimonies are meant to be shared.** Stories of meeting God, even in deep darkness, are resources that can refresh and nourish other people.
- **The practical challenge is to pick one or two relationships.** Listeners are asked to be intentional with specific people this week or month.

### Main Concepts & Theories

#### 1. The Structure of the Sabbath Reflection Series

- Session 7 opens with **seven Sabbath reflections**, one for each day of creation.
- Each reflection uses one episode of **Josh Bosse's Mishkan series** as its anchor.
- This reflection matches **Day 2 of creation** with **the curtains, frames, and veil** of the tabernacle.
- The format repeats a pattern: **Scripture reading → reflection prompt → silence**. This happens three times, followed by a final reading and a closing charge.
- The show notes include a visual presentation for anyone who wants a visual aid.

#### 2. The Second Day of Creation (Genesis 1:6–8)

- God makes a **"vault" (Hebrew *raqia*, often translated "firmament" or "expanse")** to separate water from water.
- In the ancient Near Eastern picture of the world, the sky held back the waters above, and the waters below formed the seas and the deep.
- The separation **creates a habitable space**. Order comes out of formless chaos.
- The theme is **love expressed through boundaries**. Separating things is not rejection. It is how space for life gets made.

#### 3. The Curtains and Coverings (Exodus 26:1–14)

- There are ten curtains in **two sets of five**, joined by **50 blue loops** and **50 gold clasps**.
- The stated purpose is "so that the tabernacle is **a unit**."
- Over these go layers: **goat-hair curtains** (eleven), then **ram skins dyed red**, then **durable leather**.
- **Reflection theme:** Separate pieces are joined together into one dwelling. This suggests that love creates **unity and access** to God's presence.
- **Prompt:** *How can we make God's love real for others so that there's unity and access to the presence of God?*

#### 4. The Upright Frames (Exodus 26:15–30)

- The frames are made of **acacia wood overlaid with gold**, each 10 cubits long and 1.5 cubits wide, held together by gold rings and crossbars.
- The frames give the tent **structure and definition**. They mark what is inside and what is outside.
- Moses is told to set up the tabernacle "according to the plan shown you on the mountain."
- **Reflection theme:** Boundaries and structure.
- **Prompt:** *How can you discern what to keep private and what to share generously? How and where do we draw boundaries that ease the burden on others?*

#### 5. The Veil and the Entrance Curtain (Exodus 26:31–37)

- The **veil** is made of blue, purple, and scarlet yarn and fine linen, with **cherubim** woven into it. It separates the **Holy Place** from the **Most Holy Place**.
- The **Ark of the Covenant** and the **atonement cover** sit behind the veil.
- The **table** and **lampstand** stand outside the veil in the Holy Place.
- A separate **entrance curtain** hangs at the door of the tent.
- The result is **graded zones of holiness**: courtyard, then Holy Place, then Most Holy Place.
- The cherubim echo Eden (Genesis 3:24), where cherubim guarded the way back to God's presence.

#### 6. The Rain Metaphor

- Josh's image is **rain moving between the waters above and the waters below**.
- Rain comes down from heaven, waters the earth, and rises again as vapor. It never stays in one place.
- **Reflection theme:** Believers are meant to move between sacred spaces as well, going into God's presence and carrying it out again.
- **Prompt:** *How do we flow between these sacred spaces seen in the Mishkan like water falling like rain, nourishing, slowly re-entering God's presence and taking that presence to others?*

#### 7. Connection to the Previous Reflection

- Last week's reflection (Day 1) focused on **testimonies, stories, and tangible experiences** of God.
- This week asks what to *do* with them: **how to bring them to others** wisely and lovingly.
- Even experiences of "deep darkness" become something to share.

### Examples & Applications

1. **Sharing a testimony with discernment.** Someone who has come through grief may tell a close friend the full story, including the doubts and the anger. In a church small group, they may share only how God met them. Both are honest. The amount shared matches what each setting can hold.

2. **Mentoring at different depths.** A new believer may need a "surface" experience at first: hospitality, kindness, a shared meal. A mature friend going through a crisis may need deep, intimate prayer. Loving people well means moving between these levels the way rain falls on different kinds of ground.

3. **Boundaries that lighten others' loads.** Telling every struggle to a coworker who has no capacity for it can be a burden to them. Choosing the right person and the right time for heavy things is a form of love, much like the veil marking out the Most Holy Place.

4. **Bringing people together.** Like the gold clasps joining two sets of curtains, someone might connect two people who share a similar story so they can encourage each other. That turns separate experiences into a shared community.

5. **The rhythm of retreat and return.** Spending time alone in prayer, and then going back into family life, work, or service with renewed patience, is the rain cycle in practice: going up into God's presence and coming back down to nourish others.

6. **Marty's practical challenge.** Pick **one or two specific relationships** this week or month. Ask what from your own story could be refreshing and life-giving to that person, and how deep or shallow that sharing should be.

### Potential Areas for Further Exploration

- **The *raqia* in ancient Near Eastern cosmology:** how Israel's creation account compares with Babylonian accounts such as the *Enuma Elish*, where the sky is formed by splitting a defeated chaos monster.
- **Creation–tabernacle parallels:** scholarly work (for example, by John Walton, Gordon Wenham, and Jon Levenson) on how Genesis 1 presents the cosmos as a temple and how the Mishkan works as a small-scale creation.
- **The veil in the New Testament:** the tearing of the temple curtain (Matthew 27:51) and Hebrews 10:19–22, which speaks of entering "through the curtain, that is, his body."
- **Cherubim as boundary guardians:** the link between Genesis 3:24, the woven cherubim on the veil, and the cherubim over the Ark.
- **Rain as blessing in Scripture:** Deuteronomy 11:13–17, Isaiah 55:10–11 (God's word compared to rain that does not return empty), and Hosea 6:3.
- **Graded holiness:** how the Mishkan's zones of increasing holiness shape ideas about community, intimacy, and spiritual formation.
- **Josh Bosse's Mishkan series (episodes 325–331):** going back to episode 326 for the full teaching behind this reflection.
- **Sabbath as practice:** how guided reflection and silence fit within Jewish and Christian Sabbath traditions.

### Comprehension Questions

1. What does God separate on the second day of creation, and how does that separation make room for life rather than simply dividing things?
2. According to Exodus 26, what was the purpose of the 50 loops and 50 gold clasps on the tabernacle curtains, and how does this connect to the theme of unity?
3. What did the veil separate inside the Mishkan, and what does that layered arrangement suggest about how people come near to God's presence?
4. Marty asks how we can "draw boundaries that ease the burden on others." Give an example of a boundary that is an act of love rather than self-protection.
5. How does the image of rain moving between the waters above and below describe the way believers carry God's presence into their relationships?

### Personalized Summary

This reflection asks you to slow down and sit with a simple pattern: God creates by separating, and God brings people near by joining. The waters above and below, the curtains clasped into one tent, the frames that define the space, and the veil that guards the Most Holy Place all show that boundaries and unity work together in love. Your stories of meeting God, including the ones from dark seasons, are not meant to stay locked away. They are also not meant to be poured out the same way on everyone. Like rain, you are invited to keep moving: into God's presence for renewal, then out to the people around you, giving each person what they can receive. Marty's challenge is concrete. Choose one or two relationships this week and ask what you could bring from your own story that would refresh, nourish, and draw that person closer to God's presence.
