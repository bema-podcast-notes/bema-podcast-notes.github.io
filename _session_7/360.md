### Title & Source Summary

**Episode:** 360: Session 7 Capstone (Sabbatical Reflections and the Season of Rest)  
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon and Brent Billings  
**Focus:** Sabbath and sabbatical, Ignatian spiritual practice, and how different views worked together across Session 7's episodes on spiritual disciplines (with a nod to Ecclesiastes 3, "a time for this and a time for that")

This episode closes Session 7 of the BEMA Podcast. It is Marty's first time back in the recording booth after a 100-day sabbatical. Marty reflects on what the time away was and was not. It was not a source of dramatic new insight. It was renewal through rest, play, creativity and a 10-week Ignatian prayer retreat with a spiritual director. Marty and Brent then look back at the session's mirror-image (chiastic) structure. The first half was a set of guided reflection episodes. The second half featured different hosts sharing their own spiritual practices. Marty's main takeaway is that variety of thought, and even disagreement, is good and healthy for a faith community. The hosts close with book recommendations on fasting and prayer and a preview of the coming study of Isaiah.

### Key Takeaways

- **Sabbatical doesn't have to produce something profound.** Marty came back with no new ministry, vision or big revelation. The point was to be refreshed, which he calls "just Jesus."
- **Sabbatical is a longer form of Sabbath:** stopping, resting, worshiping, playing, and working in a new rhythm.
- **Real rest changes how you see work.** Marty expected to dread returning to work, but by day 100 he felt ready to go back.
- **Slow, repeated prayer lets you face hard things.** The Ignatian exercise let Marty stay with his struggles with worry and anxiety instead of praying briefly and moving on.
- **The simplicity was the profound part.** Meeting with Jesus in the same place, in the same way, every day for 10 weeks built intimacy.
- **Different views are a feature, not a flaw.** The hosts of Session 7 disagreed at times, and Marty sees that as good.
- **"Of course" thinking:** Marty suggests answering disagreement with "of course they see it differently" instead of feeling it must be fixed or settled.
- **There is no "one right way"** to practice spiritual disciplines. Truth gets clearer as many people add to the conversation and push back on each other.
- **This goes beyond podcasting.** It applies to families, friendships, churches and ministries.
- **Don't rank what God is doing.** Different episodes and teaching styles speak to different people. Listeners can take what they need from what God is doing through others.

### Main Concepts & Theories

#### 1. Sabbatical as Extended Sabbath

Marty presents sabbatical as a longer version of the weekly Sabbath rhythm. Its parts include:

1. **Stopping:** stepping away from normal work and its demands
2. **Resting:** letting yourself be refreshed without having to produce anything
3. **Worship:** time set aside for God
4. **Play:** taking up life-giving activities usually crowded out by schedules. For Marty this meant drumming every day and cooking, especially smoking meat and making chili.
5. **Working differently:** work in a new kind of rhythm, not the absence of all work

His friend Dave, also on sabbatical, named a common pressure. People on sabbatical feel they owe everyone a profound discovery when they return. Dave's reminder, which Marty shares, is that this is not what sabbatical is for.

#### 2. Ignatian Spirituality and the Spiritual Exercises

Marty worked with a separate spiritual director just for his sabbatical and went through a form of the Spiritual Exercises of **Ignatius of Loyola**, the 16th-century founder of the Jesuits. He is not the same person as the early church father Ignatius of Antioch.

- **The full Exercises:** an intensive, secluded retreat. Marty describes it as about 40 days; the traditional form runs about 30 days.
- **The 19th Annotation:** Ignatius saw that most people (married, with children or jobs) couldn't leave for a month. He allowed the Exercises to be done "in daily life" over many months. Marty describes it as about 40 weeks.
- **The 18th Annotation:** a shorter, introductory adaptation for those who can't take on the full form. Marty did a 10-week version, which he calls "19th annotation light." This explains the "typo" Brent caught between Marty's blog post and his video.
- **A "school of prayer":** the Jesuits describe the Exercises this way because they teach many different ways of praying.

#### 3. The Chiastic Structure of Session 7

A **chiasm** is a literary structure where ideas are arranged in mirrored order (A–B–C–B′–A′), with the key point at the center. BEMA often points out chiasms in biblical texts. Brent noticed, mostly by accident, that Session 7 formed one:

- **First half:** guided reflection episodes with custom music, nature sounds and bird song
- **Center:** the interview with Kat Armstrong. Brent moved her episode back a week after he saw the pattern.
- **Second half:** episodes on practices, with different hosts sharing their spiritual disciplines

#### 4. Diversity of Thought and Healthy Disagreement

This is Marty's main theme for the episode. During recording, Reid and L, another of the session's guest hosts, both showed a hidden worry about contradicting other hosts. L joked, "maybe we can have a fight." Marty finds three sources of difference:

1. **Personality:** people find peace and notice God at work in different ways. Marty notes that Josh Bootsma, Reid and he are all wired differently.
2. **Real disagreement:** sometimes hosts honestly hold different positions.
3. **Paradox and language:** sometimes the difference is only where people start. Reid questioned "creating space" and preferred "attentiveness," then went on to describe ways to create space for attentiveness. Marty laughed at this. He and Reid often see the same thing but "enter through doors on different sides of the room."

Marty's conclusion is that the goal is not finding "the one right way." The goal is adding to the conversation and pushing back in ways that sharpen everyone's understanding. Brent connects this to "iron sharpening iron" (Proverbs 27:17): without disagreement, no one grows.

#### 5. Not Grading the Work of God

Listeners respond very differently to the same content. After Session 6, some called Josh's Mishkan (Tabernacle) series "peak BEMA." That fits BEMA's Genesis 1 analogy for its sessions, where the sixth session, like the sixth day when humans were created, is the high point. Others found the same series' textual connections a stretch. Some will love Session 7, and others will be glad to get back to the Bible. Marty encourages listeners not to rank content as good or bad. Instead, they can take what they need from what God is doing through the person next to them.

### Examples & Applications

- **Recovering neglected joys:** Marty drummed every day and cooked for fun. Readers can name one life-giving activity they usually skip "because life" and plan time for it.
- **Staying with a struggle:** Instead of praying quickly about anxiety and moving on, try returning to the same concern each day for several weeks and paying attention to what changes, or doesn't.
- **Consistent place, consistent practice:** Marty met with Jesus in the same spot, the same way, daily. Choosing a fixed time and place for prayer can deepen familiarity.
- **The Examen:** an Ignatian practice of reviewing your day prayerfully, noticing where God was present and where you turned away. Marty prayed it several times a week during his retreat.
- **Written prayers:** Marty likes structured prayers, such as those written by Thomas Merton or Pierre Teilhard de Chardin, to go alongside spontaneous prayer.
- **Ambient reflection:** Episode 351 is a 2.5-hour music-only bonus track built from the reflection episodes. Marty uses it as background music while working. Brent cited a study titled *Birdsongs alleviate anxiety and paranoia in healthy participants* to support the use of bird song.
- **"Of course" in a small group:** When a group member sees a passage or practice differently, try responding "Of course you see it that way. Tell me more" instead of arguing to resolve it.
- **Friendship as a model:** Marty admires Reid and Derek's friendship (Episode 356) as one worth imitating, even if their humor isn't for everyone.

### Potential Areas for Further Exploration

1. **The Spiritual Exercises of Ignatius of Loyola:** the full structure, the 18th and 19th Annotations, and how to find a spiritual director
2. **The Examen:** its history and modern versions for daily practice
3. **Scot McKnight, *Fasting*:** Marty's favorite book on fasting. It frames fasting as a response: a serious event (A) leads to fasting (B), and God's response follows (C).
4. **Strahan Coleman, *Beholding*:** a book on "beholding prayer" and intimacy with Jesus. Marty recommends it, and it was a gift from a listener.
5. **Sabbath theology:** how weekly Sabbath, sabbatical years (Leviticus 25) and modern ministry sabbaticals relate
6. **Chiasm in Scripture:** how mirrored structures highlight central meaning in biblical texts
7. **Ecclesiastes 3:** "a time for every purpose under heaven" and the rhythms of rest and work
8. **Contemplative writers:** Thomas Merton and Pierre Teilhard de Chardin
9. **Isaiah:** preparing for BEMA's upcoming study, which will go deeper than before but not verse by verse. It will open with two introductory episodes by Marty and Reid.

### Personalized Summary

This capstone is a gentle, honest close to a season about rest. Marty came back from 100 days away with no grand vision. He came back renewed, ready to work again, and closer to Jesus after 10 weeks of simple, repeated prayer. That included staying with his long struggle with worry and anxiety instead of rushing past it. His sabbatical shows that rest doesn't have to justify itself with results. Looking back over Session 7, Marty's biggest takeaway is how good it is to have different views. The hosts don't all pray, rest or practice the disciplines the same way, and sometimes they disagree. That is the point. When we stop looking for "the one right way" and answer differences with "of course," disagreement becomes iron sharpening iron. For your own practice, ask where you might need to stop, play, and stay with Jesus a little longer, and whose different view might be just what you need to hear.
