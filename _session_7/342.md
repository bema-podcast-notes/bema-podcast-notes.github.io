### Title & Source Summary

**Episode:** 342 — Reflection: Teaching (Session 7 Reflection Series)  
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon and Brent Billings (the episode follows Josh Bosse's Mishkan series, Episode 328)  
**Focus:** Genesis 1:14–19 (the fourth day of creation); Exodus 27:20–21 (the oil for the lamp); Exodus 28:1–38 (the priests and their sacred garments)

This episode is part of a series of seven guided reflections in Session 7. They are modeled on Josh Bosse's Mishkan (tabernacle) series, which closed Session 6. The reflection is based on Episode 328, where Josh linked the fourth day of creation, when God made the sun, moon, and stars to mark "sacred times," to the next items in the tabernacle: the olive oil that kept the lamp burning, and the priests in their sacred garments. The episode has very little teaching. Brent reads the creation text again and again alongside the tabernacle passages. Marty asks reflection questions, and long stretches of silence follow for the listener to think. The main theme is that teaching happens over time, in community, and most of all through the way a person lives, not only through what they say.

### Key Takeaways

- **Teaching is a communal role.** The reflection asks how each person can take part in teaching *with* the community, not just receive it from experts.
- **The heavenly lights teach without words.** On the fourth day, the sun, moon, and stars "serve as signs to mark sacred times." They give light and order time simply by being where God placed them.
- **The lamp needs steady tending.** Aaron and his sons keep the lamp burning "from evening till morning" as "a lasting ordinance… for the generations to come." Teaching is a long, faithful practice, not a one-time event.
- **Priests are recognized by what they wear.** The sacred garments give Aaron "dignity and honor" and make his role visible. This prompts the question of what marks someone as a priest today.
- **The priest carries the people.** Aaron bears the names of the twelve tribes on his shoulders and over his heart. A priestly role means carrying others before God.
- **Presence teaches.** The central question is what it would look like to teach the kingdom of God "not with words, but simply with your presence."
- **Everyday life is priestly work.** Marty's closing: "the way we live, the things we do, the postures we take, the energy we give and the way we stop. These are priestly roles in our world."

### Main Concepts & Theories

#### 1. The Reflection Format of Session 7

- Session 7 is meant to be different from earlier sessions. It is slower and more meditative, and it invites personal reflection instead of delivering lectures.
- Each reflection follows one of Josh Bosse's Mishkan episodes. Episode 328 is the source for this one.
- The structure repeats:
  1. Brent reads Genesis 1:14–19 (the fourth day).
  2. Brent reads a passage about the tabernacle.
  3. Marty asks a reflection question.
  4. Several minutes of silence follow for the listener to think.
- Repeating the creation text over and over is part of the method. It works like a refrain that keeps tying each tabernacle detail back to the creation story.

#### 2. The Fourth Day: Lights, Signs, and Sacred Time

- In Genesis 1:14–19, God creates "lights in the vault of the sky" for three purposes:
  - To **separate** day from night and light from darkness
  - To **serve as signs** marking "sacred times, and days and years"
  - To **give light** on the earth and to **govern** the day and the night
- The word often translated "sacred times" (Hebrew *mo'adim*) is the same word later used for Israel's appointed feasts. So the heavenly lights do more than keep time. They set the rhythm of worship.
- Josh's connection is between **teaching and time**. People learn through rhythms and seasons, and through habits repeated over years. The heavenly bodies teach the calendar without saying anything.

#### 3. The Oil and the Ever-Burning Lamp (Exodus 27:20–21)

- The Israelites are to bring "clear oil of pressed olives." This is the purest grade of oil, and it comes from crushing.
- The lamp stands in the tent of meeting, just outside the curtain in front of the Ark.
- Aaron and his sons tend it "from evening till morning," which matches the creation refrain "there was evening and there was morning."
- In the lens of this reflection:
  - The **lamp** is to the tabernacle what the **lights** are to creation: a source of light that marks time.
  - The **oil** must be supplied by the whole community. The priests tend the light, but the people supply the fuel, which makes teaching a shared effort.
  - The **ordinance is lasting**: faithful light is passed from one generation to the next.

#### 4. The Priests and Their Sacred Garments (Exodus 28:1–5)

- God sets apart Aaron and his sons (Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar) to serve as priests.
- The garments are made "to give him dignity and honor," and skilled workers make them using wisdom that God has given them.
- There are six items: breastpiece, ephod, robe, woven tunic, turban, and sash.
- The materials (gold, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, and fine linen) match the tabernacle itself. The priest is dressed like the Mishkan, so he becomes a kind of walking sanctuary.
- **Reflection question:** "How do you identify someone as a priest?" Marty asks it two ways:
  - **Literally:** by their garments and their official role
  - **Poetically and broadly:** by what you can see in a person's life that signals a priestly role

#### 5. The Ephod and Breastpiece: Carrying the People (Exodus 28:6–21, 29–30)

- **Shoulder stones:** Two onyx stones, each engraved with six tribal names "in the order of their birth," sit on the shoulders "as a memorial before the Lord." The priest literally carries the people.
- **Breastpiece of decision:** It has twelve precious stones in four rows, each engraved with a tribe's name. Aaron wears these names "over his heart" whenever he enters the Holy Place.
- **Urim and Thummim:** These are placed in the breastpiece as "the means of making decisions for the Israelites." The priest helps the people discern God's will.
- **Reflection question:** "What could it look like to teach the kingdom of God? Not with words, but simply with your presence."
- The connection: Aaron's garments speak before he says anything. Anyone who sees him knows he represents the people before God and God before the people.

#### 6. The Robe, the Bells, and the Golden Plate (Exodus 28:31–38)

- **Robe of the ephod:** It is entirely blue and has a reinforced collar "so that it will not tear."
- **Pomegranates and golden bells:** These alternate around the hem, so the priest's movement in and out of the Holy Place can be heard.
- **Golden plate on the turban:** It is engraved "Holy to the Lord" and worn on Aaron's forehead so that the people's gifts "will be acceptable to the Lord."
- In the lens of this reflection:
  - The **bells** make the priest's comings and goings audible. His presence makes a sound, and you hear him before you see him.
  - The **plate** shows his identity, devotion to God, in the most visible place on his body.
  - The priest takes on responsibility for the community ("he will bear the guilt involved in the sacred gifts").
- **Reflection question:** "How do we reveal qualities of time? How do we fill our light?" The transcript breaks up here because of audio repetition. The question seems to ask how our lives can show the sacredness of time, the way the lights and the lamp do.

#### 7. Synthesis: Teaching with Our Lives

- The heavenly lights, the lamp, and the priest all teach by **being** and **shining**, not by speaking.
- Marty's conclusion lists five ways a life teaches:
  1. **The way we live**
  2. **The things we do**
  3. **The postures we take**
  4. **The energy we give**
  5. **The way we stop.** This likely refers to Sabbath and to keeping sacred rhythms of rest, which ties back to the "sacred times" of the fourth day.
- The broader idea behind this is the priesthood of all of God's people (compare Exodus 19:6 and 1 Peter 2:9). Teaching is not limited to official teachers.

### Examples & Applications

- **A grandparent's faithfulness:** Many people say their faith was shaped less by sermons than by watching a grandparent pray, give, or stay faithful through suffering. That is teaching by presence.
- **Keeping a weekly Sabbath:** A family that reliably stops work, puts phones away, and shares a meal teaches children what sacred time is without a lecture. It is "the way we stop."
- **Tending the lamp in community:** Volunteers who show up every week, set up chairs, lead the children, and bring meals are "bringing the oil." Teaching in a community depends on everyone who keeps the light going, not just the person speaking.
- **Carrying names on your heart:** Keeping a prayer list of specific people and praying for them regularly mirrors Aaron bearing the tribes' names "over his heart."
- **Visible identity at work:** Integrity, patience, and generosity in a workplace can work like the "Holy to the Lord" plate: a visible sign of who you belong to, recognized before you say anything about faith.
- **Hearing someone before you see them:** Like the bells on the hem, some people bring a noticeable change into a room, such as calm, joy, or attention. Others feel their presence.
- **Mentorship over time:** The lamp burned "from evening till morning" for generations. Discipleship relationships that last for years often do more than short programs.

### Potential Areas for Further Exploration

- **Episode 328 (Josh Bosse):** Listen to or revisit the original conversation that this reflection is built on.
- **The Session 7 introduction:** Review why the session uses a reflective format.
- **Hebrew *mo'adim*:** Trace the word from Genesis 1:14 to Leviticus 23, the appointed feasts, and consider how creation sets up Israel's worship calendar.
- **The priesthood of all believers:** Exodus 19:6 ("a kingdom of priests"), 1 Peter 2:5–9, and Revelation 1:6.
- **The Urim and Thummim:** What they may have been, how they were used, and what "means of making decisions" suggests about the priest's role in helping people discern.
- **The menorah and light imagery:** Compare Exodus 25:31–40, Zechariah 4, Matthew 5:14–16 ("you are the light of the world"), and Revelation 1:12–20.
- **Tabernacle–creation parallels:** Explore scholarship on how the tabernacle passages mirror the seven days of creation, for example the seven speeches in Exodus 25–31.
- **High priestly garments in later tradition:** Explore how Second Temple Judaism and the book of Hebrews interpret the high priest's garments.
- **Contemplative practices:** Consider how practices like *lectio divina* and silent reflection shape spiritual formation compared with lecture-based learning.

### Comprehension Questions

1. What three purposes does God give the heavenly lights in Genesis 1:14–19, and how does the theme of "sacred times" connect to the idea of teaching?
2. Who supplies the oil for the tabernacle lamp, and who tends it? What does this division of roles suggest about how teaching works in a community?
3. Where does Aaron carry the names of the twelve tribes on his garments, and what does each location suggest about the priestly role?
4. What do the golden bells on the robe's hem and the "Holy to the Lord" plate on the turban show about how a priest's presence is noticed?
5. Marty lists five ways we teach with our lives. Name them and explain how "the way we stop" ties back to the fourth day of creation.

### Personalized Summary

This reflection slows things down on purpose. It does not explain the tabernacle; it asks you to sit inside it. By pairing the fourth day of creation with the lamp and the priestly garments over and over, the episode keeps returning to one idea: the most lasting teaching often happens without words. The sun and moon never give a lecture, but they order every day and season. Aaron's garments spoke before he did, showing that he carried the people on his shoulders and over his heart, and that his whole life was "Holy to the Lord." The invitation is personal. You are part of the community's teaching, whether by bringing oil, tending the light, or simply being present in a way that makes the kingdom visible. How you live, what you do, the postures you take, the energy you give, and how you rest are all priestly work. The question to carry forward is what someone would learn about God just by watching your life.
