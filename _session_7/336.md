### Title & Source Summary

**Episode:** 336, Session 7 Sabbath Reflection #1: The First Day of Creation and the First Items of the Mishkan  
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon and Brent Billings  
**Focus:** Genesis 1:1–5 (the first day of creation), Exodus 25:10–22 (the Ark of the Covenant and its atonement cover), Exodus 25:23–30 (the Table of the Bread of the Presence), and Exodus 25:31–40 (the Lampstand)

This episode opens Session 7 of the BEMA Podcast. The session starts with seven guided Sabbath reflections instead of teaching. They build on the series Josh Bosse used to close Session 6, which matched each of the seven days of creation to the furnishings of the Mishkan (Tabernacle). Episode 325 guides this first reflection. In that episode, Josh tied the first day of creation, when God brings light out of darkness, to the theme of *truth* and to the first items described in Exodus 25: the Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand. Brent reads Genesis 1:1–5 four times, each reading paired with one Mishkan passage, and long stretches of silence are left for reflection. Marty gives three reflection prompts. They move from physical reminders of God's realness, to practices and places that feed the spirit, to weaving the two into a restful relationship with God. He closes by inviting listeners to set up "standing stones" and to try new spiritual practices without fear of failing.

### Key Takeaways

- **Session 7 starts with practice, not lecture.** The first seven episodes are Sabbath reflections, meant to apply the Mishkan lessons instead of adding more information.
- **The end of Session 6 is the start of Session 7.** Josh Bosse's creation-to-Mishkan series is the anchor, and Episode 325 guides this reflection.
- **Day one of creation is linked to truth.** God speaking light into darkness shows truth breaking into chaos. The first Mishkan furnishings echo that moment.
- **God meets people in physical, tangible ways.** The Ark is the place where God says, "I will meet with you." The Table holds bread "before me at all times." The Lampstand lights the space in front of it.
- **Physical objects can serve as "standing stones."** Objects with a story can connect us to God, especially in dark times, as long as they point to God and are not worshiped themselves.
- **Spiritual nourishment often comes through places and practices.** Being outdoors or doing certain activities can feed the soul, much as the Bread of the Presence pictured God's ongoing provision.
- **The goal is a natural, restful relationship with God.** Objects and practices should fit together into a rhythm of life, not become a list of religious duties.
- **Experimenting is encouraged.** Marty urges listeners to try things, accept that some ideas won't work, and not fear failing as they look for new spiritual spaces.

### Main Concepts & Theories

#### 1. The Structure of a Sabbath Reflection Episode

This episode departs from BEMA's usual teaching format. It is built as a guided meditation:

1. **The anchor text:** Genesis 1:1–5, read four times, opens each section and closes the episode.
2. **The paired text:** each of the first three readings is followed by a Mishkan passage from Exodus 25.
3. **The prompt:** Marty asks one reflection question.
4. **The silence:** several minutes of unstructured time are left for the listener.

Repeating the same text several times is an old practice, close to *lectio divina* or Jewish habits of repeated study. Each time through, the familiar words can strike the listener differently depending on what came just before.

#### 2. Creation and the Mishkan: The Parallel Framework

Josh Bosse's series, which this session builds on, rests on a long-recognized pattern: the building of the Tabernacle echoes the creation story.

- In Genesis, God brings order out of chaos and makes a space where heaven and earth meet. Eden is a garden-sanctuary.
- In Exodus, Israel builds a portable sanctuary where God's presence can dwell among the people.
- Both accounts have sevenfold structures and similar wording, such as "God saw that it was good" and Moses inspecting the finished work.

**Day One and truth:** On the first day, God speaks, "Let there be light," into a world that is "formless and empty" with darkness over the deep. Light reveals what is real. That is why truth fits as the theme: truth lets us see reality as it is.

#### 3. The First Three Furnishings of the Mishkan

| Furnishing | Passage | Key Details | Reflection Theme |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ark of the Covenant** | Exodus 25:10–22 | Acacia wood covered in gold; carried on poles that are never removed; holds the tablets of the covenant; topped by an atonement cover (mercy seat) with two cherubim facing each other | Physical things that remind us God is real |
| **Table of the Bread of the Presence** | Exodus 25:23–30 | Acacia wood covered in gold; plates, dishes, pitchers, and bowls for offerings; bread kept "before me at all times" | Actions or places that nourish the spirit |
| **Lampstand (Menorah)** | Exodus 25:31–40 | Hammered from one piece of pure gold; six branches plus the center shaft; cups shaped like almond blossoms; seven lamps lighting the space in front of it; made "according to the pattern shown you on the mountain" | Weaving it all together into a restful life with God |

**Notes on each item:**

- **The Ark** is where God promises, "There… I will meet with you." It is the clearest physical sign of God's presence among Israel. The poles that are never removed suggest God's presence is always ready to move with the people.
- **The Table** holds the bread that is always "before" God. It pictures ongoing fellowship, provision, and a shared meal between God and the people.
- **The Lampstand** is shaped like a flowering almond tree, which recalls the Tree of Life in Eden, and its light ties back directly to "Let there be light." It brings the whole sequence together, so it fits the integrating third question.

#### 4. The Three Reflection Prompts

1. **After the Ark:** *"What things in your life, physical things, material things, remind you of the realness of God? Is there anything that you cling to when you are in darkness that connects you to God?"*
2. **After the Table:** *"What tangible actions or spaces nourish your spirit? Is there somewhere that you go or something that you do that fills you up?"*
3. **After the Lampstand:** *"How can you weave these things, these physical things that connect you to God, and these practices or spaces that nourish your spirit, together into a natural and restful relationship with God?"*

The prompts build on each other. The first is about **objects**, the second about **practices and places**, and the third about **bringing them together**.

#### 5. Standing Stones

In his closing, Marty returns to a familiar BEMA idea: **standing stones**. In Joshua 4, Israel piled up stones from the Jordan River so later generations would ask, "What do these stones mean?" and hear the story of God's faithfulness.

- Marty describes the top of his bookshelves as a place for his own "little standing stones," objects that each carry a story.
- In his hardest seasons, he sometimes sits in his office chair holding these objects and remembering the stories behind them.
- He warns that this is **not idolatry**. The objects don't hold power. They help us remember.

### Examples & Applications

- **Marty's bookshelf:** a dedicated shelf of meaningful objects he holds and remembers with during dark times. It is a model listeners can copy.
- **A memory shelf or desk spot:** a stone from a meaningful trip, a letter from a mentor, a grandparent's Bible, or a hospital bracelet from a hard season you came through can all act as standing stones.
- **Nourishing places:** Marty mentions being outdoors and "spending time on the top of the mountain." Others might find this in a morning walk, gardening, a quiet coffee shop corner, or a weekly hike.
- **Nourishing practices:** cooking a Sabbath meal, journaling, playing music, sharing a table with friends. These echo the Bread of the Presence as ongoing fellowship.
- **Combining objects and practices:** you might start a weekly Sabbath walk that ends at home with a few minutes holding a standing stone object, so the place and the object work together.
- **Experimenting:** try a new practice for one week, such as lighting a candle at dinner to echo the Lampstand. If it doesn't help, change it or drop it. In Marty's words, "We don't lose anything here."

### Potential Areas for Further Exploration

1. **The creation–Tabernacle parallels:** Study how Genesis 1–2 and Exodus 25–40 mirror each other, and how scholars read the Tabernacle as a "new creation" or a return to Eden.
2. **Episode 325 and Josh Bosse's full series:** Go back to the end of Session 6 to hear the full teaching behind each of the seven reflections.
3. **The atonement cover (*kapporet*):** Look into the Hebrew word and its link to *kippur* (atonement), and how the cover appears in Leviticus 16 and Romans 3:25.
4. **The Menorah as the Tree of Life:** Explore the almond blossom imagery, Aaron's staff that budded (Numbers 17), and the almond-tree wordplay in Jeremiah 1:11–12 (*shaqed* / *shoqed*, "almond" / "watching").
5. **Standing stones in Scripture:** Trace the theme through Genesis 28 (Jacob at Bethel), Joshua 4, and 1 Samuel 7:12 (Ebenezer).
6. **Material things in spiritual practice:** Think about the line between honoring God through physical objects and idolatry, using the Second Commandment and Jewish practices such as mezuzot and tefillin.
7. **Sabbath as rest, not performance:** Look at how Sabbath theology, for example Abraham Joshua Heschel's *The Sabbath*, frames rest as a "palace in time" rather than another obligation.

### Personal Summary

This episode was a deliberate slowdown. Instead of new teaching, it gave space to take in what the Mishkan has been teaching. Each time Genesis 1:1–5 was read, it was a reminder that God speaks light into darkness and that truth breaks into chaos. Pairing it with the Ark, the Table, and the Lampstand showed that God has always chosen to meet people in tangible ways: a place to meet, bread always before Him, and light filling the space. Marty's three questions lead to a practical invitation. Name the objects that remind you God is real when life gets dark, name the places and practices that refill you, and then fit them together into a rhythm that feels restful rather than forced. His bookshelf of standing stones is a simple, relatable example. The main lesson this week is to try something: set out a meaningful object, go back to a place that restores you, and accept that experimenting with faith is itself part of the Sabbath rest.
