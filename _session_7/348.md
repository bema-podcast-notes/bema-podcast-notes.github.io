### Title & Source Summary

**Episode:** 348 – Reflection Seven: Trust (Sabbath and the Mishkan)  
**Hosts:** Marty Solomon and Brent Billings  
**Focus:** Genesis 2:1–3; Exodus 31:1–11; Exodus 31:12–15; Exodus 31:16–18

This episode closes the seven-part reflection series that makes up the first half of BEMA Session 7. It doesn't follow the usual teaching format. Brent reads Scripture aloud, Marty offers short prompts to "consider and reflect," and long pauses leave room for silent meditation. The theme of trust comes from Josh Bossé's seventh Mishkan (Tabernacle) reflection in Episode 331. That reflection tied the seventh day of creation to the last item in the Mishkan instructions, which is the Sabbath. The readings set God's rest at creation beside the gifted craftsmen Bezalel and Oholiab, who build the Tabernacle, and beside the Sabbath command given as a lasting covenant sign. Together they raise one question: do we trust God enough to stop working? The episode ends by previewing a conversation with Kat Armstrong and the second half of the session, which will focus on spiritual practice.

### Key Takeaways

- **Sabbath is an act of trust.** To stop working is to believe that God, not our own effort, keeps the world going.
- **Making things and resting belong together.** The Mishkan instructions in Exodus 31 end with the Sabbath command, just as the creation story ends with God resting on the seventh day.
- **Our gifts are given to us and they matter.** God filled Bezalel with his Spirit, wisdom, understanding, knowledge and skill. Our abilities can help make spaces where God's kingdom can thrive.
- **Pausing our gifts is part of the work.** Setting our skills down on the Sabbath is itself part of making space for God.
- **Sabbath is a covenant sign.** It marks God's people and reminds them that God is "the Lord who makes you holy." Holiness comes from God, not from how much we produce.
- **Sabbath should be about relationship, not just activity.** Marty asks whether our spiritual lives connect with God at the heart level or are "all action and spiritual production."
- **A practice should be passed on.** The final prompt asks whether we know the Sabbath well enough to explain it and hand it down to the next generation.
- **Even God was "refreshed."** Exodus 31:17 says God rested *and was refreshed*. Rest is something God himself models, not a weakness to put up with.

### Main Concepts & Theories

#### 1. The Structure of Session 7's Reflections

- Session 7 opened with seven guided reflections, one for each of Josh Bossé's seven Mishkan reflections at the end of Session 6.
- Each episode pairs Scripture reading with reflection questions and silence, so listeners take part instead of just hearing information.
- This seventh reflection uses Episode 331, where Josh's theme was **trust** and his focus was the **seventh day**.

#### 2. The Creation–Tabernacle Parallel

- **Genesis 2:1–3:** God finishes his work, rests on the seventh day, blesses it and makes it holy.
- **Exodus 31:1–11:** God names Bezalel (tribe of Judah) and Oholiab son of Ahisamak (tribe of Dan) and fills them with skill to build the tent of meeting, the ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars, the basin, the priestly garments, the anointing oil and the incense.
- **Exodus 31:12–18:** Right after the building instructions comes the Sabbath command.
- **What the parallel means:** The Mishkan is pictured as a small-scale creation, a space where God lives among his people. As the creation account ends in rest, the Tabernacle instructions end in Sabbath. Building sacred space and resting in God's presence belong to the same story.

#### 3. Spirit-Filled Skill

- Bezalel is the first person in Scripture described as "filled with the Spirit of God." The filling shows up as craftsmanship: metalwork, stone cutting, woodworking and design.
- This widens the idea of what "spiritual" gifts can be. Practical and artistic talents can be expressions of God's Spirit.
- God also gave ability to "all the skilled workers," so building the Mishkan was a shared, community effort.

#### 4. Sabbath as Covenant Sign

- Exodus 31:13 calls the Sabbath "a sign between me and you for the generations to come."
- Its purpose: "so you may know that I am the Lord who makes you holy."
- The severe penalties (death, being "cut off") show how central the Sabbath was to Israel's identity. Breaking it meant rejecting the covenant relationship itself.
- Verse 16 calls it a "lasting covenant," which is why the closing prompt focuses on passing the practice on.

#### 5. Trust and Rest

- The series keeps returning to two linked themes: **rest** and **trust**.
- Stopping work exposes what we depend on. If we cannot stop, we may be trusting our own productivity more than God's provision.
- Marty's closing prayer: "May we trust the story enough to find a space to rest. May that rest remind us of who God is and how much He loves us."

#### 6. Reflection Prompts in This Episode

1. **Gifts and space:** Do we have the faith and faithfulness to use our abilities to create spaces for God's kingdom? Do we see setting those gifts down as part of creating that space? Do we trust this enough to keep the Sabbath?
2. **Personal Sabbath:** Do you have a space of Sabbath in your life? Does it help you reflect on God's nature and how it relates to you? Is there room for heart-level connection, or is it all action and spiritual production?
3. **Passing it on:** Do you know the practice of Sabbath well enough that it makes a difference for the people around you? Could you explain it to the generations after you?

### Examples & Applications

- **The craftsperson's Sabbath:** A carpenter, designer, coder or musician spends six days using God-given skill to build something good. On the seventh day they put the tools down on purpose, trusting that the work and their worth don't depend on nonstop effort.
- **Ministry leaders and "spiritual production":** Pastors and volunteers can fill their weeks with church activity and still have no unhurried time with God. A real Sabbath might mean a day without ministry tasks, used for prayer, rest and enjoying God.
- **Families passing it down:** A household might keep a weekly Sabbath meal with candles, a blessing, Scripture and conversation, so children learn the rhythm by living it, much like the Jewish Shabbat dinner tradition.
- **Marty's sabbatical:** Marty was on sabbatical when this episode aired. It was recorded in advance, so the hosts were practicing the rest they were talking about.
- **Digital Sabbath:** In a world that's always online, one practical step is a set block of time without screens, email or social media, to break the habit of constant output.
- **Silence:** The episode's long pauses are a practice in themselves. Listeners can copy this by reading a short passage slowly, then sitting in silence before responding.

### Potential Areas for Further Exploration

- **Josh Bossé's Mishkan series (Session 6, ending with Episode 331):** Revisit all seven reflections to see the full creation–Tabernacle framework.
- **Literary parallels between Genesis 1–2 and Exodus 25–40:** Study how scholars note "seven speeches" in the Tabernacle instructions that echo the seven days of creation.
- **Bezalel and Spirit-empowered work:** Compare Exodus 31 and 35 with New Testament teaching on spiritual gifts (1 Corinthians 12; Romans 12).
- **Sabbath in the Prophets and Gospels:** Explore Isaiah 58:13–14, Jeremiah 17, and Jesus's Sabbath teachings (Mark 2:23–3:6) to see how the practice was understood and disputed.
- **Hebrews 4 and "Sabbath rest":** Look at how the New Testament talks about a remaining rest for God's people.
- **Abraham Joshua Heschel's *The Sabbath*:** A classic Jewish reflection on the Sabbath as "a palace in time."
- **Kat Armstrong's Bible studies:** These are featured in the next episode as an accessible way to begin spiritual practice, alone or in a group.
- **Spiritual practices in the second half of Session 7:** Follow the hosts as they share their own approaches to spiritual practice.

### Comprehension Questions

1. How do Genesis 2:1–3 and Exodus 31 parallel each other? What does placing the Sabbath command right after the Tabernacle instructions suggest about the relationship between building and resting?
2. What qualities did God give Bezalel and Oholiab? How does their story widen our understanding of what it means to be "filled with the Spirit of God"?
3. According to Exodus 31:13, what is the purpose of the Sabbath as a sign between God and Israel, and what does it teach about where holiness comes from?
4. Why does Marty frame Sabbath as an issue of *trust*? What might it reveal about us if we find it very hard to stop working?
5. What does it mean for Sabbath to be a "lasting covenant" for "generations to come"? Why does the final reflection prompt focus on being able to explain the practice and pass it on?

### Personal Summary

This last reflection brings the series back to its center: Sabbath is about trust. God finished creation and rested, and he was even refreshed. He gave Bezalel, Oholiab and the other workers real skill to build a place for his presence, and then he told his people to stop. Our gifts matter and can make space for God's kingdom. Setting them down matters too, because it shows we believe God holds the world together and that our holiness comes from him, not from what we produce.

The episode asks three things of each of us. Do we have real Sabbath space? Does that space connect us to God's heart, or is it just more spiritual activity? Do we know the practice well enough to pass it on? Brent notes that changing long-held habits and assumptions about rest takes time, so these reflections are meant to be revisited while building a Sabbath rhythm. As Session 7 turns toward spiritual practice, the call is to trust the story enough to rest, and to let that rest remind us who God is and how much he loves us.
